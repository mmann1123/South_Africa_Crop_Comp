# Additional crop-type transfer papers and public SITS benchmarks: a missingness-shift audit

Audit date: 2026-10-28. Scope: published crop-type-mapping papers (2017-2026) that report spatial or temporal transfer of an optical satellite-time-series classifier and are NOT already in `writeup/literature/` or on the exclusion list, audited for whether different cloud/shadow/gap structure between training and holdout domains ("missingness shift") could be masquerading as transfer failure; plus an audit of the public SITS benchmarks these papers inherit cloud handling from. Reference case: AI4FoodSecurity South Africa (Sentinel-2 Western Cape 2017), where 40.6% of holdout-tile pixels lack a September observation while the training tiles have none missing, and zero-filling that month, not the region change, produced most of the apparent deep-model transfer drop (`experiments/holdout_fill_check/README.md`).

Method. Three parallel research threads (two for papers, one for benchmarks) used web search, publisher/arXiv/PMC full texts (PDFs converted with pdftotext), and shallow clones of 17 official repositories grepped in a scratchpad (never executed). Every quote is verbatim from the extracted text unless marked paraphrase. Fields that could not be verified say so explicitly. Rubric (applied as given): **3** = highly suspect (region/year changes, strong likelihood of different cloud/gap structure, no target missingness statistics, distribution-changing or undescribed gap handling, degradation attributed to phenology/spectra without testing missingness); **2** = plausible (degradation reported and gap handling described, but target gap rates not reported or not shown to match the source, or a benchmark with known cloud contamination inherited without comment); **1** = low suspicion (gap-free composites with reported valid counts per domain, identical acquisition structure, explicit missingness ablations, SAR-only, or per-domain cloud statistics compared).

Access limitations (so the writer knows what is unverified): ScienceDirect and Taylor & Francis returned 403 to unauthenticated fetches (Kerner 2022 SRS, Wang-Azzari-Lobell 2019 RSE, Konduri 2020 RSE, Xu 2020 RSE, Che 2024 GIScience & RS are audited from abstracts, precursors or public code only); PMC served a reCAPTCHA for Sudmanns 2020 and Wilson & Jetz 2016 (the latter was read from the PLOS Biology site instead); Radiant MLHub `Documentation.pdf` files (Kenya, AgriFieldNet, Spot the Crop) returned 403/not found. A subagent's request to retry a publisher with a spoofed browser user agent was denied by the permission system and not pursued.

## Key Question 1: Which additional spatial/temporal transfer papers exist, and for each, could missingness shift be masquerading as transfer failure? (audit cards and ranks)

### Takeaway
Thirteen papers were verified and carded (five rank 3, six rank 2, two rank 1). Of the thirteen, only three (Cai 2018, Wang-Di Tommaso 2020, Pham 2024) report valid-observation counts for the target domain, and only Pham 2024 runs an ablation that isolates missingness; five pipelines use literal zero fill (Kerner code, TimeSen2Crop, Gallo, Pham, and the fixed-month EuroCropsML/Sen4AgriNet-style loaders), and the two rank-1 papers are rank 1 precisely because they measured the gap structure per domain. Pham et al. 2024 (Brandenburg, same training year 2018 as DENETHOR) is independent published evidence for the reference-case mechanism: a zero-filled daily grid trained on the clearest year "performed well in the training year and poorly in unseen years" until sparsity was simulated during training, and linear interpolation of long gaps cost 20-30 OA points in sparse years.

### Card 1. Kondmann et al. 2021, DENETHOR (NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks)

Paper: https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/5b8add2a5d98b1a652ea7fd72d942dac-Paper-round2.pdf (full text read). Code: https://github.com/lukaskondmann/DENETHOR (files read: readme.md, dataset.py, s2dataset.py, random_forest.py, train_psetae.py, transforms.py).

(a) Source and target. Two 24 km x 24 km tiles in Brandenburg, Germany ("Northern Germany" in the paper; "Brandenburg, Germany" in the repo readme). Training tile: 2018 labels and imagery. Test tile: a spatially separate tile with 2019 labels and imagery. "One tile is used for training and validation, the other for testing. For the training tile, we include field masks and crop types from the year 2018 together with the respective satellite imagery. Test evaluations are based on the 2019 data." An ablation evaluates the 2018 test tile to split spatial from temporal shift.

(b) Target sensor and acquisition. Three modalities for both tiles and years: Planet Fusion daily 3 m RGB+NIR ARD (T = 365 daily steps, "we use daily observations"), Sentinel-2 L2A (12 bands at 10 m, all acquisitions, "No observations are filtered because of cloud cover (maxcc=1) to maximize temporal coverage"), Sentinel-1 GRD. The deep-learning benchmarks (Table 2) use Planet Fusion only; the random-forest fusion baseline (Table 3) uses field-mean S1/S2/Planet statistics.

(c) Cloud, shadow and gap handling. Planet Fusion: "it delivers a temporally consistent collection of images with removed clouds and shadows which in remote sensing is referred to as an analysis-ready (ARD) product. Potential gaps because of clouds are filled with different points in time. The data also includes quality assurance (QA) information that underlines from which source any pixel is taken from together with a confidence score if the observation is gap-filled." So the daily cube fed to the deep models is a vendor gap-filled (temporally interpolated/substituted) product. Sentinel-2: raw, with SCL, valid-data mask and s2cloudless probability (CLP) shipped alongside; for the RF baseline "we average all pixels of one field and consider only cloud-free observations which discards around 55% of S2 observations effectively cutting temporal resolution in half." No zero-fill, mask token or padding is applied to S2 in the paper's deep models because they do not consume S2.

(d) Target-domain missingness statistics. Not reported. The only quantitative statement is the ~55% cloudy S2 fraction above, given without year or tile. The Planet Fusion QA/gap-fill confidence layer is described but no gap-filled-pixel fraction is given for 2018 vs 2019, and Figure 1's NDVI curves are shown without any indication of which days were gap-filled.

(e) Code behaviour at inference. dataset.py (Planet loader) stacks every daily GeoTIFF in the folder into a T x D x H x W array (`image_stack = np.stack([rio.open(...).read(window=window) for tif in self.tifs])`) with no reference to the QA layer; the gap-filled values are consumed as if observed, identically for the 2018 training tile and the 2019 test tile. train_psetae.py `transform()` selects in-field pixels and zero-pads fields smaller than the image size (spatial padding only). s2dataset.py loads BANDS.npy and CLP.npy; the line that would concatenate cloud probability as an input channel is commented out (`# bands = np.concatenate([bands, clp], axis=-1)`). random_forest.py `transform(X, cld)` applies `msk = cld < 40` (s2cloudless probability < 40%) before computing max/min/median/mean/std per band, then `np.nan_to_num(X, neginf=0, posinf=0)`: cloud filtering here is a hard threshold, and the retained-observation count per field is not used as a feature or reported.

(f) Cloud likelihood in target region/season. High and year-dependent. Pham et al. 2024 (same region, Brandenburg, Fig. 1b and Supplement 1) document "large differences in annual CSO [clear sky observations] in terms of density ... and distribution ... dependent on annual weather conditions and related cloud cover", and chose 2018 as their training year precisely because it "had the highest number of clear sky observations" of 2010-2021 (https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn068235.pdf). DENETHOR therefore trains on the clearest year in a 12-year record and tests on 2019. DENETHOR itself states "on average 55% of the earth's surface is covered by clouds" and that ~55% of its S2 scenes were discarded as cloudy. Sentinel-2 coverage/cloud dynamics for Europe: Sudmanns et al. 2019, Int. J. Digital Earth, https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2019.1572799.

(g) Attribution of degradation. PseLTae validation accuracy 88.02% (2018 training tile) falls to 67.25% on the 2019 test tile; evaluating the 2018 test tile gives 78.77%, so "the spatial shift to a new tile accounts for about 9 p.p. and the temporal shift for 12 p.p. which is about 60% of the drop in total." Explanation offered: "Since the weather and growing cycles can vary notably from year to year, out-of-year generalization provides a challenge", and "Comparing the NDVI across 2018 and 2019 underlines the difficulty of out-of-year generalization in crop type mapping ... there are also significant differences, particularly for meadows in the middle of the year." Missingness, gap-fill density or the QA layer are never considered as a cause.

Suspicion rank: 3 (highly suspect).
Justification: region and year both change; the training year is the clearest in the regional record while the test year is cloudier, so the fraction of vendor gap-filled daily pixels must differ between domains; no per-domain gap statistics are given; the deep models consume a temporally interpolated/substituted daily cube whose texture depends on the local gap structure (a distribution-changing fill), and the code ignores the shipped QA confidence; the 12 p.p. out-of-year drop is attributed to weather and growing-cycle variation without any missingness test. The result is exactly the pattern of our own case (gap-filled optical input, deep temporal encoder, drop blamed on the domain). Caveat: the Planet Fusion fill is a proprietary multi-source reconstruction rather than a zero fill, so the induced shift is subtler than in our pipeline.
Confidence: medium-high. Full paper and loaders read; the 2018-vs-2019 cloud contrast comes from an independent same-region study rather than from DENETHOR's own data.
URLs: paper PDF above; https://github.com/lukaskondmann/DENETHOR; https://openreview.net/forum?id=uUa4jNMLjrL; https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dvl/publication/toker_neurips21; Pham et al. 2024 https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn068235.pdf; Sudmanns et al. https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2019.1572799.

### Card 2. Cai et al. 2018, RSE 210:35-47 (Champaign County, Landsat 2000-2015, DNN)

Paper: https://carbon.nasa.gov/pdfs/feature_may2018_2_Cai.pdf (full text read; DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2018.02.045).

(a) Source and target. Same region (Champaign County, Illinois), temporal transfer: "data from 2014 and 2015 are selected as testing data, while data from all years before 2014 were used for model training." A second experiment trains on a sliding window of years ending in 2014 and predicts 2015; a third (Fig. 11a) trains on years 2000..k and predicts year k+1; a spatial split (N/S, E/W halves) is also tested.

(b) Target sensor and acquisition. Landsat 5, 7 and 8 surface reflectance (LEDAPS/LaSRC), 1,322 scenes 2000-2015, six optical bands; 2014 had 92 scenes and 2015 90 scenes (Table 1). Field-level (USDA CLU polygon) means, "interpolated the field-level, aggregated information into a daily time step for the growing season using the Savitzky-Golay algorithm", DOY 91-270.

(c) Cloud, shadow and gap handling. "To overcome the contamination by clouds/shadows in a Landsat scene, we aggregate Landsat information by averaging values from non-cloud only pixels" within each CLU; "Even though some pixels in a polygon may be missing, there are still uncontaminated pixels that exist within most CLUs"; then "interpolated ... into a daily time step ... using the Savitzky-Golay algorithm which can further fill certain data gaps. Using different smoothing algorithms had little impact on the classification algorithm, and a simple linear interpolation has achieved almost the same performance"; and "data points that were outside of the 95% percentile of the samples conditioned for each time stamp were filtered. These filtered data were treated as missing data and the interpolation algorithm filled in the missing values." The cloud mask product used to define "non-cloud pixels" is not named (CFmask/pixel_qa is not mentioned in the text I read).

(d) Target-domain missingness statistics. Partially reported. Fig. 3 ("Data availability improvement: using CLU-based vs. pixel-based information") plots the availability percentage ("number of valid values divided by the potential maximum number ... of valid values") for pixel and CLU levels, and the authors use it per year: "if we compare results in Fig. 11(a) with Fig. 3, we can find low data availability will lead to poor performance, which can further explain low accuracies in 2004 and 2008." Table 1 gives scene counts per year. No gap-length or cloud-fraction statistics for 2014/2015 specifically are tabulated.

(e) Code. No code repository or data deposit is stated in the paper (I searched the text for "github", "code" and "available"; only data portals for Landsat and CDL are cited). What the pipeline does with missing values is therefore known only from the text: field mean of clear pixels, outlier removal, SG/linear interpolation to daily values.

(f) Cloud likelihood. Humid continental Corn Belt summer; Whitcraft et al. 2015 (RSE 156:438-447, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.10.009) show frequent early and mid-season cloud cover over agricultural areas from MODIS cloud flags; Ju and Roy 2008 (RSE 112:1196-1211, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2007.08.011) find that over the conterminous US cloud becomes a constraint "when at least two cloud-free observations are required from the same season". Landsat 7 SLC-off gaps after May 2003 add systematic striping, acknowledged by the authors.

(g) Attribution. Year-to-year drops are attributed to both data availability and phenology: "SLC-off occurred after May 31, 2003, that may explain why performance in 2002 is high but slightly decreases in 2003"; "low data availability will lead to poor performance ... low accuracies in 2004 and 2008"; "2012 is a drought year, and the temporal phenology information of crop is different from other years. As a result, the model trained with data only in normal years performs poorly." Missingness is explicitly considered as a driver, but not isolated experimentally; the spatial split result is attributed to "spatial heterogeneities".

Suspicion rank: 2 (plausible).
Justification: same region, so cloud climatology is shared, but per-year gap structure differs and the long-gap SG/linear interpolation to daily values changes the input distribution; the authors report per-year availability and explicitly link low availability to accuracy drops, which is better than most papers, yet there is no controlled ablation separating missingness from phenology, the cloud mask product is unnamed, and field-mean aggregation plus daily interpolation can hide long gaps in the test years. Not a 1 because no gap-matched comparison or missingness ablation exists; not a 3 because missingness is reported and considered.
Confidence: high (full text read).
URLs: https://carbon.nasa.gov/pdfs/feature_may2018_2_Cai.pdf; https://rsc4earth.de/publication/cai-highperformance-2018/; Whitcraft 2015 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.10.009; Ju and Roy 2008 https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/gsce_pubs/94.

### Card 3. Xu et al. 2020, DeepCropMapping, RSE 247:111946 (with Xu et al. 2019 ICML CCAI precursor)

Paper: DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2020.111946 (PII S0034425720303163). Full text is paywalled and no open copy exists (checked Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, Crossref, publisher page returned 403). Evidence below comes from (i) the official code https://github.com/Lab-IDEAS/DeepCropMapping (README, config.py, preprocessing/preprocess_ARD.ipynb, experiments/spatial_transfer.ipynb, models/dcm.py, utils/helper.py all read) and (ii) the open precursor paper, Xu et al. 2019, "Efficient Multi-temporal and In-season Crop Mapping with Landsat ARD via LSTM networks", ICML CCAI workshop, https://ccai-papers.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/icml2019/25/paper.pdf (full text read).

(a) Source and target. Code: six Landsat ARD sites in the US Corn Belt (Site_A to Site_F), years 2015-2018; spatial_transfer.ipynb trains on BASE_SITES = ["Site_A"] and evaluates on TEST_SITE = "Site_B", TEST_YEARS = [2018], with per-site hyperparameters in the README. Precursor: one 51 km x 51 km site in north-central Iowa (ARD tile h18v07), "we trained models on the data from 2015 to 2017 and validated them by comparing results with the reference classes in 2018" (temporal transfer). The RSE paper's exact site locations and which site pairs were tested could not be found (paywalled).

(b) Target sensor and acquisition. Landsat ARD surface reflectance, six bands, all available acquisitions; precursor: "composites from April 1st to September 30th ... to obtain time series with 7-day intervals"; code: interpolation grid from 22 April (INTRPL_START_DATE_STR = "0422") to 23 September ("0923") every 7 days (`range(0, ..., 7)`), i.e. 23 steps.

(c) Cloud, shadow and gap handling. Precursor: "In order to remove the gaps resulted from missing acquisitions and invalid data, we employed linear interpolation based on the nearest valid values before and after the target time step for each pixel". Code (preprocess_ARD.ipynb cell 6) derives validity from the ARD PIXELQA bits: fill bit == 0, clear bit == 1, cloud-shadow bit == 0, cloud bit == 0, and 0 <= SR <= 10000 (`flag = flag_sr_range*flag_fill*flag_clear*flag_cloud_shadow*flag_cloud`); invalid observations are zeroed "only for the convenience of debugging" and then never read. Cell 7: "If the number of valid observations after May 15 >= 7, the pixel will be included in the dataset, otherwise it will be excluded." Cell 10: for each weekly target date, `np.interp` between the nearest valid observation before and after; if none exists on one side, the two nearest on the other side are used (linear extrapolation at the series edges). No zero fill, mask token or valid-count feature reaches the model.

(d) Target-domain missingness statistics. The preprocessing logs "Validity percentage ({SITE} {YEAR})" (fraction of pixels retained by the >= 7 rule) and saves an `availability.npy` array, so the numbers exist, but whether the RSE paper reports valid-observation counts per site/year could not be found (paywalled). The precursor paper reports none.

(e) Code behaviour at inference. spatial_transfer.ipynb loads the test site's `x-2018.npy` (already interpolated, already filtered to pixels with >= 7 clear observations after 15 May), normalises with the training-site scaler (`helper.normalize_with_scaler(scaler, x_test)`), and predicts; the model (models/dcm.py, attention Bi-LSTM) has no masking input. Hence a cloudier target site is (i) silently thinned of its cloudiest pixels and (ii) fed interpolated values whose smoothness differs from the training site's.

(f) Cloud likelihood. Corn Belt summer: Whitcraft et al. 2015 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.10.009) and Ju and Roy 2008 (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2007.08.011) as in Card 2; the precursor itself notes "Cloud contamination in ARD may also lead to slight performance decreases."

(g) Attribution. From the RSE paper: could not be found (paywalled; the abstract is not exposed by Crossref or OpenAlex). The precursor attributes early-season weakness to crop stage and mentions cloud contamination only in passing (quote above). Secondary sources describe the RSE paper's claim as "improved generalizability for large-scale dynamic corn and soybean mapping" through attention Bi-LSTM, i.e. an architectural attribution.

Suspicion rank: 2 (plausible), provisionally; would become 3 if the RSE text reports no per-site valid-observation statistics and attributes site-to-site drops to spectral/phenological heterogeneity, which I could not verify.
Justification: spatial and temporal transfer with linear interpolation over gaps of unbounded length plus a sample-selection rule (>= 7 clear obs) whose bite depends on local cloudiness, both of which change the input distribution between domains; the code computes validity percentages but the model is blind to them; no missingness ablation is in the code.
Confidence: low-medium. Code fully read; paper text unavailable.

### Card 7 (substitute). Pham, Tetteh, Thiel, Erasmi, Schwieder, Frantz and van der Linden 2024, "Temporally transferable crop mapping with temporal encoding and deep learning augmentations", Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf. 129:103867

Confidence: high (full text and both code repositories read).

### Inferences
- The field-wide pattern is that transfer papers describe their cloud handling once, for the source domain, and never report how often the fill rule fired in the target. In this sample 10 of 13 papers report no target-domain valid-observation statistics; the three that do (Cai, Wang 2020, Pham) are also the three that consider missingness as a cause. Reporting the statistic and considering the cause appear to go together, which suggests the omission is a blind spot rather than a judged irrelevance.
- Zero fill of a fixed calendar slot (month or day) is the dominant practice among Sentinel-2 benchmark-style pipelines (TimeSen2Crop, Gallo, Pham, Kerner code, EuroCropsML month mode, SICKLE daily grid, PASTIS/SustainBench collate) and it is exactly the operation that produced the reference-case artefact. The alternative practices (long-gap linear interpolation in Xu, Cai and Sen4AgriNet; neighbour-average fill in Hao; vendor synthesis in Planet Fusion) also change the input distribution in proportion to the local gap rate, so they shift with the domain just as zero fill does, only less visibly.
- The two Brandenburg papers bracket the argument: DENETHOR trains on 2018, which Pham et al. identify as the clearest year in a 12-year clear-sky-observation record, tests on 2019 and attributes the 12 p.p. out-of-year drop to weather and growing cycles; Pham et al., same region and training year, show the drop tracks the annual clear-observation count and recover most of it by simulating sparsity during training. DENETHOR's Planet Fusion input hides where this happened because the QA/gap-fill confidence layer is never read by the loader.
- The Sen4AgriNet OAD result (LSTM nearly unaffected by Catalonia -> France while Transformer and TempCNN collapse to F1 ~30) is the signature expected when month-indexed inputs carry domain-specific interpolated months; the paper reads it as phenological stretching. This is a testable reanalysis: recompute the per-month empty-bin rate per country from the released netCDF `timestamp` arrays.
- Where a paper's transfer target is tropical (CropHarvest Togo/Kenya/Brazil) the cloud-regime shift from a mid-latitude-dominated training pool is large by climatology (Wilson & Jetz: tropical forest biomes ~80% annual cloud frequency), yet the best-pixel composite carries no flag for "every observation in this window was cloudy", so residual contamination and mean-filled months are indistinguishable from data at inference.
- For the manuscript, Pham et al. 2024 and Wang et al. 2020 are the citable precedents that per-domain clear-observation counts should be reported alongside transfer results; Kerner 2020, Sykas 2022, Gallo 2023, Hao 2020 and DENETHOR are the citable examples of degradation attributed to phenology without that statistic.

## Key Question 2: How do the public SITS crop benchmarks handle clouds and missing observations in the released data and official loaders, and which inherit a missingness-shift confound into transfer experiments?

### Takeaway
Across thirteen benchmark products, only ZueriCrop and EuroCropsML ship a per-observation cloud flag AND an official loader that uses it (ZueriCrop optionally, by multiplying cloudy pixels by zero; EuroCropsML by removing observations above a brightness-heuristic threshold); BreizhCrops, DENETHOR and the AI4FoodSecurity starter ship flags that the default loaders discard. Five pipelines fill gaps with a constant or statistic the model cannot distinguish from data (TimeSen2Crop zero month, CropHarvest per-band temporal mean, SICKLE zero day, EuroCropsML zero month, PASTIS/SICKLE/SustainBench zero padding in collate), Sen4AgriNet linearly interpolates or extrapolates empty monthly medians, and Planet Fusion (DENETHOR, AI4Food) fills before release without a per-day provenance flag in the loader path. None of the datasets whose official split crosses regions or years (TimeSen2Crop, DENETHOR/AI4Food-DE, Sen4AgriNet, EuroCropsML, CropHarvest, AI4Food-SA) documents the clear-observation count or missing-month distribution per domain.

### Cross-cutting observations for the missingness-shift argument
1. Only two benchmarks expose a per-observation cloud flag AND ship an official loader that uses it (ZueriCrop, optional and zero-filling; EuroCropsML, removal by a brightness heuristic). BreizhCrops, DENETHOR and the AI4Food starter ship flags but the default loaders discard them.
2. Four pipelines fill gaps with a constant or a statistic that the model cannot distinguish from data: TimeSen2Crop (zero month), CropHarvest (per-band temporal mean), SICKLE (zero day), EuroCropsML month mode (zero month) and PASTIS/SICKLE/SustainBench collate (zero padding). Sen4AgriNet linearly interpolates/extrapolates monthly medians. Planet Fusion (DENETHOR, AI4Food) fills before release with no per-day provenance flag.
3. None of the datasets whose official split crosses regions or years (TimeSen2Crop, DENETHOR/AI4Food-DE, Sen4AgriNet, EuroCropsML, CropHarvest, AI4Food-SA) documents the clear-observation count or missing-month distribution per domain. Our September finding for AI4Food-SA therefore has no published analogue to compare against, and the same confound is live in TimeSen2Crop (zero months), CropHarvest (mean months) and EuroCropsML (zero months).
4. In-domain benchmarks (PASTIS, ZueriCrop, SICKLE, SustainBench, AgriFieldNet, Kenya) are not confounded in their official form because folds share the acquisition calendar, but their loaders would propagate the confound unchanged if reused for transfer.


### Inferences
- The benchmark that most closely reproduces the reference-case mechanism is TimeSen2Crop: its official recipe zero-fills any month without a clear observation, the test tile (and the 2018-19 challenge tile) differs from the 13 training tiles in cloud and snow regime by the authors' own account, and no loader masks the zero months; any model benchmarked on the tile split or the cross-year challenge inherits the confound. CropHarvest (mean-filled months in tropical targets) and EuroCropsML month mode (zero months, LV -> EE) are the next closest.
- In-domain benchmarks (PASTIS, ZueriCrop, SICKLE, SustainBench, AgriFieldNet, Kenya) are not confounded in their official form because all folds share one acquisition calendar, but their loaders (zero-pad collate, zero-filled daily grid, cloud-multiply-by-zero) would propagate the confound unchanged if reused for a cross-tile or cross-year test, and the PASTIS-derived cross-year work of Quinton & Landrieu 2021 already has 36/27/29 valid entries per year without a per-year accuracy breakdown by valid count.
- The irregular-length datasets that resample to a fixed length (BreizhCrops 45 steps by random choice with replacement; Gallo 71) hide region-dependent sequence length (51 vs 102 observations in BreizhCrops from orbit overlap) behind a uniform tensor shape, so a cloudier or shorter region is seen through a different sampling density with no indicator.
- Planet Fusion's "gap-free" promise moves the missingness problem upstream rather than removing it: the fraction of synthesized days differs by year (2018 vs 2019 Brandenburg) and tile (SA 258N/259N vs 20E_259N) and is recoverable only from the QA layer, which neither the DENETHOR loader nor the AI4Food starter reads.
- A practical reanalysis for our manuscript would be to compute, for each public cross-domain benchmark, the per-domain fraction of filled slots from the released date vectors and flags (TimeSen2Crop flags and dates.csv; Sen4AgriNet netCDF `timestamp`; EuroCropsML DOY arrays; BreizhCrops `sequencelength`/`meanCLD` index columns; PASTIS `dates-S2`). The inputs exist for all of them; only CropHarvest and Planet Fusion products lack the information.

### Gaps
- Radiant MLHub documentation PDFs for Kenya PlantVillage, AgriFieldNet and Spot the Crop returned 403/not found; the Spot the Crop `CLM.tif` cloud mask and the AgriFieldNet Jan-Apr 2022 composite window are from search summaries only and marked unverified.
- TimeSen2Crop's cloud/shadow/snow mask product is not named in the paper or description PDF (likely a Sen2Cor-type classifier from FS-TEP, unverified).
- Whether the public SustainBench crop-type release is the cloud-filtered (`s2_cldfltr`) or unfiltered Sentinel-2 variant could not be determined from the loader or docs; the Rustowicz 2019 cloud procedure was not re-read.
- ZueriCrop: no acquisition-date array was observed in the HDF5 via the loader (`chooose_dates_2` reads a local `dates_1.txt`); unverified whether dates ship with the data.
- DENETHOR/AI4Food EOPatch Sentinel-2 timestamps: presence in the released EOPatch was not confirmed.
- No benchmark publishes a per-sample valid-observation count or filled-slot count as a first-class field; every number above would have to be derived by the user from flags and date vectors.

## Key Question 3: What cross-cutting conclusions follow for the manuscript's missingness-shift argument?

### Takeaway
The audit finds no published crop-type transfer paper that reports the exact reference-case artefact (a calendar slot missing only in the holdout domain, filled by a constant, flipping a model ranking), but it finds the enabling conditions in most of the field: constant or interpolation fills applied identically to domains with different gap rates, no per-domain gap statistics, and attribution to phenology by default. Pham et al. 2024 is the closest published demonstration of the mechanism and the best precedent for the fix (report per-domain clear-observation counts; train with simulated sparsity; avoid long-gap interpolation).

### Cited Findings
- Pham et al. 2024: "The level of decrease was closely related to the number of annual CSOs"; "the TE-only model is highly overfitted without the use of augmentation methods, i.e., only performed well in the training year and poorly in unseen years"; LI model "achieved 30 % to 54 %, while the Baseline model produced results exceeding 60 %" in sparse years 2010-2014. — [Thuenen OA PDF](https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn068235.pdf)
- DENETHOR: "the spatial shift to a new tile accounts for about 9 p.p. and the temporal shift for 12 p.p."; cause given as "weather and growing cycles can vary notably from year to year"; loader `dataset.py` stacks daily Planet Fusion tifs with no reference to the QA layer. — [NeurIPS D&B PDF](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/5b8add2a5d98b1a652ea7fd72d942dac-Paper-round2.pdf); [repo](https://github.com/lukaskondmann/DENETHOR)
- Sen4AgriNet loader: "Upsample so months without data are initiated with NaN values" then `interpolate_na(dim='time_bins', method='linear', fill_value='extrapolate')`; the paper never mentions empty months as a factor. — [PAD_dataset.py](https://github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/S4A-Models); [arXiv 2204.00951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00951)
- TimeSen2Crop: "If no cloud-free images are available for a considered pixel within the month, the harmonization process set all the reflectance values of the month to zero." — [JSTARS PDF](https://rslab.disi.unitn.it/web/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/09408357.pdf)
- Wang et al. 2020 Sci Data: "The median cloud-free image count at a pixel during key crop growing months June-August was 7" (2012: 4); Ohio 2006 failure attributed to a Landsat track with no clear images July-September. — [PMC7493954](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7493954/)
- Wilson & Jetz 2016 name "the winter rainfall Mediterranean climate of southwestern South Africa" as a region of seasonally concentrated cloud, supporting the climatological expectation that Western Cape winter-crop series are gap-prone. — [PLOS Biology](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002415)

### Inferences
- The strongest manuscript framing supported by this audit is not "nobody has noticed missingness" but "the field reports cloud handling as a preprocessing step and transfer degradation as a phenology result, and almost never connects them with a per-domain statistic". The exceptions (Cai 2018, Wang 2020, Pham 2024) show the connection is cheap to make.
- Benchmarks and papers share the failure: the datasets most used for cross-domain tests (TimeSen2Crop, Sen4AgriNet, DENETHOR, CropHarvest, EuroCropsML) encode their fill policy in loader code rather than in the paper, so downstream transfer papers inherit it without comment.

### Gaps
- The audit sample is 13 papers and 13 benchmarks; it is not a systematic review and the suspicion ranks for four paywalled papers are provisional. A follow-up with institutional access to ScienceDirect and Taylor & Francis (Kerner 2022, Xu 2020, Konduri 2020, Wang 2019, Che 2024) would firm up roughly a third of the ranks.
