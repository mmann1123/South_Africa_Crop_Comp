# Non-crop remote-sensing transfer papers: missingness-shift audit (2016 to 2026)

Scope: optical land cover, land use, forest and tree species, wetland, burned area, built-up, deforestation and ecological mapping papers that train in one region or year and report degraded performance elsewhere. Crop-type papers were excluded as instructed; where a candidate from the brief turned out to be crop-type (Rußwurm and Körner 2018/2020 BavarianCrops and BreizhCrops; Pelletier et al. 2019 TempCNN on a mixed Formosat-2 site; Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022 Germany seven-site transfer), it is noted but not carded. Twelve papers are carded below (Key Question 1), followed by the operational-systems question (Key Question 2) and the "did anyone test missingness directly" question (Key Question 3). Ranks follow the rubric in the brief: 3 = highly suspect, 2 = plausible, 1 = low suspicion. Where a detail could not be found it is stated explicitly.

Method note: full texts were obtained as PDFs (MDPI article_deploy mirrors, arXiv, WUR edepot, ISPRS Archives) and read with pdftotext; Nature articles were read through the publisher's redirect; code repositories were read via the GitHub API and raw file URLs. Quotes are from those texts.

## Key Question 1: Which widely cited "models fail to transfer spatially or temporally" papers used time series or composites whose valid-observation density plausibly differed between domains, and how suspect is each?

### Takeaway
Of twelve audited papers, five are rank 3 (Masolele et al. 2021; Stoian et al. 2019; Verhulst et al. 2024; Maleki and Rahdari 2026; Huppertz et al. 2021), five are rank 2 (Dynamic World 2022; Corbane et al. 2021; Ploton et al. 2020; Ludwig et al. 2022 with Meyer et al. 2019; and, provisionally, Pinto et al. 2020 BA-Net), and two are rank 1 contrasts (Ortega Adarme et al. 2020; Hu and Ban 2021), with Inglada et al. 2017 carded as a rank 1 operational baseline that documents its own clear-observation map. Not one of the twelve reports per-domain valid-observation counts alongside the transfer result, and the two whose own text gives the clearest qualitative clue (Stoian: mountain tiles with the fewest clear acquisitions show the largest cross-tile drop; Masolele: the authors themselves say clouds "cover most tropical areas of Africa and Asia" where accuracy was lowest) attribute degradation to landscape content without testing the gap structure.

### Cited Findings (audit cards)

#### Card 1. Masolele et al. 2021, "Spatial and temporal deep learning methods for deriving land-use following deforestation: A pan-tropical case study using Landsat time series", RSE 264, 112600. Rank 3. Confidence: high.
- (a) Source and target: continental models (Latin America, Africa, Asia) versus a pan-tropical model; land use following deforestation 2000 to 2005; "all models performed better when the scope of the problem was limited to a single continent" and the authors infer land-use spatial patterns "share more commonalities" within continents than across — [Masolele et al. 2021 PDF](https://edepot.wur.nl/551332); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112600)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: Landsat 5 and 7; "Two median composite images (January–June, July–December) were collected for each sample location from dense Landsat image time series for each year from 2000 to 2005 using Google Earth Engine"; 12 composites per location, 9 bands each (7 spectral plus NDVI, NDMI) — [Masolele et al. 2021 PDF](https://edepot.wur.nl/551332)
- (c) Cloud handling: "The collected dataset was filtered for clouds using the quality assessment band of Landsat SR data"; "Only images which contained cloud cover less than 50% were used to create a composite, this made sure we obtained enough images as possible but also we didn't lose more images." What happens to a pixel with zero valid observations in a six-month window (GEE median of an empty stack is masked/nodata) is not described anywhere in the paper — [Masolele et al. 2021 PDF](https://edepot.wur.nl/551332)
- (d) Valid-observation counts or cloud fractions per continent: not reported. The only statements are qualitative: "In some areas, the classification challenges were particularly caused by clouds and shadows that cover most tropical areas of Africa and Asia" and the models "are able to cope with these high levels of cloud coverage" — [Masolele et al. 2021 PDF](https://edepot.wur.nl/551332)
- (e) Code and data: no code or data availability statement with a URL was found in the PDF text (grep for github, zenodo, script, repository returned only citations). A Hugging Face model card (Masolele/deforestwatch-models) exists for a later Sentinel-1/2 ONNX model, which is a different model and input stack — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Masolele/deforestwatch-models). An Ethiopia attention U-Net repo is also a different study — [GitHub](https://github.com/masolele/Attention-U-Net-4-landuse-mapping-Ethiopia)
- (f) Climatology: the tropics have the lowest number of clear optical observations globally; a 2022 Harmonized Landsat-Sentinel-2 assessment found "the tropical regions have the lowest number of clear observations" — [USGS HLS 2022 study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/towards-seamless-global-30-meter-terrestrial-monitoring-evaluating-2022-cloud-free). Landsat 5/7 in 2000 to 2005 (pre-Landsat 8, with the 2003 ETM+ SLC failure adding striping gaps) over the Congo basin and insular Southeast Asia is the sparsest optical archive in the audit set.
- (g) Attribution: degradation across continents is attributed to differing spatial patterns of land use ("pointing at different spatial patterns across the tropics") and to spatial heterogeneity; cloud cover is named as a difficulty but is framed as noise the attention model copes with, never as a between-domain shift in input structure. No missingness ablation.
- Justification: target domains differ in region and sensor-era; cloud structure differs sharply between Latin America (mixed), Africa (Congo basin, very cloudy) and Asia (monsoonal, very cloudy); no valid-count statistics; median compositing over six-month windows with a 50% scene filter produces composites whose effective observation count varies by an order of magnitude across the tropics, and empty-window behaviour is undescribed; degradation is attributed to landscape pattern shift without testing. All rubric conditions for rank 3 are met.

#### Card 2. Stoian, Poulain, Inglada, Poughon, Derksen 2019, "Land Cover Maps Production with High Resolution Satellite Image Time Series and Convolutional Neural Networks: Adaptations and Limits for Operational Systems", Remote Sensing 11(17), 1986. Rank 3 (borderline 2; see justification). Confidence: high.
- (a) Source and target: eleven Sentinel-2 tiles across metropolitan France, year 2016; Section 5.3 "Cross Domain Transferability": "learning is performed on training datasets of 10 tiles, and the classification is evaluated on the testing dataset of the 11th tile"; "a drop in performance when a tile is not part of learning. Yet, it highly depends on the tile content. This drop is particularly high for mountain tiles (T30TYN and T31TGK) whose content is different from other tiles ... On the other hand, their is almost no drop for tile T30UXV"; "FG-UNET suffers from a higher decrease than RF" — [Stoian et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11171986)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: Sentinel-2 L2A (Theia/MAJA chain), all 2016 acquisitions, gap-filled and resampled to 33 common dates, 330 features per pixel — [Stoian et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11171986)
- (c) Cloud handling: "linear temporal gapfilling is used to estimate image reflectance for pixels flagged as clouds, cloud shadows, saturation or non acquired and a subsequent temporal resampling is applied in order to reconstruct temporal profiles having identical time stamps" — [Stoian et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11171986)
- (d) Valid-observation counts: only a qualitative range, not per tile: "the number of acquisitions for which the land is visible varies across tiles and even within every given tile. It ranges from five clear acquisitions in northern and mountainous areas to more than 30 in the sunny mediterranean areas where two satellite orbits overlap" — [Stoian et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11171986)
- (e) Code: "Our code is publicly available online (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.1474224; https://github.com/vpoughon/RT_DL_OSO_public)". The repository expects "input Sentinel2 image tiles (330 channels)", i.e., already gap-filled iota2 output; `Sentinel2_prepare_data.py` computes 3rd/97th percentiles per channel for normalization and excludes label pixels only where band 1 equals zero (`np.where((t_gt == j) & (t_band != 0))`); it never reads a nodata value, applies no cloud mask and has no NaN handling, so interpolated values from long gaps enter training and inference exactly like real observations — [RT_DL_OSO_public](https://github.com/vpoughon/RT_DL_OSO_public); [Sentinel2_prepare_data.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vpoughon/RT_DL_OSO_public/master/Sentinel2_prepare_data.py)
- (f) Climatology: the paper's own statement (5 clear acquisitions in northern and mountain tiles versus more than 30 in the Mediterranean) is the climatology; the two tiles with the largest cross-tile drop (T30TYN, T31TGK) are the mountain tiles, which by the authors' own description sit at the sparse end of that range.
- (g) Attribution: "whose content is different from other tiles" (landscape/class composition) and label scarcity for T30TXQ; missingness not considered as an explanation for the cross-tile drop.
- Justification: the target tile differs in region; the authors themselves document a 6x range in clear acquisitions and the largest drops coincide with the sparsest tiles; no per-tile counts are given next to Figure 19; linear interpolation across gaps of months produces input profiles unlike anything in the ten training tiles; degradation is attributed to tile content. The one factor pulling toward rank 2 is that the gap-filling method is fully described and standard. Rank 3 is given because the paper's own evidence makes the confound testable and it was not tested.

#### Card 3. Inglada, Vincent, Arias, Tardy, Morin, Rodes 2017, "Operational High Resolution Land Cover Map Production at the Country Scale Using Satellite Image Time Series", Remote Sensing 9(1), 95 (iota2 / Theia OSO). Rank 1 as an operational baseline (not a transfer experiment). Confidence: high.
- (a) Source and target: metropolitan France, Landsat-8 2014, 17 classes, kappa 0.86; training and validation are split by polygon within the same year and country with eco-climatic stratification; no cross-region or cross-year hold-out experiment — [Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: "all available images acquired during the reference period are used regardless of the amount of cloud cover"; 740 Landsat acquisitions; gap-filled and resampled to a common 16-day virtual grid — [Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095)
- (c) Cloud handling: Theia/MACCS masks; "a linear interpolation of the invalid pixels using the previous and following cloud-free dates ... applied over surface reflectance values and before the computation of derived spectral indices"; implemented in the OTB Temporal Gapfilling library (which repeats the first or last valid value at series edges) — [Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095); [iota2 L3A gapfilling docs](https://iota2.readthedocs.io/en/master/_sources/sentinel_2_L3A.rst.txt); [iota2 issue 565 on gapfilling behaviour](https://framagit.org/iota2-project/iota2/-/issues/565)
- (d) Valid-observation counts: yes, mapped. "Figure 8 displays the map of the count of clear pixels (no cloud, no shadow, no saturation) for the study area. The count goes from 2 to 29 for the whole 2014 year ... The effect of climatic areas and satellite track overlap is clearly visible." Also: "Only tiles with less than 90% cloud cover are processed by Theia, therefore, a Landsat scene can be only partially processed" — [Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095)
- (e) Code: iota2 (free software, OTB-based), referenced as tully.ups-tlse.fr/jordi/iota2 in 2017, now on framagit with readthedocs documentation; gap-filling is switched on with `sensors_data_interpolation.use_gapfilling`, interpolation is driven by the L3A masks, and the default method is linear — [iota2 docs](https://iota2.readthedocs.io/en/master/_sources/sentinel_2_L3A.rst.txt); [iota2 issue 565](https://framagit.org/iota2-project/iota2/-/issues/565)
- (f) Climatology: the paper's own Figure 8 (2 to 29 clear Landsat-8 observations in 2014 across France).
- (g) Attribution: the authors explicitly tie quality to clear-observation density: "As indicated by the confidence maps, the quality of the classification strongly depends on the amount of cloud-free imagery in every given point"; and artefacts: "Discontinuities are due to cloud cover and cloud shadows, satellite swath limits and absence of some acquisitions ... All this impacts the quality of the temporal gapfilling and resampling, making adjacent pixels which should be nearly identical, be very different" — [Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095)
- Justification: not a transfer paper, but the operational chain that Stoian 2019 and many French-school papers inherit. It is rank 1 here because it maps valid counts and names density as the dominant quality driver. It does not, however, quantify accuracy as a function of clear count or separate density effects from class-mixture effects, so it provides the diagnostic that downstream papers (Card 2) then omit.

#### Card 4. Brown et al. 2022, "Dynamic World, Near real-time global 10 m land use land cover mapping", Scientific Data 9, 251. Rank 2. Confidence: medium-high.
- (a) Source and target: training tiles from 2019 Sentinel-2 scenes stratified by three hemispheric regions and 14 RESOLVE biomes; held-out validation of 409 expert-labelled tiles; "Overall agreement between these single-image Dynamic World model outputs and the expert labels was observed to be 73.8%"; regional variation is stated only qualitatively: "Dynamic World tends to perform most strongly in temperate and tree-dominated biomes"; arid shrubland/crop confusion in Texas is the worked example — [Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: per-scene (single-date) Sentinel-2 L1C inference, no compositing; inference only on scenes with "CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE <= 35%" — [Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4); [GEE catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1)
- (c) Cloud handling: S2 Cloud Probability thresholded at 65%, intersected with the Cloud Displacement Index and a B10 cirrus threshold, morphological opening at 20 m, shadow mask by extending the cloud mask 5 km opposite the solar azimuth (directional distance transform), resampled to 100 m; "The mask is applied to training and inference images"; predictions are masked after inference using the mask derived from the unnormalized L1C image. The paper does not state what value masked pixels carry inside the FCN input before prediction — [Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4)
- (d) Valid-observation counts: not reported per region. Operational statement only: "~12,000 Sentinel-2 scenes per day, processing half on average due to a filter criteria" — [Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4)
- (e) Code: model weights and notebook at [github.com/google/dynamicworld](https://github.com/google/dynamicworld) (archived, read-only) and [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.5602141](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.5602141). The public notebook `single_image_runner.ipynb` applies no cloud mask at all: it takes `s2.filterBounds(POI).filterDate(...).first()`, selects nine bands, and normalizes with `image = np.log(image * 0.005 + 1)`, `image = (image - NORM_PERCENTILES[:, 0]) / NORM_PERCENTILES[:, 1]`, then `image = np.exp(image * 5 - 1); image = image / (image + 1)`. Clouds therefore pass through the network as ordinary bright pixels in the public code; the operational masking lives in Earth Engine, not in the repo — [single_image_runner.ipynb](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/dynamicworld/master/single_image_runner.ipynb)
- (f) Climatology: global product; the 35% scene filter means persistently cloudy regions receive far fewer predictions, and SkyTruth (quoting the developers) notes updates there "could take weeks or months" — [SkyTruth FAQ](https://skytruth.org/faq/dw-in-skytruth-alerts-questions/)
- (g) Attribution: the authors do name cloud masking as a driver: performance "varies spatially and temporally as a function of both the quality of S2 cloud masking and variability in land cover and condition" and "missed clouds are typically misclassified as Snow & Ice and missed shadows as Water". But no per-biome breakdown of error by cloud-mask quality is given, and per-biome accuracy itself is not tabulated — [Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4)
- Justification: single-scene inference with masking avoids gap-filling artefacts, which is why this is not rank 3. But (i) regional accuracy variation is asserted without numbers, (ii) cloud fraction per biome is not reported, (iii) the public inference code does no masking so the model's behaviour on cloud-contaminated context pixels is untested in the paper, and (iv) downstream annual composites of Dynamic World (mode or mean of per-scene probabilities) inherit very different valid-scene counts by region, which the paper does not discuss. Rank 2.

#### Card 5. Corbane et al. 2021, "Convolutional neural networks for global human settlements mapping from Sentinel-2 satellite imagery", Neural Computing and Applications 33, 6697-6720 (GHS-BUILT-S2 R2020A). Rank 2. Confidence: medium.
- (a) Source and target: training per UTM grid zone ("close range transfer learning") with "far range transfer learning from one UTM grid zone to" neighbouring or landscape-similar zones used for 28 zones where training data were poor; validation against building footprints at 277 AOIs grouped by continent; "both binary classifications produce good results with an average Balanced Accuracy greater than 0.7 and an average Kappa greater than 0.5"; best results "in Oceania an Asia", errors "mainly observed in mountainous areas with bare rocks" — [Corbane et al. 2021 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03267); [Springer DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-020-05449-7)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: "a global cloud-free image composite for reference year 2018 derived from Sentinel-2", 615 UTM grid zones, 15 TB, pixel-based best-available compositing (workflow in ref. [62] of the paper); composite rescaled to [0,1] for the CNN — [Corbane et al. 2021 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03267); [JRC composite dataset](https://data.jrc.ec.europa.eu/dataset/0bd1dfab-e311-4046-8911-c54a8750df79)
- (c) Cloud handling: entirely upstream in the composite; the CNN is said to be "robust to noise in satellite imagery (e.g., presence of snow, clouds, haze) and to other seasonal effects" and "robust to noise or missing data with respect to the training sets"; no explicit treatment of residual cloud, snow or nodata pixels in the model input is described — [Corbane et al. 2021 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03267)
- (d) Valid-observation counts: not reported for the composite. The paper acknowledges, for a training layer, a "lack of cloud-free satellite data in some regions resulting in large" gaps — [Corbane et al. 2021 arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03267)
- (e) Code: no model code repository was found in the paper text; the product and composite are on the JRC open data catalogue — [GHS-BUILT-S2 2018](https://human-settlement.emergency.copernicus.eu/ghs_bu_s2_2018.php)
- (f) Climatology: a single-year global best-pixel composite has a per-pixel observation count that ranges from one to dozens; tropical and high-latitude winter composites are built from the fewest and most seasonally biased observations (snow, low sun) — [USGS HLS 2022 study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/towards-seamless-global-30-meter-terrestrial-monitoring-evaluating-2022-cloud-free)
- (g) Attribution: regional errors attributed to landscape (bare rock, mountains) and training-set quality; composite quality is mentioned as a motivation but not analysed against accuracy by continent.
- Justification: continental accuracy variation is reported, composite construction is referenced, but valid-pixel counts per AOI are not compared to accuracy and residual snow/cloud handling in the CNN is asserted rather than tested. Rank 2.

#### Card 6. Ortega Adarme, Queiroz Feitosa, Nigri Happ, De Almeida, Rodrigues Gomes 2020, "Evaluation of Deep Learning Techniques for Deforestation Detection in the Brazilian Amazon and Cerrado Biomes From Remote Sensing Imagery", Remote Sensing 12(6), 910. Rank 1 (contrast). Confidence: high.
- (a) Source and target: Amazon (Landsat 8, 2 Aug 2016 and 20 Jul 2017) and Cerrado, Maranhão (3 Sep 2017 and 22 Sep 2018); models are trained and tested within each biome separately; the paper reports lower accuracy in Cerrado but does not train on one biome and test on the other (no cross-biome transfer experiment was found in the text) — [Ortega Adarme et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12060910)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: two co-registered single-date Landsat 8 OLI images per site, dry season, "when the cloud cover, a major problem over the whole BLA region, is minimum" — [Ortega Adarme et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12060910)
- (c) Cloud handling: scene selection for clear dry-season dates; no mask described because scenes were chosen clear.
- (d) Valid-observation counts: not applicable (single dates); cloud fraction of the chosen scenes not stated.
- (e) Code: none found in the paper text.
- (f) Climatology: the authors note "the Brazilian biomes present a cloud coverage for nearly the whole year, which prevents the use of optical imagery" outside the dry season — [Ortega Adarme et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12060910)
- (g) Attribution: Cerrado difficulty attributed to savanna heterogeneity and incomplete PRODES reference.
- Justification: identical acquisition structure (two clear dates) in both biomes and no gap-filling; missingness cannot masquerade as transfer failure here, and no cross-biome transfer is claimed. Rank 1. It is included as the kind of deforestation paper that is sometimes cited as cross-biome evidence but is not.

#### Card 7. Ploton et al. 2020, "Spatial validation reveals poor predictive performance of large-scale ecological mapping models", Nature Communications 11, 4540. Rank 2. Confidence: medium.
- (a) Source and target: Central Africa (Gabon, Cameroon to inland DRC), forest above-ground biomass regression from 11.8 million trees; random 10-fold CV R2 = 0.53 (RMSPE 56.5 Mg/ha) versus spatial 44-fold CV R2 = 0.14 (RMSPE 77.5 Mg/ha; null model 82); buffered leave-one-out R2 falls from about 0.50 (no buffer) to about 0.15 at 50 km and "virtually null beyond 100 km" — [Ploton et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18321-y)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: MODIS MAIAC Collection 6, "a 10-year composite (2000–2010) of 8-day images at 1-km resolution", with per-band mean and standard deviation layers, EVI2 and NDII (9 reflectance variables); plus WorldClim-2, Global-PET, SRTM, HWSD and MODIS-derived annual cloud cover frequency as predictors — [Ploton et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18321-y)
- (c) Cloud handling: "Pixels were kept using AOD and BRF quality flags" before averaging; no interpolation; the number of retained 8-day observations per pixel feeding the mean/SD is not reported.
- (d) Valid-observation counts: not reported. Cloud contamination is mentioned only as a general reason for disagreement among pantropical maps; the authors note optical "atmospheric pollution" causing 90% cover artefacts on the western (coastal) side — [Ploton et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18321-y)
- (e) Code and data: CV scripts [figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.12790085](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.12790085); AGB raster [figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.11865450](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.11865450); MAIAC predictors for the study area [figshare 10.6084/m9.figshare.12751628](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.12751628). The scripts implement the cross-validation schemes; they do not process raw MAIAC, so the valid-count handling is upstream and not inspectable from the deposit.
- (f) Climatology: Central Africa (Gabon, southern Cameroon, Congo basin) is among the cloudiest land regions in the MODIS-era cloud climatology; the authors include EarthEnv cloud frequency as a predictor, which itself is spatially structured — [EarthEnv cloud (Wilson and Jetz 2016)](https://www.earthenv.org/cloud)
- (g) Attribution: spatial autocorrelation, local overfitting and proximity-learning ("a coordinates-only model performed similarly"), plus optical saturation above about 100 Mg/ha; missingness not considered.
- Justification: the degradation is a spatial-CV finding, not a region-to-region transfer, and a 10-year mean dampens single-year gap effects, so rank 3 does not apply. But the MAIAC standard-deviation layers, built from a variable number of quality-flagged observations, encode cloudiness as a spatially smooth covariate correlated with the coast-to-interior gradient, which is exactly the signal a spatially blocked CV removes; no valid-count map is given. Rank 2.

#### Card 8. Ludwig, Bahlmann, Pebesma, Meyer 2022, "Developing Transferable Spatial Prediction Models: A Case Study of Satellite Based Landcover Mapping", ISPRS Archives XLIII-B3-2022, 135-141 (with Meyer et al. 2019, Ecological Modelling 411, 108815, and the Area of Applicability framework). Rank 2. Confidence: medium-high.
- (a) Source and target: Sentinel-2 land cover in Germany (Münster-school case study); model trained in one area and validated on "independent test data of geographically distinct areas"; test accuracy 0.71 with all 12 predictors versus 0.77 with spatially selected predictors, while spatial CV accuracy was 0.84/0.83; a coastal application area falls largely outside the Area of Applicability ("the entire coastline is outside the AOA") — [Ludwig et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-XLIII-B3-2022-135-2022)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: openEO processing chain; "a median composite of all available Sentinel-2 scene with less than 20% overall cloud cover is computed", with the Sentinel-2 scene classification used for "masking clouds, shadows and low quality pixels in each time step" — [Ludwig et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-XLIII-B3-2022-135-2022)
- (c) Cloud handling: SCL mask then median composite; empty-stack behaviour not described.
- (d) Valid-observation counts: not reported for either the training area or the geographically distinct test areas.
- (e) Code: [github.com/LOEK-RS/ISPRS2022LCC](https://github.com/LOEK-RS/ISPRS2022LCC); methods in the R package CAST. The repository was not opened in this audit; the composite is produced server-side in openEO, so the gap behaviour is in the openEO process graph rather than in local code. Meyer et al. 2019 (Marburg Open Forest; Random Forest; latitude/longitude predictors cause overfitting) is at [arXiv 1908.07805](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07805)
- (f) Climatology: single-year median composites over northern Germany and the North Sea coast; European sub-monthly Sentinel-2 series require "interpolation of up to 50% of data" in some geographies and winter months are largely cloud-covered — [Lewińska et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/22797254.2024.2372855); [CESBIO on winter cloud-free availability](https://www.cesbio.cnrs.fr/multitemp/?p=24496)
- (g) Attribution: the AOA framework attributes transfer failure to novel predictor-space combinations ("spectral properties not present in the training data"), i.e., spectral and class shift (the sand class absent from training). Missingness is not considered. However, the AOA is itself a dissimilarity index in predictor space, so a composite built from fewer valid pixels (and therefore a different median) would register as "outside AOA" without being distinguishable from genuine landscape novelty.
- Justification: gap handling is described (SCL mask, 20% filter, median), target valid counts are not; the mechanism proposed (predictor-space novelty) cannot separate missingness-induced novelty from landscape novelty. Rank 2.

#### Card 9. Verhulst, Van De Kerchove, Van Coillie, Van Meerbeek 2024 (INBO/Ghent/KU Leuven), "Temporal Transferability of Tree Species Classification in Temperate Forests with Sentinel-2 Time Series", Remote Sensing 16(14), 2653. Rank 3. Confidence: medium-high.
- (a) Source and target: Flanders, Belgium; five species; same-year models versus cross-year models for 2018 to 2022 (20 transfer scenarios); "The average losses in OA ... range from 2.81 ± 0.77%pt (2022) to 8.12 ± 2.71%pt (2021) for RF, from 3.19% ± 2.65 (2019) to 14.92% ± 3.10 (2018) for SVM, and from 2.30% ± 5.38 (2022) to 9.03% ± 2.74 for MLP"; multi-year training reduces the drop — [Verhulst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16142653)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: Sentinel-2 L2A via openEO/Terrascope; dekadal (10-day) median aggregation of 10 bands plus NDVI; full-year series each year — [Verhulst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16142653)
- (c) Cloud handling: Sen2Cor SCL mask keeping only SCL 4 (vegetation) and 5 (non-vegetated), dilated with an 11-pixel Gaussian kernel; then "linear interpolation was applied to fill the remaining gaps between time steps. After the interpolation step, gaps were sometimes still present at the beginning or the end of the time series. These gaps were filled for each spectral band with a backward or forward fill, copying the first or last available observation"; then Savitzky-Golay smoothing (window 15); then spatial aggregation per plot — [Verhulst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16142653)
- (d) Valid-observation counts per year: not reported. No figure or table of cloud-free dekads per year was found. The only acknowledgment is generic: "it is likely that interannual differences in climate and image quality play a role" — [Verhulst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16142653)
- (e) Code and data: "The data presented in this study are available on request from the corresponding author. The data are not publicly available due to being part of an ongoing study." No code repository was found — [Verhulst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16142653)
- (f) Climatology: Belgium; Sentinel-2 winter months over NW Europe have "large region with no cloud free data" — [CESBIO](https://www.cesbio.cnrs.fr/multitemp/?p=24496); and across Europe sub-monthly series need up to 50% interpolation in some geographies — [Lewińska et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/22797254.2024.2372855). The authors themselves describe 2021 as "a very wet year" with "an extreme precipitation event" in July, versus hot dry summers in 2018, 2019, 2020 and 2022; wetter years have more cloud, so the year whose NDVI curves "deviated most from the other years from January until June" (2021) is also the year most likely to have the fewest valid dekads and the longest interpolated spans.
- (g) Attribution: "the relationships between the spectral data and target classes are not necessarily consistent in time"; interannual climate and image quality named but not tested; "It is, however, difficult to attribute these differences to a specific cause."
- Justification: target differs in year; strong likelihood of different gap structure between a wet year and dry years; no valid-count statistics; linear interpolation plus forward/backward edge fill plus smoothing changes the input distribution as a function of the gap pattern (the authors' own anomaly year is the wet year); degradation attributed to spectral-class inconsistency and "image quality" without testing. Rank 3. The multi-year training remedy is also consistent with the missingness explanation (training across years exposes the model to varied gap patterns), so the paper's main finding does not discriminate between the two causes.

#### Card 10. Maleki and Rahdari 2026, "Wetland Mapping Using Machine Learning and Deep Learning Algorithms: Assessing Spatial Transferability of Recent Approaches", Remote Sensing 18(8), 1234. Rank 3. Confidence: high.
- (a) Source and target: Camargue (Mediterranean coast) and Étangs de la Champagne humide (NE France), both 2021; bidirectional transfer; within-site baselines versus cross-site: "For mapping Champagne using models trained on Camargue, the reduction in OA compared to the within-site baseline of Champagne ranges from -3.8% to -16.9% ... For mapping Camargue using models trained on Champagne, the reduction in OA ... is larger, ranging from -11.0% to -23.8%"; highest transferred OA 90% (Camargue to Champagne) versus 84% (Champagne to Camargue) — [Maleki and Rahdari 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18081234)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: Sentinel-2 via GEE, year 2021, "resampled to a regular 6-day interval, resulting in 60 observations between 1 January and 31 December 2021 for each study area"; polygon-mean spectra — [Maleki and Rahdari 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18081234)
- (c) Cloud handling: "images with high cloud coverage were removed using a threshold (CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE < 50). Second, cloudy pixels were masked using S2 quality layers, including cloud probability (MSK_CLDPRB < 1), snow probability (MSK_SNWPRB < 10), and the scene classification layer (SCL) ... After masking, the time series contained missing values due to cloud removal. These gaps were filled using linear interpolation over time for each pixel" — [Maleki and Rahdari 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18081234)
- (d) Valid-observation counts per site: not reported; Table 1 lists polygons and samples per class only. The discussion admits the approach "is sensitive to cloud cover and may limit performance for dynamic" wetlands, without numbers — [Maleki and Rahdari 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18081234)
- (e) Code: the paper cites https://github.com/Saeideh-Maleki/Wetland_Spatial_Transferability (404); the live repository is [Saeideh-Maleki/Wetland_Spatial_Transferability-](https://github.com/Saeideh-Maleki/Wetland_Spatial_Transferability-) (trailing hyphen) containing only `InceptionTime.py`, `MLP.py`, `RF.py`, `XGBOOST.py`, `ensemble_main_wetland.py`, `model.py`, `README.md`. No preprocessing, GEE export or interpolation code is in the repository, so the gap-filling at inference cannot be verified from code; the classifiers consume already interpolated 60-step series. Data "available on request".
- (f) Climatology: the two sites sit at the opposite ends of the French cloud gradient documented by Stoian et al. 2019 ("five clear acquisitions in northern and mountainous areas to more than 30 in the sunny mediterranean areas where two satellite orbits overlap") — [Stoian et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11171986). Camargue is Mediterranean and in a Sentinel-2 orbit overlap; Champagne humide is continental NE France with far more winter cloud. The two 60-step series are therefore built from very different numbers of real observations.
- (g) Attribution: "a stronger distributional mismatch between training and target domains in this transfer direction. This result suggests that the spectral–temporal characteristics of wetlands in Camargue are less well represented by models trained on Champagne data than vice versa"; also hydrology, turbidity, phenology. Missingness is not considered as the cause of the asymmetry.
- Justification: target differs in region; strong likelihood of different cloud structure (Mediterranean versus NE France); no valid-count statistics; linear interpolation to a 6-day grid makes the Champagne series far more interpolated than the Camargue series, which alone would predict the observed asymmetry (a model trained on heavily interpolated, smooth Champagne series meets sharper real Camargue dynamics); degradation attributed to spectral-temporal mismatch without testing. Rank 3.

#### Card 11. Huppertz, Nakalembe, Kerner, Lachyan, Rischard 2021, "Using transfer learning to study burned area dynamics: A case study of refugee settlements in West Nile, Northern Uganda", KDD 2021 Humanitarian Mapping workshop, arXiv 2107.14372. Rank 3. Confidence: high (for the missingness mechanism), low (for the magnitude of degradation, which is not quantified).
- (a) Source and target: trained on Portugal 2016 (ICNF burned-area polygons), applied to West Nile districts, Northern Uganda, 2015 to 2020; test Dice on Portugal 0.661 ± 0.3, IoU 0.559 ± 0.3; Uganda outputs show "a large amount of additionally detected BAs when compared with the MCD64A1 output", with validation still pending — [Huppertz et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14372)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: single-date Sentinel-2 Level-1C false-colour stacks (B8A, B3, B12) at 20 m; patches 128 x 128 within 90 days of fire end-date — [Huppertz et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14372)
- (c) Cloud handling: none. The authors flag only that L1C (atmospherically uncorrected) "may add biases". In the repository, `src/preparedata/merge_bands.py` fixes `dict_of_bands = {"SWIR-band": "B12", "NIR-band": "B8A", "Green-band": "B03"}`, reads `source_file.read(1)` and writes values unchanged with no nodata, NaN or cloud treatment; `src/preparedata/extract_patches.py` filters fires by end-date ("finish date of the fire (DHFim) should be within a timeframe before the capture date of the image", default 30 days) and area > 5 ha, with "no cloud filtering, cloud mask, QA band check, or nodata/empty-pixel check"; `sort_patches.py` only removes "empty or broken patches" — [Burned_Area_Detection repo](https://github.com/prhuppertz/Burned_Area_Detection); [merge_bands.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prhuppertz/Burned_Area_Detection/master/src/preparedata/merge_bands.py); [extract_patches.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prhuppertz/Burned_Area_Detection/master/src/preparedata/extract_patches.py)
- (d) Valid-observation counts or cloud fractions: not reported for either domain.
- (e) Code: as above; branch master.
- (f) Climatology: Portuguese fire-season scenes (summer 2016) are near cloud-free; West Nile has a bimodal wet season and savanna burning in the Dec to Feb dry season; without any cloud or shadow mask, wet-season cloud shadows enter the SWIR/NIR/Green composite as dark patches. Hu and Ban 2021 note for the same band family that "shadows" are among the "unrelated phenomena and disturbances" spectrally similar to burn scars — [Hu and Ban 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13081509)
- (g) Attribution: "the significant domain shift between Portugal and Northern Ugandan vegetation" and L1C biases; clouds and shadows are not mentioned.
- Justification: region and year differ; cloud/shadow structure differs; no statistics; no masking at all (so the input distribution shift is maximal in cloudy target scenes); the extra detections over MCD64A1 are interpreted as a sensitivity gain. Rank 3. It is a workshop paper, so its citation weight is low, but it typifies the burned-area transfer pattern.

#### Card 12. Hu, Ban, Nascetti 2021, "Uni-Temporal Multispectral Imagery for Burned Area Mapping with Deep Learning", Remote Sensing 13(8), 1509. Rank 1 (contrast). Confidence: high.
- (a) Source and target: trained on six fires (P1 Leiria and P4 Castelo Branco, Portugal; P2 Doñana and P3 Encinedo, Spain; P5 Elephant Hill, Canada; P6 Enskogen, Sweden; 2017 to 2019) and tested on three unseen fires (T1 Corinthia, Greece 2020; T2 Fågelsjö-Lillåsen and T3 Trängslet, Sweden 2018), then transferred to Landsat-8 at the same test sites; kappa about 0.9 for U-Net/HRNet in Greece, 0.79 for Fast-SCNN in Sweden; Landsat-8 transfer "keeps the same tendency" (HRNet kappa 0.85/0.78/0.83) — [Hu and Ban 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13081509)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: single post-fire Sentinel-2 and Landsat-8 scenes with listed acquisition dates (Table 1).
- (c) Cloud handling: scene selection; reference masks from Copernicus EMS products derived from VHR images "under approximately 0% cloud coverage"; the authors note that "the acquisition of Sentinel-2 or Landsat-8 testing images depends on the cloud coverage" — [Hu and Ban 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13081509)
- (d) Valid-observation counts: not applicable (single scenes).
- (e) Code: PaddlePaddle framework cited; data "available on request"; no repository for this paper found.
- (f) Climatology: Mediterranean and boreal summer post-fire scenes, hand-selected clear.
- (g) Attribution: performance differences attributed to scar compactness versus dispersed boreal burns (landscape), not clouds.
- Justification: identical acquisition structure (one clear scene) in all domains and explicit acknowledgment that scene choice is cloud-dependent; missingness cannot differ between domains. Rank 1. Useful as the clean comparator for Card 11.

### Inferences
- The strongest pattern across the twelve cards is that time-series papers with explicit gap-filling (Cards 2, 9, 10) attribute transfer loss to landscape or spectral-class inconsistency in exactly the domains (mountain tiles, a wet year, a continental versus Mediterranean wetland) where their own text or regional climatology implies the fewest real observations. In each case, the interpolated fraction of the input is a plausible, untested alternative explanation, and in two cases (Stoian; Maleki and Rahdari) the direction of the asymmetry matches what a density difference would predict.
- Per-scene classifiers with masking (Dynamic World) and single clear-scene studies (Ortega Adarme; Hu and Ban) are structurally protected from the confound at the pixel level, but Dynamic World's regional accuracy statements still cannot be separated from regional cloud-mask quality because neither is tabulated.
- Composite-based global or regional products (Corbane; Ludwig) move the confound upstream into the compositing step, where per-pixel valid counts are rarely exported alongside the composite; the Area of Applicability framework, in particular, treats composite novelty of any origin as predictor-space novelty.
- Code audits show a consistent gap: repositories ship classifiers that consume already gap-filled or composited arrays (RT_DL_OSO_public, Wetland_Spatial_Transferability-, google/dynamicworld notebook) so the inference-time behaviour on missing data is not inspectable from the published code; the one end-to-end pipeline (Burned_Area_Detection) has no cloud handling at all.

### Gaps
- Masolele et al. 2021: no code or data deposit found; how empty six-month windows were handled in the GEE export (nodata value, zero, or dropped sample) could not be determined.
- Stoian et al. 2019: per-tile clear-acquisition counts are described only as a range; Figure 19 (per-tile kappa drop) was not cross-tabulated with clear counts by the authors, and the figure values were not extractable from the PDF text.
- Dynamic World: the Zenodo inference archive (10.5281/zenodo.5602141) was not opened; whether masked pixels are zeroed or left as reflectance inside the FCN input at operational inference is not stated in the paper and is not in the public notebook.
- Ploton et al. 2020: the number of quality-flagged MAIAC observations per pixel entering the 10-year means and SDs is not reported and the figshare deposit does not include that preprocessing.
- Verhulst et al. 2024: no public data or code; per-year valid dekad counts for Flanders were not reported and could not be reconstructed.
- Maleki and Rahdari 2026: preprocessing (GEE export, interpolation) is not in the repository; per-site valid counts unreported.
- Pinto et al. 2020 BA-Net: full text not retrieved; region-transfer design and attribution unverified.
- Esri/Impact Observatory (Karra et al. 2021, IGARSS, DOI 10.1109/IGARSS47720.2021.9553499): only product documentation was found (model trained on "over 5 billion hand-labeled Sentinel-2 pixels ... from over 20,000 sites", 86% overall accuracy on validation); no per-region accuracy, no description of cloud handling at inference, no code — [Digital Earth Africa IO LULC specs](https://docs.digitalearthafrica.org/en/latest/data_specs/IO_LULC_specs.html). Not carded for lack of auditable detail.
- Tuia, Persello, Bruzzone 2016 (IEEE GRSM 4(2), 41-57) was identified but its full text was not read; its examples are very-high-resolution and hyperspectral scenes (cloud-free by selection), so it is unlikely to be a missingness case — [EPFL record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/225237); a later arXiv posting (2104.07778) describes the four method families (invariant feature selection, representation matching, classifier adaptation, selective sampling) — [arXiv 2104.07778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07778)

## Key Question 2: Do operational systems (iota2, Dynamic World, WorldCover, Sen4LDSM) document how cloudy regions degrade and separate that from spectral transfer?

### Takeaway
iota2 is the only operational chain in the audit whose foundational paper maps clear-observation counts and states that classification quality "strongly depends on the amount of cloud-free imagery in every given point"; Dynamic World names cloud-mask quality as a driver of spatial and temporal variation but tabulates neither regional accuracy nor regional cloud fraction; GHS-BUILT-S2 reports per-continent accuracy but not composite valid counts. None of them quantifies accuracy as a function of valid-observation density or separates it from spectral transfer, and WorldCover and Sen4LDSM were not reached in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- iota2 / Theia OSO: clear-pixel count map for France 2014 ranges "from 2 to 29 for the whole 2014 year", with "the effect of climatic areas and satellite track overlap ... clearly visible"; "the quality of the classification strongly depends on the amount of cloud-free imagery in every given point"; artefacts from partial tile processing (Theia processes only tiles with < 90% cloud) make "adjacent pixels which should be nearly identical, be very different" — [Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095)
- iota2 gap-filling: interpolation is driven by the L3A masks (nodata and cloud/shadow pixels are interpolated), enabled by `sensors_data_interpolation.use_gapfilling`; default method linear; a user issue reports an interpolated September curve that "looked like extrapolation" — [iota2 docs](https://iota2.readthedocs.io/en/master/_sources/sentinel_2_L3A.rst.txt); [iota2 issue 565](https://framagit.org/iota2-project/iota2/-/issues/565)
- Dynamic World: "performance varies spatially and temporally as a function of both the quality of S2 cloud masking and variability in land cover and condition"; "missed clouds are typically misclassified as Snow & Ice and missed shadows as Water"; regional accuracy given only as "most strongly in temperate and tree-dominated biomes"; inference restricted to scenes with CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE <= 35% — [Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4); [GEE catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1)
- GHS-BUILT-S2: per-continent Balanced Accuracy and Kappa at 277 AOIs ("average Balanced Accuracy greater than 0.7 and an average Kappa greater than 0.5"), best in Oceania and Asia; built from a 2018 "cloud-free" best-pixel composite with no valid-count reporting — [Corbane et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03267)
- Esri/IO: 86% overall accuracy on the validation set; no regional or cloud-related documentation found — [Digital Earth Africa IO LULC specs](https://docs.digitalearthafrica.org/en/latest/data_specs/IO_LULC_specs.html)

### Inferences
- The operational chains that gap-fill (iota2) are the ones most aware of density effects, because artefacts are visible as tile seams; the per-scene chain (Dynamic World) externalizes the problem to the mask and to downstream temporal aggregation; the composite chains (GHS, Esri) hide it in the composite. None publishes a density-conditioned accuracy curve.

### Gaps
- ESA WorldCover (Zanaga et al.) and Sen4LDSM were not searched in this pass; their documentation of cloud-driven degradation is unknown here.
- No operational system was found that reports accuracy stratified by number of valid observations in the evaluation year.

## Key Question 3: Are there papers in this domain that explicitly tested missingness as the confound and found it mattered (or did not)?

### Takeaway
Yes, for temporal transfer: Pham et al. 2024 (RSE) showed with Sentinel-2 land cover fraction regression in Germany and Poland (2017 to 2022) that models trained in high-data-density years transfer poorly to low-density years and that randomly thinning observations during training ("Random Observation Selection") improves transfer by up to about 16% in MAE; Schwieder et al. 2022 showed grassland mowing detection accuracy depends on clear-observation density with a threshold near 16 clear observations per season. No non-crop paper was found that tested missingness as the confound for spatial (region-to-region) transfer, and Rußwurm and Körner's cloud-robustness result is crop-type and therefore out of scope.

### Cited Findings
- Pham, Thiel, Frantz, Okujeni, Schug, van der Linden 2024, "Learning the variations in annual spectral-temporal metrics to enhance the transferability of regression models for land cover fraction monitoring", RSE 308, 114206: spectral-temporal metrics "are subject to variations in data densities within and between years, which may lead to variations in prediction accuracies and limit the transferability of models through time"; "2018 and 2019 had above-average data density, while 2017 and 2021 were low"; "Models trained in high-density years performed well in that same year but poorly when transferred to lower-density years. Models trained in low-density years showed less of a drop"; Random Observation Selection "improved the transferability of all models, particularly those trained on a high-density year and transferred to lower-density years"; a six-year model performed best for each year; largest relative gain about 16% MAE; Sentinel-2, northern Germany and Poland, impervious/woody/low vegetation fractions — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114206); [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.rse.2024.114206); [HU Berlin EO Lab](https://eolab.geographie.hu-berlin.de/publication/pham_2024/)
- Schwieder et al. 2022, "Mapping grassland mowing events across Germany based on combined Sentinel-2 and Landsat 8 time series", RSE 269, 112795: "Lower accuracies were found in areas that are revisited less frequently by Sentinel-2"; detection "was less influenced by data availability when at least 16 cloud-free observations were available in the grassland season"; "The distribution of available observations throughout the season also appeared to be critical" — [HU Berlin EO Lab](https://eolab.geographie.hu-berlin.de/publication/schwieder_2022/)
- Lewińska, Frantz, Leser, Hostert 2024, "Usable observations over Europe: evaluation of compositing windows for Landsat and Sentinel-2 time series", European Journal of Remote Sensing: sub-monthly series "in some geographies requires interpolation of up to 50% of data"; shorter-than-monthly composites were challenging without combining Landsat and Sentinel-2 "beyond the Mediterranean biogeographical region" — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/22797254.2024.2372855); [preprint](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202308.2174/v1); [Dryad data](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.5tb2rbp94)
- Masolele et al. 2021 (Card 1) argue the opposite direction without a test: attention-based spatio-temporal models "are able to cope with these high levels of cloud coverage" — [Masolele et al. 2021](https://edepot.wur.nl/551332)
- Global clear-observation climatology for 2022 HLS: "global mean and median cloud-free observations were 69 and 64", winter data unavailable at high latitudes due to low sun, tropics lowest — [USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/towards-seamless-global-30-meter-terrestrial-monitoring-evaluating-2022-cloud-free)
- EarthEnv MODIS cloud frequency (Wilson and Jetz 2016, PLoS Biol 14(3): e1002415): 1 km monthly cloud frequency from "15 years of twice-daily MODIS satellite images"; used as a predictor by Ploton et al. 2020 — [EarthEnv](https://www.earthenv.org/cloud)

### Inferences
- Pham et al. 2024 is the closest thing to a direct demonstration that the rank-3 mechanism is real: same region, same classes, same sensor, and the only thing that changed between years that the authors could isolate was observation density. Its remedy (randomly dropping observations during training) is a missingness augmentation, which is the natural ablation the rank-3 papers above did not run.
- The absence of any spatial-transfer paper that controls for density, combined with the French cloud gradient documented by Inglada 2017 and Stoian 2019, means the widely repeated claim that models "do not generalize spatially" rests, for time-series inputs, on experiments in which density was uncontrolled.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that tests region-to-region transfer with matched valid-observation counts (or with density as a covariate) outside crop-type mapping.
- Rußwurm and Körner 2018 (ISPRS IJGI) and 2020 (ISPRS JPRS) report that recurrent and self-attention models trained on raw, unmasked Sentinel-2 series learn to ignore cloudy observations, but on Bavarian crop parcels; excluded here as crop-type, and not verified from full text in this pass.
- The Lewińska 2024 per-year clear-observation counts for Belgium and NE France (needed to quantify Cards 9 and 10) are in the Dryad DailyData.csv and were not extracted.
