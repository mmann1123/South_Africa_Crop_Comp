# Missingness-shift audit of eight local crop-type transfer papers (set A)

Scope: eight papers whose PDFs and briefers sit in `writeup/literature/`. For each, a forensic audit card covering (a) source/target regions and years, (b) sensor and acquisition or compositing scheme, (c) cloud/shadow/gap handling, (d) whether per-domain missingness statistics are reported, (e) code or data deposit and what the code does with gaps, (f) cloud likelihood in the target domain, (g) how the paper attributes transfer loss, then a suspicion rank (1 to 3) with justification and confidence. Local PDF text was extracted with `pdftotext -layout`; repositories were read at the raw-file level where possible. Climatology statements that are not backed by a fetched source are labelled as inferences.

General sources used across cards: Sudmanns et al. 2020 on global Sentinel-2 scene availability and cloudiness, which states that a method applied in Europe sees different average cloud cover and acquisition frequency than the same method in tropical regions or deserts, and that higher acquisition frequency does not necessarily yield more cloud-free scenes ([Sudmanns et al. 2020, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7455071/)); Lewińska et al. 2024 on usable observations over Europe, whose abstract reports that sub-monthly composites were mostly feasible only for the combined Landsat plus Sentinel-2 archive after 2015 and that "in some regions up to 50% of data needed interpolation" ([Lewińska et al. 2024, Eur. J. Remote Sens. 57(1) 2372855, via HU Berlin EO Lab](https://eolab.geographie.hu-berlin.de/publication/lewinska_2024/)); and the companion global daily usable-observation dataset, which defines usable as free of cloud, snow and shade and notes that "many regions are prone to frequent cloud and snow cover" ([Lewińska et al. 2024, Data in Brief, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11546790/)). Neither Lewińska paper gave region-specific numbers in the text I could access, so regional cloud statements below rest on the papers' own statements plus labelled inference.

## Paper 1. Rusňák et al. 2023, "Crop Mapping without Labels" (Remote Sensing 15:3414). Could the Danube to East transfer loss be a missingness shift?

### Takeaway
Rank 2 (plausible), confidence medium-high. This is the only paper in the set that tabulates per-domain observation counts per month (Fig. 4) and explicitly tests whether composite quality explains transfer loss (Section 4.2), finding no coherent year-level relationship. But the two regions have very different acquisition densities (771 images over three tiles versus 215 over two tiles across the five seasons), the behaviour of pixels with zero valid observations in a month is never stated, sample pixels were dropped in 2020 and 2022 "due to observational limitations", and the region-level confound (East is both the sparser-observed and the consistently worse-transferring domain) was not tested.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Source: Danubian Lowland, Slovakia (tiles 33UYP, 33UXP, 33UYQ); target: East Slovakian Lowland (tiles 34UEV, 34UEU); years 2018 to 2022; Scenario 3 trains on Danube year Y and tests on East year Y; Scenario 4 trains on all five Danube years and tests on each East year. — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Sections 2.2 and 2.3.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- (b) Sensor: Sentinel-2 L2A from GEE (`COPERNICUS/S2_SR`), April to August only, "totaling 771 images covering Danubian lowland and 215 images covering eastern Slovakia lowland" across 2018 to 2022; monthly median composites of ten bands, giving five composites per season. — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Section 2.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- (c) Cloud handling: "We selected the good observations by masking clouds, cloud shadows, and cirrus provided in Level-2A, accompanied by the SCL (scene classification) product. A simple median compositing method was used ... over monthly time periods." The text adds that the median "is only effective when the majority of selected observations are of good quality." No interpolation, fill value or zero-observation rule is described. — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Section 2.3.1](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- (c, continued) Sampling: "2000 pixels per class/year, except for 2020 and 2022, where we could extract only 1698 and 1458 pixels per class, respectively, due to observational limitations." — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Section 2.3.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: yes. Fig. 4 gives, for every region-month-year, the share of the study region whose monthly median was built from 1, 2, or 3+ observations; the text notes "summer observations are of higher quality than spring observations" and flags "May 2021, June 2020, and May 2019" as months with distinct regional differences. — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Section 3.1 and Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- (e) Code: none. The Data Availability Statement gives only the reference dataset on Zenodo (10.5281/zenodo.7912749) and supplementary figures and Excel files on figshare (10.6084/m9.figshare.23309198); models were built in MATLAB Classification Learner. A web search for a code repository found none. — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Supplementary Materials and Data Availability](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414); [search result](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/13/3414)
- (f) Target cloudiness as stated by the paper: spring months are poorer than summer; East Slovakia is described as "more continental" with heavy hydromorphic soils; the East tiles received fewer than half the images per tile of the Danube tiles over the same seasons (215/2 versus 771/3). — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Sections 2.1 and 2.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- (g) Attribution: the authors explicitly consider observation quality and reject it as the main driver: "we did not identify any coherent relationship between the patterns of observational quality and the year-to-year classification performance. For instance, June 2020 in the Danube region had one of the poorest observational qualities, yet the performance of Scenario 1 in 2020 was the best ... in Scenario 3, May 2019 for both regions exhibited substantially lower observational quality. However, the spatial transfer of 2019 still achieved acceptable performance." They attribute the hardest cases to the 2022 summer drought and to regional soil and management differences, and attribute RF's weakness to overfitting in high-dimensional feature space. — [Rusňák et al. 2023, Sections 4.2 and 4.3](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)

### Inferences
- The acquisition asymmetry (roughly 257 images per tile over the Danube versus 108 per tile over the East for the same months and years) means East monthly medians were built from fewer observations on average, so the spatial-transfer target systematically has lower composite quality than the source. A median of one or two SCL-masked observations is more likely to carry cloud remnants or shadow and to be biased relative to a median of five. That is a quality-type missingness shift aligned with the transfer direction, and the authors' year-level null test does not address it because region and quality are confounded in every Scenario 3 and 4 run.
- "Observational limitations" reducing the per-class sample in 2020 and 2022 implies that pixels lacking a valid monthly composite were excluded from the sample. If so, the test set in bad years is pre-filtered to well-observed pixels, which hides the effect of gaps on the wall-to-wall map but also means the reported accuracies are for complete-vector pixels only. The paper does not say how the maps in Fig. 13 to 14 handle pixels with a missing month.
- Rank 2 rather than 3 because the paper does report per-domain gap statistics and explicitly discusses missingness as a candidate explanation; rank 2 rather than 1 because the zero-observation rule is undocumented, the regional confound was not tested, and the 2022 drought attribution coexists with 2022 having the fewest usable sample pixels.
- Confidence: medium-high. The paper is unusually transparent about composite quality, so the residual uncertainty is about what the pipeline did with empty months, not about whether a quality gradient exists.

### Gaps
- Not stated anywhere: what value a pixel receives when a month has zero valid SCL-clean observations (dropped, NaN, or a GEE-masked value that MATLAB would have had to impute).
- The per-region image counts (771 and 215) are totals across five seasons and different tile counts; per-tile per-month acquisition counts are not given, so the exact composite-quality gap between regions cannot be computed from the paper.
- Supplementary Table S2 and figures on figshare were not fetched; they may contain per-class F1 but are unlikely to contain additional missingness statistics.

## Paper 2. Wijesingha, Dzene and Wachendorf 2024, "Evaluating the spatial–temporal transferability of models for agricultural land cover mapping using Landsat archive" (ISPRS JPRS 213:72-86). Could the temporal and spatial transfer losses be a missingness shift?

### Takeaway
Rank 3 (highly suspect), confidence medium-high. The temporal target period (2010 to 2012) is observed only by Landsat 5 and SLC-off Landsat 7 whose stripe gaps are treated as no-data, while the training period (2013 to 2018) is observed by Landsat 7 and 8; bimonthly medians are built from whatever scenes under 60 percent cloud remain; the public notebook then removes any field-year with a missing composite via `dropna()`, so gap structure becomes silent sample selection in both domains; no per-domain scene or gap counts are reported; and the loss is attributed to environment, climate, management, label distribution and predictor dissimilarity (DI/AOA), with the sensor change acknowledged in one sentence but never quantified.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Source: North Hesse (NH, hilly, 101 to 754 m, 500 to 1300 mm rainfall) and Weser-Ems (WE, flat, 0 to 95 m, 730 mm rainfall), Germany; training years 2013 to 2018; temporal target 2010 to 2012 same region (S1); spatial target the other region 2013 to 2018 (S2); both 2010 to 2012 (S3). — [Wijesingha et al. 2024, Sections 2.1 and 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: "Landsat scenes with less than 60 % cloud coverage (from Landsat 5, 7, and 8 satellites) ... were retrieved for each year (between 2010 and 2018) from March to October ... The Landsat 5 data was available until May 2012, and Landsat 8 data was available from 2013." Scenes are grouped into four bimonthly periods (Mar-Apr, May-Jun, Jul-Aug, Sep-Oct) and the median per period is computed, then field means of 10 layers x 4 periods = 40 features. — [Wijesingha et al. 2024, Section 2.2.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)
- (c) Cloud and gap handling: "these image scenes were masked using the cloud masking algorithm from 'eemont' library" (Landsat Level-2 QA cloud mask); "the data from Landsat 7 data was not corrected to remove the gaps, and those gaps were considered as no data pixels similar to cloud and shadow areas." No imputation is described for a field-bimonth with no valid pixel. — [Wijesingha et al. 2024, Section 2.2.1.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: none. The paper gives no scene counts per bimonth, no fraction of fields with missing composites, and no SLC-gap coverage fraction. It only states, in discussion, that "the number of scenes per area in a given two-month period could differ due to the availability of scenes with clear sky observations." — [Wijesingha et al. 2024, Section 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)
- (e) Code: public at github.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability, ten files (eight notebooks plus a GEE download script and README). — [Repository](https://github.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability)
- (e, download script) `L578_WE_Download.py` filters each of `LANDSAT/LT05/C02/T1_L2`, `LANDSAT/LE07/C02/T1_L2`, `LANDSAT/LC08/C02/T1_L2` with `.filterMetadata('CLOUD_COVER', 'less_than', 60)`, calls `.maskClouds()` and `.scale()`, takes a two-month `filterDate` window and `.median()`. No `unmask`, fill, or interpolation appears, and nothing addresses SLC-off gaps; the median is computed over whatever passes the filters. — [L578_WE_Download.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability/main/L578_WE_Download.py)
- (e, model notebook) In `00_1_SR_VI_NH_Data_model_training_TF_BCNN_RF.ipynb` the field-level table is read from `NH_L578_all_norm.csv`, then `df_dropna = df_years.dropna()`; no `fillna`, `interpolate`, `nan_to_num` or imputer appears in the first 100,000 characters of the notebook. The reference test is `test_nuts = 'DE735'`, `test_years = 2017`, and training samples `groupby("CAT").sample(n = 12500)`. — [Notebook 00_1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability/main/00_1_SR_VI_NH_Data_model_training_TF_BCNN_RF.ipynb)
- (f) Target cloudiness: the paper itself reports no cloud statistics; it describes NH as a low-mountain region with 500 to 1300 mm rainfall and WE as the Northwest German lowlands with 730 mm. It states the temporal target years had only Landsat 5 and 7 and that "the quality of the observation increased from the past to the present when advanced satellite sensors were introduced" (citing Frantz et al. 2023). — [Wijesingha et al. 2024, Sections 2.1 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)
- (g) Attribution: "The performance loss for spatial transferability can be likely attributed to changes in environmental, climate, and management conditions"; cultivar selection, field size, elevation, slope, class distribution and DI/AOA share (correlation 0.9 with OA) are the explanatory variables; on sensors: "there could be an impact from changes in the satellite sensors to the model transferability", followed by the claim, citing Frantz et al. 2023, that "the number of scenes with clear sky observation is not more important than seasonal data distribution, which this study tried to keep consistently." — [Wijesingha et al. 2024, Section 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)

### Inferences
- The temporal target domain is structurally gappier than the source: Landsat 5 ended in 2012 and Landsat 7 has carried SLC-off stripes since 2003 (both stated in the paper), so 2010 to 2012 bimonthly medians over Germany were built from one TM sensor plus a striped ETM+, whereas 2013 to 2018 medians had ETM+ plus OLI. Within a 30 m field, SLC stripes remove roughly a fifth of a scene's pixels in a pattern unrelated to crops, so a field's median composite in the target years is drawn from fewer, differently distributed pixels than in training.
- Because the notebook drops any field-year with a NaN feature, missingness never enters the models as a value; it acts as sample selection. The fraction of fields removed per region-year is not reported, so the reader cannot tell whether the 2010 to 2012 test sets are a biased subset (fields in less cloudy sub-areas, larger fields that survive stripes) or how many fields the maps for those years actually cover. If the drop rate differs between domains, class composition and field-size distribution of the evaluated set differ as well, which would feed directly into the field-size and label-shift explanations the authors offer.
- The NH-RF result that temporal loss (minus 0.120 mF1) equals spatial loss (minus 0.120) and spatial-temporal loss (minus 0.118) is consistent with a floor set by input-quality change in the target years rather than by geography, since the geographic shift adds nothing once the year shift is present for that model.
- Rank 3 by the rubric: target differs in year and sensor configuration; strong likelihood of different gap structure (SLC-off plus one fewer sensor); no per-domain missingness statistics; preprocessing silently drops incomplete rows; degradation attributed to environment, management and label shift; missingness named only as a possibility and dismissed with a citation rather than a test.
- Confidence: medium-high. The code confirms `dropna()` and no gap filling; the remaining uncertainty is whether the authors ran unreported scene-count checks.

### Gaps
- No per-domain count of scenes per bimonth, no fraction of SLC-gap pixels per field, and no count of fields removed by `dropna()`, in paper or repository.
- The scenario notebooks (`01_s123_*`) were not read; they may apply additional filtering to the 2010 to 2012 tables.
- `eemont`'s `maskClouds()` for Landsat Collection 2 uses the QA_PIXEL bits; which bits (cloud, shadow, dilated cloud, cirrus, snow) were enabled is not stated in the paper or visible in the fetched script.

## Paper 3. Xie, Zhang, Liu and Sun 2025, "Investigation of the spatio-temporal transferability of large-scale random forest crop classification models and potential influencing factors" (Int. J. Digital Earth 18(2):2563211). Could the U.S. to Europe transfer differences be a missingness shift?

### Takeaway
Rank 2 (plausible), confidence medium. The pipeline is gap-free by construction (QA60/QA_PIXEL mask, two-sensor monthly median, linear interpolation of any empty month) and is applied identically on both continents, which rules out sentinel values. But the paper reports only total image counts per area, never valid-observation or interpolated-month fractions; QA60 is a coarse cloud mask without shadows; a linearly interpolated month is a different kind of feature from an observed one and will be far more frequent in cloudy European winters and springs than in Kansas; and transfer differences are attributed entirely to temperature-profile similarity.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Source: Kansas, South Dakota, Minnesota, CDL labels, 2019 to 2023; target: Germany, France, Romania, Poland, Spain, Italy, LUCAS 2018 points; cross-continent and cross-year. — [Xie et al. 2025, Section 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- (b) Sensor and compositing: all Landsat-8 Collection 2 SR and Sentinel-2 L2A (Sen2Cor) images in GEE, six matched bands resampled to 30 m, seven indices, monthly medians March to October, 13 x 8 = 104 features. Table 1 gives total image counts: U.S. states 5,461 to 6,984 per state-year; Europe 2018: Germany 12,590, France 15,536, Romania 6,820, Poland 9,494, Spain 16,093, Italy 11,935. "We did not use images from 2018 as training data because, compared to 2019 and subsequent years, there were insufficient Sentinel-2 images from 2018." — [Xie et al. 2025, Section 2.1.2 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- (c) Cloud and gap handling: "(3) Cloud masking was performed using the 'QA_PIXEL' band for Landsat-8 and the 'QA60' band for Sentinel-2; (4) Calculating the median of all valid observations within the same month"; then "gaps in the monthly time series may still occur for some regions ... due to the lack of valid observations caused by the few image data acquired or highly frequent cloud cover. Therefore ... the gaps of time series were filled using simple linear interpolation" between the nearest valid months before and after (Eq. 8). The stated purpose is "to ensure that each pixel in the entire study area has the same temporal interval so that the training area and the mapping area have consistent time series feature representation." — [Xie et al. 2025, Section 2.2.2](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: none beyond Table 1's raw image totals. No count of valid observations per month, no fraction of interpolated months per country or state. — [Xie et al. 2025, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- (e) Code and data: no code. The figshare deposit (10.6084/m9.figshare.29925809.v1) contains 21 CSV files of training (CDL, Kansas/South Dakota/Minnesota 2019 to 2023) and validation (LUCAS, six countries 2018) sample features with band, index, precipitation and temperature columns; the figshare API listing shows no GEE scripts, no Python, and no image-count or valid-observation statistics. — [figshare API record 29925809](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/29925809); [Xie et al. 2025, Data availability statement](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- (f) Target cloudiness as stated by the paper: Europe is "predominantly temperate and continental, but has an oceanic climate along the western coast and a Mediterranean climate in the southern regions", versus U.S. interior states in "humid continental and humid subtropical climate zones". Romania has the lowest image count (6,820) and the lowest OA (88.1); Spain has the highest count (16,093) and the highest OA (96.8). — [Xie et al. 2025, Sections 2.1.1, Table 1, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- (g) Attribution: "Crop phenology is often influenced by meteorological conditions"; correlation between OA and temperature-pattern similarity exceeds 0.6 (p < 0.01) in five of six countries; precipitation similarity is not significant, "reflect[ing] irrigation-dependent agriculture". Observation density or interpolation share is not analysed as a factor. — [Xie et al. 2025, Section 3.5](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)

### Inferences
- Linear interpolation makes the feature vector complete but not identically distributed: an interpolated March or April value lies on a straight line between February and May medians, with no intra-month variance, whereas an observed month carries real phenology and residual cloud noise. The proportion of such synthetic months is almost certainly higher in Germany, Poland and Romania (cloudy late winter and spring) than in Kansas, and higher in Romania than in Spain. This is a covariate shift unrelated to crops that the model never saw in training proportions. The coincidence that the sparsest-imaged country (Romania) is the worst-transferred and the densest (Spain) the best is consistent with, though far from proof of, a missingness contribution; it is also consistent with the authors' phenology story (Spain is the outlier where temperature similarity did not correlate).
- QA60 is a scene-level cirrus and opaque-cloud vector mask with no shadow component, so residual cloud and shadow contamination in monthly medians scales with cloudiness and is therefore larger in the European target than in the Kansas source. The median is robust only when clean observations are the majority within the month.
- The decision to exclude U.S. 2018 for "insufficient Sentinel-2 images" while using 2018 as the only European target year means the two domains were deliberately chosen for different data-density regimes; the authors treated density as a prerequisite for training but not as a factor in evaluating transfer.
- Rank 2 by the rubric: gap handling is described and applied identically, but target gap rates are not reported and the interpolation hides rather than removes the difference; attribution is purely climatic. Not rank 3 because there is no sentinel value, no dropping of dates, and the binary corn/wheat task is tolerant of moderate feature noise (transfer within 0.5 to 2 OA points of local models).
- Confidence: medium. Without code or per-month valid-observation counts, the share of interpolated months per country cannot be estimated from the deposit.

### Gaps
- No per-country or per-state fraction of interpolated months; the CSV deposit holds final features only, so one could in principle detect interpolated months as exactly collinear triplets, but this was not attempted.
- The supplement (Sections I to III, variable importance and deep-learning tests) was not fetched.
- Whether a month with no valid observation at either end of the season (March or October) was extrapolated, held constant or dropped is not stated; Eq. 8 requires neighbours on both sides.

## Paper 4. Nowakowski et al. 2021, "Crop type mapping by using transfer learning" (Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf. 98:102313). Is there a satellite missingness shift at all?

### Takeaway
Rank 1 (not applicable), confidence high. The "transfer" is ImageNet weights fine-tuned on drone RGB orthomosaics; evaluation is an in-region, same-campaign hold-out of distribution-matched mosaics; there is no satellite time series, no cloud mask, and no target domain in the sense of this audit.

### Cited Findings
- (a, b) Data: drone orthomosaics, Malawi (Kasungu, May 2018, 40 mosaics, about 4 cm) and Mozambique (Gaza, spring 2019, 30 mosaics, about 1.3 cm); 224 x 224 x 3 RGB patches; validation on 4 held-out mosaics per country chosen so that "a fixed test dataset has been chosen in order to have a distribution of the features similar to the one of the training dataset." — [Nowakowski et al. 2021, Sections 3 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2021.102313); [local briefer](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Crop%20type%20mapping%20by%20using%20transfer%20learning.md)
- (c) Clouds appear only as within-image nuisance: "in Fig. 3(b) shadows from clouds or close trees are observed", listed alongside roads, weeds and lighting as sources of intra-class variability that CNNs are expected to absorb. No masking or gap handling exists because there is no time series. — [Nowakowski et al. 2021, Section 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2021.102313)
- (d) No per-domain missingness statistics; none would apply.
- (e) No code repository or data deposit is cited in the PDF text (grep for github, code, availability returned only generic matches). — [Nowakowski et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2021.102313)
- (f) Not applicable; drones fly below cloud.
- (g) The paper does not report transfer degradation; it reports that fine-tuned ImageNet CNNs reach about 83 percent (Malawi) and 90 percent (Mozambique) weighted accuracy and that from-scratch training fails to converge; it acknowledges that generalisation to "different geographical areas or temporal intervals" is "not fully addressed". — [Nowakowski et al. 2021, Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2021.102313)

### Inferences
- The paper cannot exhibit a missingness confound because there is no missingness mechanism in RGB drone mosaics and no out-of-domain evaluation. It should be excluded from any count of transfer-failure papers; its relevance to our argument is only as an example of in-region evaluation.
- Confidence: high.

### Gaps
- None material. Whether cloud shadows in the orthomosaics differed between training and validation mosaics is unknowable from the paper and immaterial to the satellite-missingness question.

## Paper 5. Pandžić, Pavlović, Marko and Kilibarda 2026, "Transfer Learning for Cross-Regional Crop Mapping" (Geodetski vestnik 70(2):268-288). Could the Slovenia to Vojvodina naive-transfer collapse be a missingness shift?

### Takeaway
Rank 2 (plausible), confidence medium. Cloud Score+ masking, parcel medians and cubic-spline resampling with edge padding are applied identically to both domains, so no model sees a sentinel value, and the paper reports acquisition counts per region (617 versus 510 Sentinel-2 images). But valid (cloud-free) observation counts per parcel and the fraction of interpolated or padded time steps per domain are never reported, the code applies no minimum-observation filter, Slovenia (Alpine to humid temperate, fragmented parcels) and Vojvodina (Pannonian plain) plausibly have different cloud regimes so the spline fills different gaps in each domain, and the transfer loss is attributed to parcel-size, management and KS-confirmed covariate shift with interpolation presented as the cure for cloudiness rather than as a possible source of shift.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Source: Slovenia, 159,533 parcels from mandatory declarations; target: Vojvodina, Serbia, 2,399 field-surveyed parcels; both 2021 season, 1 April to 30 September; nine crops. Serbian surveys "prioritized large, homogeneous parcels". — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Section 2.2 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)
- (b) Sensor and acquisition counts: Sentinel-2 L2A (12 bands plus ten indices) and Sentinel-1 GRD IW (VV, VH, ratio); Table 1 reports "S2 images 617, S1 images 324" for Slovenia and "510, 303" for Serbia. Per-parcel medians per acquisition date, then resampling to a 15-day grid. — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Table 1 and Section 3.1](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)
- (c) Cloud and gap handling: "cloud and cloud shadow masking was performed using the Cloud Score+ quality assessment product"; parcel medians use "every available satellite image in the time series (except for those excluded on the basis of Cloud Score+ due to high cloud cover)"; "Since the number of acquisitions, as well as the very dates of acquisitions, could vary between the two test areas (or even between parcels of the same region), and especially having in mind varying cloud cover over the regions that affect the usability of images, it was decided to preprocess downloaded data in order to achieve the time series of the same length, without gaps. For this reason, we applied cubic spline interpolation ... and selected dates at a 15-day interval. Instead of extrapolating, in the case of missing data at the beginning or end of the time period, forward and backward padding was used." — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Sections 2.4 and 3.1, Eq. 2](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: acquisition totals only (Table 1). No count of cloud-free observations per parcel, no distribution of K (observed time points before interpolation) per region, no fraction of padded steps. — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)
- (e) Code: the URL printed in the paper (github.com/milospandzic/TL_4_crossregional_crop_mapping) returns 404; the live repository is `TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping` (hyphenated), described as "Codes for second phd paper", with files `00_GEE.ipynb`, `01_making_df.py`, `02_duplicate_n_pivot.py`, `03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py`, `04_add_classes_to_df.py`, `helpers.py`, `hypp_optim_generalized.py`, `inference.py`, `transformers_modules.py`. — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Software availability](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288); [GitHub profile](https://github.com/milospandzic); [Repository](https://github.com/milospandzic/TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping)
- (e, interpolation script) `03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py` uses `CubicSpline(group['Date'].astype('int64'), group[col], bc_type='natural', extrapolate=False)` per parcel ID and column, then `interpolated_dfs.groupby('ID').ffill().groupby('ID').bfill()` to pad edges (comment: "Ovo su tzv padding kod Ogija"). There is no `dropna`, no `fillna` other than the ffill/bfill chain, and no minimum-observation filter: "Every ID group is interpolated regardless of how many observations it has." The script is hard-coded to `os.chdir('SRB')` and `Total_SRB_S1.pkl`, with no region branches. The fetched summary also reports that the date grid is built with `pd.date_range(... freq=DateOffset(months=1))` from 2021-04-15 to 2021-09-30 while the comment says "Generate new dates at 15-day intervals"; this discrepancy should be verified by reading the file directly. — [03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/milospandzic/TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping/main/03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py)
- (f) Target cloudiness as stated by the paper: Slovenia "exhibits pronounced landscape heterogeneity due to diverse relief, climate" with climate "from temperate humid to continental and Alpine"; Vojvodina has a "temperate climate [with] hot humid summers, cold [winters]" on Chernozem/Vertisol plains. — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Section 2.1](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)
- (g) Attribution: 324 of 325 feature-wise Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests reject identical marginals, "confirming strong domain shift"; the discussion names "climate, topography, management practices, and image availability" as differences between regions, parcel size (0.78 ha versus 23.25 ha), fragmentation, mixed pixels and terrain-induced radar distortion for low Slovenian scores, and "phenological similarity with wheat and differing regional management practices" for clover confusion. Interpolation is framed as the step that "mitigat[es] the problem of irregular data acquisitions due to e.g. cloudiness". — [Pandžić et al. 2026, Sections 4.1 and 5](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)

### Inferences
- Because Cloud Score+ removes cloudy pixels before the parcel median, a parcel-date on which all pixels are masked is simply absent from that parcel's series, and the natural cubic spline then bridges the gap. A spline across a long gap (several weeks of cloud in May or June) produces smooth synthetic values that are not seen in a parcel with dense clear observations. If Slovenian parcels (mountain-adjacent, more cloud, 0.78 ha so more edge pixels masked by the Cloud Score+ buffer) have more and longer gaps than Vojvodina parcels, the source domain contains more spline-synthesised steps than the target. The model then learns on smoother, more interpolated curves and is tested on sharper, observed ones. That is a missingness shift in the opposite direction from the usual worry (source gappier than target), and it would plausibly contribute to the KS rejections the authors read as crop-related covariate shift.
- The absence of any minimum-observation filter means a parcel with two or three clear dates is spline-interpolated to the full grid with heavy ffill/bfill padding, yielding flat segments. The share of such parcels per domain is unreported and unknowable from the paper.
- "Image availability" is listed among the inter-regional differences in the discussion, so the authors are aware of acquisition asymmetry, but no analysis separates it from climate and management.
- The near-identical RF loss across modalities (about 38 to 41 F1 points for S1, S2 and S1+S2) while the Transformer's loss shrinks with richer inputs argues that at least part of the collapse is model-family behaviour under a covariate shift, not purely a gap artefact; both models receive the same interpolated vectors.
- Rank 2 by the rubric: gap handling is described and identical across domains, but valid-observation counts and interpolation fractions are not reported, and a plausible climate-driven difference in gap structure exists.
- Confidence: medium. The code confirms the spline and padding; the monthly-versus-15-day discrepancy and the missing GEE notebook contents leave the exact grid uncertain.

### Gaps
- `00_GEE.ipynb` was not read, so the Cloud Score+ threshold and whether partially cloudy parcels are kept (median over surviving pixels) or dropped are unknown.
- No per-domain distribution of observed time points before interpolation (K in Eq. 2) is given anywhere.
- The fetched summary of the interpolation script reports a monthly `DateOffset` despite the "15-day" filename and paper text; this needs direct confirmation before being cited as an error.

## Paper 6. Rustowicz et al. 2019, "Semantic Segmentation of Crop Type in Africa" (CVPR Workshops). Could the Germany versus Ghana/South Sudan performance gap be a missingness shift?

### Takeaway
Rank 1 (low suspicion, with a caveat), confidence medium-high. This is not a model-transfer paper: separate models are trained and tested within each country. Clouds are treated as a first-class input problem rather than masked away: Sentinel-2 is left unmasked, a cloud-mask band can be appended, time steps can be sampled with probability weighted by cloud-free fraction, scenes with fewer than 25 observations are dropped, and an ablation on cloud information is reported. The authors explicitly name cloudiness as a reason Ghana scores lower than South Sudan. The caveat is that cloud fractions per country are never quantified and the Germany comparison uses a different group's dataset with different preprocessing.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Domains: Ghana (labels 2016, Planet imagery 2017), South Sudan (labels 2017), Germany (Rußwurm and Körner dataset, 2016); each with its own 80/10/10 tile split "attempt[ing] to best preserve the relative percentages of all crops". No model is transferred between countries. — [Rustowicz et al. 2019, Section 3](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/rustowicz2019_CropType_Africa.pdf)
- (b) Sensors: Sentinel-2 (ten bands), Sentinel-1 (VV, VH, ratio), PlanetScope (four bands), day-of-year band; "The number of satellite observations for each scene varies from 25 to 100+ observations, as we filter out all scenes with less than 25 observations"; "due to GPU memory constraints, we take at most 40 samples from all the observations over a given grid in a year." — [Rustowicz et al. 2019, Sections 3 and 5.1](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/rustowicz2019_CropType_Africa.pdf)
- (c) Cloud handling in the paper: ablation rows "Cloud Band" and "Sample w/ Clouds" in Tables 4 and 5; for the RF baseline "we group observations from a satellite into bi-weekly buckets and take the average of each bucket for S1 imagery, the median for cloud-filtered planet imagery, and the minimum for unfiltered S2 imagery." — [Rustowicz et al. 2019, Sections 4 and 5.4](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/rustowicz2019_CropType_Africa.pdf)
- (c, code) `preprocess.py`: `remap_cloud_stack` maps cloud-mask classes so that clear pixels score highest; `sample_timeseries` computes `scores = np.mean(remapped_cloud_stack, axis=(0,1))` and draws time steps with `np.random.choice(timestamps, size=num_samples, replace=False, p=probabilities)`; `get_least_cloudy_idx` picks the clearest date; `preprocessClouds` scales the cloud band as `(clouds - 1.5)/1.5` and appends it; the `vectorize` path for RF uses `np.nanmedian` per bucket and `fill_NA`. No `nan_to_num` and no interpolation. — [preprocess.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping/master/preprocess.py)
- (c, code) `datasets.py`: `sample_w_clouds`, `include_clouds`, `least_cloudy`, `num_timesteps`, `var_length` flags; `pad_to_equal_length` zero-pads variable-length sequences to the batch maximum and returns `lengths` for the recurrent model; `split_and_aggregate` substitutes `np.zeros(...)` for an empty bucket; indices are set to 0 where the denominator band is 0. — [datasets.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping/master/datasets.py)
- (c, code) `remove_bad_timestamps.py` removes grids listed in a precomputed `bad_timestamp_grids_list.npy` plus one hard-coded grid; the criterion for "bad" is defined upstream and not in the repository. — [remove_bad_timestamps.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping/master/remove_bad_timestamps.py)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: qualitative only. "Ghana is a much cloudier region"; smallholder datasets "have smaller training sets, high cloud cover, and complex landscapes". No numeric cloud fraction per country. — [Rustowicz et al. 2019, Section 5.3.2](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/rustowicz2019_CropType_Africa.pdf)
- (e) Code and data: public at github.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping; datasets on Radiant Earth MLHub and SustainBench. — [Repository README](https://github.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping)
- (f) Target cloudiness per the paper: Ghana (northern, 9.4°N) "a much cloudier region" than South Sudan; Planet 2017 used in Ghana instead of 2016 "due to higher data availability". — [Rustowicz et al. 2019, Sections 3 and 5.3](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/rustowicz2019_CropType_Africa.pdf)
- (g) Attribution: Germany outperforms because the African sets have "smaller training sets, high cloud cover, and complex landscapes"; South Sudan beats Ghana "likely because Ghana is a much cloudier region"; "explicitly including information about clouds does not improve maximum model performance, though in some cases, such as in Ghana, including this information does seem to improve convergence speed." — [Rustowicz et al. 2019, Sections 5.3.2 and 5.4](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/rustowicz2019_CropType_Africa.pdf)

### Inferences
- Missingness cannot masquerade as transfer failure here because there is no transfer: each country's model is trained and tested on the same country's cloud regime, and the authors explicitly attribute cross-country differences to cloud cover among other things. The paper is therefore an example of a study that puts cloudiness on the table rather than one that hides it.
- Within-country, the zero-padding in `pad_to_equal_length` and the `np.zeros` fallback for empty aggregation buckets are the same sentinel-value pattern our September artefact arose from; because train and test tiles share one country and one year, the pattern is distributed identically across splits and is not a confound for their reported numbers. It would become one if anyone applied these models across countries without retraining.
- The Germany comparison is cross-dataset, with Rußwurm and Körner's preprocessing on the German side, so "Germany easy, Africa hard" conflates label volume, field size, class count, cloud regime and pipeline; the authors do not claim otherwise.
- Rank 1 by the rubric (explicit missingness controls and ablations, SAR included, cloud named as an explanation), with the reservation that per-domain cloud statistics are qualitative.
- Confidence: medium-high.

### Gaps
- No numeric cloud-free fraction per country in paper or README; the HDF5/GeoTIFF cloud-mask stacks on MLHub would allow computing it but were not accessed.
- The upstream criterion for `bad_timestamp_grids_list.npy` is not in the repository.
- The local briefer's claim that "RF macro-F1 62.4 ≥ best deep net 57.3" in Ghana conflicts with Table 2 of the PDF text (Ghana RF macro F1 40.5, 2D U-Net+CLSTM 57.3; 62.4 is the Germany RF figure); the PDF should be treated as authoritative.

## Paper 7. Tufail, Tassinari and Torreggiani 2025, "Deep Learning Applications for Crop Mapping Using Multi-Temporal Sentinel-2 Data and Red-Edge Vegetation Indices" (Remote Sensing 17:3207). Is there a transfer or a missingness shift to audit?

### Takeaway
Rank 1 (not applicable, no transfer reported), confidence high. Single Sentinel-2 tile (T32TPQ, Emilia-Romagna), single season, eleven hand-selected dates with under 5 percent scene cloud cover, stratified random polygon split. There is no target domain, so no missingness shift can be masquerading; the design is, however, non-transferable by construction because the eleven dates would not exist elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Domain: Emilia-Romagna extending into Lombardy and Veneto, Northern Italy, Sentinel-2 tile T32TPQ, November 2022 to August 2023; 60/20/20 stratified random polygon split within the tile. — [Tufail et al. 2025, Sections 2.1 and 2.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)
- (b) Acquisition scheme: "The study utilized multitemporal data corresponding to key crop growth stages ... spanning the period from November 2022 to August 2023. Only imagery with less than 5% cloud coverage over the study area was included in the analysis (Figure 3)." Eleven dates, ten bands plus NDVI, IRECI, MTCI, S2REP per date. — [Tufail et al. 2025, Section 2.2.1](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)
- (c) Cloud handling: scene-level filter only; no pixel-level mask (SCL, s2cloudless) is mentioned, no compositing, no interpolation, no fill value; the keyword sweep of the full text for cloud, mask, gap, interpolation, SCL and composite returned only the 5 percent filter sentence and a natural-colour composite figure caption. — [Tufail et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: not applicable (one domain); no within-domain cloud statistics beyond the scene filter.
- (e) Code and data: "Data will be made available on request." No repository. — [Tufail et al. 2025, Data Availability Statement](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)
- (f) Not applicable; the authors chose dates to avoid cloud. The Po Valley's winter fog and stratus season (November to February) is the reason only a few of the eleven dates fall in that window (inference from Figure 3's date list, which was not readable in the extracted text).
- (g) No transfer degradation is reported; the limitations paragraph concedes the need to test "generalizability to other areas". — [Tufail et al. 2025, Section 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)

### Inferences
- With one scene-filtered tile and interleaved train/test polygons, both splits have exactly the same eleven dates and residual cloud, so no missingness differential exists. The paper belongs in the "in-region only" bin, not in the transfer-failure bin.
- If the ranking (2D CNN-GRU 99 percent) were ever evaluated on another tile or year, the first failure mode would be the absence of the same eleven acquisition dates, which is a missingness shift of the most extreme kind (every date missing), before any phenological or spectral shift could matter.
- Confidence: high.

### Gaps
- Figure 3's exact acquisition dates were not recoverable from the text layer.
- Whether the under-5 percent filter refers to the tile metadata `CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE` or to a local AOI estimate is not stated.

## Paper 8. Schulthess et al. 2023, "Optimal Sample Size and Composition for Crop Classification with Sen2-Agri's Random Forest Classifier" (Remote Sensing 15:608). Is there a transfer or a missingness shift to audit?

### Takeaway
Rank 1 (not applicable, no transfer reported; careful about gaps), confidence high. Single irrigation district (Yaqui Valley, Sonora), single winter season, twelve cloud-free Sentinel-2A images, MAJA masking with linear interpolation to a 10-day composite series inside the Sen2-Agri system, random 80/20 field split. The authors explicitly credit the "dry and relatively cloud-free winter" for their accuracies and caution that results need testing "in regions with ... frequent cloud cover".

### Cited Findings
- (a) Domain: Yaqui Valley, Sonora, Mexico, winter 2016 to 2017, Sentinel-2 tile 12RXR; 6,084 digitised fields; fixed 20 percent validation fields drawn randomly within the district. — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Sections 2.1 and 2.4](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)
- (b) Acquisition scheme: "All in all, 12 images, acquired by Sentinel-2A for tile 12RXR between 13 November 2016, and 22 April 2017, could be used for the analysis"; "During the two months of rapid canopy growth between January and February, we had five cloud-free images available to generate the ten-day composite images." — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Sections 2.2 and 4](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)
- (c) Cloud and gap handling: Sen2-Agri "masks out clouds and shadows and applies an atmospheric correction using the MACCS ATCOR Joint Algorithm (MAJA). Masked-out pixels are gap filled, based on a linear interpolation between cloud-free pixels of the previous and subsequent image(s). This results in a series of images with a 10-day interval spanning the entire growing season." — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Section 2.3](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)
- (d) Per-domain missingness statistics: single domain; the twelve acquisition dates are listed on Figure 3 and the January to February count of five is stated. — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Figure 3 and Section 4](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)
- (e) Code and data: no code (operational Sen2-Agri toolbox, which the authors could not modify: they "could not tune hyperparameters or try other algorithms"); field labels deposited at CIMMYT Research Data Repository, hdl.handle.net/11529/10548637. — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Data Availability Statement](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)
- (f) Target cloudiness per the paper: "wheat is the only crop widely grown under irrigation during the dry and relatively cloud-free winter months, such as in Egypt, the Indo-Gangetic Plain, or the North China Plain ... This is different from, e.g., Europe or tropical regions during the rainy season, where the creation of composite images based on optical satellites is more challenging." — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Section 4](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)
- (g) No transfer degradation is reported; the conclusion flags "additional testing should be done in regions with more than one dominant crop or with frequent cloud cover" and speculates that the 10-day standardised series "might" allow applying a classifier across years. — [Schulthess et al. 2023, Section 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)

### Inferences
- There is no transfer and therefore nothing to masquerade. As a gap-handling exemplar it is the cleanest of the eight: an operational MAJA mask, documented interpolation, a listed acquisition calendar, and an explicit statement that the cloud-free season is a precondition for the result. This is the paper to cite as a positive counter-example when arguing that completeness should be treated as a prerequisite of transfer.
- Confidence: high.

### Gaps
- The fraction of pixels gap-filled per 10-day composite is not reported, though with twelve cloud-free scenes it is presumably small.
- The Sen2-Agri version and MAJA parameters are not given.

## Summary ranking across the eight papers

### Takeaway
Only one paper reaches rank 3 (Wijesingha et al. 2024: sensor and SLC-gap change between source and target years, `dropna()` sample selection, no gap statistics, attribution to environment and labels). Three are rank 2 (Rusňák 2023, Xie 2025, Pandžić 2026): all describe their gap handling and apply it identically to both domains, but none reports valid-observation or interpolated-step fractions per domain, and each has a plausible climatological or acquisition-density asymmetry that its attribution never tests. Four are rank 1: Rustowicz 2019 because it models clouds explicitly and names them as an explanation; Nowakowski 2021, Tufail 2025 and Schulthess 2023 because they report no transfer at all.

### Cited Findings
- Rusňák 2023: Rank 2. Per-domain observation-count figure present, year-level null test present, zero-observation rule absent, East tiles imaged at under half the per-tile rate of Danube tiles (215/2 versus 771/3). — [Rusňák et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- Wijesingha 2024: Rank 3. Target years 2010 to 2012 on Landsat 5 and SLC-off Landsat 7 with stripes as no-data; `dropna()` in the notebook; no scene or gap counts; attribution to environment, management, label shift and AOA. — [Wijesingha et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020); [Notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability/main/00_1_SR_VI_NH_Data_model_training_TF_BCNN_RF.ipynb)
- Xie 2025: Rank 2. QA60/QA_PIXEL masks, monthly medians, linear interpolation of empty months on both continents; only raw image totals reported; attribution to temperature-profile similarity; Romania the least-imaged and worst-transferred country. — [Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211); [figshare deposit](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/29925809)
- Nowakowski 2021: Rank 1 (N/A). Drone RGB, ImageNet fine-tuning, in-region distribution-matched hold-out. — [Nowakowski et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2021.102313)
- Pandžić 2026: Rank 2. Cloud Score+ mask, parcel medians, natural cubic spline with ffill/bfill padding and no minimum-observation filter; acquisition totals reported (617 versus 510 S2 images) but not valid counts; attribution to KS-confirmed covariate shift, parcel size and management. — [Pandžić et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288); [Interpolation script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/milospandzic/TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping/main/03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py)
- Rustowicz 2019: Rank 1. No cross-country model transfer; cloud band, cloud-weighted time-step sampling, 25-observation scene floor, cloud ablation; "Ghana is a much cloudier region" given as an explanation. — [Rustowicz et al. 2019 repository](https://github.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping)
- Tufail 2025: Rank 1 (N/A). Single tile, eleven dates under 5 percent cloud, no transfer. — [Tufail et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)
- Schulthess 2023: Rank 1 (N/A). Single district, twelve cloud-free scenes, MAJA plus linear interpolation to 10-day composites, authors credit the cloud-free season. — [Schulthess et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)

### Inferences
- A pattern across the three rank-2 papers: each solved the "unequal feature length" problem (monthly median, linear interpolation, cubic spline) and then treated the resulting complete vector as if it were identically distributed across domains. None reports how many of those values are synthetic per domain, so none can rule out that the model learned on a different mixture of observed and reconstructed values than it was tested on. Our own corrected pipeline (training-tile median fill applied to every model) belongs to the same family, which is a reason to report the per-domain filled fraction ourselves rather than to criticise these papers for omitting it.
- The direction of the shift differs by paper and matters for interpretation: in Wijesingha the target years are gappier than the source; in Rusňák the spatial target (East) is less densely acquired than the source; in Pandžić the source (Slovenia) is plausibly cloudier than the target (Vojvodina); in Xie the European target is plausibly cloudier than Kansas. A missingness confound does not always inflate the apparent transfer loss; a model trained on smoother interpolated series and tested on sharp observed ones (the Pandžić case) could fail for input-shift reasons that look like covariate shift in a KS test.
- Papers with no deposited code (Rusňák, Xie, Tufail) cannot be audited further at the pipeline level; the two with code (Wijesingha, Pandžić) both confirmed the paper's description and both revealed details the paper omits (`dropna()`; no minimum-observation filter and a possible monthly rather than 15-day grid).

### Gaps
- No fetched source gave region-specific Sentinel-2 or Landsat clear-observation counts for Slovakia, Hesse versus Weser-Ems, Slovenia versus Vojvodina, Kansas versus the six European countries, northern Ghana versus South Sudan, Emilia-Romagna or Sonora; the Lewińska 2024 Europe paper (Eur. J. Remote Sens. 57(1) 2372855) and its global dataset exist and would supply them, but the full text and data were not accessible through the tools used here (publisher pages returned 403 or redirects; the PMC Data in Brief text reports no regional numbers). Regional cloud statements in the cards above are therefore the papers' own statements or labelled inference.
- Supplementary materials for Rusňák (figshare 23309198) and Xie (Sections I to III) were not read.
- Only one Wijesingha notebook and one Pandžić script were read; the scenario notebooks and the GEE notebook could change details of row filtering and cloud thresholds.
