# Missingness confound audit of transfer papers, set B: domain adaptation, pretraining and benchmark papers

Scope: eleven papers assigned to this researcher. For each paper the card records (a) source/target regions and years, (b) sensor and acquisition/compositing scheme, (c) cloud/shadow/gap handling, (d) whether target-vs-source missingness statistics are reported, (e) code/data repository and what the code does with missing data, (f) regional cloud likelihood for the target season, (g) how the paper attributes degradation, then a suspicion rank (1 low, 2 plausible, 3 highly suspect) with justification and confidence. Local PDFs were read with `pdftotext` from `writeup/literature/`; repositories were read on GitHub; publisher full texts of papers 1 and 11 were not accessible (ScienceDirect, ResearchGate and Semantic Scholar all returned either 403 or "closed access"), so those two cards rest on the abstract, introduction and ScienceDirect section snippets only.

## Ranking summary across the eleven papers

### Takeaway
Three papers are highly suspect (rank 3): TimeMatch (cloudy observations deliberately left in, different acquisition structure per tile, no per-tile cloud statistic, degradation attributed entirely to temporal shift), the Hexi Corridor transfer paper (humid US Midwest source to arid NW China target with QA60 masking, weekly max composites, linear interpolation, and cloud-affected target samples discarded while source samples were not), and, provisionally, the Wang et al. 2023 DACCN paper (US Corn Belt to NE China across years, preprocessing not visible in any accessible text). Five are plausible (rank 2): the SFUDA paper (careful valid-pixel filter and per-site validity table, but linear interpolation and 86 to 100 percent validity spread), the MODIS cross-year DG paper (bilinear interpolation in a region with 15 to 45 percent clear-sky frequency, no per-year gap counts), the EuroCropsML benchmark (brightness-threshold cloud screening, no per-country observation counts), Presto (quality-mosaic composites with no per-pixel mask, but explicit missing-month ablations and no transfer claim) and Mask-PSTIN (training-time masking only, full text unavailable). Three handle the issue well enough for rank 1: WorldCereal's deployment paper (SCL dilated mask, nodata passed as a Presto mask token, and an explicit SSL step motivated by the processing shift between pretraining and deployment data), SITS-BERT (Sen2Cor scene-classification removal, irregular DOY-encoded series, pretext task that simulates cloud noise, per-region image counts reported) and the hyperspectral single-date paper (no time series, so no temporal missingness is possible; its failure is spectral/sensor shift).

### Cited Findings
- Rank table (this researcher's assessment, evidence in each card below): 1 Wang 2023 DACCN = 3 (medium-high confidence, full text read 28 Oct 2026); 2 Mohammadi 2024 SFUDA = 2 (medium-high); 3 Nyborg 2022 TimeMatch = 3 (high); 4 Shibuya 2025 MODIS-SOM = 2 (medium); 5 Pankajakshan 2025 hyperspectral = 1 (medium-high, not applicable to temporal missingness); 6 Hexi Corridor 2025 = 3 (medium-high); 7 Reuss 2026 EuroCropsML = 2 (medium); 8 WorldCereal deployment = 1 leaning 2 (medium); 9 Presto = 2 (medium); 10 SITS-BERT = 1 (medium); 11 Mask-PSTIN = 2 (low). — Sources are the per-paper cards below.

### Inferences
- The two rank-3 papers with verifiable code (TimeMatch) or verifiable text (Hexi) share the same structural problem: an input pipeline that lets cloud or gap structure differ between source and target, no per-domain gap statistic, and a degradation narrative built entirely on phenology.
- The papers that handle missingness carefully all do so by changing the model interface (irregular sequences with date encoding plus a mask, or mask tokens for nodata) rather than by filling values, and none of them uses a fixed monthly grid with zero fill.

### Gaps
- Full texts of papers 1 and 11 could not be obtained (publisher 403, no open-access copy on Semantic Scholar, ResearchGate blocked), so their data-handling cards are incomplete and their ranks provisional.

## Paper 1: Wang, Feng, Zhang, Tian (2023), An unsupervised domain adaptation deep learning method for spatial and temporal transferable crop type mapping using Sentinel-2 imagery (ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 199, 102-117)

### Takeaway
Cross-continent (US Corn Belt to NE China) and cross-year (2018 to 2019) UDA with MK-MMD alignment. The full text (obtained 28 October 2026, `writeup/literature/An unsupervised domain adaptation deep learning method for spatial and.pdf`) describes cloud masking, 10-day median compositing, linear interpolation of empty windows and Savitzky-Golay smoothing, reports no per-domain valid-observation or interpolated-step statistic, has no code or data availability statement, and attributes domain shift to "meteorology-induced phenological and crop calendar differences" and the "exceptionally wet spring" of 2019. Rank 3 confirmed, confidence medium-high.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Regions and years: "Four states in the United States (U.S.) Corn Belt and two provinces in northeastern China were used as study areas, where samples ... were collected based on time-series Sentinel-2 imagery and reference maps in 2018 and 2019"; the four states are Minnesota, Nebraska, Wisconsin and Iowa; "three experiments were designed to verify the transferability of DACCN across space, time, and space-time" — [ScienceDirect abstract and Study areas snippet, local PDF](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)
- (b) Sensor: Sentinel-2 "Top-of-atmosphere (TOA) reflectance images (level-1C) in 2018 and 2019", 10 bands (Blue, Green, Red, RE1 to RE4, NIR, SWIR1, SWIR2) plus NDVI and NDWI, extracted "from the beginning of April to the end of October"; "a total of 252 features were adopted for modeling, including 12 features and 21 temporal observations for each"; preprocessing "performed on Google Earth Engine platform", following You et al. (2021). Samples are 3,000 random pixels per class per region, labelled from the CDL (confidence layer thresholded at 90 percent, upsampled to 10 m) and the You et al. 2021 Northeast China maps. NDVI temporal profiles per region and year are in Fig. 4 — [full text, Sections 2.2 and 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)
- (c) Cloud/gap handling: "cloudy observations were removed using a cloud masking algorithm (Oreopoulos et al., 2011). 10-day composites were generated using the median values of the valid observations, with data gaps filled by linear interpolation. Further, Savitzky-Golay algorithm was adopted to smooth the resulting time series data by fitting successive seven adjacent data points with a quadratic polynomial (Feng et al., 2021a)." The Oreopoulos et al. 2011 reference is a band-threshold cloud mask developed for MODIS land bands and implemented on Landsat; it has no cloud-shadow test. No minimum number of valid observations per composite or per pixel is stated — [full text, Section 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)
- (d) Target vs source missingness statistics: none anywhere in the full text; no count of valid observations, empty composites or interpolated steps for any of the twelve region-years. The supplement is cited only for Tables S1 to S6 (training and validation accuracies) — [full text](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)
- (e) Code/data: the full text has no code or data availability statement and no repository link; the only back matter is a Declaration of Competing Interest and "Appendix A. Supplementary data" (Tables S1 to S6). Semantic Scholar reports no open-access PDF (status null); GitHub code and repository searches for DACCN, the authors and the MK-MMD crop pipeline return nothing — [full text, back matter](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002); [Semantic Scholar API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002?fields=openAccessPdf)
- (e2) Recipe code: Section 2.4 says "Following You et al. (2021), we then preprocessed the satellite imagery". You et al. 2021 (Scientific Data 8:41) state "JavaScript code used to generate the cropland layer and crop type maps are available from the figshare repository" and describe the same chain: "cloudy observations of the S2 TOA data were removed based on the adjusted cloud score algorithm" (citing Oreopoulos et al. 2011), "(1) 10-day composites were generated with the median values of the valid S2 observations; (2) data gaps were filled by the linear interpolation to achieve full coverages throughout the temporal domain, and (3) 10-day time series data were smoothed by using the Savitzky-Golay (SG) filter ... window size of 70 days (7 observations) and the 3rd order polynomial". The figshare record (10.6084/m9.figshare.13090442.v1, CC BY 4.0) holds "GEE code for crop classification" and "GEE code for cropland classification"; both are copied with a README to `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/you2021_gee_code/` — [You et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-021-00827-9); [figshare 13090442](https://figshare.com/articles/figure/The_10-m_crop_type_maps_in_Northeast_China_during_2017-2019/13090442)
- (e3) What the script does: `cloudMask` is the Earth Engine simple cloud score (Hancher, Hewig, Housman) with `cloudScoreThreshold = 0.2` on L1C TOA and no shadow test despite the comment "simply the cloud_and_shadow_mask"; composites are `median()` over `ee.Filter.dayOfYear(start, start+10)` with an empty composite tagged `length` 0; interpolation loops over composites 9 to 30 (DOY 90 to 300, 22 steps) and takes `before` and `after` as `.mosaic()` of the nearest valid composite within `LIC.slice(i-9, i)` and `LIC.slice(i+1, i+10)`, fills masked pixels with `targetImg.select(bands).unmask(Y)`, and returns `filledImage.unmask(0)`; smoothing is `s2Tools.sgsmooth(interpolated, bands, 3, 7)` from the private module `users/nanshany1993/common:sentinel2`, not in the deposit. No valid-observation count is computed or exported — [local copy](research_notes/Missingness%20confound%20audit%20of%20transfer%20papers/you2021_gee_code/GEE_code_for_crop_classification.js)
- (f) Regional cloud likelihood: the same first author's follow-up multi-source UDA paper transfers from 11 US states to three NE China provinces with Sentinel-2 composites, which indicates the group's pipeline is composite-based, but the tandfonline full text returned 403 so the composite interval could not be verified — [GIScience & Remote Sensing 2023, DOI page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15481603.2022.2156123)
- (g) Attribution: "due to meteorology-induced phenological and crop calendar differences, spectral information for crops of the same type may vary across regions and years. The difference between source and target data distribution is known as domain shift" and "due to the differences in climate conditions across regions and years, trained crop ..." (Advantages section snippet). The results add that "the four U.S. states experienced a later planting of corn and soybean in 2019 due to an exceptionally wet spring, which can be easily detected by comparing the NDVI profiles" and that "it is the differences in crop phenology between regions and years that lead to the problem of domain shift"; the mapping discussion attributes Marquette's corn-to-soybean confusion to "a late start and delayed development of corn in 2019 due to prolonged cool and wet spring (Schnepf, 2020)". Missingness, cloud frequency and the interpolated fraction are never mentioned as a source of shift — [full text, Sections 4.1 and 5.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)
- Reported transfer results: DACCN overall accuracies "ranging from 0.835 to 0.922" across transfer cases, versus DCCN (same net without alignment), RF and SVM. Table 1 (cross-space, same year): DACCN / DCCN OA 2018 MN to WI 0.870 / 0.848, NE to IA 0.884 / 0.870, MN to HLJ 0.866 / 0.808, NE to JL 0.855 / 0.750; 2019 MN to WI 0.867 / 0.852, NE to IA 0.905 / 0.885, MN to HLJ 0.864 / 0.791, NE to JL 0.855 / 0.738. Table 2 (cross-year, same region): DACCN 0.845 to 0.924, DCCN 0.797 to 0.916, with the largest unadapted cross-year losses in IA (DCCN 0.822 for 2018 to 2019) and WI (0.797 for 2019 to 2018). County mapping (Table 4, 2018 to 2019): DACCN OA 0.882 Marquette, 0.893 Marshall, 0.763 Keshan, 0.878 Yushu, with macro-F1 0.645 to 0.889 — [full text, Tables 1, 2 and 4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)

### Inferences
- The rubric's rank-3 conditions are all met on the full text: different region and year, strongly different climate zone (humid continental Corn Belt summer vs monsoon-influenced NE China, with the record-wet 2019 Corn Belt spring), a fill rule (linear interpolation of empty 10-day composites, then a seven-composite Savitzky-Golay window) whose footprint grows with local cloudiness, no missingness statistic for any region-year, and a purely phenological attribution. The revise-to-2 condition (per-domain valid counts) is not met.
- The 2019 "exceptionally wet spring" the authors read as late planting is also a cloudier April to June, so the 2019 US windows the NDVI profiles are interpreted on are the ones most likely to be interpolated; the paper cannot distinguish the two readings.
- The Oreopoulos 2011 mask has no shadow test and L1C has no scene classification, so residual cloud and shadow enter the medians and are then smeared across up to seven composites by the smoother; the amount differs by region-year and is unmeasured.
- If DACCN ran the You et al. 2021 script as stated, its "linear interpolation" has a hidden zero-fill branch: a pixel with no cloud-free observation within 90 days on either side, or at either edge of the April to October window, is set to 0 in all 12 features before smoothing, which is the same sentinel-value mechanism as our September artefact. A cloudy April 2019 in the Corn Belt (first composites, one-sided search) is exactly where this branch fires. DACCN's 21 steps versus the script's 22 suggest a slightly different window, so the edge behaviour is an inference.
- The interpolated and zero-filled fraction per region-year is now recoverable: re-run the script's compositing and interpolation stage for 2018 and 2019 over MN, NE, WI, IA, HLJ and JL and export the `length` flags and the pre-`unmask` validity mask over the sampled pixels. Earth Engine credentials and an `ee` conda environment exist on this machine (checked 28 Oct 2026).

### Gaps
- No per-region-year valid-observation, empty-composite or interpolated-step count in the paper; recoverable by re-running the You et al. 2021 script (above), not yet done.
- The Savitzky-Golay module `users/nanshany1993/common:sentinel2` is a private Earth Engine repository and was not retrieved; it does not affect the valid-count measurement.
- The group's open-access follow-up (GIScience & RS 2022, CC BY, DOI 10.1080/15481603.2022.2156123) could not be downloaded: Taylor & Francis returns a Cloudflare challenge to every unauthenticated fetch, and Unpaywall and OpenAlex list no repository copy.
- No code repository and no data availability statement in the article; whether one exists elsewhere is unknown.
- Supplementary Tables S1 to S6 (in-domain training and validation accuracies) are not in the local PDF, so the in-domain to transfer drop cannot be computed from the article alone.

## Paper 2: Mohammadi, Belgiu, Stein (2024), A source-free unsupervised domain adaptation method for cross-regional and cross-time crop mapping from satellite image time series (Remote Sens. Environ. 314, 114385)

### Takeaway
The most carefully documented pipeline in this set: a per-pixel valid-observation filter (not filled, shadow, cloudy, dilated cloud or unclear), a minimum of two valid observations in every third of the season, then linear interpolation to a 28-step weekly grid, applied identically to all models, with a per-site, per-year table of the fraction of pixels retained (86.39 to 99.99 percent). Still rank 2 because the gap rate per time step is not reported, linear interpolation across long gaps changes the input distribution more in cloudier site-years (Missouri 2020 retains 86 percent versus Iowa 2020 99.99 percent), the TimeMatch sub-experiment inherits unmasked clouds, and degradation is attributed to phenology and label shift with clouds listed only as a limitation.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Regions/years: three 45 km x 45 km US sites in Iowa, Missouri and Mississippi, "from both Dfa Cold and Cfa Temperate climatic zones", source year 2019, targets 2020 and 2021 same site (cross-time) and other sites (cross-regional); plus a 450 km x 300 km Site L (2021) spanning six ARD tiles; plus PASTIS (France, Sentinel-2, 2018/2019) and TimeMatch tiles 32VNH Denmark and 31TCJ France (2017) — [local PDF Sec. 3.1, 3.3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- (b) Sensor/acquisition: "Landsat C2 ARD surface reflectance composites", Landsat 8 OLI and Landsat 7 ETM+, six optical bands, "time series data with intervals of seven days ... from March 20 to September 25, covering a duration of 28 weeks" — [local PDF Sec. 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- (c) Cloud/gap handling: "a valid observation is defined as a pixel observation that is not labeled as filled, shadow, cloudy, dilated cloud, or unclear. Then, we selected the pixels that contain at least two valid observations in every third of the growing season as the valid pixels and filtered out the remaining pixels ... Finally, we used linear interpolation to fill in the gaps caused by missing acquisitions and invalid observations" — [local PDF Sec. 3.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- (d) Missingness statistics: Table 1 "Statistics of valid pixels" gives validity percentage per site-year: IA 99.37/99.99/96.92 (2019/2020/2021), MO 91.99/86.39/99.60, MS 98.78/97.59/99.65, Site L 2021 97.15, Site FR 100 ("already preprocessed by the authors") — [local PDF Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- (d, TimeMatch sub-experiment) "In the TimeMatch dataset, all available observations with cloud coverage <= 80% and coverage >= 50% between January 2017 and December 2017" were used, i.e. the TimeMatch inputs are taken as-is — [local PDF Sec. 3.3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- (e) Code/data: "The code and data are available at https://github.com/Sina-Mohammadi/SFUDA-CropMapping"; the repository distributes "preprocessed data for the three sites in the USA" (Sites A, B, C = IA, MO, MS; years 2019, 2020, 2021) and boolean 1500x1500 masks that "indicate which pixels were retained after preprocessing"; the README contains no nan, fill, interpolation or cloud code because interpolation was done upstream — [SFUDA-CropMapping README](https://github.com/Sina-Mohammadi/SFUDA-CropMapping)
- (f) Regional cloud likelihood: the paper's own Table 1 is the best available evidence; Missouri 2020 lost 13.6 percent of pixels to the validity filter versus 0.01 percent in Iowa 2020, implying materially different cloud/gap structure across site-years before interpolation — [local PDF Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- (g) Attribution: Fig. 4 and text attribute shift to changed "temporal discriminativeness patterns and key phenological periods across different regions and years" and to label shift ("distribution of crop types in the target domain dataset differs"); clouds appear only as Limitation 4: "the presence of clouds in optical satellite images makes crop mapping more challenging as it affects the integrity of the phenological information ... we followed the procedure introduced in Xu et al. (2020, 2021) to deal with the missing data. Future work can investigate other methods" — [local PDF Secs. 3.1, 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)
- Degradation magnitude (source-only, macro F1 averages): cross-time CNN 72.4, Transformer 72.9, LSTM 82.5, RF 74.3; cross-regional CNN 46.1, Transformer 51.9, LSTM 51.9, RF 45.3; cross-country CNN US to France 20.0 to 40.6 — [local briefer, Table 2 summary](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/A%20source-free%20unsupervised%20domain%20adaptation%20method%20for%20cross-regional%20and%20cross-time%20crop%20mapping%20from%20satellite%20image%20time%20series.md)

### Inferences
- Pixels excluded by the validity filter are also excluded from evaluation, so the scores describe the cleaner part of each landscape; the cleaner the site-year (IA), the fewer interpolated steps, so interpolation density itself is a domain covariate that differs between MO 2020 and IA 2019. This is a plausible but untested contributor to the 21 to 31 point cross-regional loss.
- The US part of the paper is the closest to rank 1 in this set; the Denmark/France experiments inherit TimeMatch's rank-3 inputs.

### Gaps
- Per-time-step fraction of interpolated values per site-year is not reported, only the pixel-level validity percentage.
- No test of whether interpolation density correlates with error.

## Paper 3: Nyborg, Pelletier, Lefevre, Assent (2022), TimeMatch: Unsupervised cross-region adaptation by temporal shift estimation (ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 188, 301-313)

### Takeaway
Rank 3, high confidence. Four Sentinel-2 L1C tiles in Denmark, mid-west France, southern France and Austria for 2017, selected only by a tile-level cloudy-pixel percentage of at most 80 percent and data coverage of at least 50 percent; no per-pixel cloud mask is downloaded or applied for L1C, cloudy pixels remain in the parcel arrays, every tile has a different number and spacing of acquisition dates, no per-tile cloud statistic is reported, and the 43-point source-only collapse is attributed entirely to a 30 to 35 day temporal shift.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Regions/years: "Four Sentinel-2 tiles are chosen in various climates: 33UVP (Austria), 32VNH (Denmark), 30TXT (mid-west France), and 31TCJ (southern France), abbreviated as AT1, DK1, FR1, and FR2"; five tasks DK1->FR1, DK1->FR2, DK1->AT1, FR1->DK1, FR1->FR2; all 2017 — [local PDF Sec. 4.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.04.018)
- (b) Sensor/acquisition: "Sentinel-2 Level-1C products in top-of-atmosphere reflectance ... We use all available observations with cloud coverage <= 80% and coverage >= 50% between January 2017 and December 2017. Fig. 5 shows the resulting acquisition dates for the four tiles ... The inputs are irregularly sampled with variable temporal length"; bands 1, 9, 10 dropped, 20 m bands bilinearly interpolated to 10 m — [local PDF Sec. 4.2, Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.04.018)
- (c) Cloud handling in the paper: no cloud masking, gap filling or interpolation is described anywhere in the text; the briefer records "no cloud masking or gap filling is applied; cloudy dates are left in" and lists "the contribution of cloud contamination (cloudy dates are left in and never analysed)" among untested factors — [local briefer](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/TimeMatch%3A%20Unsupervised%20cross-region%20adaptation%20by%20temporal%20shift%20estimation.md)
- (e) Code, downloader: `s2_download(..., min_coverage=100.0, max_cloudy_pct=100.0, ...)` filters with `s2_images = list(filter(lambda x: x.cloudy_pct <= max_cloudy_pct, s2_images))` using `cloudy_pct = tileinfo['cloudyPixelPercentage']`; CLI default `--max_cloudy_percentage 80.0`; L1C band list is `['B02','B03','B04','B05','B06','B07','B08','B8A','B11','B12']` with metafiles `['metadata','tileInfo']` only; the cloud mask `qi/MSK_CLOUDS_B00` is fetched only for L2A ("also download cloud masks for L2A data"), "SCL is not referenced anywhere", and "No code reads or applies either mask" — [preprocessing/downloader.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/preprocessing/downloader.py)
- (e) Code, pixel preparation: "no nodata, NaN, or fill-value filtering and no temporal interpolation"; for L2A only, `cloud_mask = cloud_mask * (2**16-1)  # set clouds as max pixel value` is appended as an extra band; parcel arrays are stored as `(T, C, S)` zarr with `dates = [int(tile.yyyymmdd) for tile in s2_images]`; "Pixels are not filtered by cloud or validity before storage" — [preprocessing/prepare_pixels.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/preprocessing/prepare_pixels.py)
- (e) Code, loader: sample shape `(len(self.dates), 10, parcel["n_pixels"])`; "No padding is applied. Instead, GroupByShapesBatchSampler groups samples by (T, n_pixels)"; the only mask is `"valid_pixels": np.ones(...)`; "There are no references to nan, fill, or cloud"; `date_positions = days_after(start_date, dates)` — [dataset.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/dataset.py)
- (e) Code, transforms: `Normalize` is `np.clip(sample['pixels'], 0, self.max_pixel_value)/self.max_pixel_value`; `RandomSampleTimeSteps` draws `sorted(random.sample(range(t), self.seq_length))`; `RandomTemporalShift` adds `shift` to `positions`; "No transform detects clouds or masks cloud-affected observations. No transform checks for or replaces NaN values" — [transforms.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/transforms.py)
- (e) Data deposit: Zenodo record 5636422, `timematch_data.zip` (73.9 GB) and `trained_models_and_results.zip`; description contains no statement on clouds or missing data — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/5636422); README credits "Sentinel-2 imagery were accessed from the AWS bucket managed by Sinergise" — [GitHub README](https://github.com/jnyborg/timematch)
- (d) Per-tile cloud statistic: none in text, README or Zenodo description; only the acquisition-date strip plot (Fig. 5) — [local PDF](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.04.018)
- (f) Regional cloud likelihood: Europe's 1991-2020 mean cloud cover is 57 percent with 2021 at 55 percent and documented regional anomalies — [Copernicus ESOTC 2021](https://climate.copernicus.eu/esotc/2021/clouds-and-sunshine-duration); Lewinska et al. 2024 report that for Europe-wide Landsat/Sentinel-2 composites "cloud-, shadow-, and snow-free availability varies in space and time" and sample a 20 km grid "to capture the West-East and South-North climatic and phenological gradients", but the per-country counts were not retrievable — [Dryad dataset page](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.5tb2rbp94)
- (g) Attribution and magnitude: source-only macro F1 28.3/29.0/43.4/24.9/70.3 (avg 39.2) vs target-trained 74.6/72.4/86.9/90.6/85.7 (avg 82.0); estimated shifts FR1->DK1 +32 days, DK1->FR1 -35 days; the paper reads the collapse as temporal shift and proposes "class-wise alignments or time warping" as future work; spectral shift acknowledged but unaddressed — [local PDF Table 1 and Conclusion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.04.018)

### Inferences
- A 55 N Danish tile and a 43 N southern French tile in 2017, each filtered only by tile-level cloudiness up to 80 percent, will differ in both the number of retained dates and the share of cloud-contaminated pixels within retained dates; the LTAE positional encoding absorbs date differences but nothing removes cloud spectra. Part of what the shift estimator and ShiftAug recover may be the model learning to down-weight cloudy dates in the target rather than pure calendar alignment, which the paper cannot distinguish because no experiment varies cloud contamination.
- Because the SFUDA paper (card 2) and several later UDA papers reuse this dataset unchanged, the rank-3 input structure propagates.

### Gaps
- Number of acquisition dates per tile is shown only graphically (Fig. 5) and was not extracted numerically.
- Per-pixel cloud fraction per tile for 2017 was not computed by the paper and could not be found elsewhere; it could be computed from the Zenodo archive if the project wants a quantitative test.

## Paper 4: Shibuya et al. (2025), Cross-temporal domain generalization approach combined with self-organizing-maps for classifying MODIS time series data (Int. J. Remote Sens.)

### Takeaway
Same region (Mato Grosso), cross-year transfer from a 2019/2020 training year to 2016/2017, 2020/2021 and 2021/2022 ground samples, using MOD13Q1/MYD13Q1 16-day composites with the pixel-reliability layer and bilinear interpolation applied identically in all years. Rank 2: the fill strategy is consistent, but Mato Grosso's wet-season cloudiness is extreme and varies by year, no per-year count of interpolated composites is given, and the 8 to 10 point drop is attributed to sugarcane/pasture spectral confusion and resolution, with clouds mentioned only as a general limitation.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Region/years: Mato Grosso, Brazil; training labels from TerraClass 2020 (crop year 2019/2020); test on Embrapa ground samples from 2016/2017, 2020/2021 and 2021/2022 (5,528 samples pooled); interannual application 2004 to 2022 — [local PDF Secs. 2.1, 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2025.2527988)
- (b) Sensor/acquisition: "18-year time series (2004 to 2022) of 16-day composite products ... derived from MOD13Q1 and MYD13Q1 ... These composites are generated by selecting the highest radiometric and geometric quality pixels within a 16-day period, prioritizing observations with minimal cloud contamination" — [local PDF Sec. 2.2](https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2025.2527988)
- (c) Cloud/gap handling: "the data stack included a CLOUD band, derived from the pixel reliability layers available in MOD/MYD13Q1 products ... ## Paper 7: Reuss et al. (2026), Benchmarking for practice: Few-shot time-series crop-type classification on the EuroCropsML dataset (ISPRS Open J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 19, 100117)

### Takeaway
Rank 2, medium confidence. EuroCropsML is Sentinel-2 L1C 2021 parcel-median series for Estonia, Latvia and Portugal, cloud-screened by a red-band brightness threshold (cloud probability from B4 between 0.07 and 0.25, observations above 50 percent removed) rather than a scene-classification product, with irregular series of up to 216 steps; the benchmark pads to 366 days with a mask, so no values are filled, but no per-country observation counts are reported, and the degradation when Portugal is added to pre-training is attributed to climate, phenology and soils. ### Cited Findings
- (a) Regions/years: "EuroCropsML dataset provides data for three European countries ... - (c) Model interface: "the model is able to deal with irregular sampling problems caused by the presence of noise in the time series. Accordingly, no smoothing, gap filling, or compositing method is needed"; SVM and RF inputs were "padded ... to the maximum length by filling in the missing observations with zeros"; CNN-1D "utilized zero-padding to keep the sequence length unchanged"; scripts take `--max_length 64` — [local PDF Secs. III, VII.A; README](https://github.com/linlei1214/SITS-BERT)
- (c) Pretext task: 15 percent of observations corrupted with noise, "a 50% chance that all elements of the vector is added a positive noise to simulate abnormal reflectance increases caused by clouds and snow/ice; and a 50% chance that ... subtracted ... to simulate abnormal reflectance decreases caused by shadows" — [local PDF Sec. - (d) Missingness statistics: image counts per dataset are reported; per-pixel clear-observation distributions are not — [briefer](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Self-Supervised%20Pretraining%20of%20Transformers%20for%20Satellite%20Image%20Time%20Series%20Classification.md)

### Inferences
- This design (per-observation masking by SCL, irregular DOY-encoded sequences, cloud-noise pretext) is the template the suspect papers lack; it should be flagged as a rank-1 exemplar even though it does not test transfer.

### Gaps
- How SITS-BERT itself pads variable-length sequences to `max_length 64` (zero padding with attention mask or otherwise) is not stated in text or README.
- No test-time missing-observation experiment.

## Paper 11: Zhang, Cai et al. (2024), Improving crop type mapping by integrating LSTM with temporal random masking and pixel-set spatial information (Mask-PSTIN; ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 218B, 87-101)

### Takeaway
Rank 2, low confidence. Three regions (Auvergne, central France; Otog Front Banner, Inner Mongolia; five counties of the Jianghan Plain, Hubei, per publisher snippets) are evaluated separately, so this is not a cross-region transfer study; temporal random masking is a training-time augmentation that removes observations at some time steps; the accessible text gives neither the masking rate, nor the sensor and compositing scheme, nor any natural gap rate in test data, nor a test of robustness to missing observations at inference.

### Cited Findings
- (a) Regions: "Region A (44.6 N - 46.8 N, 2 E - 4.5 E) is Auvergne situated in central France, including four departments: Allier, Puy-de-Dome, Cantal, and Haute-Loire"; regions B and C are reported in publisher search snippets as Otog Front Banner (Inner Mongolia) and five counties on the Jianghan Plain (Hubei) — [local PDF Study area snippet](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.10.013); [ScienceDirect listing via search](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271624003897)

## Cross-cutting question: for TimeMatch and the two UDA papers, which tiles/years, were raw Sentinel-2 series cloud-masked, and is there a per-tile cloud statistic?

## Cross-cutting question: for the pretraining papers, how is input masked or padded at inference, and could an unmasked cloudy target look like a different distribution?

### Takeaway
Presto masks missing bands with a mask tensor and zero values but has no per-pixel cloud mask upstream, so a cloudy month is an observed token, not a masked one; WorldCereal fixes this by passing nodata 65535 as masked values after SCL masking and by an SSL step; SITS-BERT removes cloudy observations per pixel and consumes irregular DOY sequences. Yes, an unmasked cloudy target would be out-of-distribution for Presto's structured-masking pretraining, which is exactly WorldCereal's hypothesis H3.

### Cited Findings
- Presto inference: `mask` ones and `x` zeros for unobserved bands, mask 0 where data present; no interpolation of missing timesteps — [presto/dataops/utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nasaharvest/presto/main/presto/dataops/utils.py)
- Presto/CropHarvest composites: `qualityMosaic("cloudShadowScore")`, no SCL or cloud-probability mask — [cropharvest sentinel2.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nasaharvest/cropharvest/main/cropharvest/eo/sentinel2.py)
- WorldCereal: "Presto uses the least cloudy Sentinel-2 pixel within a time window ... while we only keep cloud-free observations ... Presto may struggle to adapt to this shift in data processing" — [local WorldCereal PDF Sec. 3.3](file:///home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Deploying%20Geospatial%20Foundation%20Models%20in%20the%20Real%20World%3A%20Lessons%20from%20WorldCereal.pdf); nodata 65535 "pass[ed] through as masked values" — [presto-worldcereal data README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldCereal/presto-worldcereal/main/data/README.md)
- SITS-BERT: cloud, cirrus, shadow, snow discarded per observation; irregular sequences; pretext noise simulates clouds and shadows — [SITS-BERT README](https://github.com/linlei1214/SITS-BERT)
- EuroCropsML benchmark: sequences padded to 366 days with padded values masked; PRESTO-XTS deterministic masking motivated by "persistent cloud cover in Sentinel-2" — [local PDF Secs. 4.2, App. B.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophoto.2026.100117)

### Inferences
- The structured-masking pretraining of Presto makes it robust to tokens that are declared missing, not to tokens that are present but cloudy; the two failure modes should not be conflated when citing Presto's "robustness to missing data".

### Gaps
- Presto's `load_tif(fillna=True)` behavior and the fraction of cloudy months in its pretraining corpus are unknown.

## Cross-cutting question: for EuroCropsML and WorldCereal, what does the pipeline do with clouds and are valid-observation counts reported per region?

### Takeaway
EuroCropsML removes observations whose B4-brightness cloud probability exceeds 50 percent and keeps irregular parcel-median series up to 216 steps; WorldCereal applies a dilated SCL mask, composites by median (monthly in current code, 10-day in the v1 system paper) and in v1 linearly interpolates remaining gaps, with 65535 nodata masked for Presto. Neither reports valid-observation counts per region.

### Cited Findings
- EuroCropsML cloud rule: B4 thresholds t1 = 0.07 and t2 = 0.25, remove > 50 percent cloud probability, keep lowest-cloud tile for multi-tile parcels; "No per-country statistics on time series length or observation counts are given" — [arXiv 2407.17458](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17458v2)
- WorldCereal code: SCL dilation kernels 17 and 77 px, erosion 3 px, `median_compositing(... period="month")`, no interpolation in file — [preprocessing.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldCereal/worldcereal-classification/main/src/worldcereal/openeo/preprocessing.py); v1 system: 10-day median composites, "remaining missing values due to prolonged cloudy periods were linearly interpolated", no regional availability breakdown — [ESSD 2023](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/5491/2023/)

### Inferences
- Both benchmarks are usable for a missingness audit only if the user recomputes observation counts from the released data (EuroCropsML series lengths; WorldCereal nodata fractions), because the papers do not.

### Gaps
- Per-region observation-count tables absent in both.
