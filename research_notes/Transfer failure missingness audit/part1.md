# Could missing data contribute to reported cross-region transfer failures? Part 1 (seven papers)

Date: 2026-10-28. Scope: seven papers that report a loss in accuracy when a crop-type model is moved to a new region (and in some cases a new year). For each one I asked whether a missing-data artifact like the one in our study (a cloud gap present only in the target, filled with a constant before a training-fitted scaler) could plausibly account for part of the reported loss. Sources: the local briefers in `writeup/literature/`, full text read with pdftotext (TimeMatch, Mohammadi, Wijesingha and Pandžić from the local PDFs; T3S and Sen4AgriNet from arXiv; Hoppe from the DLR eLib copy of the MDPI PDF, because MDPI returns HTTP 403), and code cloned at the commits given below. The codebase audit in `research_notes/SITS nodata handling audit/audit.md` and the earlier cards in `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/` (`local_transfer_papers_A.md`, `local_transfer_papers_B.md`, `additional_crop_transfer_papers_and_benchmarks.md`) were used as leads. I checked every claim taken from them against the paper or the code. Where my verdict is milder than those earlier cards, the reason is given. Nothing was executed except text extraction and grep.

Overall: none of the seven papers shows our specific failure mode, a constant written into a step that training always observed, applied only at inference. Two papers leave clear room for a missing-data contribution: TimeMatch, which T3S's cross-region experiment inherits, and Sen4AgriNet. In both cases the evidence is circumstantial, and each paper also shows another mechanism (phenological shift, label shift) that it demonstrates or at least measures. For the other papers, a material missing-data contribution is unlikely, or limited to one sub-scenario.

Climatology used below comes from `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/earthsearch_s2_l1c_cloudcover_by_month_2018-2024.csv`. These are granule-level Sentinel-2 L1C cloud-cover statistics for one point per region over 2018 to 2024. They are context only. They are not the study years, and they include orbit overlaps.

## 1. Nyborg, Pelletier, Lefèvre and Assent 2022, TimeMatch (ISPRS J. 188:301-313)

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** The data are four Sentinel-2 L1C tiles from 2017: 32VNH Denmark (DK1), 30TXT mid-west France (FR1), 31TCJ southern France (FR2) and 33UVP Austria (AT1). There are five tasks: DK1→FR1, DK1→FR2, DK1→AT1, FR1→DK1 and FR1→FR2. The model is PSE+LTAE, plus re-implemented UDA competitors on the same backbone (Sec. 4.2-4.3). Source-only macro F1 (Table 1) is 28.3, 29.0, 43.4, 24.9 and 70.3 (average 39.2). Target-trained models score 74.6, 72.4, 86.9, 90.6 and 85.7 (average 82.0), so the average deficit is about 43 points. ShiftAug alone raises source-only to an average of 49.0, and TimeMatch reaches 58.2. Code is at github.com/jnyborg/timematch (read at commit a652df9) and data at Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.5636422. Both are public.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** The paper uses "all available observations with cloud coverage ≤ 80% and coverage ≥ 50%" (Sec. 4.2) and describes no cloud masking, gap filling or interpolation. The code agrees. `preprocessing/prepare_pixels.py` defaults to `--max_cloudy_percentage 80.0`, `--min_coverage 50.0` and `--data_collection l1c` (lines 318-323). A cloud-mask band is appended only for L2A (lines 80-81), and models default to `--input_dim 10` (train.py line 293), so in the default path no mask reaches the model. Inputs are irregular sequences with day-of-year positions per tile, so a missing date is simply absent. Nothing is filled, so no fill value meets the normalization. Normalization is a fixed clip-and-divide (`Normalize`, transforms.py). Training draws 30 random dates per sample (`RandomSampleTimeSteps(30)`, train.py lines 133-137, transforms.py lines 46-68). At test time PSE+LTAE uses the full sequence; the subsampling is applied there only for `tsnet` (dataset.py lines 211, 234). So the model has a form of temporal dropout during training. Cloudy observations stay in the series as raw top-of-atmosphere reflectance in both domains.

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** Yes, in two ways. The number and spacing of acquisition dates differ by tile (Fig. 5, shown only graphically). And the share of cloud-contaminated pixels within retained scenes is uncontrolled, because scenes up to 80 percent cloudy are kept. Denmark at about 55 N and southern France at about 43 N have different cloud regimes, but the paper reports no per-tile cloud statistic. The 2018-2024 point climatology gives similar growing-season granule cloudiness for Jutland and northern France, so it cannot show a gap in either direction for these specific tiles.

**(4) Verdict: possible.** The mechanism is not ours: no constant fill, and missing dates drop out of an irregular sequence that the training augmentation already thins. What remains is raw cloudy observations at rates that may differ by tile. That would show up as spectral shift, which the paper cannot separate from phenology. Against a large missing-data share, the paper shows a coherent temporal mechanism. The estimated shifts are physically sensible (FR1→DK1 +32 days, DK1→FR1 -35 days), and shift augmentation alone recovers about 10 F1 points without touching clouds. Target-trained models also reach 82 F1 with the same cloudy inputs, so clouds do not prevent classification. They can only shift its distribution. The earlier card (`local_transfer_papers_B.md`) ranked this paper "highly suspect". I rate it lower because there is no fill artifact and the temporal explanation is demonstrated. **Data check:** from the Zenodo archive, compute per tile the number of dates and the per-date fraction of cloudy parcel pixels, using s2cloudless or the L1C MSK_CLOUDS product for the same granules. Then re-evaluate the released source-only models on the target with cloudy dates removed from the sequence. LTAE handles this natively, with no fill. If source-only F1 rises materially, clouds are part of the gap.

## 2. Turkoglu et al. 2025/2026, T3S thermal-time sampling (arXiv 2506.12885; CVPPA at ECCV 2026 per README)

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** The cross-region experiment (Sec. 5.6, Table 3) uses "the two most climatically contrasting subregions in the TimeMatch dataset", described as South of France and Denmark (the exact tile is not named; not verified whether FR2 31TCJ), with the PSE+LTAE backbone "following the TimeMatch protocol". Unadjusted PSE+LTAE gets accuracy 31.6 ± 3.3 (F1 31.8) for South of France→Denmark and 36.5 ± 2.2 (F1 36.3) for Denmark→South of France. T3S gets 38.6 and 57.7 accuracy. No in-target upper bound is reported in Table 3. The main experiments are cross-year on SwissCrop (Switzerland, Sentinel-2 L2A, 2021-2023, U-TAE, T = 24). Code is at github.com/moturkoglu/T3S (commit 66da0d4) and SwissCrop is on Hugging Face (EOA-team/SwissCrop25). The released code covers SwissCrop only; it contains no TimeMatch or PSE+LTAE experiment.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** On SwissCrop (`src/dataset.py`): duplicate dates are collapsed, keeping the copy with the fewest missing values (lines 51-63). The T3S branch then picks, in each thermal-time bin, the acquisition with the most clear pixels by the `s2_mask` cloud mask (lines 65-81). If bins are empty, it pads with evenly spaced indices that can repeat real dates (line 80). The baseline U-TAE branch takes `np.linspace` indices with no cloud check (line 101). Normalization uses fixed channel constants after selection (line 105). There is no interpolation or fill, and no mask is passed to the model. NaN handling in the reflectance arrays is not verified. For the TimeMatch experiment, the paper does not say how the "least cloudy" choice was made on TimeMatch's L1C data, which ships without a per-pixel mask in its default form. Not verified. The baseline in Table 3 is PSE+LTAE with "standard calendar-based sampling", which inherits TimeMatch's raw cloudy observations (see paper 1).

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** For the cross-region experiment, yes to the same degree as TimeMatch, with no per-tile statistics reported. The paper's own SwissCrop results show that cloud contamination matters for transfer. Deformable Sampling (calendar bins, least-cloudy pick, no thermal time) raises six-fold average cross-year accuracy from 71.2 (U-TAE) to 75.4, while full T3S reaches 77.0 (Table 1). The authors acknowledge that cloud filtering "independently improves accuracy" (App. E.1). They isolate it with a DS+TPE control on SwissCrop only (Sec. 5.7), not on TimeMatch.

**(4) Verdict: possible.** The unadjusted 31.6 and 36.5 percent baselines are TimeMatch source-only models, so they carry whatever cloud contribution TimeMatch has. In addition, the T3S gain on TimeMatch mixes thermal alignment with cloud-aware selection, which the paper separates only on SwissCrop, where cloud selection alone accounts for about 4 of the 6 accuracy points. Nothing suggests a constant-fill artifact. **Data check:** run the TimeMatch protocol with Deformable Sampling only (least-cloudy pick per calendar bin, no thermal re-indexing) for both directions. If it closes most of the gap between the 31.6/36.5 baseline and T3S, the cross-region improvement is mostly cloud handling. The per-tile cloud statistics from paper 1 apply as well.

## 3. Sykas, Sdraka, Zografakis and Papoutsis 2022, Sen4AgriNet (IEEE JSTARS 15; arXiv 2204.00951)

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** Sentinel-2 L1C, LPIS labels, 11 classes. Scenario 1 uses random splits over Catalonia 2019+2020 and France 2019. Scenario 2 trains on Catalonia 2019+2020 and tests on France 2019. Scenario 3 trains on France 2019 and tests on Catalonia 2020 (arXiv v-current, Table II and Sec. IV). OAD (parcel-level) weighted accuracy, Table IV: scenario 1 LSTM 88.52, Transformer 88.36, TempCNN 90.08; scenario 2 LSTM 91.55, Transformer 39.17, TempCNN 36.90; scenario 3 LSTM 60.60, Transformer 51.21, TempCNN 52.32. These match the 39.2 / 36.9 / 91.6 in the brief, and the metric is weighted accuracy. PAD (patch-level U-Net, ConvLSTM, ConvSTAR) also degrades in scenarios 2 and 3. Code is at github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/S4A-Models (commit ae5a376) and github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/S4A, with data at sen4agrinet.space.noa.gr / Hugging Face orion-ai-lab/S4A. All public.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** Scenes are filtered at scene level only: "All Sentinel-2 L1C images with less than 10% cloud coverage are selected" (Sec. III-B). There is no pixel cloud mask before compositing. Monthly medians are computed, and the paper says the model window is "the medians of months 4 (April) through 9 (September)" (Sec. III-C). In the code (`object-based-csv.py` lines 68-83, identical logic in `utils/PAD_dataset.py` lines ~288-307), observations are binned by month and the median is taken. Empty months become NaN through `resample` and are then filled by `interpolate_na(dim='time_bins', method='linear', fill_value='extrapolate')`. Interior empty months are therefore linearly interpolated, and edge months are linearly extrapolated. Filling happens on raw values, and per-parcel mean and std are then computed per month (lines 117-122). The OAD models get no input standardization that I could find (`model/OAD_*.py`; the Transformer multiplies raw inputs by sqrt(d_model), OAD_Transformer.py line 109). No mask is given. In PAD, Hollstein cloud masks are optional and off by default (`pad_experiments.py` lines 167-170). The pipeline is identical in all domains. Two code anomalies limit what the OAD numbers mean. First, `utils/OAD_datamodule.py` slices time-bin columns from index 4 to the 11th (0-indexed from January, lines 52-53), which reads as May to November, not April to September; I did not resolve this. Second, in the `fit` stage the training set is loaded from `{stem}_test.csv.gz`, the same file used for validation and test (lines 90-99). This has been unchanged since the initial commit of 2022-04-04. I could not verify whether the published runs used this code path. If they did, the OAD scenario numbers would not measure transfer.

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** Yes, plausibly by a lot. The paper says acquisition counts vary "from 30 to 50 timestamps for one year" by region (Sec. III-B) but gives no per-country or per-month counts. With a 10 percent scene-cloud cutoff, retained scenes depend strongly on climate. In the 2018-2024 granule climatology, the share of granules under 20 percent cloud from June to August is 0.61 to 0.84 at Lleida (Catalonia), against 0.19 to 0.27 at the northern French points (Somme, Rennes). French months are therefore much more likely to be empty and interpolated, or median-composited from one scene. That is a missingness difference aligned with the Catalonia→France split.

**(4) Verdict: possible**, the strongest candidate in this set, but not "likely". The fill is consistent and mostly in-distribution (linear interpolation between real medians), not a constant. The LSTM holds 91.55 on France with the same filled inputs that the Transformer and TempCNN fail on, which argues against the inputs being broken. Other explanations are at least as strong: the paper documents a label shift (wheat pixels "two orders of magnitude less in the train set than in the test set" in scenario 2), the OAD models receive unnormalized inputs, and the train/test file anomaly above may make the OAD results uninterpretable as transfer. The earlier card (`additional_crop_transfer_papers_and_benchmarks.md`) ranked it highly suspect. I agree the missingness asymmetry is real, but the evidence that it drives the reported drop is weak. **Data check:** from the netCDF patches, count for each parcel and month (April to September or May to November) the number of retained scenes, and flag interpolated or extrapolated months, for Catalonia 2019/2020 and France 2019. Compare the fraction of filled months between domains. Then evaluate a model (after confirming a correct train file) on France parcels with zero filled months against those with one or more.

## 4. Hoppe et al. 2024, Remote Sensing 16(9):1493

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** The study area is Brandenburg, Germany, split into north and south, in 2018. It uses Sentinel-2 L2A at 20 m and five crops (field grass, silage maize, winter rapeseed, winter rye, winter wheat). Models are RF, XGBoost, SGD, MLP, SVM and majority voting, on 272 per-parcel features (17 inputs times 16 statistics; Sec. 2.4, Table 5). There are 18 to 20 scenarios (Table 6), which train on north and test on south over windows of a day, a week, a month, April-May (half season) or a full season. Abstract: combined F1 82 percent (XGBoost) on a single day, 72 percent (XGBoost) on the half season and 61 percent (majority voting) over the full season. Majority-vote T10 per-crop F1 north→south (Table 7) is grass 83→75, maize 81→62, wheat 78→52, rye 75→51 and rapeseed 94→85. The Data Availability Statement points to public CAP data (GEOBROKER) and Copernicus imagery. No code is released.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** "All 2018 scenes are used regardless of cloud cover, as only cloud-free plot patches are extracted", with the SCL excluding cloud, shadow, invalid and water pixels, and an NDVI plausibility filter of 0.28 to 0.87 (Sec. 2.2). Each sample is one cloud-free parcel observation on one date ("The pre-processing routine retains parcel fields as samples when cloudless images of the parcels within the scenes are available", Sec. 2.4). The classifier is single-date. There is no time series, so a missing date never becomes a filled value. The window only decides which dates' observations are pooled into training and test.

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** The count of cloud-free observations differs: 24,303 north against 12,056 south (Table 4 and Sec. 3.2; Sec. 3.2 also prints a total of "26,359" against 36,359 in Table 4). So does their timing: "most cloud-free observations were collected in April and May" (Sec. 3.1). The per-date distribution by region is not reported. The day and week scenarios used the day or week "with the most cloud-free observations" (Sec. 2.5).

**(4) Verdict: unlikely.** No fill or composite is involved, so there is no fill artifact. The decline with window length is what you would expect when a single-date classifier pools phenological stages, and the paper attributes it to that (Sec. 3.2). The one missing-data channel is that north and south may have a different mix of dates within a window, so the test set covers a different phenological mix. That is sampling from cloud-free dates, not a fill. It could add to the full-season drop, but the single-day scenario (same date in both regions) still loses 9 to 33 F1 points per crop, which no date-mix effect can explain. **Data check:** request or rebuild the per-date count of cloud-free parcel observations by region, then reweight or match test dates to the training date distribution in the half-season and full-season scenarios.

## 5. Wijesingha, Dzene and Wachendorf 2024, ISPRS J. 213:72-86 (DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** The regions are North Hesse (NH) and Weser-Ems (WE), Germany. Landsat 5/7/8 runs March to October, 2010-2018, with training years 2013-2018. S1 is temporal (2010-2012, same region), S2 is spatial (other region, 2013-2018) and S3 combines both. The models are RF and a Bayesian CNN on 40 field-mean features (10 layers times 4 bimonthly medians). Table 4 (OA / mF1): NH-RF S1 74.1/0.645, S2 85.0/0.645, S3 84.4/0.647; NH-CNN 83.7/0.678, 82.8/0.614, 80.5/0.598; WE-RF 85.4/0.698, 82.2/0.678, 70.0/0.588; WE-CNN 83.6/0.695, 75.5/0.643, 62.1/0.563. Against references of roughly 0.72 to 0.77 mF1, losses run from -0.024 to -0.177 mF1. Code is at github.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability (commit 7671c70). The field-level CSVs are read from local paths, and I did not check whether they are released.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** Scenes under 60 percent cloud are kept and masked with eemont `maskClouds()`. Landsat 7 SLC-off stripes are "considered as no data pixels similar to cloud and shadow areas", and the median of what remains is taken per bimonth (Sec. 2.2.1.1; `L578_WE_Download.py`). No imputation is described. In every model notebook, `preapre_df` applies `df_years.dropna()` to all years 2010-2018 (for example `01_s123_sr_vi_NH_tf_cnn_bcnn_rf_S1.ipynb`, cell 8). A field-year with any empty bimonth is therefore removed from training and from the target alike, and nothing is filled. The notebooks import Min-Max and Standard scalers and define a per-row min-max `norm()`. Exactly which one is applied to the target data is not verified.

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** For the temporal scenarios, yes. The 2010-2012 target is observed by Landsat 5 (to May 2012) and SLC-off Landsat 7 only, against Landsat 7+8 for 2013-2018 (paper Sec. 2.2.1.1). Its composites are therefore built from fewer scenes with stripe gaps. For the spatial scenario (same years, same sensors), only climate differs. The paper says temperature and precipitation show no clear shift between regions or periods (Sec. 4-5, per the briefer). No per-domain scene counts, gap fractions or counts of dropped fields are reported. The paper acknowledges that "the number of scenes per area in a given two-month period could differ" (Sec. 5).

**(4) Verdict: possible for the temporal and spatial-temporal scenarios (S1, S3); unlikely for the spatial scenario (S2).** Because of `dropna()`, missing data never enters the models as a value. It works through composite quality (medians from fewer, striped, residually cloudy scenes) and through silent sample selection that may differ between domains. Both channels are plausible in 2010-2012 and largely absent in S2. The authors give other explanations backed by evidence (DI/AOA share correlates 0.9 with OA, plus field size, terrain and class distribution), but none of them is a test of sensor-era input quality. **Data check:** from the field tables, count per region-year the field-years removed by `dropna()` and the scenes contributing to each bimonthly median. Then compare S1 accuracy on fields whose composites draw on at least N scenes against the rest, and compare the 2010-2012 class mix after dropping against the pre-drop mix.

## 6. Mohammadi, Belgiu and Stein 2024, RSE 314:114385 (source-free UDA)

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** Three 45 km US sites (Iowa, Missouri, Mississippi), Landsat 7/8 C2 ARD, source year 2019. Cross-time targets are the same site in 2020 and 2021; cross-regional targets are the other sites in 2020 and 2021. Classes are corn, soybean and other. Backbones are CNN, Transformer, LSTM, RF and MLP. There are also cross-country experiments with PASTIS (France) and a six-class TimeMatch DK/FR task (Sec. 3.1-3.3). Source-only macro F1 averages (Table 2, per briefer): cross-time CNN 72.4, Transformer 72.9, LSTM 82.5, RF 74.3, MLP 73.2; cross-regional CNN 46.1, Transformer 51.9, LSTM 51.9, RF 45.3, MLP 46.9. The worst case is MS19→IA20/21, where the LSTM gets 19.8 and 20.2. TimeMatch six-class source-only: DK→FR 34.8, FR→DK 25.5 (Table 3). Code and preprocessed US data are at github.com/Sina-Mohammadi/SFUDA-CropMapping.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** "A valid observation is defined as a pixel observation that is not labeled as filled, shadow, cloudy, dilated cloud, or unclear". Only pixels "that contain at least two valid observations in every third of the growing season" are kept, and "we used linear interpolation to fill in the gaps caused by missing acquisitions and invalid observations", onto 28 weekly steps from 20 March to 25 September (Sec. 3.1, following Xu et al. 2020). Filtering and interpolation happen upstream and identically for all models and domains. There is no constant fill and no mask. How edge gaps before the first or after the last valid observation are filled is not stated (not verified). Pixels that fail the filter are excluded from training and evaluation. The PASTIS data are described as already preprocessed (validity 100 percent, Table 1). How PASTIS's irregular dates were mapped to the 28-step grid is not verified. TimeMatch data are used as released (Sec. 3.3).

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** Somewhat. Table 1 gives the valid-pixel share per site-year: IA 99.37 / 99.99 / 96.92 (2019 / 2020 / 2021), MO 91.99 / 86.39 / 99.60 and MS 98.78 / 97.59 / 99.65. Missouri 2020 is clearly gappier. The fraction of interpolated weekly steps per site-year is not reported. The same table shows a much larger label shift: corn is 54.5 percent at IA19, against 13.4 to 16.5 percent at MO and 10.4 to 13.4 percent at MS.

**(4) Verdict: unlikely for the US experiments; possible for the TimeMatch sub-experiment (inherited from paper 1).** In the US data, gaps are filled by interpolation between real observations, the same way in every domain, after a minimum-observation filter. The resulting values are in-distribution, and our failure mode cannot occur. Interpolation density may still differ by site-year, but the validity shares are high everywhere. The worst transfer pairs (MS→IA) involve the cleanest targets (IA 99.99 and 96.92 percent), and the paper documents a large label shift and a shift in the discriminative week (Fig. 4). These point away from missing data as the main driver. The earlier card rated this paper "possible". I rate the US part lower for these reasons. **Data check:** from the Landsat ARD QA bands, compute per site-year the fraction of the 28 steps that were interpolated, per pixel. Then stratify target error by that fraction within each transfer pair. If there is no trend within a pair, interpolation density is not a driver.

## 7. Pandžić, Pavlović, Marko and Kilibarda 2026, Geodetski vestnik 70(2):268-288

**(1) Claimed drop, protocol, availability.** Source is Slovenia (159,533 LPIS parcels) and target is Vojvodina, Serbia (2,399 field-surveyed parcels), both in the 2021 season, 1 April to 30 September, with nine crops. Sentinel-2 L2A and Sentinel-1 GRD are used alone and together. Models are RF and a Transformer, applied naively or fine-tuned (Sec. 2-3, Table 5 per briefer). Naive transfer F1 (S1 / S2 / S1+S2): RF 35.12 / 40.67 / 46.49 and Transformer 42.68 / 57.98 / 66.80. The Serbian in-region benchmarks are RF 76.34 / 79.17 / 84.35 and Transformer 76.10 / 83.04 / 76.51. Slovenian in-region scores are low (RF 39.22 / 60.90 / 62.88, Transformer 48.47 / 68.92 / 72.23). Code is at github.com/milospandzic/TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping (the printed URL without the hyphen returns 404; read at commit 62378f8). No data are released with the code.

**(2) Missing and cloudy handling.** Cloud and shadow masking uses Cloud Score+, followed by parcel medians per acquisition (Sec. 2.4). Cubic-spline interpolation then maps the series onto a regular grid, with forward/backward padding at the ends instead of extrapolation (Sec. 3.1, Eq. 2). In the committed script `03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py`, a natural `CubicSpline(..., extrapolate=False)` runs per parcel (line 18) and then `groupby('ID').ffill().groupby('ID').bfill()` (line 34). The grid in the committed code is `freq=DateOffset(months=1)` from 2021-04-15 to 2021-09-30 (line 21), which contradicts the "15-day" comment and paper text. The script is hard-coded to the Serbian S1 file (lines 7-9). It applies no minimum-observation filter. Processing is identical across domains, and no mask is given. I found no input scaling in the `.py` files. The GEE notebook was not read, so the Cloud Score+ threshold is not verified.

**(3) Does missingness plausibly differ?** Possibly. Table 1 reports 617 S2 images for Slovenia against 510 for Serbia, but gives no per-parcel valid-observation counts or interpolated fractions. Slovenia's relief and Alpine-influenced climate make more cloud gaps plausible there than on the Pannonian plain.

**(4) Verdict: unlikely.** The fill (spline plus edge padding) is consistent and built from real observations, and the paper's own numbers argue against missing data as a main driver. Sentinel-1, which clouds do not affect, shows the largest naive RF drop (35.12 against 76.34). The Transformer's naive S1+S2 transfer to Serbia (66.80) is close to its in-region Slovenian score (72.23). So most of the "drop" is relative to a Serbian model trained on Serbian labels, not a collapse below source performance. Parcel size (0.78 against 23.25 ha), survey bias toward large homogeneous Serbian parcels, and KS-confirmed covariate shift are documented alternatives. The earlier card rated this paper "plausible". The S1 evidence is the reason for my lower rating. **Data check:** per parcel and region, count the Cloud Score+-valid S2 observations before interpolation and the share of padded grid steps. Then compare naive-transfer S2 accuracy for Serbian parcels with dense against sparse valid observations. The S1-only drop is a ready-made control that missing optical data cannot explain.
