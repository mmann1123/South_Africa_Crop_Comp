# Methodological pitfalls in deep-vs-classical benchmarking (remote sensing, SITS, TSC), 2019–2026

Scope note. These notes cover (a) the benchmark-artifact literature that reversed or undermined a "deep beats classical" or "classical beats deep" ranking, (b) guidance on missing-data handling for deep EO models, and (c) precedents for corrected or revised model-ranking claims. Research date: 2026-10-21. Items marked [paywalled] are behind a publisher wall; the DOI is given. Items marked [not independently verified] rest on my background knowledge of the paper plus a stable DOI, because the search run did not return the paper's full text.

Bottom line for our case. I found no published EO or SITS paper that reports exactly our artifact (a nodata/cloud gap present only in the inference region, zero-filled by one model family's pipeline and median-filled by another's, flipping the model ranking). The closest documented analogues are: (i) the Sentinel-2 Processing Baseline 04.00 radiometric offset, which is explicitly described as a train/inference distribution shift that makes models "yield incorrect results"; (ii) Kapoor and Narayanan's civil-war case study, where fixing imputation-related leakage erased the claimed advantage of complex ML over logistic regression (AUC gap 0.14 to 0.01); (iii) Shwartz-Ziv and Armon, where deep tabular models' advantage disappeared outside their own papers' datasets; and (iv) the bake-off literature, where re-running authors' code under a uniform protocol demoted deep models (LSTM-FCN "heavily overfit the test data" and fell below ResNet). The "missingness shift" literature in ML (Zhou, Balakrishnan, Lipton 2023) gives the formal name for our failure mode. The novelty of our note appears real: we can position it as the first SITS/crop-type documentation of a nodata-fill inconsistency reversing a deep-vs-sparse ranking.

## Key question 1: Has anyone explicitly reported that zero-encoding nodata/cloud gaps at inference silently degraded a deep model in EO?

### Takeaway
No paper I found reports the specific mechanism (zero-fill of a gap absent from training data silently degrading a deep model and flipping a ranking). The closest explicit statements are about the Sentinel-2 PB 04.00 offset shift and about provider-dependent preprocessing creating train/deploy distribution shift; SITS papers mostly report the opposite direction (attention/recurrent models tolerate raw, cloud-contaminated or gappy inputs when trained on them), which is consistent with our finding that the problem is train/test inconsistency rather than gaps per se.

### Cited Findings
- Microsoft Planetary Computer's Sentinel-2 baseline-change notebook states that a model trained on the old baseline and applied to unadjusted newer imagery "will yield incorrect results," and that training on a mix of old and unadjusted new data "will likely result in much worse performance." — [Planetary Computer baseline-change notebook](https://nbviewer.org/github/microsoft/PlanetaryComputerExamples/blob/main/datasets/sentinel-2-l2a/baseline-change.ipynb)
- Since PB 04.00 (January 2022) a radiometric offset of −1000 DN is added to all Sentinel-2 bands; the offset must be read from the PROCESSING_BASELINE metadata field, not inferred from acquisition date, because ESA's Collection-1 reprocessing (2024) put the offset convention on the whole archive; some platforms (GEE harmonized collections) apply it upstream so it "must not be applied a second time." — [Sialelli et al. 2026, "From Machine Learning to Large-Scale EO Products: Best Practices for Making Maps", Appendix C, arXiv:2607.24532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24532)
- Same paper: "processing pipelines may differ between platforms ... This can lead to distribution shifts when a model trained on data from one provider is deployed on data from another." and "Each correction applied during training must be replicated identically at inference time." — [Sialelli et al. 2026, §2 and §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24532)
- ESA STEP forum thread documents users asking whether negative reflectance after the PB 04.00 offset should be clamped to 0 (NODATA), showing the zero/nodata confusion is live in practice. — [ESA STEP forum: "Clamp negative reflectance to 0 (NODATA) for Baseline 04.00"](https://forum.step.esa.int/t/clamp-negative-reflectance-to-0-nodata-for-baseline-04-00-with-new-offset-correction/37518)
- Rußwurm and Körner (2020) trained self-attention, RNN and CNN models on raw (cloud-contaminated, non-interpolated) versus preprocessed Sentinel-2 time series and found self-attention and recurrent networks "outperformed convolutional neural networks on raw satellite time series"; attention scores "focus selectively on few classification-relevant observations." The key point for us: robustness to clouds was obtained by training on the contaminated distribution, not by post-hoc filling. — [Rußwurm & Körner 2020, ISPRS JPRS 169:421–435, doi:10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2020.06.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2020.06.006) [paywalled; preprint arXiv:1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)
- Turkoglu, D'Aronco, Perich, Liebisch, Streit, Schindler, Wegner (2021) note "RNNs normally exhibit good robustness against missing data due to clouds" and that adding the cloud mask as an extra input channel left performance "practically the same" (again, missingness pattern matched between train and test). — [arXiv:2102.08820](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.08820)
- Che et al. (2024) compared gappy 16-day HLS composites (15.35% of periods missing on average) with linearly interpolated ones for Bi-LSTM and Transformer land-cover classifiers and found <0.2% (Bi-LSTM) and <0.5% (Transformer) OA difference, "both smaller than model training randomness"; the models were trained and tested on the same missingness regime. — [Che et al. 2024, ISPRS JPRS 212:73–95](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271624001558) [paywalled; exact DOI not retrieved, see Gaps]
- Presto (Tseng et al. 2023) was explicitly designed so that it performs "well even with incomplete inputs (i.e., when there are missing timesteps, channels, or both)", using structured masking (random, channel-group, contiguous timesteps, whole timesteps) during pretraining. — [Tseng et al., "Lightweight, Pre-trained Transformers for Remote Sensing Timeseries", arXiv:2304.14065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14065)
- Sialelli et al. (2026) recommend simulating corruptions during training: "Temporal augmentations, such as dropping timesteps, can simulate cloud-induced data gaps or missing modalities", and "Simulating narrow no-data seams in Sentinel-1 GRD mosaics ... can build resilience to specific sensor artifacts." — [Sialelli et al. 2026, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24532)
- Rolf, Klemmer, Robinson, Kerner (ICML 2024 position paper) state "Distribution shifts are inevitable and pervasive in SatML contexts", "Covariate shifts occur across time, space, spectrum, and scale", and "Previous work suggests that ML models may generalize worse for satellite data than natural images." The paper does not discuss nodata or cloud-gap handling. — [Rolf et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.01444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01444)
- Rolf (2023), "Evaluation Challenges for Geospatial ML": "Unique structures in remotely sensed and geospatial data complicate or even invalidate use of traditional ML evaluation procedures." — [arXiv:2303.18087](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.18087)

### Inferences
- The EO literature has documented the general class (preprocessing differences between training and deployment sources cause silent distribution shift) through the PB 04.00 offset, but not the specific nodata-fill instance for temporal models. Our case is a temporal analogue of the offset problem: a constant (zero) injected at inference that the training distribution never contained.
- The SITS robustness papers (Rußwurm & Körner; Turkoglu et al.; Che et al.) all establish robustness under a *matched* missingness regime. None tests the mismatched regime (gap pattern present at test only). This is precisely the gap our note fills, and it reconciles their "deep models are robust to gaps" with our "deep models collapsed on a gap": both are true, conditioned on whether the fill convention was seen in training.
- The formal ML term for our artifact is "missingness shift" (see KQ2); EO papers have not adopted this vocabulary.

### Gaps
- I could not locate any EO/SITS paper, blog post (Rußwurm, Tseng, Kerner) or TorchGeo documentation page that explicitly warns "nodata encoded as zero at inference silently degrades a trained deep model". Searches on TorchGeo nodata handling returned only generic library pages; TorchGeo's own docs should be checked directly for a nodata/`fill_value` discussion before claiming no guidance exists.
- The exact DOI for Che et al. 2024 was not retrieved; the ScienceDirect link above is inferred from the volume/pages and should be verified.
- "Shaw et al., Pitfalls in remote sensing ML" (named in the brief) did not surface under that author/title; the nearest matches are Rolf 2023 (Evaluation Challenges for Geospatial ML) and an Environmental Science & Technology review of 148 highly cited environmental ML papers on pitfalls (sample size, leakage, splitting, evaluation) — [Princeton listing](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/machine-learning-in-environmental-research-common-pitfalls-and-be/). I could not confirm the Shaw reference exists.
- Kattenborn et al. (CNN vegetation review) was not returned by searches and I did not verify any nodata guidance in it.

## Key question 2: What do methodological reviews recommend for equal missing-data treatment across model families?

### Takeaway
Explicit cross-model-family guidance is thin. The clearest rules are (1) Kapoor and Narayanan's leakage taxonomy item L1.2 (pre-processing such as imputation must be fit on training data only and applied identically to test), (2) Sialelli et al.'s "each correction applied during training must be replicated identically at inference time", and (3) the bake-off principle of regenerating all results under one common protocol rather than quoting published numbers. The theoretical framing is Zhou et al.'s "domain adaptation under missingness shift", which shows that when missingness indicators are observed the problem reduces to covariate shift (an argument for mask channels), and that without indicators adaptation is required.

### Cited Findings
- Kapoor & Narayanan taxonomy [L1.2] "Pre-processing on training and test set": "Using the entire dataset for any pre-processing steps, such as imputation or over/under sampling, results in leakage." [L1] requires that the test set "must not interact with training data in any preprocessing, modeling, or evaluation step." — [Kapoor & Narayanan 2023, Patterns 4(9), PMC10499856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856); [project site](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/)
- Kapoor & Narayanan propose "model info sheets" with 21 questions "that elicit precise arguments needed to justify the absence of leakage", structured around clean train-test separation, feature legitimacy, and test-set representativeness. — [same](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856)
- Zhou, Balakrishnan & Lipton (AISTATS 2023) define domain adaptation under missingness shift (DAMS), "where source and target data differ only in their missing-data mechanisms"; when missingness indicators are observed the problem "reduces to covariate shift"; without indicators (underreporting completely at random) covariate shift no longer holds and adaptation is needed. — [PMLR v206 pp. 9577–9606](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/zhou23b.html); [arXiv:2211.02093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02093)
- Follow-ups extend this to MNAR missingness shift reframed as an imputation problem ([arXiv:2504.00322](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.00322v1)) and to "Robust prediction under missingness shifts" ([arXiv:2406.16484](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16484v1)).
- Sialelli et al. 2026: "Data selection and preprocessing choices are not neutral; they encode assumptions that propagate into the training pipeline"; gaps and artifacts "can either be handled explicitly before the data reaches the model, or left for the model to learn to handle through exposure to natural variation and targeted data augmentation"; and "whatever pipeline is adopted for training must also be reproduced at inference time over the full mapped domain." — [arXiv:2607.24532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24532)
- Sialelli et al. distinguish "model validation (estimating predictive performance on data drawn from the same collection as the training set) from map validation (assessing the final product against independently collected reference data with a known sampling design)". — [same, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24532)
- Bake off redux (Middlehurst, Schäfer, Bagnall 2024): "Performance on these datasets ... has been used to support every proposed approach, so this allows us to make a fair comparison of algorithms. We have regenerated all results for classifiers described both in the original bake off and this comparison." Train/test pairs are resampled 30 times with seeded resampling "to aid with reproducibility". — [Middlehurst et al., Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery, doi:10.1007/s10618-024-01022-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-024-01022-1); [arXiv:2304.13029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)
- Bake off redux on a distance-based method (GRAIL): "we ... have made some modifications to prevent the test set from being visible during the initial clustering ... This would introduce bias, and may explain the discrepancy with published results." — [same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)
- Fawaz et al. (2019) "implemented each of the compared approaches" in one open-source framework and trained 8,730 models on 97 datasets; the abstract itself does not use the word "fair", so attribute the common-protocol principle rather than a quotation. — [Fawaz et al. 2019, DMKD 33:917–963, doi:10.1007/s10618-019-00619-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-019-00619-1); [arXiv:1809.04356](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04356)
- McElfresh et al. (NeurIPS 2023), 176 datasets: "light hyperparameter tuning on a GBDT is more important than choosing between NNs and GBDTs for many datasets." — [NeurIPS 2023 poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/73658)
- TabArena (2025): with tuning and ensembling "the best deep learning methods are equal to or better than gradient-boosted decision trees", and TabM, LightGBM and RealMLP "would rank significantly lower without this strategy"; some DL models are "overrepresented in cross-model ensembles due to validation set overfitting." — [arXiv:2506.16791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16791)
- Rolf et al. 2024: "assigning data instances to training and evaluation splits uniformly at random will likely overestimate model performance"; "SatML benchmarks must be designed to catalyze impact." — [arXiv:2402.01444](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01444v1)
- Spatial-leakage guidance (background-knowledge items, DOIs stable): Karasiak, Dejoux, Monteil, Sheeren (2022) "Spatial dependence between training and test sets: another pitfall of classification accuracy assessment in remote sensing", Machine Learning 111:2715–2740 — [doi:10.1007/s10994-021-05972-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-021-05972-1) [not independently verified; HAL preprint](https://hal.inrae.fr/hal-03238347); Ploton et al. (2020) "Spatial validation reveals poor predictive performance of large-scale ecological mapping models", Nat Commun 11:4540 — [doi:10.1038/s41467-020-18321-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18321-y) [not independently verified]; Meyer & Pebesma (2022) "Machine learning-based global maps of ecological variables and the challenge of assessing them", Nat Commun 13:2208 — [doi:10.1038/s41467-022-29838-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29838-9) [not independently verified]. The HAL preprint of Karasiak confirms the thesis that "spatial dependence between training and test sets can lead to overestimation of generalisation capabilities if ignored."

### Inferences
- A defensible "equal treatment" protocol for our paper, assembled from these sources: (1) one shared imputation/fill operator fitted on the training tiles and applied unchanged to every model's inputs; (2) a missingness-indicator (mask) channel, which by Zhou et al. turns missingness shift into ordinary covariate shift; (3) a train-time augmentation that drops timesteps/months so the inference-time gap is inside the training support (Sialelli et al.; Presto); (4) a leakage/model-info sheet documenting which fill was used by which model; (5) regenerate every baseline under the shared pipeline rather than citing numbers from the baselines' original papers.
- Zero-fill is a special case of "nodata encoded as an in-range value", which is exactly what the PB 04.00 offset notebooks warn about; we can cite the offset literature to argue the pitfall is recognized in EO even though the temporal-gap instance is not.

### Gaps
- No remote-sensing review found that enumerates "use the same imputation for all baselines" as an explicit checklist item. The rule exists only implicitly (Kapoor L1.2, Sialelli "replicate identically").
- Grinsztajn et al. 2022 ("Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular data?") is relevant to the sparse-vs-dense framing but was not retrieved in this run; cite from memory only with caution ([arXiv:2207.08815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815), [not independently verified]).

## Key question 3: Precedents for a paper revising its central claim because of a data-handling artifact, and how it was framed (covers part (c))

### Takeaway
I found no remote-sensing journal corrigendum in which a model ranking reversed after a preprocessing fix. The published precedents are either (i) outside-party re-analyses that reverse a claim (Kapoor & Narayanan civil-war case; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon; bake-off re-evaluations), or (ii) author corrections in RS journals framed as "results re-run, numbers changed, conclusions unchanged." The standard framing in the re-analysis papers is neutral and mechanistic: name the leakage/preprocessing step, re-run under the corrected protocol, report the before/after gap.

### Cited Findings
- Kapoor & Narayanan: of 12 civil-war prediction papers with code, data and a train/test split, "errors appeared in 4 of the 12, exactly the 4 that claimed complex ML models beat baseline logistic regression." Muchlinski et al. "impute the training and test data together"; Colaresi and Mahmood, and Wang, "incorrectly reuse an imputed dataset". After correction, "complex models perform no better than LR". — [PMC10499856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856)
- Same paper reports leakage found in 17 fields affecting 294 papers (Patterns version); the living website now lists 41 papers across 30 fields affecting 648 papers (table updated May 2024). Cite the version you use. — [Patterns](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856); [website](https://reproducible.cs.princeton.edu/)
- Shwartz-Ziv & Armon (2022): across 11 datasets, "XGBoost outperformed the deep models" and each deep model (TabNet, NODE, DNF-Net, 1D-CNN) did best mainly on its own paper's datasets; "No deep model consistently outperformed the others." They rule out implementation error because "results on the original datasets matched the original papers" and attribute the gap to "selection bias" and because "Each paper may have set the model's hyperparameters based on a more extensive hyperparameter search." — [Information Fusion 81:84–90, 2022; arXiv:2106.03253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03253) [journal version paywalled, doi:10.1016/j.inffus.2021.11.011]
- Bake off redux reviewer response on LSTM-FCN: "the authors employ a process of selecting the optimal model based on test data and manually adjusting hyper-parameters ... Upon re-evaluation ... the LSTM-FCN study heavily overfit the test data, and its actual rank on accuracy is worse than ResNet. Consequently, we have opted to exclude this paper." — [arXiv:2304.13029 (reviewer-response appendix in the arXiv PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)
- Bake off redux on another suggested method: "there seems to be leakage between train and test and we were not able to reproduce their results. We know of another research group who did the same." — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)
- Bake off redux conclusion on deep learning: "there has not been an Alexnet for TSC"; "H-InceptionTime performs well overall, but ... it often performs terribly, and this makes its overall performance worse", and it "improves relative to other algorithms as the number of training cases increases." — [same](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)
- RS-journal corrections that re-ran experiments after a data-preparation error: an MDPI Remote Sensing correction for an FY-4A cloud-classification CNN paper states "Part of the data for 2019 was incorrectly included in this dataset during training-set preparation"; the authors "re-executed the experiment". — [MDPI Remote Sensing 15(18):4368](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/18/4368)
- Chapiro et al. AR-DAVID errata (30 Jan 2026): "inconsistent labels were found in a small portion of the datasets after publication"; new results "did not change the observations or conclusions, but the reported numbers are slightly different." — [errata text](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/chapiro2024_AR-DAVID_errata.txt)
- Frontiers corrigendum (pineapple inflorescence counting) swapped validation/test loss labels and states this "does not change the scientific conclusions of the article in any way." — [PMC8166251](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8166251/)
- Retractions in RS crop-mapping found were for publication-process manipulation, not data handling (e.g., J. Electronic Imaging retraction after "systematic manipulation of the publication process, including compromised peer review"). — [SPIE JEI 31(5):051407](https://nanophotonics.spiedigitallibrary.org/journals/journal-of-electronic-imaging/volume-31/issue-5/051407/RETRACTED--Application-of-remote-sensing-image-ecological-land-classification/10.1117/1.JEI.31.5.051407.short)
- Leakage surveys inside RS: patch-based hyperspectral classification "suffers from train–test pixel overlap, causing data leakage and overly optimistic performance" — [MDPI Remote Sensing 18:979 (2026)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-00979/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-00979.pdf); "Information Leakage in Deep Learning-Based Hyperspectral Image Classification: A Survey" — [MDPI Remote Sensing 15(15):3793](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/15/3793).

### Inferences
- Precedent for framing: the re-analysis papers (Kapoor & Narayanan; Shwartz-Ziv & Armon; bake off redux) all use the same three-part structure: identify the inconsistent step, re-run under a uniform protocol, report ranking before/after with the mechanism named. For an in-revision correction of our own claim, mirroring this structure (and explicitly stating that the original conclusion was an artifact of pipeline asymmetry, with the before/after table) matches the strongest existing norm. Kapoor & Narayanan's model-info-sheet could be adapted as a supplement table listing, per model, the fill operator and whether the inference missingness pattern was in the training support.
- There is a vocabulary gap: RS corrections use "re-executed the experiment" language and nearly always claim conclusions unchanged; a correction that reverses the headline claim would be unusual in RS journals, which strengthens the case for publishing the reversal as a result rather than as an erratum.

### Gaps
- No RS-journal corrigendum, retraction, or expression of concern was found where a deep-vs-classical ranking reversed because of imputation/nodata handling. Retraction Watch and Crossref were not queried directly; a DOI-level search there might still surface one.
- The CEA/RSE/ISPRS/TGRS publisher sites were not searched one by one for "corrigendum" + "normalization"/"imputation".

## Key question 4: Typical magnitude of artifact-induced ranking changes

### Takeaway
Reported magnitudes range from sub-percent (when the missingness regime is matched) to the complete erasure of a claimed advantage (civil-war AUC gap 0.14 to 0.01; deep tabular models losing 7–14% relative performance on unseen datasets versus 3.3% for XGBoost). Our observed reversal (dense models from last to first after consistent fill) is at the large end but has analogues in the leakage literature.

### Cited Findings
- Civil-war case: for Wang (2019) the AUC gap between complex ML and logistic regression "drops from 0.14 to 0.01" after fixing imputation leakage; Blair & Sambanis' smoothed AUC 0.85 had a 95% CI of 0.66–0.95 with only 11 onset cases in the test set and was not significantly better than baselines (p = 0.14–0.34). — [PMC10499856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856)
- Shwartz-Ziv & Armon Table 3, average relative performance deterioration on unseen datasets: XGBoost 3.34%, NODE 14.21%, DNF-Net 11.96%, TabNet 10.51%, 1D-CNN 7.56%, deep ensemble without XGBoost 6.91%, deep ensemble with XGBoost 2.32%. — [arXiv:2106.03253](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.03253)
- Che et al. 2024: interpolation vs no interpolation changed OA by <0.2% (Bi-LSTM) and <0.5% (Transformer), smaller than seed variance, when train and test share the gap regime. — [ISPRS JPRS 212:73–95](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271624001558)
- Rolf et al. 2024 cite a drop of up to 30% in building-damage detection accuracy on a new disaster site as an example of SatML distribution shift, and note field-delineation models "transfer poorly across countries due to differences in farming practices." — [arXiv:2402.01444](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01444v1)
- Bake off redux: LSTM-FCN moved from a claimed top-tier rank to below ResNet after re-evaluation without test-set model selection (Figure C7 of the arXiv PDF). — [arXiv:2304.13029](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)
- TabArena: TabM, LightGBM and RealMLP "would rank significantly lower" without tuning + ensembling; the ranking of deep vs GBDT flips with protocol. — [arXiv:2506.16791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16791)
- Original bake off (Bagnall et al. 2017): only 9 of 18 algorithms were significantly better than 1-NN DTW and Rotation Forest on 85 UCR datasets, a benchmark-wide deflation of many published claims. — [Bake off redux summary of Bagnall 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)

### Inferences
- The matched-regime results (Che et al.) and the mismatched-regime leakage results (Kapoor & Narayanan) bracket the effect: essentially zero when the missingness pattern is in the training support, and large enough to reverse the headline when it is not. Our reversal is consistent with this bracket and can be presented as a controlled demonstration of the mismatched regime in SITS.
- No study reports a magnitude specifically for zero-fill vs median-fill in EO; our before/after table would be the first such number.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of nodata-fill-value choice (0 vs mean vs median vs mask token) on downstream SITS classification under train/test missingness mismatch was found.
- The 30% building-damage figure is cited second-hand by Rolf et al.; the primary paper was not retrieved.

## Part (a) catalogue: benchmark-artifact literature that reversed or undermined a deep-vs-classical conclusion

### Takeaway
Across TSC, tabular ML, political science, and EO, the recurring artifact classes are: test-set tuning or leakage into model selection; preprocessing (imputation, normalization, offset) fit or applied inconsistently; baselines not re-tuned under the same budget; random rather than spatial/temporal splits; and dataset selection bias toward the proposer's method. Each has reversed at least one published deep-vs-classical ranking.

### Cited Findings (one line each; see KQ sections for details)
- Bagnall et al. 2017, "The great time series classification bake off" (DMKD 31:606–660): 18 algorithms, 85 datasets, 100 resamples; only 9 beat the DTW/RotF benchmarks. [doi:10.1007/s10618-016-0483-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-016-0483-9) [not independently verified; summarized in the redux](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)
- Fawaz et al. 2019, "Deep learning for time series classification: a review" (DMKD 33:917–963): 8,730 models, 97 datasets, single open framework; RNN/LSTM "found ... to perform poorly" (as characterized by Middlehurst et al.). [doi:10.1007/s10618-019-00619-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-019-00619-1); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04356)
- Middlehurst, Schäfer, Bagnall 2024, "Bake off redux" (DMKD): regenerates all results; excludes LSTM-FCN for test-set overfitting; "no Alexnet for TSC". [doi:10.1007/s10618-024-01022-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-024-01022-1); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13029)
- Shwartz-Ziv & Armon 2022, "Tabular data: deep learning is not all you need" (Information Fusion 81:84–90): deep models win only on own-paper datasets. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03253)
- McElfresh et al. 2023, "When do neural nets outperform boosted trees on tabular data?" (NeurIPS): GBDT light tuning matters more than model family. [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/poster/73658)
- TabArena 2025: rankings flip with tuning/ensembling protocol; validation-set overfitting by DL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16791)
