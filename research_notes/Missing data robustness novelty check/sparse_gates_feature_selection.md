# Sparse attention, sparsemax/TabNet gates, and learned feature selection in SITS crop classification (2019–2026): what the sparsity is claimed to buy

Scope note. Searches ran on Google-style web search, arXiv/ar5iv, OpenAlex, and (rate-limited) Semantic Scholar; full texts were extracted for Tian et al. 2023, Shah et al. 2022, Christofi et al. 2025, Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022, Pankajakshan et al. 2025, Rußwurm & Körner 2020, Garnot et al. 2021, Ofori-Ampofo et al. 2021, Zhao et al. 2021, Zhou et al. 2023, Miranda et al. 2025, Grinsztajn et al. 2022, L-TAE, and Obadic et al. The three OpenAlex sweeps ("TabNet crop classification remote sensing", "sparsemax attention remote sensing satellite") returned no paper applying sparsemax or entmax attention to Sentinel time series; the only TabNet-in-RS-imagery papers found are the two MDPI items below. Items marked PAYWALLED/UNREAD were not read beyond abstract or metadata.

## Key Question 1: Which remote-sensing papers use TabNet or sparsemax/entmax attention (or learned channel/band gates) for crop or land-cover classification, and what do they claim the sparsity buys?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of remote-sensing papers use TabNet or sparsemax-family attention, and all of them evaluate in-region. They claim accuracy (Tian 2023, Shah 2022), interpretability (Shah 2022, Christofi 2025), and in one case "generalization" or "anti-overfitting" by analogy to random forests (Tian 2023), but none tests cross-region transfer, low-data regimes, or corrupted inputs against a dense control. No paper was found that puts sparsemax or entmax attention inside a SITS temporal encoder (L-TAE, TempCNN, Transformer).

### Cited Findings
- Tian et al. 2023 (Remote Sens. 15(8):1990, DOI 10.3390/rs15081990) use a TabNet branch on the 12 Sentinel-2 bands of the patch's centre pixel, single-date imagery, Henan peanut/rice/corn. Ablation (Table 6, in-region): baseline mF1 0.7770 → +selective patch module 0.9010 → +TabNet branch 0.9446 → +multitask 0.9520 → +superpixel post-processing 0.9652. Their rationale: TabNet "simulate[s] the RF operation process", RF-like methods "are more dependable when training small samples and have a better anti-overfitting ability", and "the unique structure of TabNet can enhance the generalization ability of the network". No transfer or corruption experiment is reported. — [Tian et al. 2023 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-15-01990/article_deploy/remotesensing-15-01990.pdf)
- Shah, Du & Xu 2022 "Enhanced TabNet" (Remote Sens. 14(3):716, DOI 10.3390/rs14030716) apply TabNet to hyperspectral pixels (Indian Pines, Pavia, Salinas), adding a 2D CNN inside the attentive transformer for spatial soft feature selection and a structure-profile preprocessing. The attentive transformer uses entmax: "Entmax normalization inherits the desirable sparsity of sparsemax and can provide smoother, and differentiable curvature", with the TabNet prior P[i-1]=∏(γ−M[i]) where "a feature is used at one decision step when γ=1 and features can be used in multiple decision steps when γ increases". Abstract: OA improves "from 11.29% to 12.61%, 3.6% to 7.67%, and 5.97% to 8.01% in comparison to other classification techniques, at the cost of increases in computational complexity by factors of 1.96 to 2.52, 2.03 to 3.45, and 2.67 to 5.52". Indian Pines (10% training per class) OA for the best proposed variant 96.36 vs 73.79–85.07 for the comparison methods. Claimed benefits: "interpretability and efficient learning". The paper's "less noise" statements refer to salt-and-pepper noise in output maps, not input corruption. No transfer test. — [Shah et al. 2022 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-00716/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-00716.pdf)
- Christofi et al. 2025 (Proc. SPIE 13816, RSCy2025, DOI 10.1117/12.3073215) embed a "dynamic feature selection mechanism" that learns to weight Sentinel-2 band×date inputs for grassland/non-grassland classification in Cyprus. With vs without the mechanism: accuracy 0.954±0.004 vs 0.953±0.003 (MCC 0.726±0.027 with). Their own reading: "while the feature selection mechanism does not significantly boost accuracy", its value is identifying the informative bands and dates (best single date 24/08/2017, accuracy 0.916±0.003). Dense weighting (not sparsemax); single region. — [Christofi et al. 2025 PDF](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/36330/1/1381610.pdf)
- Garnot & Landrieu 2020, L-TAE (arXiv 2007.00586): channels of the temporal input are split into H groups of E′=E/H, one per head; on S2-Agri PSE+L-TAE reaches OA 94.3±0.2 / mIoU 51.7±0.4 vs PSE+TAE 94.2/50.9, CNN+TempCNN 93.3/47.5, Transformer 92.2/42.8, RF 91.6/32.5 (all ≈150k params). Head ablation mIoU: H=2 51.6, 4 51.0, 8 51.2, 16 51.7, 32 51.2. Attention is softmax; the 16-head model's masks are shown qualitatively as "highly specialized" per head and crop. No sparsity, transfer, or robustness claims. — [L-TAE on ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.00586)
- Rußwurm & Körner 2020 (ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 169:421–435, DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2020.06.006; arXiv 1910.10536): softmax self-attention and LSTM on raw (non-cloud-masked) Sentinel-2 series, Bavaria. Table 1 (23 classes, preprocessed/raw accuracy): RF 0.83/0.71, LSTM 0.85/0.81, Transformer 0.85/0.80, TempCNN 0.86/0.79, MS-ResNet 0.83/0.79. Gradient analysis: "Only time-points where the surface was not obscured by clouds had non-zero gradients... the LSTM-RNN and Transformer models have been optimized to automatically identify and suppress time instances that are not classification relevant, e.g., through cloud coverage"; convolutional models "seem to have some non-zero gradients on cloudy observations... especially true for the comparatively shallow TempCNN". Conclusion: attention/recurrent models are "more robust in dealing with noise in the data and... could better suppress cloudy observations in raw time series". — [arXiv 1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)
- Jin et al. 2024, Sparse Focus Network (IGARSS 2024, arXiv 2406.01245): "sparse attention is employed in Transformer block for HSI and SAR/LiDAR feature extraction, thereby the most useful self-attention values are maintained"; motivation is that dense methods are "susceptible to irrelevant information interference". Berlin and Houston2018 datasets; no numbers or dense-vs-sparse ablation visible in the abstract. PAYWALLED/UNREAD beyond abstract. — [arXiv 2406.01245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01245)
- Wu, Zuo & Li 2026 (Smart Agriculture 8(2):118–132, DOI 10.12133/j.smartag.SA202510010): channel, temporal and spatial attention fusing Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 for crop classification; OA 97.5% on PASTIS, 93% on Yishui; under heavy cloud OA drops 3.6 pp, described as the smallest degradation among compared models; "removing any attention component resulted in a clear reduction" but no per-component numbers on the page. Dense (softmax) attention. UNREAD beyond landing page. — [sciopen page](https://www.sciopen.com/article/10.12133/j.smartag.SA202510010)
- Ribalta Lorenzo et al. 2020 (arXiv 1811.02667, IEEE Access draft): attention-based CNN band selection for hyperspectral images, "gating mechanisms to find the most informative parts of the spectrum", yielding "refined and extremely compact sets" of bands; claims are compactness and interpretability, no noise or transfer claims. — [arXiv 1811.02667](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02667)
- Zimmer, Lindenbaum & Glickman 2025 (ECAI 2025, arXiv 2401.11420): two supervised embedded band-selection methods (structured L0 / Gumbel-softmax selector per a search summary) reach "state-of-the-art performance while selecting only a minimal number of bands" on three RS benchmarks; no few-sample or noise claims in the abstract. — [arXiv 2401.11420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11420)
- OpenAlex sweep for TabNet + crop: the only crop-type TabNet item besides Tian 2023 is "Architecture diverse TabNet ensemble for crop type classification using harmonized multisource soil data" (Sci. Rep. 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-75366-7), which uses soil tabular data, not imagery. PAYWALLED/UNREAD. Other TabNet-agriculture hits are yield prediction (Cogent Food & Agric. 2025, DOI 10.1080/23311932.2025.2529369) and disease grading (Agronomy 2025, DOI 10.3390/agronomy15071530). — [OpenAlex query](https://api.openalex.org/works?search=TabNet%20crop%20classification%20remote%20sensing&per-page=15)
- A 2026 Remote Sensing paper applies TabPFN (attention-based in-context tabular model) with recursive feature elimination to Sentinel-2 time-series features in Yuxi, China: OA 96.27%, kappa 0.9558, macro-F1 0.956 in main validation, in-region. — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-18-01857/article_deploy/remotesensing-18-01857.pdf)

### Inferences
- The RS literature treats TabNet as a "neural random forest" for small samples and spectral tabular inputs; the inferred benefit is in-region accuracy and interpretability. Nobody has yet reported TabNet or a sparsemax gate evaluated on a spatially disjoint holdout, so our finding that TabNet is a strong-but-not-top transferer once inputs are fair has no published comparator.
- Rußwurm & Körner's gradient result is the closest existing RS statement that attention-like selection buys robustness to corrupted observations (clouds in raw series), but it is for dense softmax attention and recurrence, not sparse gates, and it is qualitative.
- Christofi et al. is a direct precedent for our clean-data result: a learned input gate gave +0.001 accuracy over the dense baseline.

### Gaps
- No paper found that uses sparsemax or entmax attention in a SITS crop model. Our L-TAE-S appears to be the first.
- Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 429 on every attempt, so its index was not swept; OpenAlex and web search were used instead.
- Shah et al. 2022 comparison-column labels for the OA table were not extracted; only the abstract's relative-gain ranges are reported here.

## Key Question 2: Does any paper show sparse gates/attention or feature selection improve cross-region transfer against a controlled dense baseline? Any showing no effect?

### Takeaway
No RS paper tests a sparse-attention gate against a dense/softmax control under spatial transfer. The nearest controlled evidence comes from classical feature selection with spatial cross-validation (Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022), whose authors conclude it had "no substantial effect on the spatial transferability" while keeping accuracy with 8% of the features, and from Pankajakshan et al. 2025, where a low-capacity PCA+SVM pipeline transfers and a 3D-CNN does not, but without any gating ablation. The frequently cited Obadic et al. result is about dependence on the training class set, not on region.

### Cited Findings
- Orynbaikyzy, Gessner & Conrad 2022 (Remote Sens. 14(6):1493, DOI 10.3390/rs14061493): RF crop-type models across seven German sites, Sentinel-1/2, 11 crops, spatially independent test sets. Max F1 0.85 in training sites, 0.79 in transfer regions; SAR-only had the lowest transfer loss (average F1loss −0.04). Spatial feature selection (forward selection inside spatial CV) kept "only eight percent from all single optical-SAR features" with "marginally improved absolute accuracies... and reduced accuracy losses" in target systems, but "median F1-score differences between no feature selection and spatial feature selection approaches remained below 3% for all sensor groups" and "the difference... never exceeded 0.05 F1-score". Conclusion: "performing spatial feature selection had no substantial effect on the spatial transferability of Random Forest models for crop type mapping." — [Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-01493/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-01493.pdf)
- Mahmood et al. 2025 (PFG, DOI 10.1007/s41064-024-00329-4) examine spatial effects of backward feature elimination with RF in Punjab irrigation systems. PAYWALLED/UNREAD (Springer redirected to an authorization page); numbers not obtained. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41064-024-00329-4)
- Pankajakshan, Padmasanan & Sundar 2025 (Sensors 26(1):174, DOI 10.3390/s26010174): a 3D-CNN reaching 99.49% train / 97.24% validation accuracy on Indian Pines "was not transferable and the results were very poor" on PRISMA imagery over Piedmont, while a PCA (≥0.90 variance) + RBF-SVM + spatial-spectral kernel pipeline trained on 1–10% labels transferred to KSC, Botswana and Piedmont without retraining (Botswana AA/κ 97.4/98.3, KSC OA/κ 97.1/99.2). The transfer region has no field labels; validation is by proxy (Sentinel-1 rice mask, NDVI, WorldCover). No learned gate or sparse-vs-dense ablation; the mechanism is low capacity and few parameters, not attention sparsity. — [Pankajakshan et al. 2025 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sensors/sensors-26-00174/article_deploy/sensors-26-00174.pdf)
- Obadic, Roscher, Oliveira & Zhu (arXiv 2210.13167, v2 Apr 2025), BavarianCrops, 12 crops, Sentinel-2 2018, transformer F1 0.64: the sensitivity analysis varies only the training class set (removing grassland, winter barley or corn, retraining from scratch). Removing grassland moved winter-barley attention from early July to late April/May; F1 went 0.64→0.67 (no grassland), 0.59 (no winter barley), 0.62 (no corn). Removing the top-5 attended dates cut accuracy by about 5%; about 15 dates recover reference accuracy. Key sentences: "The identified phenological events are conditioned on the presence of other crop types within the dataset" and "the interpretation of the attention weights is conditional to the classes in the dataset." Region, year and geography were not varied. — [ar5iv 2210.13167](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.13167)
- Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux 2022 (NeurIPS 2022, arXiv 2207.08815), 45 tabular datasets: removing RF-ranked uninformative features "narrows the gap between MLP/ResNet and the other models (FT-Transformer and tree-based)"; adding Gaussian noise features widens it; a GBT's accuracy "is barely affected by removing up to half the features"; conclusion "MLPs are less robust to uninformative features". The attention-based FT-Transformer groups with the robust models. Rotating features reverses the ranking (NNs above trees), which the authors read as the original axis-aligned basis carrying information. — [ar5iv 2207.08815](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.08815)
- A 2024 Sentinel-2 attention U-Net study (A_2_SegNet, US Great Lakes, IJAEOG Nov 2024) reports cross-year and cross-space transfer and concludes "the attention mechanism helps knowledge transfer", but no isolated channel-attention ablation was visible in the record. UNREAD. — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/87d19d80ae074b0d98f165d99a040ddf)

### Inferences
- The two controlled "selection vs no selection" transfer comparisons in RS (Orynbaikyzy; Christofi for in-region) both find near-zero effect on accuracy, consistent with our TempCNN ±0 / L-TAE +0.00–0.01 / CNN-BiLSTM −0.02 result. Our null transfer result is therefore not anomalous; it is the modal published outcome where a dense control exists.
- Grinsztajn et al. supply the tabular analogue: robustness to uninformative features is what separates trees (and FT-Transformer) from MLPs, which is a feature-noise argument rather than a transfer argument.
- The manuscript currently cites Obadic et al. as evidence that attended dates are "contingent on the training region". The paper's own wording is contingent on the training classes; this citation should be reworded to avoid a reviewer catching the mismatch.

### Gaps
- No RS paper found that ablates sparsemax vs softmax (or hard gate vs none) under spatial transfer. The claim "first controlled sparse-vs-dense transfer test in SITS" appears defensible but could not be verified against IEEE Xplore full text (not accessible here).
- Mahmood et al. 2025 PFG numbers unavailable (paywall).

## Key Question 3: Does any paper link sparse feature selection or sparse attention to robustness against missing or corrupted inputs (RS or tabular), and has anyone framed sparse gating primarily as a robustness-to-corruption mechanism?

### Takeaway
Robustness to missing optical observations in SITS has been studied repeatedly, but always via multimodal fusion, temporal dropout augmentation, imputation-free training, or reconstruction, never via a sparse channel gate. In tabular ML, robustness to noisy or missing features is attributed to tree splits, contextual embeddings (TabTransformer) or in-context attention (TabPFN), not to TabNet's sparsemax masks; no study was found that benchmarks TabNet under missingness. No paper found frames sparsemax/entmax gating primarily as a corruption-robustness mechanism; the closest are NLP arguments that softmax disperses mass onto uninformative tokens (Vasylenko et al. 2026) and Rußwurm & Körner's gradient evidence that attention suppresses cloudy dates.

### Cited Findings (remote sensing)
- Sainte Fare Garnot, Landrieu & Chehata 2021 (arXiv 2112.07558, PASTIS-R): random Sentinel-2 acquisitions removed from the test set only. S2-only "drops sharply", falling below radar-only at 70% occlusion; multimodal fusion "stays nearly constant for up to 50% missing optical acquisitions"; early fusion least robust, decision fusion most (still above radar-only with 90% optical removed); auxiliary supervision and temporal dropout "make both unimodal and multimodal models more resilient". Panoptic with 30% optical: S2 33.0 PQ vs late fusion 37.6. Main numbers: parcel mIoU S2 73.9, late fusion + aux + temporal dropout 77.2; semantic mIoU S2 63.1 (63.6 with temporal dropout), late fusion 66.3. — [ar5iv 2112.07558](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2112.07558)
- Ofori-Ampofo, Pelletier & Lang 2021 (Remote Sens. 13(22):4668, DOI 10.3390/rs13224668), PSE-TAE, Brittany, Table 4 F-score with n% of Sentinel-2 dates: S2-only 0.443 (20%, 5 obs), 0.537 (40%), 0.576 (60%), 0.587 (80%), 0.587 (100%); TAE-level fusion 0.558, 0.575, 0.590, 0.605, 0.611; early fusion 0.478→0.612; late 0.540→0.591; S1-only 0.522. — [Ofori-Ampofo et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13224668)
- Zhao et al. 2021 (Remote Sens. 13(14):2790, DOI 10.3390/rs13142790), Hengshui, 43.5% of Sentinel-2 values missing, masked (unfilled) vs interpolated (filled) inputs, OA over five splits: 1D CNN 86.43 vs 86.25; LSTM 80.57 vs 82.18; GRU 81.53 vs 81.67; LSTM-CNN 86.57 vs 85.75; GRU-CNN 85.98 vs 85.61; filled-data SDs were larger, "mainly caused by the transfer error of interpolation and smoothing methods". — [Zhao et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13142790)
- Zhou et al. 2023 (Remote Sens. 15(20):5009, DOI 10.3390/rs15205009): contrastive learning maps incomplete Sentinel-2 parcel series to a complete representation, then XGBoost/LSTM; ">3% in overall accuracy and 0.04 in F1 scores over comparison methods" in Dijon and Zhaosu. — [Zhou et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15205009)
- Wang et al. 2025 (arXiv 2505.19159): joint feature-reconstruction and prediction for incomplete SITS with temporal masks and a teacher trained on complete series; a secondhand summary reports +6.93% cropland extraction and +7.09% crop classification over baselines. UNVERIFIED numbers. — [arXiv 2505.19159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19159)
- Miranda, Mena & Dengel 2025 (arXiv 2504.06915): Monte Carlo Temporal Dropout at inference for EO regression, "randomly dropping time-steps during inference", with a learned Concrete dropout ratio; improves accuracy and calibration on three EO datasets; notes that simulated missingness "could potentially differ from scenarios involving actual missing data". — [arXiv 2504.06915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06915)
- Rußwurm & Körner 2020: see KQ1; gradients through LSTM and Transformer vanish on cloudy dates while TempCNN and MS-ResNet keep non-zero gradients; authors conclude these models "could better suppress cloudy observations in raw time series". Dense softmax attention. — [arXiv 1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)
- TimeSenCLIP (Jain et al., ISPRS JPRS 236:99–119, 2026, arXiv 2508.11919): a search summary attributes an ablation showing "temporal dropout enhances robustness to missing observations"; the arXiv abstract itself does not mention this. UNVERIFIED. — [arXiv 2508.11919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11919)
- Jin et al. 2024 Sparse Focus Network: sparse attention motivated as a defence against "irrelevant information interference" in multi-source HSI/SAR/LiDAR fusion; this is the only RS paper found that frames sparse attention as noise-filtering, but there is no corruption experiment or dense ablation in the abstract. — [arXiv 2406.01245](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01245)

### Cited Findings (tabular / general ML)
- Arik & Pfister, TabNet (AAAI 2021, arXiv 1908.07442): synthetic Syn1–Syn6 datasets (Chen et al. 2018) in which only a subset of features is salient; TabNet "outperforms others (Tree Ensembles, LASSO regularization, L2X) and is on par with INVASE", and for Syn4–Syn6 "eliminating instance-wise redundant features" improves selection. - Huang et al., TabTransformer (arXiv 2012.06678): noise injected by "replacing a certain number of values by randomly generated ones from the corresponding columns", plus missing categorical values; TabTransformer "is more robust than MLP", attributed to contextual embeddings; categorical features only, three datasets. — [arXiv 2012.06678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.06678)

- TabularBench (NeurIPS 2024 D&B): adversarial robustness for deep tabular models, "7 robustification mechanisms", 200+ models; does not address missing or zero-filled features. — [NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8f5defaa8dc728c3e4aaee59ac8ca942-Abstract.html)
- Dynamic instance-wise classification in correlated feature spaces (IFC²F, arXiv 2106.04668): selects per-instance feature subsets; "accuracy achieved by IFC²F is robust for up to 10% missing features" at test time. — [arXiv 2106.04668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.04668)

### Inferences
- The SITS missing-data literature manipulates the time axis (drop dates, mask values, fuse SAR) and measures the drop; none manipulates the channel axis with a learned sparse gate. Our result that a sparsemax channel gate recovers +0.06 macro-F1 under a zero-filled month is therefore a new mechanism in this literature, and the natural controls are the ones already used there: temporal-dropout augmentation (Garnot 2021; Miranda 2025), masking rather than imputation (Zhao 2021), and SAR fusion.
- The mechanistic story that fits all the evidence: a zero-filled month is an "uninformative token"; softmax attention and dense convolutions still spend mass or gradient on it (Rußwurm & Körner's TempCNN result, Vasylenko's dispersion argument, Grinsztajn's MLP result), whereas a sparsemax gate can send its weight to exactly zero. That reasoning exists in NLP and tabular ML but has not been written down for SITS.
- On framing (part d of the objective): no paper found frames sparse gating primarily as a corruption-robustness mechanism rather than an accuracy, interpretability or transfer mechanism. TabNet's own paper frames it as accuracy plus interpretability; InterpreTabNet and Meister et al. dispute the interpretability half; Jin et al. gesture at noise-filtering without an experiment; Vasylenko et al. frame entmax as a long-context dispersion fix. Reframing the gate as "no accuracy or transfer gain on clean data, but graceful degradation under a missing month" appears to be novel, and the literature above supplies both the dense-control expectation and the mechanism.
- Caution for the manuscript: Meister et al. and InterpreTabNet undercut any claim that the gate's masks are themselves interpretable; keep the claim to robustness, measured behaviourally.

### Gaps
- No tabular benchmark of TabNet under MCAR/MAR missingness or zero-filling at test time was found; the TabTransformer noise test is the nearest and excludes TabNet.
- The "F-Act" test-time feature-availability method surfaced in a search summary without a resolvable URL; not cited.
- Figure 8 of Garnot et al. 2021 holds the per-fraction mIoU values; only the text's qualitative summary and the panoptic 30% numbers were recoverable.
- Whether TimeSenCLIP actually reports a temporal-dropout robustness ablation could not be confirmed from the arXiv abstract.

## Key Question 4: Is there work showing band/feature selection acts as regularization that helps only in low-data regimes?

### Takeaway
The claim that band selection helps most when labels are scarce is widespread in hyperspectral work but rests on curse-of-dimensionality reasoning and in-region benchmarks (Indian Pines, Pavia) rather than on controlled training-fraction sweeps against a dense control. No SITS paper was found that sweeps training-set size with and without a learned gate; our null result at 10–25% of fields has no direct published counterpart.

### Cited Findings
- Hyperspectral band-selection papers motivate selection by "the 'curse of dimensionality' in classification for a low number of training samples" and high inter-band correlation; a joint deep band-selection/classification network reports OA 89.08% (Indian Pines) and 97.78% (Pavia University) at a fixed band count, above supervised band-selection baselines. — [search summary of par.nsf.gov and MDPI RS 15:4460](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-15-04460/article_deploy/remotesensing-15-04460.pdf)
- Zimmer et al. 2025 (ECAI, arXiv 2401.11420): embedded selectors "achieve strong performance, with CHBS excelling when selecting few bands", with "sensitivity to hyperparameters such as learning rate and regularization weight"; no training-fraction sweep in the abstract. — [arXiv 2401.11420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11420)
- Shah et al. 2022 Enhanced TabNet trains on 10% of Indian Pines samples per class and 200 samples per class on Pavia/Salinas and reports OA gains, but does not vary the fraction, so the low-data attribution is untested. — [Shah et al. 2022 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-00716/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-00716.pdf)
- Tian et al. 2023 justify the TabNet branch because RF-like methods "are more dependable when training small samples", but their ablation is at a single training-set size. — [Tian et al. 2023 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-15-01990/article_deploy/remotesensing-15-01990.pdf)
- Pankajakshan et al. 2025 train on 1–10% of labels and present this as "a data-efficient and scalable few-shot learning solution", but the comparison is to deep nets at full data, not to the same architecture with and without dimensionality reduction. — [Pankajakshan et al. 2025 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sensors/sensors-26-00174/article_deploy/sensors-26-00174.pdf)
- Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022: models using 8% of features were "comparable or even better" than all-feature models and "models built with fewer predictor variables showed better spatial transferability as have already been reported by [13,54]", but all training sites were fully labelled; no low-data sweep. — [Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022 PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-01493/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-01493.pdf)
- Grinsztajn et al. 2022's uninformative-feature experiments are at medium data (~10K samples) and do not sweep sample size. — [ar5iv 2207.08815](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.08815)

### Inferences
- The low-data benefit of learned selection is asserted far more often than it is measured. Our 10–25% ablation with a matched dense control is, as far as this search shows, the first such sweep for a sparse channel gate in SITS, and its null result is informative precisely because the folk expectation runs the other way.
- Where a low-data advantage is reported it is for whole model families (RF/SVM vs CNN), confounding capacity, inductive bias and selection; a gate added to a fixed backbone isolates the selection term, which is what our experiment does.

### Gaps
- No paper found that sweeps training fraction with a learned sparse gate vs a dense control in RS; the claim "helps only in low-data regimes" could not be sourced to any controlled experiment.
- Full texts of the hyperspectral band-selection papers (par.nsf.gov items, MDPI RS 15:4460) were not opened; their numbers come from search summaries.
