# Missing / cloud-gapped timesteps at inference in SITS crop classification: dense vs sparse models (novelty check)

Scope: how the satellite-image-time-series (SITS) literature handles missing or cloud-gapped observations at inference, what is known about the sensitivity of dense temporal deep models (TempCNN, CNN-BiLSTM/LSTM, Transformer, PSE-TAE/L-TAE) versus sparse / feature-selecting models (TabNet, sparsemax gates, tree ensembles), and whether anyone has reported the specific failure mode in the Western Cape finding (one month zero-filled only in the test region; dense models lose 0.08-0.14 macro-F1; median fill restores them; TabNet and a sparsemax-gated L-TAE almost unaffected).

Method note: searched Google/Bing-style web search, arXiv, MDPI, ScienceDirect, ISPRS, IEEE/DOAJ records, 2019-2026. Full texts of Zhao et al. 2021, Garnot et al. 2022, Russwurm & Koerner 2020, Ofori-Ampofo et al. 2021, Zhou et al. 2023 and Miranda et al. 2025 were downloaded and read (pdftotext). Several others were read only as abstracts / HTML; those are flagged. Total: roughly 50 tool calls.

## Key Question 1. Has anyone shown dense temporal deep models collapse when timesteps are missing or zero-filled only at test time (train/test missingness mismatch)?

### Takeaway
The literature repeatedly shows that dense SITS models degrade when optical observations are removed at inference, with drops of the same order as ours (0.10-0.22 F1/mIoU at 50-80% removal), and one 2025 paper isolates exactly the "train complete / test incomplete" protocol (U-TAE mean-F1 48.5 vs 70.7 at 75% missing). However, nobody tests the specific case of a single month zero-filled before standardization in the test region only, nobody reports the magnitude for a 1-of-10 timestep gap, and the only statement about zero-filling being biased is an unmeasured assertion (Zhao et al. 2021). Our finding is therefore consistent with the literature but not duplicated by it.

### Cited Findings

**Train-complete / test-incomplete protocol (closest match to our setting)**
- Wang, Belgiu, Wu, Zhong, Cao, Tao (2025), "A Joint Learning Framework with Feature Reconstruction and Prediction for Incomplete Satellite Image Time Series in Agricultural Semantic Segmentation", arXiv:2505.19159 (preprint, no peer-reviewed venue listed). Baseline = model trained on complete SITS and tested directly on incomplete SITS. At 75% of timesteps dropped, U-TAE baseline mean-F1 was 48.50 on the France+Catalonia Sen4AgriNet crop-classification set vs 70.70 with their reconstruction+prediction scheme; on Hunan Sentinel-2 cropland extraction 79.43 vs 86.40 (CL-F1 67.53 vs 79.36); Hunan PlanetScope 65.87 vs 82.10 (CL-F1 44.02 vs 72.44). Backbones evaluated: ConvLSTM, ConvGRU, U-ConvLSTM, FPN-ConvLSTM, 3D U-Net, U-TAE, TSViT. Reconstruction baselines: Linear-IN, Closest-IN, Last-IN, U-TILISE, GANFilling; augmentation baselines: window slicing (DA-WS) and temporal dropout (DA-TD), each applied to 50% of training samples. Mask ratios 0/25/50/75%. The paper does not state whether dropped steps are zero-filled or removed (input is defined as having T' < T steps, so removal is implied, an inference). It criticises augmentation because it "can only handle limited missing patterns, leading to poor generalization." — [arXiv 2505.19159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19159); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19159v1)

**Random removal of optical acquisitions at test time (model trained on full series, possibly with temporal dropout)**
- Garnot, Landrieu, Chehata (2022), "Multi-modal temporal attention models for crop mapping from satellite time series", ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 187:294-305, DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.03.012 (arXiv:2112.07558). "Varying Cloud Cover Experiment": random optical acquisitions removed from the PASTIS-R test set while radar is kept. "The performance of the S2-only model drops drastically as the number of available optical observations decreases for both parcel classification and semantic segmentation, performing worse than unimodal radar models for a ratio of 70% of artificial occlusion." From Fig. 8 the S2-only U-TAE (with temporal dropout) falls from ~74-75 mIoU at 90% availability to below the S1-only ~64.5 near 30% availability (values read from the plotted curves; exact numbers not tabulated). For panoptic segmentation: "when performing inference on only 30% of the optical observations, the S2 baseline model drops to 33.0 PQ. In contrast, our late fusion model maintains a score of 37.6 PQ." Multimodal fusion models "maintain an almost constant level of performance for up to 50% missing optical acquisitions"; decision fusion "even with 90% of optical images removed ... still outperforms the radar modality". — [arXiv 2112.07558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07558); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271622000855)
- Ofori-Ampofo, Pelletier, Lang (2021), "Crop Type Mapping from Optical and Radar Time Series Using Attention-Based Deep Learning", Remote Sensing 13(22):4668, DOI 10.3390/rs13224668. Sec. 5.3 "Sparser Time Series": PSE-TAE on Brittany; a random n% of the 27 Sentinel-2 dates is sampled at each of 5 runs. Sentinel-2-only F-score: 0.587 (100%, 27 obs.) -> 0.587 (80%, 22) -> 0.576 (60%, 16) -> 0.537 (40%, 11) -> 0.443 (20%, 5 obs.), i.e. -0.144 at 20%. Sentinel-1 only: 0.522 constant. TAE fusion: 0.611 -> 0.558 at 20%. Early fusion: 0.612 -> 0.478. No cloud masking or gap-filling was applied; the series were kept irregular (two least-cloudy acquisitions per month, scenes with <80% cloud). The text does not make explicit whether the subsampling applies to training as well as test data (likely both, an inference). — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/22/4668)

**Zero-filling: an asserted but unmeasured bias**
- Zhao, Duan, Liu, Sun, Reymondin (2021), "Evaluation of Five Deep Learning Models for Crop Type Mapping Using Sentinel-2 Time Series Images with Missing Information", Remote Sensing 13(14):2790, DOI 10.3390/rs13142790. Hengshui, China, 37-date Sentinel-2 series, 10 bands, total missing rate 43.5%. Sec. 3.1: "LSTM units cannot compute null values ... if we set null values as zeros, the training and testing results will be highly biased. Thus, inspired by Che et al. [GRU-D], a Mask layer ... was adopted, which made the pixels covered by the cloud not participate in the calculation." For 1D CNNs the missing elements "were set as zeros" (and the authors note the conv kernel then "does not contain the features of elements denoted as zeros"). Intro: "in the remote sensing community, the cloud pixels in optical imagery have been treated as noise in RNN models or zeros in CNN models." Results (Table 3, mean OA over 5 random splits, unfilled-with-mask vs linear-interpolation+Savitzky-Golay-filled): 1D CNN 86.43 vs 86.25; LSTM 80.57 vs 82.18; GRU 81.53 vs 81.67; LSTM-CNN 86.57 vs 85.75; GRU-CNN 85.98 vs 85.61. SDs were larger for filled TSD (e.g. 2.62 vs 1.25 for 1D CNN), "mainly caused by the transfer error of interpolation and smoothing methods." Missingness was identical in train and test (same region). — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13142790)

**Raw cloudy input (noise, not NaN) as a proxy for corrupted timesteps: convolutional models most fragile**
- Russwurm & Koerner (2020), "Self-attention for raw optical Satellite Time Series Classification", ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 169:421-435, DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2020.06.006 (arXiv:1910.10536). BavarianCrops, raw (cloudy, unfiltered) vs preprocessed (cloud-filtered, interpolated) Sentinel-2. 12-class kappa, pre -> raw: RF 0.86 -> 0.62; LSTM-RNN 0.87 -> 0.83; Transformer 0.87 -> 0.82; DuPLO 0.89 -> 0.78; MS-ResNet 0.85 -> 0.78; TempCNN 0.88 -> 0.71. 12-class class-mean F1: RF 0.55 -> 0.34; LSTM 0.60 -> 0.63; Transformer 0.66 -> 0.64; DuPLO 0.66 -> 0.51; MS-ResNet 0.64 -> 0.55; TempCNN 0.60 -> 0.41. 23-class kappa: RF 0.76 -> 0.53; LSTM 0.78 -> 0.71; Transformer 0.79 -> 0.71; DuPLO 0.81 -> 0.68; MS-ResNet 0.76 -> 0.69; TempCNN 0.80 -> 0.67. Authors: "models based on recurrence and self-attention achieved better accuracy metrics on noisy raw data compared to convolutional models" and "self-attention mechanisms are a key tool in suppressing the non-classification-relevant cloudy observations which explain the zero-gradients" (attention scores avoided cloudy peaks). Note models were retrained on raw data, so this is not a train/test mismatch test. — [arXiv 1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)

**Generic (non-SITS) evidence that zero-filling specifically harms neural networks**
- Yi, Lee, Kim, Hwang, Yang (2020), "Why Not to Use Zero Imputation? Correcting Sparsity Bias in Training Neural Networks", ICLR 2020 (arXiv:1906.00150). Zero imputation induces a "variable sparsity problem": the network output shifts with the input missing rate, degrading performance; they propose Sparsity Normalization. Quantitative results are in the full paper (not read here). — [arXiv 1906.00150](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00150)
- Lipton, Kale, Wetzel (2016), "Modeling Missing Data in Clinical Time Series with RNNs", MLHC 2016 (arXiv:1606.04130). LSTM with zero-imputation plus missingness indicators performed best; indicators improved both imputation strategies; LSTMs did better with zero-filling than with imputed values when indicators were present. — [PMLR](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v56/Lipton16.pdf); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.04130)
- Che, Purushotham, Cho, Sontag, Liu (2018), "Recurrent Neural Networks for Multivariate Time Series with Missing Values" (GRU-D), Scientific Reports 8:6085. Masking + time-interval decay inside the GRU rather than imputation; this is the mechanism Zhao et al. 2021 adopted for SITS. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-24271-9)

**Missingness shift (train/test missingness differs) in tabular ML**
- Lee, Kang, Kim (2025), "MIRRAMS: Learning Robust Tabular Models under Unseen Missingness Shifts", arXiv:2507.08280. Defines missingness shift as a change in the missingness distribution between training and test even when the feature distribution is stable, arising because "data collection policies can vary across time or locations"; states such shifts degrade performance; proposes mutual-information robustness losses. No tree-vs-NN comparison; numbers only in full PDF (not read). — [arXiv 2507.08280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08280)
- Rockenschaub et al. (2024), "Robust prediction under missingness shifts", arXiv:2406.16484. When missingness depends only on observed data ("ignorable" shift) the Bayes predictor is unchanged and consistent estimators remain robust; under non-ignorable shifts the Bayes predictor changes and neither approach they compare recovers it; "different methods appear robust to different types of shift." — [arXiv 2406.16484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16484)
- Salvador, Sequeira, Cardoso (2026), "When Does Task-Aware Imputation Matter? An Analysis of Robustness Under Train-Test Mismatch", arXiv:2610.20342. 36 datasets; training missing rate 0.1/0.3, test rate 0.1-0.7. Recovery ratio under rate shift: 0.82 for a joint (task-aware) imputer vs 0.69-0.70 for classical imputers (best classical: column median 0.70); mean accuracy gains +0.12 to +0.14 for 0.1->0.5 and 0.3->0.5 shifts; shifts to equal or lower test rates were slightly negative. Classifier was a single-hidden-layer MLP; zero imputation and tree models were not evaluated. Column mean was "a competitive baseline". — [arXiv 2610.20342](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20342)

### Inferences
- The Wang/Belgiu 2025 baseline is the published protocol closest to ours (dense model trained on complete series, tested on incomplete), and its 22-point mean-F1 gap at 75% missing is far larger than our 8-14 point macro-F1 gap at "one month missing for 41% of pixels"; our gap is nevertheless large relative to the amount of missing data, which is consistent with the zero-fill-before-standardisation pathway (a standardized zero becomes a large negative z-score, i.e. an out-of-distribution value, rather than an absent observation). No paper found makes this standardization argument explicitly; it is our inference.
- Russwurm & Koerner's ranking inversion (TempCNN best-or-equal on preprocessed data, worst deep model on raw) is the closest published precedent for "a preprocessing choice changes the model ranking", but it is about raw-vs-cleaned input with retraining, not about an imputation inconsistency between regions.
- Zhao et al.'s claim that zeros make results "highly biased" is a design justification, not a measured result; our experiment appears to be the first in SITS to quantify it.

### Gaps
- No SITS paper found that zero-fills a timestep in the test set only (or in one region only) and reports the resulting drop; none examines the interaction of zero-filling with StandardScaler/z-score normalisation.
- No paper found quantifying the effect of a single missing monthly composite (1 of 10) rather than 25-80% random removal.
- Miranda, Mena, Dengel (2025, IDA 2025, arXiv:2504.06915) analyse Monte Carlo temporal dropout at inference on regression tasks (SwissYield, LFMC, PM2.5) and report "higher dropout ratios or missing time-steps consistently result in" declining performance, but give no classification results and no train/test mismatch analysis; architectures are not the SITS classifiers of interest. — [arXiv 2504.06915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06915)

## Key Question 2. Has anyone shown sparsemax/TabNet-style gates or tree models are robust to missing features/timesteps in remote sensing or tabular settings?

### Takeaway
Tree ensembles have well-documented native missing-value handling (missing-incorporated-attribute / default-direction splits) that benchmarks show predicts as well or better than imputation; expressive models like XGBoost are also shown to depend less on imputation quality. For TabNet/sparsemax there is no empirical paper (tabular or remote sensing) demonstrating robustness to missing inputs; the only support is theoretical (sparse attention is more noise-robust than dense softmax attention) and the qualitative observation that self-attention in Transformers learns to down-weight cloudy dates. Our L-TAE-S / TabNet result is therefore novel as an empirical demonstration in SITS.

### Cited Findings

**Tree ensembles**
- Perez-Lebel, Varoquaux, Le Morvan, Josse, Poline (2022), "Benchmarking missing-values approaches for predictive models on health databases", GigaScience 11:giac013. Conclusion: "native support for missing values in supervised machine learning predicts better than state-of-the-art imputation with much less computational cost"; the best native approach is the missing-incorporated-attribute (MIA) strategy for gradient-boosted trees; if imputing, add indicator columns. Neural networks not benchmarked. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9012100/)
- Le Morvan & Varoquaux (2025), "Imputation for prediction: beware of diminishing returns", ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2407.19804). On real data with powerful models, "improving imputation only has a minor effect on prediction performance"; more expressive predictors such as XGBoost are less reliant on high-quality imputation (smaller slope of prediction-R2 vs imputation-R2); missingness indicators help even under MCAR. Compares XGBoost (native and with indicator), an MLP and SAINT. — [arXiv 2407.19804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19804); [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/30481)
- Counter-evidence on trees: TabFSBench (2025, arXiv:2501.18935) reports that under simulated *feature shifts* (features present in training but absent/changed at test) "tree-based models are particularly susceptible to negative impacts". This is feature-set shift, not value-level missingness, but it cautions against a blanket "trees are robust" claim. — [arXiv 2501.18935](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.18935)
- Russwurm & Koerner 2020 (above): Random Forest on *raw* cloudy series was the most fragile model (12-class kappa 0.86 -> 0.62, F1 0.55 -> 0.34), i.e. trees are robust to absent values flagged as missing, not to corrupted values presented as real reflectance. — [arXiv 1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)

**TabNet / sparsemax**
- Arik & Pfister (2021), "TabNet: Attentive Interpretable Tabular Learning", AAAI 2021 (arXiv:1908.07442). Sparsemax in the attentive transformer produces instance-wise sparse feature-selection masks; the paper's only missing-data component is masked self-supervised pretraining (predicting masked columns from others). It does not test prediction accuracy under missing inputs. — [arXiv 1908.07442](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07442)
- Martins & Astudillo (2016), "From Softmax to Sparsemax", ICML 2016 (arXiv:1602.02068). Introduces sparsemax (exact zeros in attention); matched softmax accuracy in NLI with more compact attention; no robustness-to-noise evaluation. — [arXiv 1602.02068](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.02068)
- PLADIS (2025, arXiv:2503.07677) gives a theoretical argument that sparse (alpha-entmax-type, alpha>1) attention is more noise-robust than dense softmax attention: noise affects sparse retrieval error roughly linearly but dense retrieval error exponentially, and higher sparsity further reduces error. Context is diffusion models, not time series. — [arXiv 2503.07677](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07677)
- Tamayo-Rousseau et al. (2025), "Your Attention Matters: to Improve Model Robustness to Noise and Spurious Correlations", arXiv:2507.20453. Compared Softmax, Sigmoid, Linear, Doubly Stochastic and Cosine attention in ViTs under corrupted data; Doubly Stochastic was most robust (0.1-5.1% relative accuracy over next best). Sparsemax was not tested. — [arXiv 2507.20453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20453v3)
- TabTransformer (WeaSuL 2021 workshop, arXiv:2107.03690) reports TabTransformer "shows better stability than MLP in handling missing values", attributed to contextual embeddings, not sparsemax; TabNet not included. — [arXiv 2107.03690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03690)
- Search for TabNet + crop classification + missing-data robustness returned no matching study; the only remote-sensing TabNet variant found is TabNets (spatial attention in the attentive transformer) for hyperspectral classification, with no missing-data experiment. — [MDPI Remote Sensing 14(3):716](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/3/716/htm)

**Attention-based SITS models down-weighting cloudy dates**
- Russwurm & Koerner 2020: attention scores "did not focus on the strong positive peaks in the time series, which indicates a cloudy observation. Hence, we can conclude that self-attention mechanisms are a key tool in suppressing the non-classification-relevant cloudy observations." — [arXiv 1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)
- Garnot, Landrieu, Giordano, Chehata (2020), "Satellite Image Time Series Classification with Pixel-Set Encoders and Temporal Self-Attention", CVPR 2020. States the temporal self-attention model "presents the same robustness to cloud-obstructed observations as RNN-based models" (search snippet; full text not re-read in this session). — [CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Garnot_Satellite_Image_Time_Series_Classification_With_Pixel-Set_Encoders_and_Temporal_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- Softmax attention can *down-weight* an out-of-distribution timestep but cannot zero it; a sparsemax gate can. The PLADIS linear-vs-exponential error argument is the only published mechanism found that would predict the L-TAE-S advantage under zero fill, and it has not been tested on SITS. Our +0.06 (zero fill) / -0.02 (proper fill) result for a sparsemax gate on CNN-BiLSTM is, as far as this search found, the first such measurement.
- Trees' robustness in our setting is likely due to (i) native missing handling in XGBoost/LightGBM and (ii) field-level xr_fresh features being aggregated statistics in which one missing month perturbs a value rather than inserting an out-of-distribution token. No paper found tests (ii).

### Gaps
- No paper (tabular or remote sensing) was found that empirically measures TabNet or sparsemax attention accuracy under injected missing features or missing timesteps.
- Chen & Guestrin (2016) XGBoost "sparsity-aware split finding" (arXiv:1603.02754) is the canonical reference for default-direction handling of missing values; it was not fetched in this session, cite from the paper directly.

## Key Question 3. Standard practices (temporal dropout, masking tokens, interpolation, gap-filling, irregular-time encodings, masked pretraining) and whether any paper compares imputation strategies at inference

### Takeaway
Temporal dropout / random timestep masking is now a standard SITS augmentation with reported gains (e.g. +0.6 to +1.1 mIoU in PASTIS-R, +3.5 OA in Mask-PSTIN), masked-reconstruction pretraining (SITS-BERT, SITS-Former, U-BARN, Presto) is the dominant self-supervised recipe for gap tolerance, and GRU-D-style mask layers or learnable "missing" tokens are used to avoid zero-filling. Explicit inference-time comparisons of imputation strategies exist (Zhao 2021: linear+SG fill vs mask; Wang/Belgiu 2025: Linear/Closest/Last interpolation, U-TILISE, GANFilling vs augmentation vs their method), but none includes zero-fill or median-fill as a condition and none varies the strategy between train and test regions.

### Cited Findings

**Temporal dropout / random timestep masking as augmentation**
- Garnot, Landrieu, Chehata (2022, ISPRS): temporal dropout defined as dropping each observation with modality-specific probability p_m during training, "At inference time, the network can use all available observations"; p_0 = 0.4 used. Parcel classification mIoU (5-fold, PASTIS-R): S2-only 73.9 -> 74.5 with temporal dropout; Early fusion 74.9 -> 76.5; Mid 75.1 -> 75.9; Late 73.0 -> 73.6 (77.2 with auxiliary supervision + TD); Decision 72.5 -> 72.8. "Auxiliary supervision and temporal dropout make both unimodal and multimodal models more resilient to missing optical acquisitions for semantic segmentation" (Fig. 8b). — [arXiv 2112.07558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07558)
- Zhang et al. (2024), "Improving crop type mapping by integrating LSTM with temporal random masking and pixel-set spatial information", ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 218(B):87-101, DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.10.013 (Mask-PSTIN). Adding the pixel-set encoder raised mean OA from 80.9% to 83.9%; adding temporal random masking raised it to 87.4% (mean F1 0.865). Motivation: gaps and noise from clouds/rain reduce accuracy, especially early season. Full text paywalled (CGIAR repository record restricted). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271624003897); [CGSpace](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/c4de6c63-5254-49f8-9052-6a170a92d675/full)
- Wang/Belgiu et al. 2025: names window slicing and temporal dropout as "the most commonly used methods" for simulating gaps, and states that exposing models to missing data in training "has proven effective for improving its ability to handle time gaps during prediction", while arguing augmentation alone generalises poorly to unseen missing patterns. — [arXiv 2505.19159](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19159)
- Miranda, Mena, Dengel (2025, IDA 2025): Monte Carlo Temporal Dropout (drop timesteps at inference, predefined ratio) and Concrete Temporal Dropout (learned ratio); MC-ConcTD improved R2 by up to 6 percentage points over MC-TD (PM2.5); learned dropout ratios varied widely by dataset (e.g. near 0.01 for SwissYield); limitation acknowledged that simulated missingness "could potentially differ from scenarios involving actual missing data." — [arXiv 2504.06915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06915)
- TimeSenCLIP (2025, arXiv:2508.11919): random masking of a contiguous block of temporal frames "to mimic seasonal gaps from cloud cover". — [arXiv 2508.11919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11919)
- ISPRS 2026 congress abstract, "Random Temporal Masking and Neural ODE Optimization for Crop Type Mapping with Inconsistent Remote Sensing Time Series Data": random temporal masking in training plus a neural-ODE smoothing term, integrated into TSViT, evaluated on PASTIS. — [ISPRS 2026 programme](https://program.isprs2026.org/indexd848.html)

**Masking layers / missing tokens instead of zero-fill**
- Zhao et al. 2021: GRU-D-inspired Mask layer so cloud-covered elements "do not participate in the calculation" in LSTM/GRU; zeros used only in the 1D-CNN branch. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13142790)
- "Foundation Models Meet Agriculture: Challenges Beyond Pretraining" (2026, arXiv:2608.30392): uses a learnable per-modality "missing" token for invalid timesteps so the model can distinguish a real data gap from pretraining masking (search snippet; not read in full). — [arXiv 2608.30392](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30392)
- Miller et al. (2024), "Deep Learning for Satellite Image Time Series Analysis: A Review", IEEE GRSM (arXiv:2404.03936): notes zero-padding is used to keep series at fixed length after cloud filtering, and lists clouds, shadows, aerosols and sensor faults as gap sources. — [arXiv 2404.03936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.03936)

**Irregular-time positional encodings**
- Garnot et al. CVPR 2020 (PSE-TAE) and Garnot & Landrieu (L-TAE, 2020) encode acquisition day-of-year positionally so irregular / missing dates are handled without resampling; TimeMatch / Thermal Positional Encoding (Nyborg et al., CVPRW 2022, arXiv:2203.09175) replaces calendar time with thermal time to fix cross-region phenological shift, noting that existing attention methods "generalize poorly to regions not seen during training mainly due to not being robust to temporal shifts of the growing season". These address irregular sampling and phenology shift, not a systematically absent month. — [arXiv 2203.09175](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.09175)

**Masked-reconstruction pretraining**
- Tseng et al., "Lightweight, Pre-trained Transformers for Remote Sensing Timeseries" (Presto), arXiv:2304.14065 (v4 Feb 2024). "A key requirement for Presto was to perform well even with incomplete inputs (i.e., when there are missing timesteps, channels, or both)"; four masking strategies (random tokens, channel groups, contiguous timesteps, random timesteps). CropHarvest with all 12 months: Presto_R F1 0.835 / AUC 0.917 vs TIML 0.802 / 0.890 vs MOSAIKS-1D 0.738 / 0.806; "Using a subset of the 12 months, Presto surpassed the performance of TIML and MOSAIKS-1D which used all input months" (Fig. 3; exact subset values not tabulated). Fuel moisture with 3 timesteps: RF RMSE 23.84 beat Presto_FT 25.28. — [arXiv 2304.14065](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4); [GitHub](https://github.com/nasaharvest/presto)
- Yuan & Lin (2021), SITS-BERT, IEEE JSTARS 14:474-487: pretraining task is to predict randomly contaminated (noise-injected) observations across the pixel time series. (DOAJ record; DOI not verified in this session.) — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/4fb58140e0d948d39692eecad8be09a1)
- Yuan, Lin, Zhao, Jiang (2022), SITS-Former, Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf.: pretrained with a missing-data imputation task (randomly masked patches regressed from the rest). — [NWPU record](https://pure.nwpu.edu.cn/zh/publications/sits-former-a-pre-trained-spatio-spectral-temporal-representation/)
- Dumeur, Valero, Inglada (2024), U-BARN, IEEE JSTARS: BERT-style masked pretraining for irregularly sampled Sentinel-2 series evaluated on PASTIS crop segmentation; masking rate is an important hyperparameter. — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/78300210f8244fe1be47ded252e25742)
- EGU26 abstract EGU26-8646: a temporal masked autoencoder that treats clouds as a natural mask reconstructed growth curves with only 20% valid observations and beat Bi-LSTM and TempCNN models relying on interpolation or Whittaker smoothing (conference abstract, no peer review). — [EGU26](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-8646.html)

**Gap-filling / representation before classification**
- Zhou et al. (2023), "Contrastive-Learning-Based Time-Series Feature Representation for Parcel-Based Crop Mapping Using Incomplete Sentinel-2 Image Sequences", Remote Sensing 15(20):5009, DOI 10.3390/rs15205009. Maps gappy spectral series to a gap-free representation (type-wise TS2Vec), then XGBoost/LSTM. Dijon: XGB on clean-only images OA 76.92% / F1 0.606; LSTM on all raw gappy series 80.95% / 0.704; XGB on raw series 79.81% / 0.674; LSTM on learned representation 83.94% / 0.746; XGB on learned representation 84.52% / 0.755. No zero-fill or interpolation baseline. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15205009)
- Wang/Belgiu 2025 (above) is the only SITS paper found that benchmarks several reconstruction methods (Linear-IN, Closest-IN, Last-IN, U-TILISE, GANFilling) against augmentation (DA-WS, DA-TD) and a no-handling baseline on the same backbones at the same missing rates. — [arXiv 2505.19159](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19159v1)
- Ofori-Ampofo et al. 2021 cite Inglada et al. as showing that Sentinel-1+Sentinel-2 "without gap-filling cloudy areas can yield results equivalent to using Sentinel-2 only in a cloud-free setting", and therefore use no pixel-based cloud masking or gap-filling. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/22/4668)
- A 2026 Scientific Reports benchmark of Sentinel-2 gap-filling methods ("Evaluating Sentinel-2 gap filling techniques for cloud removal and data reconstruction") found spatio-temporal deep learning performed well but "failed in generalizing over all scenarios" and kriging struggled with large irregular gaps; this is reconstruction accuracy, not classification. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-39488-2)

### Inferences
- Every published strategy assumes the same handling of gaps in training and inference. The failure mode we observed (a gap pattern never seen in training, filled with a constant that is then standardized) is orthogonal to the augmentation / pretraining literature, which makes gaps *seen* in training. Temporal dropout would plausibly have prevented our collapse (Garnot 2022 Fig. 8b shows TD-trained models are more resilient to removed acquisitions), but only if the dropped step is represented the same way at train and test time.
- Median fill with training-set statistics is, in effect, "column median" imputation, which Salvador et al. 2026 found to be the strongest classical imputer under rate shift and Le Morvan & Varoquaux 2025 found to be nearly as good as sophisticated imputers for expressive models; this supports our restoration result but the SITS literature has not stated it.

### Gaps
- No SITS paper compares zero-fill vs mean/median-fill vs interpolation vs mask at inference on a fixed trained model.
- Full texts of Zhang et al. 2024 (Mask-PSTIN; DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.10.013), SITS-BERT (Yuan & Lin 2021, JSTARS) and SITS-Former (Yuan et al. 2022, IJAEOG 106:102651, DOI not verified) were not accessed; numbers above come from abstracts/snippets.
- MCTNet ("A lightweight CNN-Transformer network for pixel-based crop mapping using time-series Sentinel-2 imagery", Comput. Electron. Agric. 2024, ScienceDirect S0168169924007610) reportedly adds an attention-based learnable positional encoding to handle varying missing rates and claims robustness to missing values, but the page returned 403 and a follow-up search could not confirm the title or numbers. Download via DOI from the ScienceDirect PII. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169924007610)
- Weikmann et al. TimeSen2Crop, Vuolo et al. gap-filling, and Interdonato et al. DuPLO were not individually searched beyond DuPLO's appearance in Russwurm & Koerner's table (DuPLO 12-class kappa 0.89 -> 0.78 raw).

## Key Question 4. Is inference-time cloud-gap handling identified anywhere as a cause of apparent cross-region transfer failure, and has any paper reported that a published model ranking changed after fixing a train/test imputation or preprocessing inconsistency?

### Takeaway
No. Cross-region transfer papers attribute failure to phenological shift, climate/management differences and (loosely) to differing cloud-driven data availability, but none isolates inference-time gap handling as the cause, and no paper was found reporting that correcting a train/test imputation inconsistency reversed a model ranking. The closest items are Russwurm & Koerner's raw-vs-preprocessed ranking change (TempCNN from co-best to worst deep model), SustainBench dropping prior baselines after changing crop-type inputs, and generic warnings about preprocessing heterogeneity making comparisons unreliable.

### Cited Findings
- Spatial transferability of RF with Sentinel-1/2 across seven German sites (Remote Sensing 14(6):1493, 2022): transfer failures attributed to phenological stages occurring "later for a few days or even weeks in some sites"; SAR models transfer better where clouds are frequent, but "for areas with no persistent cloud cover issues, such spatial transferability study outcomes could be the opposite." — [DOI 10.3390/rs14061493](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14061493)
- Five-year Slovakian Sentinel-2 transfer study (Remote Sensing 15(13):3414, 2023): "the quality of the input temporal composites from Sentinel-2 data might cause a potential degradation in transferability performance", with cloud cover and limited clear observations as contributing factors; not isolated experimentally. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/13/3414)
- Nyborg et al. (TPE, 2022): cross-region failure of attention models attributed to temporal (phenological) shift, addressed by thermal positional encoding; missing data not discussed as the cause. — [arXiv 2203.09175](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.09175)
- Russwurm & Koerner 2020: on preprocessed data "all evaluated deep learning models performed similarly well"; on raw data LSTM and Transformer led and TempCNN fell behind by 0.03-0.07 kappa and 5-11% accuracy in the 12-class setting, i.e. the ranking depended on preprocessing. — [arXiv 1910.10536](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)
- SustainBench (Yeh et al., 2021, arXiv:2111.04724): crop-type task inputs were changed from the original setup and "due to these changes, we do not include baseline models from [83] for this iteration of the dataset", an acknowledgement that preprocessing changes invalidate prior rankings, without quantifying it. — [arXiv 2111.04724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.04724)
- MIRRAMS (2025) explicitly motivates missingness shift by data collection policies varying "across time or locations", the tabular analogue of a region-specific gap. — [arXiv 2507.08280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08280)

### Inferences
- The combination in our finding (region-specific systematic gap + constant fill + standardization + dense models vs sparse-gated/tree models + ranking reversal after correction) does not appear in the SITS literature. The individual components (dense models degrade with removed acquisitions; trees handle missing natively; sparse attention is theoretically noise-robust; temporal dropout helps) are each published, so the novelty claim should be framed as "first to show that an inference-time imputation inconsistency between regions can masquerade as a transfer failure and reorder a SITS model benchmark", not as "first to show dense models are sensitive to missing timesteps".

### Gaps
- A search summary reported a Sentinel-2 study where spatial transfer "could decrease the macro F1 value between 0.02 and 0.15" attributed to environmental/management differences, but the specific paper was not identified in the results and is not cited here.
- No erratum, reproducibility audit or corrected benchmark for a named SITS crop dataset (BreizhCrops, PASTIS, TimeSen2Crop, AI4FoodSecurity) attributing a ranking change to imputation was found.
