# Local literature audit: what the collection already covers on the four revised findings

Scope: the ~50 per-paper briefers in `writeup/literature/*.md`, the synthesis `writeup/literature/0_findings_summary.md`, the earlier report `reports/Cross region crop mapping literature.md`, the 47-entry `writeup/CEA_submission/cea.bib`, and the manuscript `writeup/CEA_submission/cea-article.tex` (Introduction, Related Work, Results, Discussion, Conclusion). No web search was used. All "Source" links below are local file paths. Quotes are short and verbatim from the briefers unless marked as paraphrase.

Context the report writer should hold in mind (from the repo, not the literature): `experiments/holdout_fill_check/README.md` records that 40.6% of holdout-tile pixels (39.3% of fields) lack a September observation while the training tiles have none missing, and that the published pipelines filled this gap three different ways (raw-input deep nets: zero before scaling; pixel TabNet: holdout column medians; xr_fresh classical: NaN-aware features). With a consistent training-median fill, "the top six are pixel deep temporal models (0.63 to 0.66)", pixel TabNet "barely changes (0.61 → 0.62)", and "the published near-flat L-TAE curve was an artifact of the zero fill; every model gains with more training fields" ([fill-check README](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/README.md); [table1_filled.csv](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/table1_filled.csv)). The manuscript's Data section (line 79) describes the May/June exclusion but never mentions the holdout September gap ([cea-article.tex](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/CEA_submission/cea-article.tex)).

The four revised findings, abbreviated below as F1–F4:
- F1: with consistent imputation, dense deep temporal pixel models transfer as well as or better than tree/linear/TabNet models; in-region and out-of-region rankings no longer invert.
- F2: sparse gates (TabNet, L-TAE-S) buy robustness to a missing/zero-filled timestep, not better transfer on complete data.
- F3: zero-filling a month that was never missing in training cut dense models' holdout macro-F1 by 0.08–0.14; TabNet and the sparse gate were almost unaffected.
- F4: learning curves: all models ~0.55 macro-F1 at ~10% of fields; deep models pull ahead with more data; logistic regression plateaus at 0.61.

## (a) Which briefers and bib entries bear on the four findings, and which manuscript claims they currently support

### Takeaway
The collection was assembled to corroborate the old thesis (sparse/tree-like models transfer, dense nets overfit the training region), so most of its "transfer" evidence is now either neutral or needs reframing. Only a handful of items speak to missing-data robustness (Presto, Rußwurm 2018, Cropformer, the XGBoost sparsity-aware split, the gap-filling steps in Ienco/TempCNN/Kussul), and only the Bake-off redux, Maxwell 2019, the DL-aerial review, and Cropformer's few-sample scenario speak to learning curves. Several Discussion citations (belgiu2016, maxwell2019, pankajakshan2025, rustowicz2019, jin2019, mai2025, tabnet_orig, obadic2022) are attached to the "tree-like bias transfers / dense nets overfit" paragraph and must be detached or re-pointed.
