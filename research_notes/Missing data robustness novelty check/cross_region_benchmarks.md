# Cross-region (spatial out-of-domain, no adaptation) comparisons of deep SITS crop classifiers vs classical/tree/linear models, 2019–2026

Scope: papers that train in one region and evaluate on a spatially disjoint region without target labels or adaptation, reporting which model family won and the size of the transfer drop. Adapted / fine-tuned results are noted only as context. Our reference result: AI4FoodSecurity South Africa, adjacent Sentinel-2 tile, 5 winter crops, out-of-region macro-F1 CNN-BiLSTM 0.66, TempCNN 0.65, L-TAE 0.65, Transformer 0.64, TimesNet 0.63, pixel TabNet 0.62, XGBoost/LightGBM/LogReg/MiniRocket 0.59–0.61; deep-pixel transfer gaps −0.06 to −0.14; no ranking inversion.

Access notes: MDPI and Taylor & Francis HTML pages returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so Hoppe 2024, Orynbaikyzy 2022 and Rusňák 2023 were read from the publisher PDFs via pdftotext; TimeMatch, TPE, BreizhCrops and Pandžić 2026 were read from PDFs the same way. Papers I could not open are flagged in each Gaps section.

---

## Key question 1: Which papers benchmark TempCNN / L-TAE / Transformer / LSTM against RF / XGBoost under spatial holdout, and who wins by how much?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of papers put deep temporal models and tree/linear models on the same spatially disjoint test region with no adaptation. Where they do (BreizhCrops regional folds; Pandžić 2026 Slovenia→Serbia; Rusňák 2023 Slovakia with an MLP; Chen & Liu 2021 GF-6 China; Wijesingha 2024 Landsat Germany), the deep sequence model wins or ties in all but Wijesingha 2024, and the deep-over-tree margin on the held-out region is typically 2–5 pp OA (BreizhCrops), 5–6 pp OA (Rusňák MLP vs RF) or ~20 pp F1 (Pandžić Transformer vs RF). No paper I found compares L-TAE, Transformer, TempCNN and LSTM together against XGBoost/LightGBM on engineered features under a same-year adjacent-tile holdout; the closest is BreizhCrops (RF only, no engineered time-series features, no in-region reference).

### Inferences
- The literature contains no direct precedent for a 27-model, same-year adjacent-tile comparison that includes TempCNN, L-TAE, Transformer, LSTM, TimesNet, TabNet, MiniRocket, XGBoost, LightGBM and logistic regression under identical input handling. BreizhCrops (8 models, RF the only non-deep baseline, no in-region reference) and Pandžić 2026 (2 models) are the nearest.
- Where deep sequence models and trees are compared on a spatially disjoint region without adaptation, the deep model wins in BreizhCrops (+2–3 pp OA, +4–6 pp class-average accuracy), Pandžić 2026 (+20 pp F1), Rusňák 2023 (MLP +5.4 pp OA over RF), and the Chen & Liu snippet (LSTM <10 % drop vs "significant" RF drop). The one counter-example with numbers is Wijesingha 2024 (RF macro-F1 0.66 vs CNN 0.62 under spatial transfer), and that CNN is a 2D spatial CNN on Landsat, not a temporal pixel model.
- Our margins (deep pixel 0.63–0.66 vs trees/linear 0.59–0.61 macro-F1, i.e. +0.02 to +0.07) sit in the same band as BreizhCrops' deep-over-RF margin on class-average accuracy (0.55–0.59 vs 0.53), so the direction and size of the effect are consistent with the only comparable regional-fold benchmark.

### Gaps
- Chen & Liu 2021 (GF-6, Lindian→Hailun) is the only paper that benchmarks the full BreizhCrops model zoo plus RF under an adjacent-region transfer, but the site refused connection; the "LSTM <10 % drop, Transformer and RF dropped significantly, 7.2–41 %" numbers come from a search-engine snippet of the Chinese abstract and should be verified before citing.
- Wijesingha 2024 numbers come from the repository abstract via a search summary; the paper (ISPRS JPRS, likely paywalled; Kassel OA copy exists) was not opened.
- "Pankajakshan et al. 2025 (Deep architectures fail to generalize)", "Mohammadi 2024", "Wang 2023", "Li 2025", "Mai 2025", "Tufail 2025", "Bellemans", "Mandal" and "Lucas et al. 2021 (Landsat cross-year)" could not be identified with four targeted searches each; no paper with the title phrase "Deep architectures fail to generalize" exists in the indexes searched. The only Tufail hit is a Bologna PhD thesis (LiteTemporalConv, MS-ConvBiGRU-Attn vs ML baselines, Northern Italy, no unseen-region test; https://amsdottorato.unibo.it/id/eprint/12764/).
- Ge, Zhang & Zhu 2023 (National Remote Sensing Bulletin 27(12):2796–2814, "Spatial generalization ability analysis of deep learning crop classification models") compares deep segmentation models (MultiResUNet best) across Chinese regions and reports that SR data transfer better than TOA, but the site refused connection and no RF baseline is mentioned in the abstract. https://www.ygxb.ac.cn/en/article/doi/10.11834/jrs.20221408
- No paper was found that benchmarks MiniRocket or TabNet under spatial holdout for crops.

## Key question 2: Does any paper document a ranking inversion between within-region CV and out-of-region holdout?

### Takeaway
Yes, in both directions, but always with two or three models and small samples: Pandžić 2026 shows RF > Transformer in-region (F1 84.4 vs 76.5) flipping to Transformer > RF under naive transfer (66.8 vs 46.5); Wijesingha 2024 shows CNN ≥ RF in-region (0.75 vs 0.74) flipping to RF > CNN under spatial transfer (0.66 vs 0.62); Sen4AgriNet shows TempCNN > LSTM in-domain flipping to LSTM >> TempCNN cross-country. No paper reports a many-model ranking that is stable across in-region and out-of-region evaluation, which is what our result shows.

### Cited Findings
- Pandžić et al. 2026, S1+S2: in-region from-scratch RF F1 84.35 % vs Transformer 76.51 %; naive SLO→SRB transfer RF 46.49 % vs Transformer 66.80 % (inversion in favour of the deep model). With S2 only the in-region order is already TR > RF (83.04 vs 79.17) and widens under transfer (57.98 vs 40.67). — [PDF](https://www.geodetski-vestnik.com/arhiv/70/2/268_Pandzic_at_al.pdf)
- Wijesingha et al. 2024: reference macro-F1 RF 0.74, CNN 0.75; spatial transfer RF 0.66, CNN 0.62; spatio-temporal RF 0.62, CNN 0.58 (inversion in favour of RF; temporal transfer keeps CNN ahead 0.68 vs 0.67). — [Kassel repository](https://kobra.uni-kassel.de/items/1dfa9786-3981-4178-9cac-598b6137126e/full)
- Sen4AgriNet parcel-level: in-domain TempCNN 90.08 > LSTM 88.52 ≈ Transformer 88.36 (wAcc); Catalonia→France LSTM 91.55 vs Transformer 39.17 vs TempCNN 36.90; France→Catalonia 2020 LSTM 60.60 vs TempCNN 52.32 vs Transformer 51.21. — [arXiv 2204.00951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00951)
- Rusňák et al. 2023: "All classification algorithms showed good performance when trained and validated in the given year (data not shown)", yet under spatial transfer RF is lowest (mean OA 81.62 vs 85.4–87.3) and "less robust ... particularly when the distribution of the unseen domains deviated significantly from that used for training" (2022 drought year: RF 72.10 vs NN 83.80). — [DOI 10.3390/rs15133414](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- Hoppe et al. 2024: XGBoost has the highest spatially transferred F1 in short-window scenarios (82 %, 72 %) but majority voting is the "best transferability model" in the full-season day scenario (F1 and accuracy 0.65); SGD "performed worst, which could be due to an overfitting"; the authors conclude "models cannot be transferred to other areas without adaptation". No in-region ranking table is given, only Figure 4. — [DOI 10.3390/rs16091493](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16091493)
- BreizhCrops and CropGlobe report no in-region numbers, so no inversion can be assessed; CropGlobe does document rankings changing between transfer pairs (AlphaEarth_MLP beats CropNet only on FRA→USA, 79.53 vs 73.34; Harmonic_MLP beats Median_MLP only on FRA→CHN, 74.60 vs 44.64). — [arXiv 2509.03497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03497)
- SustainBench (Kenya crop type): LDA outperformed RF cross-region; in-region ranking not reported. — [arXiv 2111.04724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04724)
- Nyborg TPE: removing the positional encoding from PSE+LTAE raises leave-one-region-out F1 from 68.0 to 75.9 even though it discards temporal order, i.e. an architectural choice that hurts in-region modelling helps out-of-region. — [arXiv 2203.09175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09175)

### Inferences
- The inversions in the literature are two-model anecdotes whose direction depends on the deep architecture (2D spatial CNN loses to RF; temporal Transformer beats RF) and on the input (RF loses more when given SAR-poor S2-only inputs in Pandžić). None uses a consistent input pipeline across more than three model families, so a claim that "in-region and out-of-region rankings do not invert across 27 models with consistent inputs" is not contradicted by any published result and has no published precedent.
- Sen4AgriNet's Transformer/TempCNN collapse (−49 to −53 pp) with no explanation beyond LSTM bidirectionality is the strongest published "deep models fail across regions" datapoint, and it is deep-vs-deep, not deep-vs-tree; it is also a cross-country shift with a different-year component in Scenario 3.

### Gaps
- No paper found reports a Spearman/Kendall rank correlation or any formal stability statistic between in-region and out-of-region model rankings.
- Chen & Liu 2021 may contain a 7-model in-region vs transferred table (which would be the closest precedent to ours); unverified.

## Key question 3: What transfer drops are typical for adjacent-tile vs cross-country transfer?

### Takeaway
Adjacent-region, same-year drops cluster at −0.04 to −0.13 (F1 or OA fraction) for both trees and deep models; cross-country or cross-climate drops without adaptation are −0.15 to −0.55, with calendar-time attention models hit hardest (TimeMatch −0.43 average). Our deep-pixel gaps of −0.06 to −0.14 are squarely in the adjacent-region band.

### Inferences
- The size of the drop is governed more by phenological/climatic distance than by model family: the same PSE+LTAE loses 15 pp within France but 44–66 pp Denmark↔France, and Xie's RF loses almost nothing US→Europe when temperature regimes match.
- An adjacent-tile gap of −0.06 to −0.14 for deep pixel models is unremarkable relative to Orynbaikyzy's RF (−0.04 to −0.09) and Wijesingha's CNN (−0.13); the novelty is in the ranking stability and the deep-over-tree ordering, not in the gap size.
- Logistic regression's zero gap at a low level is consistent with Kluger/SustainBench (LDA beating RF cross-region) and Rusňák (QDA transferring as well as SVM/NN): low-capacity linear models trade in-region accuracy for stability.

### Gaps
- A search-summary claim that random hold-out validation reports 3–5 % higher accuracy than spatially stratified CV was attributed to the Benzhair 2026 review, but the OpenAlex abstract does not contain that statement; treat as unverified.
- No paper reports adjacent-tile transfer drops for XGBoost/LightGBM on xr_fresh-style engineered time-series features; Hoppe 2024 (272 GLCM/statistical features) is the closest but gives only combined F1 by scenario, not in-region vs transferred per model.

## Key question 4: Which benchmark datasets were built for this, and what do their baselines show about model families?

### Takeaway
BreizhCrops (regional folds), TimeMatch (four tiles, leave-one-out), Sen4AgriNet (Catalonia↔France), EuroCropsML (Latvia/Portugal→Estonia few-shot), SustainBench Kenya (three regions), CropGlobe (eight countries) and SwissCrop25 (cross-year) are the purpose-built benchmarks. Only BreizhCrops and SustainBench include a classical baseline; every other benchmark's baseline table is deep-only, so the published benchmark evidence on model families under spatial shift is thin.

### Cited Findings
- BreizhCrops: four NUTS-3 regions of Brittany; train FRH01–03, test FRH04, plus three-fold held-out averaging; RF vs 7 deep models; Transformer/LSTM best (OA 0.80–0.81, kappa 0.74–0.76), RF 0.77–0.78 (kappa 0.69–0.71), TempCNN 0.79–0.80 (kappa 0.73–0.74). — [arXiv 1905.11893](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11893)
- TimeMatch dataset: tiles 33UVP (AT1), 32VNH (DK1), 30TXT (FR1), 31TCJ (FR2), 15 classes; baselines are all PSE+LTAE variants and UDA methods (MMD, DANN, CDAN+E, ALDA, JUMBOT, FixMatch); best UDA without ShiftAug CDAN+E 47.4, TimeMatch 58.2, source-only 39.2, target-trained 82.0 macro-F1. — [arXiv 2111.02682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02682)
- Sen4AgriNet: 2016–2020 Catalonia and France, 42.5 M parcels, LPIS labels; three scenarios (random; Catalonia→France; France 2019→Catalonia 2020); deep baselines only (U-Net, ConvLSTM, ConvSTAR; LSTM, Transformer, TempCNN). — [arXiv 2204.00951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00951)
- EuroCropsML (Reuss et al. 2025, Scientific Data 12:664; arXiv 2407.17458): 706 683 labelled S2 time series, 176 classes, Estonia/Latvia/Portugal, built for transnational few-shot transfer. Companion benchmark (Reuss et al. 2025, arXiv 2504.11022v2): MAML meta-learning slightly beats supervised transfer and SSL; "supervised methods benefit most when pre-trained and fine-tuned on geographically close regions"; "difficulties of knowledge transfer across diverse geographic regions"; no tree/linear baseline and no zero-shot (no fine-tuning) row found. — [arXiv 2407.17458](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17458); [arXiv 2504.11022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11022)
- SustainBench Kenya crop type: regions Bungoma, Busia, Siaya; train one, test two; harmonic-feature baseline macro-F1 ≈0.30; LDA > RF. — [arXiv 2111.04724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04724)
- CropGlobe (Tong & Wang 2025): ~301 884 samples, 8 countries, 2023; nine zero-shot transfer pairs grouped as cross-country, cross-continent, cross-hemisphere; CropNet > Median_MLP > Presto_RF. — [arXiv 2509.03497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03497)
- SwissCrop25: 2019–2025, 73 crop classes + 5 non-crop, leave-one-year-out; TSViT > U-TAE (+12 pp macro-mIoU) > Galileo; "architecture-specific generalization gaps"; temporal not spatial. — [arXiv 2608.09497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09497)
- T³S (Turkoglu et al. 2025/2026, arXiv 2506.12885): thermal-time resampling evaluated on SwissCrop (cross-year) and TimeMatch (Denmark↔France); PSE+LTAE baseline South-of-France→Denmark 31.6±3.3 % vs T³S 38.6±2.1 % (five seeds). — [arXiv 2506.12885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12885)
- AI4FoodSecurity (our dataset): the only published description of a competing entry's spatial-shift behaviour is Albrecht et al.'s PSE+LTAE poster (2nd place, no metrics). Radiant Earth "Spot the Crop" winners (Kiminya, Ferreira, Le) have no published cross-region analysis. — [hereon.de](https://datahub.hcdc.hereon.de/events/data-science-symposium-7/submissions/49); [Zindi](https://zindi.africa/learn/meet-the-winners-of-the-radiant-earth-spot-the-crop-challenges)
- TimeSen2Crop (Weikmann et al. 2021, JSTARS): >1 M pixel S2 series, 16 classes, Austria; no spatial cross-validation protocol or spatially held-out results found. — [JSTARS PDF](https://rslab.disi.unitn.it/web/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/09408357.pdf)
- Rusňák 2023 Table 1 summarises prior transfer studies (best cases): spatial RF 89 % (England/France→10 countries, 4 crops), spatial DTW 69–75 % (Zeeland→Flevoland/Friesland, 10 crops), temporal RF 70–92 %, temporal DNN 96 % (Illinois, 2 crops). — [DOI 10.3390/rs15133414](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)

### Inferences
- Because TimeMatch, Sen4AgriNet, EuroCropsML and SwissCrop25 omit tree and linear baselines, there is no benchmark-level evidence either for or against "trees transfer better"; the deep-only baselines show that calendar-time attention models are fragile across climate zones, which is a separate claim from deep-vs-tree.
- BreizhCrops is the only benchmark whose protocol (train three adjacent regions, test the fourth, same year) matches ours, and its result (deep ≥ RF by 2–5 pp) matches our ordering.

### Gaps
- PASTIS, DENETHOR and AgriFieldNet were not checked in this pass; PASTIS folds are spatially blocked within one S2 tile pair (not cross-region) and DENETHOR is a cross-year split by design, but I did not fetch their papers to confirm baseline tables.
- EuroCropsML benchmark PDF (31 pp, 20 tables) was downloaded but its result tables (Table 4, B.16) could not be parsed reliably from pdftotext; no zero-shot row appears in the grep, consistent with a few-shot-only protocol.

## Key question 5: Are there 2024–2026 "lessons learned" / "pitfalls" papers on comparing deep and classical models for crop mapping, and does any paper explicitly claim deep models overfit the training region while trees transfer better (or the opposite)?

### Takeaway
No paper found makes the explicit numeric claim "deep models overfit the training region and trees transfer better" for pixel time-series crop classification; the nearest are Wijesingha 2024 (2D CNN loses 0.04 macro-F1 to RF under spatial transfer) and a 2025 MODIS cross-year study (RF > TempCNN > L-TAE). The opposite claim, with numbers, appears in Rusňák 2023 (NN/SVM/QDA > RF), Pandžić 2026 (Transformer > RF by 20 pp F1 zero-shot), Gilcher 2021 (U-Net > RF), BreizhCrops and the Chen & Liu snippet. The "pitfalls" literature (Hoppe 2024, Rusňák 2023, Reuss 2025, Benzhair 2026, Xie 2025) focuses on protocol design and warns against RF-only transfer studies and random hold-outs, not against deep models.

### Cited Findings
Claims that trees/linear transfer better (with numbers):
- Wijesingha et al. 2024: RF spatial macro-F1 0.66 vs CNN 0.62; spatio-temporal 0.62 vs 0.58; authors attribute loss to "disparity in environmental, climatic, and crop calendar conditions". — [Kassel](https://kobra.uni-kassel.de/items/1dfa9786-3981-4178-9cac-598b6137126e/full)
- MODIS cross-temporal (IJRS 2025): RF OA 0.917 vs TempCNN 0.886 vs L-TAE 0.859 (cross-year, not cross-region). — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01431161.2025.2527988)
- SustainBench/Kluger: LDA > RF cross-region (linear over tree, not tree over deep). — [arXiv 2111.04724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04724)
- Hoppe 2024: SGD (linear) transferred worst "which could be due to an overfitting of the data"; XGBoost best in short windows; conclusion that no ML model transfers without adaptation. — [DOI 10.3390/rs16091493](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16091493)

Claims that deep/neural transfer better (with numbers):
- Rusňák et al. 2023: "quadratic discriminant analysis, support vector machines, and neural networks exhibiting better generalization potential compared to random forests"; RF "less robust ... sensitivity to overfitting in complex multidimensional feature spaces"; RF was "the only algorithm that showed improvement on the feature reduction test when PCA was applied"; "many transferability studies ... have exclusively used random forest algorithms; hence, the need for multi-algorithm comparison studies is emphasized". Spatial mean OA RF 81.62 vs NN 87.02. — [DOI 10.3390/rs15133414](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)
- Pandžić et al. 2026: naive transfer "notable drops, particularly for RF" (46 % vs 67 % F1); after transfer learning Transformer 91 % vs RF 87 %. — [PDF](https://www.geodetski-vestnik.com/arhiv/70/2/268_Pandzic_at_al.pdf)
- Gilcher & Udelhoven 2021: "convolutional neural networks have potential to generalize better than pixel based models"; U-Net F1 0.61 temporal / 0.77 spatial; geometry-memorisation risk judged small. — [DOI 10.3390/rs13040775](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13040775)
- Sen4AgriNet: LSTM retains 91.55 % wAcc Catalonia→France while Transformer/TempCNN collapse to 37–39 %; no explanation offered beyond bidirectionality. — [arXiv 2204.00951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00951)
- Nyborg TPE / TimeMatch: calendar-time positional encoding is identified as the cause of poor spatial generalisation; removing it or replacing with thermal time recovers +7.9 to +15.3 F1. — [arXiv 2203.09175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09175)
- Tong & Wang 2025: "CropNet achieves the best performance across all transfer settings" (not strictly true for FRA→USA where AlphaEarth_MLP is higher); Presto_RF never best. — [arXiv 2509.03497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03497)

Protocol / pitfalls papers 2024–2026:
- Hoppe et al. 2024: proposes an 18–20 scenario test methodology (spatial, temporal, phenological, quantitative); "simple training on the entire data set is not sufficient to ensure the transferability"; "classification accuracy varies significantly when models are trained in different regions". — [DOI 10.3390/rs16091493](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16091493)
- Xie et al. 2025: "models trained on merged regions or years did worse than the best single-region models, so broader training data does not guarantee better transferability"; temperature similarity is the key predictor of transfer success. — [DOI 10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)
- Reuss et al. 2025 benchmark: prior few-shot evaluations "frequently lacked real-world scenarios"; meta-learning's small accuracy gain "comes at the cost of increased computational demands and training time". — [arXiv 2504.11022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11022)
- Benzhair et al. 2026 (Geocarto Int. 41(1), PRISMA review of 111 studies): "contextual factors such as dataset size, crop complexity, and validation protocols strongly affect reported accuracy, which calls for standardized benchmarks". — [DOI 10.1080/10106049.2026.2690363](https://doi.org/10.1080/10106049.2026.2690363)
- Posch et al. 2026: thousands of configurations across ten algorithms with year-wise CV; "uncertainty from interannual variability is large"; SVM best. — [arXiv 2606.05731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05731)
- Orynbaikyzy 2022: motivates spatial CV and spatial feature selection because classifiers "often overfit to the training sites". — [DOI 10.3390/rs14061493](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14061493)
- Bourriz et al. 2025/2026 (ISPRS Archives XLVIII-4/W17): SVM/RF vs 1D-CNN/HybridSN vs HyperSIGMA/Prithvi on Indian Pines hyperspectral under label reduction (not spatial transfer); RF > SVM at high label counts, HybridSN drops to 65.75 % OA at 1 % labels, foundation model most robust. — [ISPRS Archives](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLVIII-4-W17-2025/69/2026/)

### Inferences
- The "deep overfits the region, trees transfer" narrative is not supported by any 2019–2026 pixel-time-series crop paper with numbers; it appears only for a 2D spatial CNN on Landsat (Wijesingha) and for cross-year MODIS. For temporal pixel models the weight of published evidence (BreizhCrops, Rusňák, Pandžić, Chen & Liu, Gilcher) points the other way or to parity, which is what our corrected result shows.
- The literature's actual pitfall warnings are (a) RF-only transfer studies, (b) random hold-outs instead of spatial CV, (c) calendar-time positional encodings, and (d) assuming more or merged training data improves transfer. A novelty statement can therefore be framed as: first many-model, consistent-input, adjacent-tile audit showing stable rankings and no tree advantage, closing the RF-only gap Rusňák et al. identify.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper specifically titled or framed as "lessons learned / pitfalls in comparing deep and classical crop classifiers" was found; the pitfall content is distributed across discussion sections of the papers above.
- The InSAR-coherence transfer-learning ensemble (CEA 2025, ScienceDirect S0168169925014383) reportedly found 3D U-Net, Transformer and RF all "underperformed" on two 2018 target sites; paywalled, not read. The geo-aware RF paper (RSE 2024, S0034425724006114) is also paywalled.
- The Journal of Big Data 2023 Brittany→Vojvodina Transformer transfer paper reports fine-tuned OA 0.94 but I did not confirm whether it reports a zero-shot row. https://doaj.org/article/c60d8c8763d64ca4ba34dc266eac5446
