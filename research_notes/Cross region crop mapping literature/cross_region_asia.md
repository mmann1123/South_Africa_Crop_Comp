# Cross-region / domain-generalization crop mapping, 2023–2026: China and the rest of Asia

Scope note: all DOIs below were checked against Crossref or OpenAlex metadata on 2026-10-09 unless flagged. Citation counts are Crossref/OpenAlex/Semantic Scholar counts on that date, and they differ by index. Several Elsevier abstracts could not be retrieved (403 on ScienceDirect, no abstract in Crossref/OpenAlex). For those papers the summaries rely on search-engine snippets and are flagged "(snippet-level)". Verify the numbers before quoting them in the manuscript.

## Curated annotated list (17 papers, CEA papers marked [CEA])

1. **Wang, Y., Feng, L., Zhang, Z., Tian, F. (2023).** An unsupervised domain adaptation deep learning method for spatial and temporal transferable crop type mapping using Sentinel-2 imagery. *ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens.* 199, 102–117. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002 (about 82–90 citations, the most-cited Asia-linked UDA crop paper in the window). Method: DACCN, a deep adaptation network (DAN/MMD-style) trained on labelled source samples plus unlabelled target samples. Transfer setting: four U.S. Corn Belt states and two northeastern Chinese provinces, Sentinel-2 2018–2019, tested across space, across time, and across both. Result (snippet-level): OA 0.835–0.922 across transfer cases. The paper also cites an RF trained on 2020 and applied to 2019 in Hetao Irrigation District reaching OA 0.92. Relation to our paper: this is the benchmark "label-free target" UDA design. It needs unlabelled target imagery during training. Our approach needs none and instead relies on model inductive bias. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271623000850)

2. **Wang, Y., Feng, L., Sun, W., Zhang, Z., Zhang, H., Yang, G., Meng, X. Setting: 11 U.S. states as multi-source domains, three Northeast China provinces as separate targets. Relation: multiple source domains as a route to generalization. Photogramm. Remote Sens.* 193, 200–215. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.09.010 (48–54 citations; 2022, seminal). Method: Phenology Alignment Network (PAN), a Siamese deep recurrent network. Two weight-shared branches encode source and target time series, and their hidden-state distributions are aligned so the model adapts with no target labels. Winter crops at Chinese sites in different phenological regions. A CVPRW 2021 version exists. Quantitative gains not retrieved. Relation: makes the phenological (temporal) shift explicit as the domain gap, which is the same mechanism likely at work between our Western Cape tiles. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092427162200257X); [CVPRW 2021 version](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021W/AgriVision/papers/Wang_Phenology_Alignment_Network_A_Novel_Framework_for_Cross-Regional_Time_Series_CVPRW_2021_paper.pdf)

4. **Peng, S., Zhang, L., Xie, R., Qu, Y. (2025).** CSTN: A cross-region crop mapping method integrating self-training and contrastive domain adaptation. *Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf.* 136, 104379. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2025.104379. Method: target pseudo-labels from self-training plus contrastive alignment with class-aware prototypes. Result: +12.29% average F1 over state-of-the-art methods, largest gains for maize. Also works early in the season. Chinese authors; the target regions were not retrieved. Relation: self-training/pseudo-labelling UDA, and it reports in F1, so the number is comparable to our F1-macro framing. — abstract via [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.jag.2025.104379)

5. **Wang, H., Yao, Y., Liu, J., Zhang, X., Zhao, Y., Li, S., Liu, Z., Zhang, X., Zeng, Y. Method: ClimID-UDA. A climate indicator (barycenter of six ERA5-Land variables for light, heat, water and pressure) is computed per region, and the climate discrepancy is used to correct the target SITS before applying the source model. Tested in four regions in China and Europe, with several sensors (Sentinel-2, GF-1), classifiers and years. Result: more than 11% gain in average accuracy. Relation: a label-free, model-agnostic input correction that does not depend on learning domain-invariant features. It is consistent with our view that the gap is driven by environment and phenology. — [Crossref abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.34133/remotesensing.0439); [SPJ](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.34133/remotesensing.0439)

Method: MDACCN, middle fusion of Sentinel-2 and SAR plus local MMD (class-conditional) alignment. Setting: Arkansas (U.S.) as source, Heilongjiang (China) as target. Result: target OA 0.878, Kappa 0.810, macro-F1 0.746, beating supervised DL and traditional ML baselines. ## Q1: Unsupervised domain adaptation for crop mapping in China/Asia

### Takeaway
Chinese groups dominate UDA for crop SITS from 2022 to 2026. The methods are MMD/DAN-style feature alignment (DACCN, MDACCN, PLCM), Siamese phenology alignment (PAN), self-training with contrastive class prototypes (CSTN), and climate-driven input correction (ClimID-UDA). Typical targets are Northeast China (Heilongjiang, Jilin: maize, soybean, rice), Xinjiang (cotton) and the winter-crop regions. Reported gains over no adaptation are about 11–12 points of accuracy or F1. All of these methods need unlabelled target imagery at training time.

### Cited Findings
- DACCN (U.S. Corn Belt and NE China, Sentinel-2 2018–2019) handles spatial, temporal and spatiotemporal transfer with missing target labels. OA 0.835–0.922 across transfer cases (snippet-level) — [Wang et al. 2023, ISPRS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271623000850)
- PAN aligns hidden-state distributions of Siamese recurrent branches for cross-phenological-region winter-crop mapping in China — [Wang Z. et al. 2022, ISPRS](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092427162200257X)
- CSTN (self-training plus contrastive DA) improves average F1 by 12.29% over state-of-the-art methods, especially for maize — [Peng et al. 2025, JAG](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.jag.2025.104379)
- ClimID-UDA corrects the target SITS with a climate-indicator discrepancy and improves average accuracy by more than 11% across four China/Europe regions, multiple sensors and years — [Wang H. et al. 2025, J. Remote Sens.](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.34133/remotesensing.0439)
- MDACCN (Arkansas to Heilongjiang, optical plus SAR with LMMD) reaches target OA 0.878 but macro-F1 only 0.746 — [Feng et al. 2024, Remote Sens.](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/rs16081464)
- PLCM (PSAE + L-TAE + class-aware MMD; U.S. 2022 to Xinjiang 2024) reaches macro-F1 96.56%, with better results on hard classes (cotton). Parcel-level pixel-set aggregation is critical in ablations — [Li et al. 2025, Remote Sens.](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/rs17223762)
- Multi-source UDA from 11 U.S. states to three NE China provinces — [Wang Y. et al. 2022, GIScience & RS](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15481603.2022.2156123)

### Inferences
- Many Chinese UDA studies are U.S. to China, using CDL as a free label source. Few are within-country tile-to-tile transfers like ours, where the domain gap is small and mostly phenological. Our setting is a "near transfer" case that this literature underexplores.
- The OA versus macro-F1 gap in MDACCN (0.878 vs 0.746) supports our choice of macro-F1. Accuracy hides minority-class collapse under shift.
- PLCM uses L-TAE plus parcel aggregation and gets its robustness partly from aggregation. That parallels our finding on field-level aggregation and our sparsemax L-TAE.

### Gaps
- Exact source-only versus adapted numbers for DACCN, PAN and CSTN could not be retrieved (paywall). The CSTN study regions were not confirmed.
- No retrieved UDA paper isolated model capacity or sparsity as a factor.

## Q2: Domain generalization without target data (phenology, knowledge-guided features, sample migration)

### Takeaway
The dominant Chinese route to label-free mapping is not deep DG. It is knowledge-guided sample generation and migration: phenological similarity and DTW to transfer or create labels, then a shallow classifier, usually RF or TrAdaBoost. These pipelines report OA of about 0.85–0.94 in new regions or years. Deep-model DG with temporal-shift augmentation comes mostly from European groups (TimeMatch/ShiftAug). Explicit input normalization (histogram matching, climate correction) appears in Chinese deep pipelines.

### Cited Findings
- KT-SimSeg (CEA 2024) labels pixels automatically through two-layer phenological similarity to winter wheat, refines them, and trains RF. Reported high accuracy across seven regions worldwide and three years (snippet-level) — [Yang G. et al. 2024, CEA](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1016/j.compag.2024.109536)
- FKAN (CEA 2025) predicts samples from historical data. The migrated model reached above 85% accuracy across regions and years (snippet-level) — [Cheng et al. 2025, CEA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169925007951)
- DTW-based temporal sample migration in Heilongjiang gave 95% migration accuracy and 94.13% OA with RF in a year without ground samples — [Zhang S. et al. 2023, IJRS](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/01431161.2023.2192881?fields=abstract)
- CMSTA (phenology-informed instance plus feature transfer, RF) reached OA 0.90 versus 0.44 for feature-only and 0.69 for instance-only transfer, North China Plain — [Zhang J. et al. 2026, SRS](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.srs.2026.100442)
- DSH's histogram-matching module closes the train/test gap. Transfer worked best between sites with similar planting structures — [Che et al. 2024, GIScience & RS](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/15481603.2024.2395700?fields=abstract)
- For context (non-Asian, foundational), ShiftAug temporal-shift augmentation raised source-only target F1 from 39% to 49% — [TimeMatch, Nyborg et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.02682)

### Inferences
- The Chinese DG literature implicitly concludes that a small set of phenologically meaningful features plus a simple classifier transfers well. This is close to our thesis that sparse feature selection (TabNet masks, sparsemax L-TAE attention) aids transfer. Our contribution is that the sparsity is learned inside the model rather than hand-engineered.
- These pipelines are usually single-crop (winter wheat, rice) or a few major crops. Our 5-class winter-crop setting, with spectrally similar cereals (wheat, barley, small-grain grazing), is harder for rule-based phenological similarity.

### Gaps
- No Asian study found applies temporal-shift augmentation for spatial DG of crop types in a peer-reviewed journal.

## Q3: Cross-year (temporal) transfer and its relation to spatial transfer

### Takeaway
Most Chinese label-free work is cross-year: historical samples are reused for a new year in the same place, and it reaches high accuracy (OA above 0.9). Spatial and spatiotemporal transfer are consistently harder. Studies that test both find that phenological offset (planting or transplanting dates) and climate or temperature dissimilarity are the main drivers of degradation.

### Cited Findings
- Multi-year migrated samples beat single-year sources for 2021 mapping in Heilongjiang — [Zhang S. et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01431161.2023.2192881)
- Peak-season-trained DSH transferred best temporally. Spatial transfer worked better between sites with similar planting structures. Spatiotemporal average OA was 88.1% over eight sites in three years — [Che et al. 2024](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/15481603.2024.2395700?fields=abstract)
- Transplanting-period differences reduced the generalization of a transferred rice model across NE China regions — [Yang L. et al. 2023, Remote Sens.](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/rs15092443)
- Temperature similarity between regions was the key predictor of RF transferability. Model accuracy varied little across training years (0.6–2.1%) but by up to 21.6% across training regions — [Xie et al. 2025, IJDE](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211?fields=abstract)
- ClimID-UDA treats cross-region and cross-year shift as the same climate-driven SITS discrepancy — [Wang H. et al. 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.34133/remotesensing.0439)

### Inferences
- Spatial shift dominates temporal shift (21.6 versus 2.1 points in Xie et al.). This supports framing adjacent-tile spatial transfer as a meaningful test even at short distances, and it suggests our tiles differ climatically or phenologically.

### Gaps
- No Asian study found evaluates adjacent-tile spatial transfer at our scale (about 100 km).

## Q4: Do simpler / regularized / feature-selected models transfer better than deeper ones?

### Takeaway
The evidence is mixed and mostly indirect. Several Chinese studies find that simple classifiers (RF, TrAdaBoost) with good features or migrated samples transfer well, and that more training data or domains does not guarantee better transfer. Where deep models beat RF under transfer, they rely on explicit alignment or normalization modules (histogram matching, MMD), not raw capacity. No Asian paper found tests sparsity or attention-based feature selection as a transfer mechanism, so our paper fills that gap.

### Cited Findings
- Models trained on merged regions or years performed worse than the best single-region model, so broader data does not guarantee transferability — [Xie et al. 2025](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211?fields=abstract)
- DSH (deep plus histogram matching) outperformed RF, HRNetV2 and DeepLabV3+ in spatiotemporal transfer. Plain DeepLabV3+ (higher capacity, no matching) was inferior — [Che et al. 2024](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/15481603.2024.2395700?fields=abstract)
- MDACCN outperformed supervised deep learning and traditional ML baselines in the target domain, but only with LMMD alignment — [Feng et al. 2024](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/rs16081464)
- Zero-target-data transfer with RF, XGBoost and TrAdaBoost reached OA 73.88% in the Hexi Corridor — [Mai et al. 2025](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.3390/rs17091494)
- Feature-domain-only transfer collapsed (OA 0.44). Phenology-informed instance plus feature transfer with RF reached 0.90 — [Zhang J. et al. 2026](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.srs.2026.100442)
- A U.S. to Xinjiang UDA built on an L-TAE backbone with parcel-level attention aggregation reached macro-F1 96.56% — [Li et al. 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/rs17223762)

### Inferences
- A defensible framing for the revision: Asian work implicitly favours compact, phenology-focused representations, either hand-built (sample migration, KT-SimSeg) or enforced by alignment losses. Our results show that learned sparsity (TabNet, sparsemax L-TAE) provides a similar inductive bias without target data or alignment losses.
- Field and parcel aggregation appears as a robustness ingredient (PSAE in PLCM) and supports our aggregation finding.

### Gaps
- No direct capacity-versus-transfer ablation (deep versus shallow, sparse versus dense) was found in the Asian 2023–2026 literature.
- India, Southeast Asia (beyond rice products), Central Asia and the Middle East: no peer-reviewed 2023–2026 cross-region crop-type DA/DG papers of comparable visibility were found. Indian work is mostly within-region RF/SVM mapping. Southeast Asian work is mostly rice-extent products (e.g., the 20 m mainland SE Asia rice map, ESSD 2023, trained in Thailand and applied regionally) — [ESSD 2023](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/1501/2023/). This absence is itself a finding and may reflect the limits of the searches.
- Not retrieved or verified: a 2026 ISPRS source-free DA paper for in-season soybean ("CROPS"), seen only in a search snippet. A 2026 *Remote Sensing* paper on Xinjiang cotton cross-year/cross-region transfer (rs18-00980) was seen only as an MDPI PDF link.
