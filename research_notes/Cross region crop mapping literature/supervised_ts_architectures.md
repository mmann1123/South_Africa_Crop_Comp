# Supervised Time-Series Architectures as New Baselines for Out-of-Region Crop SITS Classification (2023–2026)

Scope: supervised architectures trained from scratch (pretrained foundation models such as Presto are covered by a separate researcher). Target protocol: AI4FoodSecurity South Africa, pixel-level monthly composites, about 8–10 time steps x 6 channels, field-level aggregation, train on two tiles, test on an adjacent tile. Research date 2026-10-09. Items marked "(from prior knowledge, not re-verified this session)" should be checked before entering a .bib file.

## Q1. Which recent architectures are used as crop SITS baselines in 2023–2026, and which are reported as strong?

### Takeaway
The crop-SITS baseline canon in 2023–2026 is still TempCNN, L-TAE/PSE-TAE, a vanilla Transformer, LSTM variants and RF, with TSViT and U-TAE as the patch/segmentation standards and Exchanger and Mamba variants as newer patch-based entrants. General-purpose TSC/forecasting models (TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer, MiniRocket) are essentially absent from published crop-SITS benchmarks; I found only one crop paper that ran TimesNet, and none that ran MiniRocket/MultiRocket.

### Cited Findings
- BreizhCrops (Brittany, Sentinel-2, pixel-set/parcel time series) compared RF, TempCNN, MS-ResNet, InceptionTime, OmniscaleCNN, LSTM, StarRNN and Transformer; on held-out region FRH04 (L1C) OA was RF 0.77, TempCNN 0.79, MS-ResNet 0.78, InceptionTime 0.79, OmniscaleCNN 0.77, LSTM 0.80, StarRNN 0.79, Transformer 0.80 (kappa 0.69–0.74). Citation: Rußwurm, M., Pelletier, C., Zollner, M., Lefèvre, S., Körner, M. (2020). BreizhCrops: A Time Series Dataset for Crop Type Mapping. ISPRS Int. Arch. Photogramm. Remote Sens. Spatial Inf. Sci. XLIII-B2-2020, 1545–1551. arXiv:1905.11893 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.11893v2); [ISPRS Archives PDF](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLIII-B2-2020/1545/2020/isprs-archives-XLIII-B2-2020-1545-2020.pdf)
- TimeSen2Crop (single-pixel S2 time series, 1,212,224 samples) benchmarks InceptionTime, MS-ResNet, TempCNN, Transformer, LSTM, StarRNN; the time-convolution models were slightly worse than LSTM and attention models (InceptionTime 81.90%, MS-ResNet 81.80%, TempCNN 81.71% OA). Citation: Weikmann, G., Paris, C., Bruzzone, L. (2021). TimeSen2Crop: A Million Labeled Samples Dataset of Sentinel 2 Image Time Series for Crop-Type Classification. IEEE JSTARS 14, 4699–4708, doi:10.1109/JSTARS.2021.3073965 (DOI from prior knowledge) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351002244_TimeSen2Crop_A_Million_Labeled_Samples_Dataset_of_Sentinel_2_Image_Time_Series_for_Crop-Type_Classification)
- A transformer encoder outperformed LSTM (0.832), StarRNN (0.820), MS-ResNet (0.819), TempCNN (0.816) and OmniScaleCNN (0.807) in an early-season crop mapping study (2026, Computers and Electronics in Agriculture-family ScienceDirect article) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168169926007337)
- TSViT: Tarasiou, M., Chavez, E., Zafeiriou, S. (2023). ViTs for SITS: Vision Transformers for Satellite Image Time Series. arXiv:2301.04944 (CVPR 2023, pp. 10418–10428 per prior knowledge; code github.com/michaeltrs/DeepSatModels, prior knowledge). It tokenizes SITS into non-overlapping spatial and temporal patches, factorizes temporal-then-spatial attention, and reports SOTA "by a significant margin" on three public SITS datasets — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04944)
- Independent 2024/2026 comparison on Munich (48x48 patches): TSViT OA 94.50 / mIoU 83.01 (best), 3D U-Net 94.39 / 81.25, VistaFormer 93.60 / 79.63, Swin UNETR 92.51; Lombardia: TSViT OA 74.74, 3D U-Net 74.46 (best mIoU 58.50). TSViT is 3.26M params but 74.02 GFLOPs (vs VistaFormer 0.57M / 1.97 GFLOPs). Citation: Gatti, M., Gallo, I., Landro, N., Loschiavo, C., Rehman, A.U., Boschetti, M., La Grassa, R. A Comparative Study of Transformer and Convolutional Models for Crop Segmentation from Satellite Image Time Series. arXiv:2412.01944v2. Code: github.com/mattiagatti/sits-crop-segmentation-models — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01944)
- Exchanger: Cai, X., Bi, Y., Nicholl, P., Sterritt, R. (2023). Revisiting the Encoding of Satellite Image Time Series. arXiv:2305.02086, doi:10.48550/arXiv.2305.02086. Self-attention-free "collect–update–distribute" temporal encoder; pre-trained on pixel-set format and fine-tuned for dense prediction; +2.5 mIoU and +8.8 PQ over prior best on PASTIS. No code link on the arXiv page — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02086). (A later BMVC 2023 venue is from prior knowledge, unverified.)
- SITSMamba: Qin, X., Su, X., Zhang, L. (2024). SITSMamba for Crop Classification based on Satellite Image Time Series. arXiv:2409.09673. CNN spatial encoder + Mamba temporal encoder + auxiliary reconstruction branch; motivated by quadratic cost of attention on long series — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09673v2). I did not retrieve its benchmark numbers.
- STU-Mamba (IEEE TGRS, listed 2026): U-shaped selective-SSM segmentation model; 79.10% mIoU on MTLCC, SOTA on MTLCC and PASTIS — [BIT Pure](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/en/publications/stu-mamba-a-u-mamba-with-cross-scale-alignment-and-temporal-adapt/)
- The only crop paper found that ran TimesNet: Cheng, W., Ye, H., Wen, X., Zhang, J., Xu, J., Zhang, F. (2024). Low-Resource Crop Classification from Multi-Spectral Time Series Using Lossless Compressors. arXiv:2405.18119. Baselines: MLP, DLinear, LSTM, DCM, TempCNN, TimesNet, Transformer, Informer on German (Munich), T31TFM-1618 and PASTIS; per-model numbers not recoverable from the HTML; text reports the compressor method averages 2.14% below CNN-based models and 1.30% below Informer on German. PatchTST/iTransformer are only discussed, not run — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18119v2)
- Searches for MiniRocket/MultiRocket/ROCKET applied to Sentinel-2 crop or land-cover SITS returned no papers — [search context: S-Rocket](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03445); [MiniRocket KDD](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447548.3467231)
- A 2026 MDPI Remote Sensing review of SITS benchmark datasets exists (Remote Sens. 18(10):1581) but returned HTTP 403 — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/18/10/1581)

### Inferences
- Adding TimesNet/PatchTST would be a genuine novelty for crop SITS (few or no published results), which makes it informative but also means there is no external reference number to sanity-check against.
- TSViT, U-TAE, Exchanger and the Mamba variants are dense-prediction/patch models; their headline gains are on PASTIS/MTLCC semantic/panoptic segmentation, not pixel/parcel classification of short sequences.

- Could not read the 2026 MDPI benchmark review (403). No crop-SITS results located for PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer, InceptionTime beyond BreizhCrops/TimeSen2Crop, or any ROCKET family member.
- SITSMamba numbers and baselines not retrieved.

## Q2. Input compatibility (pixel 1D ~10 x 6), code/libraries, training cost

### Takeaway
TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer, InceptionTime and the ROCKET family all accept per-pixel multivariate sequences directly and have mature libraries (THUML TSLib, tsai, aeon/sktime). TSViT, U-TAE, Exchanger (in its published form) and SITSMamba require image patches/tiles; only a degenerate 1x1 or "temporal-encoder-only" variant would fit the existing pixel protocol, and that would effectively reduce to a Transformer/L-TAE already in the paper.

### Cited Findings
- TSViT requires spatio-temporal patches ("splits a SITS into non-overlapping spatial and temporal patches") — [arXiv 2301.04944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04944); benchmarked at 48x48 patches with ~74 GFLOPs per sample on Munich — [Gatti et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01944)
- Exchanger's temporal backbone can be pre-trained on pixel-set format, but the reported SOTA is from pairing it with a CV segmentation model for dense prediction on image sequences — [arXiv 2305.02086](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02086)
- THUML Time-Series-Library (TSLib) supports a classification task (UEA, 10 subsets, accuracy) and includes TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer, Informer, Autoformer, Crossformer, etc.; classification run via `--task_name classification --data UEA`. The March 2024 classification leaderboard ranked TimesNet 1st, Non-stationary Transformer 2nd, Informer 3rd; iTransformer/TimeMixer ranked top only for forecasting. An aggregator claims TSLib went maintenance-only in April 2026 (unverified) — [GitHub thuml/Time-Series-Library](https://github.com/thuml/time-series-library); [DeepWiki](https://deepwiki.com/thuml/Time-Series-Library)
- On UEA classification, TimesNet vs PatchTST is dataset-dependent: e.g., CharacterTrajectories 0.9843 vs 0.8982, but Epilepsy 0.5564 vs 0.9891 (from a time-series pretrained-model survey) — [arXiv 2305.10716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10716)
- MiniRocket is up to 75x faster than ROCKET on larger datasets, almost deterministic, keeps essentially the same accuracy, uses only the PPV feature and a small fixed set of 84 base kernels; ROCKET defaults to 10,000 random kernels with a linear classifier. Citation: Dempster, A., Schmidt, D.F., Webb, G.I. (2021). MiniRocket: A Very Fast (Almost) Deterministic Transform for Time Series Classification. KDD 2021, doi:10.1145/3447548.3467231 — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447548.3467231); kernel details per [S-Rocket arXiv 2203.03445](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.03445)
- MultiRocket adds the first-order difference series and more pooling operators to MiniRocket — [Detach-ROCKET arXiv 2309.14518](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.14518)
- Gatti et al. trained patch models with SGD, cosine LR, 200 epochs, batch 32; reported params/FLOPs but no wall-clock — [arXiv 2412.01944](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01944)

### Inferences
- With T≈8–10, PatchTST's patching (typical patch length 8–16) collapses to one or two tokens; it would need patch length 2–3, stride 1–2 to be meaningful, which moves it away from its intended regime. TimesNet's FFT-based period discovery on 8–10 monthly points can find at most one or two periods, so its 2D-reshaping inductive bias is largely inactive. Both still run, but the comparison mostly tests "a bigger Transformer/CNN" rather than their signature mechanism. iTransformer (tokens = channels, 6 tokens) runs fine on short sequences.
- MiniRocket in sktime/aeon requires series length ≥ 9 by default (prior knowledge; check `aeon` docs); with 8 monthly steps it may need padding or the multivariate variant's length handling. This is a concrete implementation check.
- Training cost: MiniRocket transform + RidgeClassifierCV on a few hundred thousand pixels x 10 steps is CPU-minutes; TimesNet/PatchTST on short sequences are small GPU jobs comparable to L-TAE. TSViT would require constructing patch tensors and is the most expensive option (two orders of magnitude more FLOPs than lightweight patch models).
- Library routes: `tsai` (PyTorch, includes MiniRocketPlus, InceptionTimePlus, PatchTST, TSiT); `aeon`/`sktime` (MiniRocket, MultiRocket, Hydra, InceptionTime); THUML TSLib (TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer). The tsai/aeon contents are prior knowledge, not re-verified this session.

### Gaps
- Did not verify current tsai/aeon versions, the MiniRocket minimum-length constraint, or TSViT's official repo contents this session.

## Q3. Evidence on robustness under spatial/geographic transfer; do TimesNet/PatchTST beat TempCNN/L-TAE on crop SITS?

### Takeaway
There is no published evidence that TimesNet or PatchTST beat TempCNN/L-TAE on crop SITS, in-distribution or under transfer. Cross-region evidence consistently shows that model rankings compress or reorder out of region: in BreizhCrops' cross-region evaluation all deep models land within a few points of each other and of RF, the higher-capacity CNNs (InceptionTime, MS-ResNet) are the least stable, and in Sen4AgriNet the Transformer and TempCNN collapse (~37–39% acc) under Catalonia→France transfer while an LSTM holds (~92%). Larger architectures do not reliably buy transfer robustness; feature/representation choice does.

### Cited Findings
- BreizhCrops cross-region evaluation (mean ± SD OA across FRH01/FRH02/FRH04, L1C): Transformer 0.80±0.01, StarRNN 0.80±0.02, TempCNN 0.79±0.02, OmniscaleCNN 0.79±0.01, LSTM 0.79±0.04, RF 0.76±0.02, MS-ResNet 0.72±0.06, InceptionTime 0.71±0.07; the higher-capacity convolutional MS-ResNet and InceptionTime show the largest drop and variance across regions — [arXiv 1905.11893](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.11893v2)
- Sen4AgriNet OAD (parcel-aggregated statistics, 13 bands) Scenario 1 random split: LSTM 88.52, Transformer 88.36, TempCNN 90.08 weighted acc. Scenario 2 (train Catalonia 2019–20, test France 2019): LSTM 91.55, Transformer 39.17, TempCNN 36.90 (weighted F1 91.34 / 31.45 / 30.14). Scenario 3 (France 2019 → Catalonia 2020): LSTM 60.60, Transformer 51.21, TempCNN 52.32. Authors attribute LSTM's robustness to how time is encoded, noting phenological cycles "do not simply shift across time, but are also stretched or shrunk". Patch models (U-Net, ConvLSTM, ConvSTAR) all degraded similarly (Scenario 2 weighted F1 52–58 vs 80–85 in-domain). Caveat: the LSTM had ~60M params vs Transformer ~270k and TempCNN ~719k, and train/test class pixel distributions differed by up to two orders of magnitude. Citation: Sykas, D., Sdraka, M., Zografakis, D., Papoutsis, I. (2022). A Sentinel-2 multi-year, multi-country benchmark dataset for crop classification and segmentation with deep learning. IEEE JSTARS 15, 3323–3339, doi:10.1109/JSTARS.2022.3164771 (venue/DOI from prior knowledge; arXiv:2204.00951) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00951)
- TimeMatch shows deep crop classifiers lose performance across regions because of temporal shift in phenology; on a four-region European dataset TimeMatch beats competing UDA methods by 11% average F1 across five adaptation scenarios. Citation: Nyborg, J., Pelletier, C., Lefèvre, S., Assent, I. (2022). TimeMatch: Unsupervised Cross-Region Adaptation by Temporal Shift Estimation. ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 188, 301–313 (venue from prior knowledge), arXiv:2111.02682; code github.com/jnyborg/timematch — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02682v3)
- Tong & Wang (2025), Invariant Features for Global Crop Type Classification, arXiv:2509.03497 (CropGlobe, ~300k pixels, 8 countries): a small CropNet (4.69M params) matches or beats ResNet50/EfficientNetV2-S/ConvNeXt-Tiny (20–28M) in most transfer settings; deeper networks gave no significant gains; RF with 1D median features beats CropNet with harmonic features in most settings; feature choice (2D median Sentinel-2 features) is the dominant factor for invariance. Example cross-continent OA: FRA→USA 75.19%, USA→FRA 86.97% vs ~99% in-region — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03497v2)
- A 2025 cross-continental UDA paper (PLCM: pixel-set encoder + L-TAE + class-aware MMD) states standard transfer methods only work well when the target shares agro-ecological conditions with the source — [Remote Sensing, doi:10.3390/rs17223762](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17223762)
- The only crop paper running TimesNet (Cheng et al. 2024) reports no cross-region experiments — [arXiv 2405.18119](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18119v2)

### Inferences
- The evidence aligns with the paper's thesis: in BreizhCrops and CropGlobe, extra capacity (InceptionTime, MS-ResNet, ResNet50) does not translate into transfer robustness, and simple models/RF stay competitive. Sen4AgriNet's LSTM result is a counterexample worth citing honestly, though it is confounded by a ~80–200x parameter gap and label-distribution shift.
- The South Africa holdout tile is adjacent (same agro-climatic zone, same season), so shift is milder than Catalonia→France; expect smaller gaps, and differences between modern architectures may fall within seed variance. Report multi-seed means with paired bootstrap CIs (the repo already has `experiments/paired_bootstrap/`).
- Prediction (inference, not evidence): TimesNet/PatchTST will roughly match TempCNN/Transformer in-region and will not exceed L-TAE-S out of region; MiniRocket + ridge is likely to be close to XGBoost on raw time series, since both are high-dimensional fixed-feature + linear/tree classifiers.

### Gaps
- No cross-region crop results found for TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer, TimeMixer, Mamba variants, MiniRocket or TSViT. Exact TimeMatch per-model (PSE-TAE) drop figures not retrieved.

## Q4. Which 1–2 models are most defensible and informative to add?

### Takeaway
Recommended pair: (1) MiniRocket (or MultiRocket+Hydra) with a ridge/logistic classifier as the "strong, cheap, non-deep" baseline, justified by its state-of-the-art standing on general TSC benchmarks and its near-zero tuning cost; and (2) one modern general-purpose deep TSC model run through THUML TSLib on the same pixel sequences, with TimesNet the most defensible choice because it is the reviewer's first-named model and tops TSLib's classification leaderboard (PatchTST as alternative if the reviewer emphasis is transformers, but its patching is ill-suited to ~10 steps). Patch models such as TSViT/U-TAE are less informative here because they change the input modality and are already partially represented by the 3D CNN.

### Cited Findings
- Bake off redux: MultiROCKET+Hydra and HIVE-COTE v2 significantly outperform all other TSC approaches on 112 UCR problems plus 30 new datasets. Citation: Middlehurst, M., Schäfer, P., Bagnall, A. (2024). Bake off redux: a review and experimental evaluation of recent time series classification algorithms. Data Mining and Knowledge Discovery 38(4), 1958–2031, doi:10.1007/s10618-024-01022-1 (DOI from prior knowledge); arXiv:2304.13029 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)
- MiniRocket: near-deterministic, up to 75x faster than ROCKET with essentially the same accuracy — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447548.3467231)
- TimesNet ranks 1st on TSLib classification (UEA) leaderboard as of March 2024; PatchTST/iTransformer/TimeMixer do not place in the classification top three — [TSLib GitHub](https://github.com/thuml/time-series-library)
- TimesNet vs PatchTST on UEA classification is inconsistent across datasets — [arXiv 2305.10716](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10716)
- Smaller/simpler models match deeper ones under cross-continent crop transfer, and feature choice dominates — [Tong & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03497v2); high-capacity CNNs were least stable across BreizhCrops regions — [Rußwurm et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.11893v2)

### Inferences
- Why MiniRocket is informative for the thesis: it is a dense, unselected random-feature expansion (~10k features) fed to a linear model. If it transfers worse than sparse xr_fresh/L-TAE-S, that directly supports "sparse selection outweighs capacity"; if it transfers well, it shows that a cheap fixed-feature model rivals deep nets, which is still consistent with "capacity is not what matters". Either outcome is interpretable. It also complements the paper's XGBoost-on-raw-reflectance experiment.
- Why TimesNet (or PatchTST) is informative: it is the "high-capacity modern" model the reviewer named, with an official reproducible implementation, and has essentially no published crop SITS results, so the result is novel. It should be framed as a test of whether general-purpose TS advances transfer to short, irregular crop sequences.
- Practical recipe (inference): feed the same pixel tensors (N x T x 6) used for TempCNN/L-TAE; same FID-wise split, random_state=42, same class weighting, same field majority vote/mean pooling, and the same 5-seed protocol used for the patch CNNs. For PatchTST use patch_len 2–3/stride 1–2; for TimesNet set top_k=1–2 and small d_model (16–64) to avoid gross overparameterization on ~10 steps. For MiniRocket use the multivariate transform (aeon `MiniRocketMultivariate` or tsai `MiniRocketPlus`) + `RidgeClassifierCV`, padding to length ≥ 9 if required.
- Alternatives if the reviewer insists on a "UTAE/TSViT variant": a lightweight U-TAE (Garnot & Chehata 2021, ICCV, "Panoptic Segmentation of Satellite Image Time Series with Convolutional Temporal Attention Networks", arXiv:2107.07933; prior knowledge) applied to the existing small patches is cheaper than TSViT (74 GFLOPs per 48x48 sample per Gatti et al.). This is a third-choice option because it mostly re-tests the patch-vs-pixel axis already covered by the 3D CNN.
- Recommended bibliography additions (verify DOIs): Dempster et al. 2021 (MiniRocket, KDD); Dempster, Schmidt, Webb 2023 MultiRocket (Data Min. Knowl. Disc. 36:1623–1646, 2022, prior knowledge); Wu, H., Hu, T., Liu, Y., Zhou, H., Wang, J., Long, M. (2023) TimesNet: Temporal 2D-Variation Modeling for General Time Series Analysis, ICLR 2023, arXiv:2210.02186 (prior knowledge); Nie, Y., Nguyen, N.H., Sinthong, P., Kalagnanam, J. (2023) A Time Series is Worth 64 Words: Long-term Forecasting with Transformers (PatchTST), ICLR 2023, arXiv:2211.14730 (prior knowledge); Liu, Y. et al. (2024) iTransformer, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.06625 (prior knowledge); Middlehurst et al. 2024; Tarasiou et al. 2023; Cai et al. 2023; Qin et al. 2024; Sykas et al. 2022; Rußwurm et al. 2020; Tong & Wang 2025; Nyborg et al. 2022.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence on how MiniRocket or TimesNet behave on crop SITS under spatial shift; the recommendation rests on general TSC benchmarks plus the capacity-vs-transfer pattern in BreizhCrops/CropGlobe/Sen4AgriNet.
- Exact bibliographic data for items marked "prior knowledge" (TimesNet, PatchTST, iTransformer, MultiRocket, U-TAE, TSViT CVPR pages, Sen4AgriNet/TimeMatch volumes, Bake-off DOI) were not re-verified this session; run them through Crossref before adding to BibTeX.
