# Cross-region crop-type mapping, spatial transfer, domain adaptation and generalization (2023-2026): Europe, the Americas, Africa

Scope note: DOIs marked "(Crossref-verified)" were resolved against api.crossref.org on 2026-10-09 and the title, authors, venue and year matched. Quantitative results come from the sources linked next to them. Where I only saw an abstract or search snippet, I say so. China/Asia work is out of scope (another researcher covers it), but some multi-continent papers include Chinese sites.

## Europe: EuroCrops / multi-country transfer, TimeMatch and follow-ups, thermal-time normalization, cross-country EU transfer

### Takeaway
Europe has the strongest cross-region benchmarks: TimeMatch (DK/FR/AT tiles), EuroCropsML, and SwissCrop. Two results recur. First, uncorrected cross-region transfer loses a lot of accuracy, mostly because of phenological (temporal) shift. Second, the most effective fixes are cheap, model-agnostic re-indexing of time (thermal time / GDD) or explicit temporal-shift estimation, not bigger models. Transfer between geographically distant regions (e.g., Estonia to Portugal) remains hard for every algorithm tested.

### Cited Findings
- **Nyborg, J., Pelletier, C., Lefèvre, S., Assent, I. (2022). TimeMatch: Unsupervised cross-region adaptation by temporal shift estimation. ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 188, 301-313. DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.04.018 (Crossref-verified).** Seminal 2022 paper, kept because every later European cross-region SITS paper benchmarks against it. Method: estimate the temporal shift of an unlabeled target region with a source-trained model, then adapt with semi-supervised self-training. Setting: Sentinel-2 tiles in Denmark (DK1), mid-west France (FR1), southern France (FR2) and Austria (AT1), five source-to-target tasks. Result: beats competing UDA methods by about 11 F1 points on average across the five tasks. Relevance: shows that cross-region failure is largely a phase shift in phenology, which no amount of within-region capacity fixes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.02682); [ScienceDirect](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271622001216)
- **Nyborg, J., Pelletier, C., Assent, I. (2022). Generalized Classification of Satellite Image Time Series with Thermal Positional Encoding. CVPR EarthVision workshop, arXiv:2203.09175.** Replaces calendar-day positional encoding with growing-degree-day (thermal) encoding. Evaluated on the TimeMatch data (four European regions with daily temperature), training on multiple regions instead of single-source UDA. Relevance: an early "normalize time by GDD" approach; a domain-generalization (no target labels, no target adaptation) alternative to UDA. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09175)
- **Turkoglu, M.O., Ledain, S., Lauber, T., Zweidler, J., Aasen, H. (2025). T³S: Think in Thermal Time for Generalizable Crop Mapping from Satellite Image Time Series. arXiv:2506.12885 (Agroscope/ETH Zurich; a related EGU25 abstract by Turkoglu and Aasen is "Cross-Year Crop Mapping with Thermal Calendar", DOI 10.5194/egusphere-egu25-2532).** Method: re-index Sentinel-2 observations by cumulative GDD (base 0 °C), split the cGDD range into 24 bins, and keep the least-cloudy image per bin. Model-agnostic; tested with U-TAE, PSE+L-TAE and the Galileo foundation model. Data: the new SwissCrop dataset (2021-2023, 50 crops, leave-one-year-out) and the TimeMatch cross-region benchmark. Results: on SwissCrop with U-TAE, accuracy rises from 71.2% to 77.0% and calibration error (ECE) falls from 5.1% to 1.1%; with Galileo, accuracy rises from 70.1% to 74.7%. Cross-region TimeMatch FR→DK: accuracy 38.6% vs 31.6% for PSE+L-TAE, F1 36.4 vs 31.8. DK→FR: accuracy 57.7% vs 36.5%, F1 59.2 vs 36.3. Relevance: the absolute numbers show how severe cross-region collapse is (a PSE+L-TAE gets about 31-37% accuracy across DK/FR), and that the fix is input re-indexing, which helps both small and foundation-model backbones. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12885v5)
- **Reuss, J., Macdonald, J., Becker, S., Richter, L., Körner, M. (2025). The EuroCropsML time series benchmark dataset for few-shot crop type classification in Europe. Scientific Data 12, 664. DOI 10.1038/s41597-025-04952-7 (Crossref-verified).** A Sentinel-2 parcel-level, analysis-ready few-shot benchmark built on EuroCrops (pre-training in one region, fine-tuning in another; Latvia, Estonia and Portugal as the core regions). Scenarios include shared and non-shared classes between pre-training and fine-tuning regions. Finding: transfer between geographically distant regions such as Estonia and Portugal "poses significant challenges to all investigated algorithms". Cross-year shift is treated as a separate challenge. Relevance: European evidence that pre-training on distant domains does not reliably transfer, and an example of the label-space mismatch problem. — [Nature Sci. Data (search snippet)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04952-7); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390947794_The_EuroCropsML_time_series_benchmark_dataset_for_few-shot_crop_type_classification_in_Europe)
- **Reuss, J., Macdonald, J., Becker, S., Gikalo, ..., Schultka, ..., Richter, L., et al. (2026). Benchmarking for practice: Few-shot time-series crop-type classification on the EuroCropsML dataset. ISPRS Open J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 19, 100117. DOI 10.1016/j.ophoto.2026.100117 (Crossref-verified).** A Transformer is trained on a source region and evaluated on a geographically distinct target. Pre-training on geographically or spectrally distant data strengthens the domain shift, "potentially degrading overall accuracy but also regularizing against dominance by specific classes". Self-supervised pre-training helps minority classes most. Relevance: more pre-training data or capacity from far-away regions is not a free win, which agrees with the paper's capacity-versus-transfer finding. — [ScienceDirect (snippet only)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667393226000037)
- **Hoppe, H., Dietrich, P., Marzahn, P., Weiß, T., Nitzsche, C., Freiherr von Lukas, U., Wengerek, T., Borg, E. (2024). Transferability of Machine Learning Models for Crop Classification in Remote Sensing Imagery Using a New Test Methodology: A Study on Phenological, Temporal, and Spatial Influences. Remote Sensing 16(9), 1493. DOI 10.3390/rs16091493 (Crossref-verified).** Germany, Sentinel-2. Compares RF, XGBoost, SGD, MLP, SVM and majority voting in 18 spatial-transfer test scenarios. Transferred F1 falls as the training window lengthens, because phenological phases differ between regions: 82% (XGBoost, single date), 72% (XGBoost, half season), 61% (majority vote, full season). Relevance: shows that more temporal features can *hurt* spatial transfer by encoding region-specific phenology. This is indirect support for sparse feature selection. XGBoost was the best single model. — [MDPI/DLR PDF](https://elib.dlr.de/205064/1/remotesensing-16-01493-with-cover.pdf)
- **Orynbaikyzy, A., Gessner, U., Conrad, C. (2022). Spatial Transferability of Random Forest Models for Crop Type Classification Using Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2. Remote Sensing 14(6), 1493. DOI 10.3390/rs14061493 (Crossref-verified; 2022, included as a heavily cited baseline).** Germany. Optical+SAR RF reached F1 0.85 in the training sites and at most 0.79 in unseen areas. SAR-only RF had the smallest transfer loss (average F1 loss 0.04). Relevance: puts a number on the in-sample-versus-transfer gap for tree models and shows that feature choice drives transferability. — [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359352212_Spatial_Transferability_of_Random_Forest_Models_for_Crop_Type_Classification_Using_Sentinel-1_and_Sentinel-2)
- **Rusňák, T., Kasanický, T., Malík, P., Mojžiš, J., Zelenka, J., Sviček, M., et al. (2023). Crop Mapping without Labels: Investigating Temporal and Spatial Transferability of Crop Classification Models Using a 5-Year Sentinel-2 Series and Machine Learning. Remote Sensing 15(13), 3414. DOI 10.3390/rs15133414 (Crossref-verified).** Slovakia (5-year Sentinel-2 series), testing temporal and spatial transfer of ML classifiers with no target labels. I could not open the full text (HTTP 403), so I have no numbers. The title and DOI are verified. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/13/3414)
- **Mohammadi, S., Belgiu, M., Stein, A. (2024). A source-free unsupervised domain adaptation method for cross-regional and cross-time crop mapping from satellite image time series. Remote Sens. Environ. 314, 114385. DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385 (Crossref-verified).** ITC (Netherlands) authors, US Landsat time series (corn/soybean/other). Adapts a source-pretrained model with a mutual-information loss and no access to the source data. Average macro-F1 gains with CNN, Transformer and LSTM backbones are +12.9, +7.1 and +5.8 points cross-time, and +20.1, +12.5 and +8.8 points cross-region. Relevance: the largest cross-region gains go to the CNN backbone, which suggests that different architectures suffer different amounts of unadapted transfer loss. — [UTwente record / search summary](https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/a-source-free-unsupervised-domain-adaptation-method-for-cross-reg/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425724004115)
- **Wang, ..., (2025). Unsupervised Cross-Regional and Cross-Year Adaptation by Climate Indicator Discrepancy for Crop Classification (ClimID-UDA). Journal of Remote Sensing 5, 0439. DOI 10.34133/remotesensing.0439 (Crossref-verified).** Corrects target-domain time series using climate-indicator differences between domains. Tested in 4 regions in China and Europe, across sensors, classifiers and years. A climate/phenology-normalization approach in the same family as thermal time. No numbers retrieved. — [SPJ](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.34133/remotesensing.0439)

### Inferences
- European evidence agrees that the main obstacle to cross-region transfer is phenological or temporal misalignment. This supports the paper's argument that models which select a small number of robust temporal-spectral cues (TabNet's sparse attention, sparsemax-gated L-TAE) are less exposed to region-specific timing than dense models that use the whole sequence. Hoppe et al. (2024) is the most direct empirical support: more of the season meant worse transfer.
- The paper's adjacent-tile Western Cape setting is "mild" relative to FR↔DK or EE↔PT. That is worth saying when positioning results, because published drops scale with the size of the shift.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a 2023-2026 peer-reviewed PASTIS-specific domain-shift study or a Sen4Map transfer study within the tool budget.
- I found no 2023+ peer-reviewed follow-up to TimeMatch by the original authors beyond the 2022 thermal-encoding workshop paper.
- The full-text numbers for Rusňák et al. (2023) and ClimID-UDA were not retrieved.

## Americas: US Corn Belt / CDL cross-state and cross-year transfer, Brazil/Argentina, Canada

### Takeaway
US work uses CDL as abundant source labels and focuses on cross-year and cross-region transfer, through source-free UDA, deep adaptation networks, and large HLS transformers trained across many years. South American work increasingly transfers US-trained models to Argentina and Brazil with no local labels. Reported adaptation gains are large (about 3 to 20 macro-F1 points), but baselines vary a lot from study to study.

### Cited Findings
- **Wang, Y., Feng, L., Zhang, Z., Tian, F. (2023). An unsupervised domain adaptation deep learning method for spatial and temporal transferable crop type mapping using Sentinel-2 imagery. ISPRS J. Photogramm. Remote Sens. 199, 102-117. DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002 (Crossref-verified).** Adapts a Deep Adaptation Network (MMD-based) to Sentinel-2 crop time series. Sites are four US Corn Belt states plus two NE China provinces, 2018-2019, with cross-space, cross-time and combined transfer. Against plain DNN, RF and SVM, adapted OA ranges from 0.835 to 0.922. In the large-area case, macro-F1 is 92.6% adapted vs 89.2% unadapted. Relevance: RF and SVM are included as baselines, and the gain comes from feature alignment rather than capacity. — [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271623000850)
- **Zhang, H.K., Shen, Y., Zhang, ..., Li, ..., Yang, ..., Xu, ... (2025). Robust and timely within-season conterminous United States crop type mapping using Landsat Sentinel-2 time series and the transformer architecture. Remote Sens. Environ. 329, 114950. DOI 10.1016/j.rse.2025.114950 (Crossref-verified).** HLS time series, CDL labels 2016-2022, and a transformer trained with simulated cross-year acquisition-date variation (augmentation) for robustness when applied to new years. The authors name domain adaptation and phenology normalization as future work. Relevance: in the CDL-rich US, the response to shift is scale plus augmentation. That differs from the label-scarce setting of the paper. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425725003542); [USDA NASS PDF](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Research_and_Science/Cropland/docs/In-Season%20Crop%20Mapping%20Transformer-compressed.pdf)
- **Xu, ..., Zhang, ... (2026). An open-set domain adaptation approach for cross-region crop mapping. Int. J. Remote Sens. 47(10), 4304-4326. DOI 10.1080/01431161.2026.2649953 (Crossref-verified).** Corn Belt to Great Plains, Sentinel-2, USDA Crop Sequence Boundaries labels 2019-2022. Handles target-only classes (label-space mismatch). Gains over direct transfer are +37.29% OA and +0.1919 macro-F1. Relevance: shows how badly naive transfer can fail when class sets differ across regions. — [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01431161.2026.2649953)
- **Kwon, ..., Ryu, ..., Ciais, P., Makowski, D., Choi, ..., Feng, ... (2026). Enabling maize mapping in double-cropping regions of Argentina and Brazil without ground reference data by leveraging multiple satellite platforms. Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf. 152, 105489. DOI 10.1016/j.jag.2026.105489 (Crossref-verified).** A US-trained classifier on Sentinel-2 NDVI time series generates maize/non-maize labels in Argentina and Brazil, then Landsat-8 features are used for wall-to-wall mapping. Relevance: a no-target-label, cross-hemisphere transfer setup in the Americas. Accuracy numbers were not retrieved. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S156984322600405X)
- **Tong, X.-Y., Wang, S. (2025/2026). Invariant Features for Global Crop Type Classification. arXiv:2509.03497 (v3, Apr 2026; not yet peer reviewed).** CropGlobe: about 300k Sentinel-2 pixels from 8 countries (Argentina, Australia, Belgium, China, France, Netherlands, UK, USA; 2023 labels). Tests cross-country, cross-continent and cross-hemisphere transfer. Overall accuracy: FRA→BEL 96.99 (best baseline) vs 98.24 (CropNet+aug); USA→FRA 81.42 vs 87.76; FRA→USA 79.53 vs 80.37; USA→AUS 77.01 vs 88.45; FRA→ARG 53.24 vs 77.03. Performance falls as the shift grows (country → continent → hemisphere). A 1.04M-parameter spectral-temporal CNN beats ResNet50 (23.6M), EfficientNetV2-S and ConvNeXt-T under large shifts (FRA→USA 73.3% vs 60.9% for ResNet50). The authors state that "inductive bias toward joint spectral-temporal structure is more critical for transfer than model scale or pretraining". Presto and AlphaEarth embeddings drop to about 28-46% in cross-continent and cross-hemisphere settings. Neural models beat RF on median features. Relevance: this is the strongest recent evidence that *capacity does not buy transfer*, which matches the paper's thesis. It also cuts against tree models, and it is a preprint. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03497)
- Source-free in-season soybean mapping (CROPS) across six target sites in the USA, China, Brazil and Argentina reports average end-of-season macro-F1 of 92.44% and IoU 79.73%. The authors note that multi-crop reference data for Argentina were not available. Bibliographic status is unclear: Crossref returns only an EGU26 abstract (Tang et al., DOI 10.5194/egusphere-egu26-8435), and the ScienceDirect PII suggests an ISPRS JPRS 2026 article. Verify before citing. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271626003254)

### Inferences
- In the Americas, cross-region gains come mostly from adaptation methods that need unlabeled target imagery and deep backbones. The paper's setting (no target labels, no target adaptation, just model choice and aggregation) is a domain-*generalization* setting that the US literature rarely isolates. That is a gap the paper can claim to address.

### Gaps
- No Canada-specific 2023-2026 cross-region crop-type transfer paper was found within budget (AAFC Annual Crop Inventory transfer studies may exist).
- I found no Kerner-lab or Lin et al. 2023+ CDL cross-state paper with verifiable details. The early-season "no current-year labels" US literature (e.g., Lin et al. 2022) was not re-verified.
- The full CROPS citation is unconfirmed.

## Africa / smallholder: CropHarvest, NASA Harvest, few- or no-label mapping, AI4FoodSecurity

### Takeaway
African work is dominated by label scarcity. Pretrained lightweight time-series models (Presto) and global systems (WorldCereal) are the main 2023-2025 responses, and existing cropland maps are often inaccurate in SSA. I found no peer-reviewed results paper from the AI4FoodSecurity challenge. The South Africa track is mostly reused as one of several datasets in foundation-model or field-boundary benchmarks, which leaves room for the paper's contribution.

### Cited Findings
- **Kerner, H., Nakalembe, C., Yang, A., Zvonkov, I., McWeeny, R., Tseng, G., Becker-Reshef, I. (2024). How accurate are existing land cover maps for agriculture in Sub-Saharan Africa? Scientific Data 11, 486. DOI 10.1038/s41597-024-03306-z (Crossref-verified).** Assesses 11 public land-cover maps against statistically rigorous reference data in 8 SSA countries. It reviews earlier findings that cropland accuracy was below 65% for four maps and that seven maps fully agreed on only 2.15% of Africa. Reference data are released for evaluation only. Relevance: global products do not transfer well to African agriculture. That motivates locally evaluated, out-of-region testing like the paper's. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11087537/)

Relevance: the operational reference for global and agro-ecological-zone transfer, including Africa. — [ESSD/IIASA](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/18979)

- The paper fills a gap. It is apparently the first peer-reviewed out-of-region analysis on the AI4FoodSecurity South Africa data that uses Sentinel-2 only and reports field-level results, while other uses of these data treat it as one of many datasets.

- No commercial-to-smallholder transfer study was found.

## Benchmarks / position papers on geographic generalization, DA reviews, and simple-vs-deep evidence

### Takeaway
New global benchmarks (Fields of The World, CropGlobe, EuroCropsML, the IGARSS harmonized crop set) make geographic generalization an explicit test, and all of them report degradation that scales with the size of the shift. On simple versus deep models the evidence is mixed. Tree and feature-selected models often transfer comparably (Hoppe 2024: XGBoost best; Orynbaikyzy 2022: RF with careful features). The strongest recent deep-model evidence (Tong & Wang) favors *small, inductive-bias-rich* networks over large or pretrained ones. Neither line supports "bigger is better" for transfer.

### Cited Findings
- **Kerner, H., Chaudhari, S., Ghosh, A., Robinson, C., Ahmad, A., Choi, E., et al. (2025). Fields of The World: A Machine Learning Benchmark Dataset for Global Agricultural Field Boundary Segmentation. Proc. AAAI 39, 28151-28159. DOI 10.1609/aaai.v39i27.35034 (Crossref-verified).** 24 countries, 4 continents, 70,462 samples, multi-date Sentinel-2. Mean field size ranges from about 1.6 ha (SSA) to 121 ha (North America). Models pretrained on diverse FTW data beat others zero-shot and fine-tuned on held-out countries. South Africa (AI4FoodSecurity-derived) test: pixel IoU 0.82, precision 0.85. — [AAAI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/35034); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16252v2)
- Li, ..., Liu, ..., Huo, ..., Li, ..., Yin, ..., Ma, ... (2025). A Class-Aware Unsupervised Domain Adaptation Framework for Cross-Continental Crop Classification with Sentinel-2 Time Series. Remote Sensing 17(22), 3762. DOI 10.3390/rs17223762 (Crossref-verified). Cross-continental UDA with class-aware alignment. Details not retrieved. — [MDPI DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17223762)
- Review sources: Miller et al., "Deep Learning for Satellite Image Time Series Analysis: A Review" (arXiv:2404.03936, 2024), which lists TimeMatch and other SITS UDA methods; and "Domain Adaptation for Big Data in Agricultural Image Analysis: A Comprehensive Review" (arXiv:2506.05972, 2025). I found no dedicated peer-reviewed 2023-2025 review of DA for crop *type* mapping. — [arXiv 2404.03936](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.03936); [arXiv 2506.05972](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05972)
- Simple vs deep. **For trees and feature selection:** XGBoost was the best single transferable model, and longer feature windows hurt transfer ([Hoppe et al. 2024](https://elib.dlr.de/205064/1/remotesensing-16-01493-with-cover.pdf)). SAR-only RF lost only 0.04 F1 when transferred ([Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/359352212_Spatial_Transferability_of_Random_Forest_Models_for_Crop_Type_Classification_Using_Sentinel-1_and_Sentinel-2)). **For small deep models:** a 1M-parameter CNN beats 20-28M-parameter ResNet/ConvNeXt and GFM embeddings under large shifts, and neural nets beat RF on the same features ([Tong & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03497)). **Pretraining can hurt:** distant-region pre-training strengthens domain shift ([Reuss et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667393226000037)). **Adapted deep models beat RF/SVM** once UDA is applied ([Wang et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271623000850)).

### Inferences
- Taken together, the evidence supports a nuanced claim the paper can make. Transfer depends more on inductive bias, feature parsimony and temporal alignment than on capacity. Sparse feature selection (TabNet, sparsemax L-TAE) is one such inductive bias, and field-level aggregation is another cheap robustness lever. Neither is well studied in the cross-region literature, which mostly concentrates on UDA.
- None of the retrieved papers tests sparsity or attention-sparsity as a transfer mechanism directly, or isolates field-level aggregation under spatial shift. Both are novelty claims for the paper, made with appropriate hedging.

### Gaps
