# Enhanced Crop Classification Through Integrated Optical and SAR Data: A Deep Learning Approach for Multi-Source Image Fusion

**Citation:** Liu, N.; Zhao, Q.; Williams, R.; Barrett, B. (2024). Enhanced crop classification through integrated optical and SAR data: a deep learning approach for multi-source image fusion. *International Journal of Remote Sensing*, 45(19-20), 7605-7633. (Published online 17 Jul 2023.) DOI: 10.1080/01431161.2023.2232552

## Objectives

Develop a patch-based, multi-temporal deep-learning framework for county-level annual crop mapping in Bei'an County, Northeast China, that fuses time-series Sentinel-1 polarimetric SAR features with Sentinel-2 multispectral reflectance. The headline crops are maize, soybean, and wheat (plus an "other crops" catch-all). The authors propose a novel architecture, **3D-ConvSTAR**, combining a three-layer 3D-CNN front end with a bidirectional convolutional recurrent (ConvSTAR) module, explicitly to (a) exploit joint spatio-temporal structure, (b) handle severe class imbalance (maize/soybean dominate; wheat is 2.9% of sown area), and (c) test whether m-chi polarimetric decomposition and optical fusion beat SAR backscatter alone.

## Methods

Inputs: 23 Sentinel-1B SLC acquisitions (dual-pol, processed in SNAP to backscatter dB and m-chi compact-polarimetric decomposition: surface `Ps`, double-bounce `Pd`, volume `Pv` scattering) and 3 cloud-free Sentinel-2A acquisitions (BOA reflectance; Bands Red, Red Edge, SWIR selected for soybean/maize separability), all resampled to 10 m and collocated into a time-series sequence, global min/max normalized. Square image patches of 11x11 centered on labelled crop parcels (final tensor 11x11x26x3, width x height x sequence x channel) serve as model input — a scene-classification-style patch approach. Patches spanning multiple polygons or containing missing values were dropped.

Architectures compared: TCNN (1D temporal CNN, Pelletier et al.), 3D-CNN (Ji et al.), hybrid 3D-2D CNN (Roy et al.), ConvSTAR (Turkoglu et al.), and the proposed 3D-ConvSTAR. Training: Adam, cross-entropy, lr 0.001, weight decay 1e-4, batch 128, early stopping, best-val-accuracy checkpoint. Data-augmentation strategies benchmarked against an imbalanced baseline: oversampling+rotation/flip, inverse-frequency (balanced-loss) weighting, and mix-up. Model interpretation via softmax prediction-score distributions and vanilla-backpropagation saliency maps.

**Evaluation protocol.** Ground truth came from a 2017 CAAS field survey of 21,257 parcels. Crucially, the authors recognize spatial autocorrelation: pixels within the same parcel are highly correlated, so each crop polygon is treated as an indivisible entity and polygons are further grouped on a **10 km grid** so that all parcels in a grid cell go wholesale into one of train/val/test. Ten percent of ground samples are stratified-split 60/20/20 train/val/test. This is meaningfully better than pooled-pixel random splitting and approaches **field/grid-disjoint k-fold within a single scene** — it blocks same-parcel and short-range adjacent-pixel leakage. However, the primary accuracy table and confusion matrix are still computed on a held-out partition of the **same Bei'an 2017 tile and year**; this is in-region (grid-disjoint) validation, NOT a spatially disjoint holdout tile or a cross-year test. Generalization across the broader county is partially probed by reporting per-site accuracy at three randomly selected sub-locations (Sites A/B/C) and by producing a full-county inference map, but no independent region or year is held out. The authors explicitly list interannual and spatial transferability as unmeasured future work.

## Key Findings

- **3D-ConvSTAR wins overall:** OA 91.7%, Kappa 85.7%, mean F1 87.7% with Optical+m-chi inputs — best among all five architectures.
- **Polarimetric decomposition beats backscatter:** m-chi features lifted OA/Kappa substantially over raw backscatter for every model; the gain was largest for the simplest temporal model (TCNN: OA +>20%, Kappa +>30%), shrinking for spatio-temporal models that already extract structure.
- **Optical fusion mainly rescues the minority class:** Adding a few Sentinel-2 acquisitions raised wheat F1 dramatically (3D-ConvSTAR wheat F1 90.9% with Optical+m-chi vs 69.4% backscatter, 81.8% m-chi) while maize/soybean improved <10% (already well-trained by abundant labels). Multispectral info "contributes mostly to the enhancement of mapping minority crops."
- **Per-class confusion matrix (OOS within scene):** maize 96.6%, soybean 94.9%, wheat 96.1% correctly classified; "other crops" only 86.9% (11.2% leaked to soybean) — the heterogeneous catch-all class is the weak point, paralleling our setting.
- **Augmentation is model-dependent:** oversampling generally helped (TCNN mean F1 64.8%->83.5%); mix-up boosted majority crops but hurt minority crops; no single augmentation dominated. Authors conclude augmentation does not reliably overcome imbalance.
- **Interpretability:** prediction-score confidence ~90% for maize/soybean/wheat but ~71% for "other crops", consistent with its lower F1; saliency maps show coherent field shapes for confident classes and scattered importance for the heterogeneous class.
- 3D-ConvSTAR's cost: a much larger parameter count and longer training than baselines — an explicit efficiency caveat.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This is one of the closest methodological neighbors to our study and a productive point of contrast. Three connections stand out.

First, the **grid-disjoint, polygon-as-entity splitting** is a more rigorous cousin of our FID-disjoint splitting — both authors independently identify same-field pixel correlation as the leakage threat and block it. We can cite this as precedent that field/grid-wise splitting is the minimum responsible standard, while noting that even this paper stops at in-region validation and does not take the further step we take: a spatially disjoint holdout tile and the resulting model re-ranking. Their own listed future work ("model's generalization needs further assessment based on interannual and spatial transferability") is precisely the gap our manuscript fills.

Second, their **minority-class result mirrors ours.** Maize/soybean (their lucerne/medics analogue — the data-abundant majority) are easy and barely move with extra signal, while wheat and the heterogeneous "other crops" class are where models separate and where balanced metrics matter. Their use of macro-mean F1, per-class F1, Kappa, and confusion matrices — not just OA — is the balanced-evaluation posture we adopt and can cite approvingly.

Third, this paper is a strong **example of the dense-DL paradigm we expect to overfit out-of-sample.** 3D-ConvSTAR is a high-capacity spatio-temporal-recurrent net with a large parameter count that wins by 1-4 points on an in-region partition. Our thesis predicts exactly such models would lose rank on a disjoint holdout. Because Liu et al. never test that, their result is consistent with, but does not falsify, our claim — and we can use them to argue that the field's "best model" verdicts are protocol-bound. Their finding that m-chi gains shrink for already-structured models is a useful nuance: added input richness substitutes for, and is partially redundant with, model capacity.

Finally, the SAR/optical fusion design contrasts with our deliberately **optical-only** feature set (`B2`, `B6`, `B11`, `B12`, `EVI`, `hue`). Their evidence that optical mainly helps minority crops suggests that in our optical-only setting the burden of minority-class skill falls entirely on the temporal/spectral engineering and the model's inductive bias — reinforcing why sparse feature-selecting models may transfer better than dense ones when the signal is thinner.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **In-region validation, not a disjoint holdout.** Despite excellent anti-leakage splitting, all headline metrics are on a held-out partition of the same Bei'an 2017 tile/year. Grid-disjoint k-fold within one scene is stronger than pooled splitting but still flatters models relative to a truly independent region — the exact gap our study targets. No cross-year or cross-region test is performed.
- **Patch-based granularity bakes in spatial context** that may not transfer; 11x11 patches centered on parcels can still share neighborhood structure across the grid boundary, and the chosen 10 km grid size is a tunable that affects effective separation.
- **"Best model" is protocol-contingent.** The 1-4 point margin by which the high-capacity 3D-ConvSTAR wins is well within the range that our work shows can reverse under a disjoint holdout; the paper offers no evidence the ranking is transfer-stable.
- **Class imbalance handled but not solved.** The authors themselves conclude augmentation (oversampling, balanced loss, mix-up) does not reliably fix minority-class performance; the "other crops" class remains a persistent failure (F1 ~52-74%), and its heterogeneity is a confound for any single-label scheme.
- **Saliency-map interpretation is weak by the authors' admission** (11x11 spatial extent too small to localize importance reliably) — a useful caution for any XAI claims we make.
- **Generalization explicitly unmeasured.** Interannual and spatial transferability are deferred to future work, so the paper's strong numbers say nothing about robustness to the domain shift that defines real deployment.
