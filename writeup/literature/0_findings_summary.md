# Crop-Classification Literature: Project-Centric Findings Synthesis

Internal decision-support synthesis for the Western Cape Sentinel-2 crop-classification study. Organized around our modeling choices, evaluation bar, and concrete action items — **not** a general review (that is [general_literature_review.md](<general_literature_review.md>)). Every number below is tagged by the evaluation protocol that produced it; numbers from different tiers are not comparable.

**Our study, in one line:** twenty-three classical / deep / patch models on multi-temporal Sentinel-2 optical-only imagery (`B2 B6 B11 B12 EVI hue`, 10 months, May/June excluded), benchmarked twice — in-region field-wise CV on the training tiles (`34S_19E_258N`, `34S_19E_259N`) and on a spatially disjoint holdout tile (`34S_20E_259N`). Primary metric macro-F1; also Kappa, weighted-F1, log-loss. Five crops, lucerne/medics-dominated (43% train, 56% holdout). Central claim: in-region validation misranks models; spatial transfer reorders them; sparse, feature-selecting/tree-like inductive bias (gradient-boosted trees, TabNet via sparsemax masks, and our sparse-attention L-TAE-S) transfers, dense temporal/patch nets overfit. Active manuscript: `writeup/tgrs-article.tex` (+ `tgrs-supplement.tex`).

## 1. Every paper sorted by evaluation protocol

**Tier 1 — true spatial transfer (different tile/region/zone, or cross-year/cross-sensor; no leakage of place):**
- `pankajakshan` — strongest in corpus: params fixed on one HSI dataset, applied *without retraining* to disjoint, cross-sensor, cross-region, open-set scenes (KSC, Botswana, PRISMA/Piedmont). 3D-CNN 97–99% in-region → "very poor" on transfer; sparse SVM+kernel transfers (AA/κ ~97–99%).
- `butsko2025worldcereal`: the only crop study in the corpus that reports the *same models* under a pooled random 80/20 point split, a whole-country geographic holdout (zero target labels), and a cross-year (2021) holdout, with macro-F1 for crop type. Compares CatBoost (expert features, cropland only; raw series for both tasks) against Presto pre-trained and randomly initialized. Crop-type geographic macro-F1 0.563 (raw CatBoost) to 0.650 (pre-trained Presto), with a near-uniform random→geographic drop of ~0.16–0.18 for every model. **Caveats:** point/pixel-level only (no field aggregation); global multi-sensor inputs (S1+S2+DEM+weather, 255K crop-type points, 8 classes); South Africa not held out; single runs, no CIs; Presto's pre-training corpus saw unlabeled imagery of the held-out countries, so the holdout withholds labels, not imagery; the random split may leak within parcels (treat it as Tier-3-like).
- `cropformer2023` — cross-region transfer + in-season cross-year. Cross-region OA ~62–64% (pre-trained) vs RF 25.2%. **Caveat: see §6 — no independent reproduction, no code, GaoFen-1 not Sentinel-2.**
- `jin_gee` — cross-landscape national holdout + external ground crop-cuts. Maize OA 79% Tanzania → 63% Kenya (visible transfer penalty). Binary-ish task, SAR fusion.
- `rustowicz2019` — cross-*difficulty* (Germany vs Ghana/South Sudan); deep-net edge over RF evaporates/reverses in smallholder regime (Ghana RF 62.4 ≥ best deep 57.3 macro-F1). Confounds region + data volume + field size; not a same-region disjoint tile.
- `mann_ethiopia` — spatial-autocorrelation-controlled CV + temporal rare-event holdout (forecasts 2015 drought). Loss forecasting, not crop-type classification; methodological ancestor (engineered temporal features + balanced metrics + anti-leakage).
- `mai2025_hexi` — **cross-continental** instance-based transfer: tree models trained on US `CDL` pixels, applied to the Hexi Corridor, China (5 crops, optical+SAR). Tier-1 *only in the source→target direction*, which is the largest spatial shift in the corpus. **Important split caveat:** the reported accuracies (best `RF_transfer` OA 0.92 / macro-F1 0.84 / κ 0.87) are computed on a *random 30% point holdout within the target*, i.e. in-region once target labels are added — not a spatially-disjoint sub-region. The clean transfer number is the zero-target "naive transfer": `XGBoost_naive` OA 0.78 / **macro-F1 0.45** / κ 0.60 (weighted-F1 0.76 — see the macro/weighted gap in §6). Also tests cross-year (2022→2023, supplement). Tree/boosting only; no deep nets.

**Tier 2 — field/parcel-disjoint or block-disjoint k-fold within a single scene/region (no spatial transfer):**
- `ltae2020` — field-wise 5-fold, one French region, one season. **Prime in-region exhibit:** crowns L-TAE (mIoU 51.7), buries RF (32.5) — the exact ordering our holdout reverses.
- `tempcnn` — parcel-disjoint k-fold, one 24×24 km scene. TempCNN > RF > RNN in-region.
- `penabarragan2011` — 650-field independent holdout, one county/year. Sparse decision tree (336→24 features); OA 79%, κ 0.75.
- `teixeira` — polygon 60/20/20, one N. Italy scene. 2D CNN-GRU 99.1% OA / macro-F1 99.1% (interleaved split).
- `maize_kenya` — field-level 65/35, one area, 87 fields. Flowering-phase OA 86%, κ 0.71.
- `russwurm` — 3.84 km spatial-block split, single AOI, pixel-scored. ConvGRU 89.7% OA. (Block-wise = stronger than pooled but still in-region.)
- `convstar_sar` — 10 km grid-disjoint 60/20/20, single Bei'an tile/year. 3D-ConvSTAR OA 91.7%, mean-F1 87.7%. (Best precedent for FID-disjoint as a *minimum* standard.)
- `nowakowski` — mosaic-disjoint but distribution-*matched* test, single season; authors concede geographic generalization "not fully addressed." ~83–90% weighted OA but macro recall ~0.5.
- `tseng2023presto`: mixed, task-dependent protocols; its only crop-type task (S2-Agri100) is a parcel-disjoint split inside one Sentinel-2 tile (Tier 2): pre-trained Presto OA 68.9 / κ 0.58 / macro-F1 40.4 vs pre-trained SITS-Former 67.0 / 0.56 / 42.8, and random-init Presto collapses to macro-F1 27.5. CropHarvest binary tasks use benchmark test sets of unstated spatial separation (mean F1 0.835); the only explicit geographic holdouts are two US *regression* tasks, where a raw-input RF is best on fuel moisture. Not a crop spatial-transfer comparator.

**Tier 3 — pooled / random k-fold; spatial-autocorrelation & FID leakage likely (numbers inflated):**
- `saini2018` (random pixel 70/30; RF 84.2% OA), `hcrnn` (random 10/90 pixel, contiguous patches — 97.6% OA, severe leakage), `ienco2017` (pooled 5-fold), `cnnrf_hybrid` (pooled pixel 25/25/50; Conv1D-RF 94.3% OA), `multimodal_naip` (random patch; 98.4%), `unet_kenya` (random patch 80/10/10; U-Net 95.3% OA but 73.6% F1, intercrop ~15–25%), `optsar_iran` (pooled point 50/50; fused 89% vs 77% single-sensor), `bandar2024` (pooled 80/20 on artificially rebalanced 7-class subset; hybrid F1 0.82), `cai_dl` (in-region, thin deep margin ~1.4 pts; protocol incompletely recoverable), off-domain RGB: `cnnsvm_grape` 97.6%, `crop_disease` ~90%, `weeds_tl` 99.3% micro-F.

**Tier 4 — reviews, theory, method papers, separability studies:**
- Tree/boosting foundations: `breiman2001`, `friedman2001`, `chen2016`, `ke2017`. Sparse-deep: `tabnet_orig`, `sparsemax`. RS reviews: `belgiu2016` (RF — and §6.1 documents RF spatial non-transferability), `maxwell2019`, `ball2017`, `dl_aerial_review` (DL beat classical in 35/36 in-region studies), `hohl2024` (XAI). Transfer-learning framing: `ma2024_tl` (canonical 300+-cite survey of transfer learning in environmental RS — names the domain-shift taxonomy (geographic/temporal/sensor = covariate/prior shift) and the five mitigation techniques: fine-tuning, feature extraction, UDA, DANN, self-supervised; the conceptual backbone for why cross-domain evaluation, not in-region k-fold, is the deployable bar). Context/premise: `begue2020` (Africa policy), `wardlow` (MODIS separability — crops separable only in specific phenological windows; same-crop signature shifts ~1 month across sub-regions).
- General time-series-classification (TSC) methods and benchmarks, not remote sensing: `dempster2021minirocket`, `middlehurst2024bakeoff`, `wu2023timesnet`. Filed here as method papers, but their *evaluation protocol* is Tier-3-like: UCR resamples pool and re-split each dataset (MiniRocket, bake-off: 30 stratified resamples), and TimesNet uses fixed UEA splits with no validation set (so model selection may touch test). All are i.i.d. within-dataset accuracy, accuracy-only (bake-off adds balanced accuracy/NLL), univariate (MiniRocket, bake-off) and on series of 24 to thousands of steps. Their rankings answer "which algorithm fits a new i.i.d. problem," never "which survives a domain shift." Cite them for the baseline menu and for compute, not for transfer.

## 2. Directly comparable papers (define our realistic targets)

No paper is an exact match (Western Cape, Sentinel-2 optical-only, field-level, five crops, adjacent-tile holdout). The closest, in priority order:

1. **`pankajakshan` — the thesis-confirming comparator.** Independent demonstration that a high-capacity dense net at 97–99% in-region collapses under spatial/cross-sensor transfer while a sparse few-parameter model transfers. Different sensor (HSI) and task framing, but the *mechanism* and *direction* are exactly ours. Use as the primary external corroboration.
2. **`butsko2025worldcereal`: the closest-protocol comparator.** The only crop study in the corpus that runs a GBDT and a transformer under both a pooled split and a spatially disjoint (country) holdout, with macro-F1. It splits two ways for us. Cropland (binary): the from-scratch transformer (`Presto-Rnd`) drops most (0.810→0.705 F1, −0.105) and ends worst, CatBoost holds (−0.046 expert features, −0.051 raw), and only *pre-trained* Presto beats CatBoost (0.829): our dense-net collapse plus the Cropformer-style "pre-training is the missing prior" reading, in one table. Crop type: no inversion; every model drops ~0.16–0.18 macro-F1, ranking preserved, and even `Presto-Rnd` beats raw CatBoost (0.620 vs 0.563). Cite that honestly as a counterpoint, with its confounds: CatBoost there is the weakest tree configuration (raw series, untuned, no expert features for crop type), training is global, multi-sensor, and 255K points (a different data regime from our two tiles), units are points with no field aggregation, single runs with tiny per-country test sets, and South Africa is not held out. Its geographic crop-type macro-F1 (0.56–0.65 overall; 0.23–0.63 for held-out African countries) is the number that corresponds in kind to ours; its random-split 0.81 is not.
3. **`rustowicz2019` — the geography/regime comparator.** Global-South smallholder, optical(+SAR), RF-vs-deep, macro-F1 primary. Quantifies deep-net collapse toward/below RF in the hard regime. Our targets should sit near its smallholder-regime macro-F1s (best ~57–70). SAR-doesn't-help-small-fields validates our optical-only choice.
4. **`jin_gee` — the operational-transfer comparator.** Cross-landscape holdout with an honest transfer penalty (79→63% maize). Confirms that out-of-sample discipline exposes drops in-region CV hides; sparse RF on parsimonious composites transfers — aligns with our feature-reduction story.
5. **`mai2025_hexi` — the crop-type naked-transfer + imbalance comparator.** Closest crop-type analogue to our zero-adaptation regime: tree/boosting models, 5 crops, optical(+SAR), macro-F1/κ reported. Its source-only "naive transfer" (`XGBoost_naive` macro-F1 0.45, weighted-F1 0.76) is a textbook majority-class-masking case and a direct precedent for our holdout numbers — under a *much larger* (cross-continental) shift than ours, so a macro-F1 ~0.45 there is consistent with our ~0.60 under a milder adjacent-tile shift. Tree models beat instance-transfer boosting (`TrAdaBoost`); no deep nets, so it corroborates rather than tests our tree-vs-deep claim. Quote the *naive-transfer* numbers, not the in-target-augmented 0.92 (see §6).
6. **`cropformer2023` — the complicating comparator.** The one dense model reported to transfer well (cross-region OA ~62–64% vs RF ~25%). Must be cited *with* its confound (massive self-supervised pre-training our dense nets lack) — see §6/§7. `butsko2025worldcereal`'s Presto vs Presto-Rnd ablation now supplies independent, open-code evidence for the same reading.
7. **`ltae2020` — the in-region foil and same-model-family anchor.** Field-level temporal-attention crowned by field-wise CV, RF buried; we run L-TAE ourselves and show it degrades/reorders under the holdout (and recovers with field aggregation).

## 3. Feature-design findings (discounted by tier)

- **Raw bands, especially red-edge & SWIR, carry most of the discriminating signal; derived indices add little once bands are present.** `cnnrf_hybrid` (Tier 3) flags B5/B12 most discriminative; `tempcnn` (Tier 2) — spectral indices add little over bands; `maize_kenya` (Tier 2) — full bands beat derived VIs. Supports keeping `B11`/`B12` (SWIR) in our set; we lack an explicit red-edge band — note as a possible gap.
- **Spectral indices transfer across regions more stably than raw bands** (`belgiu2016`, Tier 4, §6.1). Mild support for retaining `EVI`/`hue` as transfer-robust complements — a Tier-4 claim, not measured.
- **Phenological windows are decisive; separability is stage-dependent and shifts across sub-regions** (`wardlow`, `maize_kenya`, `penabarragan2011`). Same-crop signatures drift ~1 month across a single state (`wardlow`) — the seed mechanism of holdout reordering. Reinforces our May/June exclusion and multi-month design.
- **Automated time-series feature extraction is under-used and largely untested in crop classification.** `mann_ethiopia` is the lineage (41 phenological metrics + balanced metrics + anti-leakage CV). Our `xr_fresh` evaluation is first-of-kind *in crop classification*; in general TSC its analogue already exists (FreshPRINCE = full TSFresh set + Rotation Forest, `middlehurst2024bakeoff`), so frame the novelty as the crop/transfer setting, not the idea.
- **Engineered summary features are competitive but not the accuracy frontier, and their transfer value is modest.** In-distribution (Tier-3-like UCR resamples), FreshPRINCE is best of the feature-based family but only seventh of eight best-of-category methods (`middlehurst2024bakeoff`). Under geographic transfer (Tier 1), WorldCereal's expert-feature CatBoost degrades less than raw-series CatBoost on cropland (0.810 vs 0.777), but the ordering flips cross-year (0.830 vs 0.874) (`butsko2025worldcereal`). Both match our controlled reviewer-4.2 result (raw ≈ `xr_fresh` under transfer, XGBoost ST macro-F1 0.59 vs 0.56; Table `tab:repr`): representation matters less than model family, and `xr_fresh`'s value is operational. The bake-off also found the *full* TSFresh set beat FRESH-filtered features; not in conflict with our thesis, which concerns selection learned inside the model (tree splits, sparsemax), not a pre-training filter.
- **Random-kernel PPV features are a third representation arm.** MiniRocket's PPV ("fraction of the season a local shape pattern exceeds threshold") is a generic, automatically generated analogue of `xr_fresh` statistics (`dempster2021minirocket`, method paper). Its zero-sum kernels are blind to absolute level (e.g. peak `EVI`), which may cost in-region accuracy but could help if the holdout differs by a radiometric/phenological offset. Untested under transfer anywhere in the corpus.
- **Optical+SAR fusion helps in-scene (~12 OA pts, `optsar_iran`; minority-class rescue, `convstar_sar`) but the benefit is scale-dependent and may not survive to small fields** (`rustowicz2019`: SAR didn't reliably help smallholders). All fusion gains are Tier 2/3 (in-scene), so they are upper bounds, not transfer-validated. Supports our deliberate optical-only scope; flag SAR as future work, not a current omission. **Now with a transfer-validated data point:** `mai2025_hexi` (Tier 1) ran a multi-sensor stack (Sentinel-1+2, Landsat-8) and found that of the top-200 `RF_transfer` features, **191 are Sentinel-2, 7 Landsat-8, only 2 Sentinel-1** — SAR backscatter contributed almost nothing to discriminating its 5 crops under transfer. Strong support that optical carries the signal; we lose little by skipping SAR. (WorldCereal and Presto both use S1+S2+DEM+weather, so part of their transfer numbers may reflect richer inputs, not architecture.)
- **Optical signal concentrates in red-edge and SWIR (transfer-validated).** `mai2025_hexi`'s top transfer features are red-edge/SWIR indices and bands (VGCI, kNDVI, NDVI at the greening week; `B5`–`B7`, `B11`/`B12`) — Tier-1 corroboration of the Tier-2/3 red-edge+SWIR claim below. Reinforces keeping `B11`/`B12`; sharpens the missing-red-edge-band note in §6. Their features are engineered per-date index/band composites (xr_fresh-adjacent) fed to trees — same family as our pipeline.

## 4. Model-architecture findings (discounted by tier)

- **In-region, dense temporal/patch nets win — by thin and unstable margins.** `ltae2020` (Tier 2) L-TAE >> RF; `teixeira` (Tier 2) 2D CNN-GRU 99%; `tempcnn` (Tier 2) TempCNN > RF > RNN; `dl_aerial_review` (Tier 4) DL beat classical in 35/36 *in-region* studies. But margins are often ~1–4 pts (`cai_dl`, `convstar_sar`, `hcrnn`) and rankings among temporal nets flip across datasets (LSTM best in some, worst in `cai_dl`). Thin, unstable in-region margins are exactly what should not be trusted to survive transfer — our core point.
- **Even in-distribution, the general TSC literature finds no decisive deep win.** `middlehurst2024bakeoff` (UCR resamples, Tier-3-like): no deep model is an "AlexNet for TSC," the best deep learner (H-InceptionTime) has the widest spread and "often performs terribly," and no recent deep/Transformer TSC paper credibly beats InceptionTime; defaults are MultiROCKET-Hydra and HIVE-COTE 2, with QUANT as the fast option. `wu2023timesnet` (fixed UEA splits, accuracy only) leads on average by ~1 pt over Rocket but loses outright to Rocket or XGBoost on 4 of 10 datasets, with no significance test. If dense nets lack a robust edge in-distribution, they have none to spend under shift.
- **Under genuine transfer the ranking inverts: sparse/few-parameter models hold, dense nets collapse.** `pankajakshan` (Tier 1, decisive), `rustowicz2019` (Tier 1, RF ≥ deep in smallholder regime), `jin_gee` (Tier 1, sparse RF transfers), and `butsko2025worldcereal` cropland (Tier 1: from-scratch Presto drops 0.105 F1 and ends worst; CatBoost drops ~0.05). Counters: `cropformer2023` (pre-training-confounded) and WorldCereal crop type, where all models drop alike and a from-scratch transformer beats raw, untuned CatBoost (0.620 vs 0.563) given 255K global multi-sensor labels. The honest scope: inversion is the norm when training data are regional and modest (our regime); with very large, globally diverse training pools the holdout is less out-of-distribution and dense models can keep their in-region lead.
- **Pre-training, not architecture, is what lets dense temporal nets transfer.** `butsko2025worldcereal` (Tier 1): pre-trained Presto is the only transformer to beat CatBoost on the cropland geographic split (0.829 vs 0.810), while the identical architecture from scratch is worst (0.705). `tseng2023presto` (Tier 2, within-tile): random-init Presto loses ~13 macro-F1 points on S2-Agri100 (40.4→27.5). With `cropformer2023`, three papers now agree that the transferable ingredient is a learned global prior our supervised from-scratch nets (L-TAE, TempCNN, CNN-BiLSTM, Transformer, TimesNet) do not have. Caveat: that prior was trained on unlabeled imagery including the held-out regions, which is target exposure, not domain generalization (§5, §6).
- **GBDTs are the robust incumbent under geographic shift.** WorldCereal's deployed operational model is CatBoost, and under its country holdout CatBoost's random→geographic drop is half that of the from-scratch transformer on cropland (`butsko2025worldcereal`, Tier 1). On Presto's only explicit geographic holdouts (regression, not crops) a default raw-input RF beats every Presto variant on fuel moisture (`tseng2023presto`). Both papers' tree baselines are untuned or raw-input, so the robustness is a floor, not a ceiling, for a tuned GBDT on engineered features like our XGBoost.
- **Among tree/boosting transfer methods, robust bagged/boosted trees beat fancier instance-transfer boosting under imbalance + small target samples.** `mai2025_hexi` (Tier 1): `RF_transfer` and `XGBoost` outperform `TrAdaBoost` (instance reweighting), which the authors attribute to TrAdaBoost's hyperparameter sensitivity and source intra-class heterogeneity. Consistent with the broader "robust, regularized, axis-aligned models transfer" story — added sophistication did not help.
- **Naked cross-domain transfer collapses the macro/weighted-F1 gap wide open.** `mai2025_hexi` (Tier 1) zero-target transfer: weighted-F1 0.72–0.76 but **macro-F1 0.43–0.45** — minority crops absorbed into the majority classes; ~0.40 macro-F1 recovered only after target labels are added. A clean precedent for our holdout behaviour and for reporting macro-F1 as primary. `tseng2023presto` adds two metric-discipline examples: Presto beats SITS-Former on OA and κ but loses on macro-F1 (S2-Agri100), and a raw RF scores F1 0.000 on Brazil coffee despite AUC 0.941.
- **Swapping a dense softmax head for a sparse/tree-like head reduces overfitting** even in-region: `cnnrf_hybrid` (RF head over CNN features, +1.3–1.7 OA), and off-domain `cnnsvm_grape`/`weeds_tl` (CNN features → SVM/XGBoost head, small train/test gap). Presto's authors recommend the same pattern as the cheap deployment mode: frozen encoder + default RF (`Presto_RF`) or logistic regression (`Presto_R`), and `Presto_RF` beats full fine-tuning on TreeSatAI-S2 (`tseng2023presto`, Tier 2/3). Mechanistic support for why TabNet groups with boosted trees. The ROCKET family is the instructive in-between case: a fixed, non-learned representation (cannot co-adapt to region-specific signal) with a *dense* L2 ridge head (no selection) (`dempster2021minirocket`). Running it, and an L1/XGBoost-head variant on the same PPV features, would separate "not learning region-specific features" from "explicit sparse selection" as the operative property.
- **Ensembling per se is not the lever.** `middlehurst2024bakeoff` (Tier-3-like) finds no significant effect of ensembling on ranks; random-transform + regularized-linear pipelines match large ensembles. Parallels our ablation (Appendix D / supplement): the gain is architectural and aggregation-driven.
- **Field/parcel aggregation is a real variance-reduction lever** (`ltae2020`'s Pixel-Set Encoder; `penabarragan2011`'s object level). Matches our finding that aggregation recovers transfer for dense temporal models (L-TAE) but is redundant for already-sparse ones. Presto pools pixel embeddings (mean+std) to parcels and names this crude pooling its main failure mode when signal lives in a minority of pixels (`tseng2023presto`); WorldCereal is point-level and never tests aggregation.
- **The sparse inductive bias is shared by our transfer-robust set:** tree split criteria implicitly select features (`breiman2001`, `friedman2001`, `chen2016` sparsity-aware splits, `ke2017` EFB), and TabNet inherits it via `sparsemax` attentive masks (`tabnet_orig`, `sparsemax`). This is the §5 backbone of our mechanistic claim.

## 5. Conceptual / theoretical framing

- **Domain shift is the named, central problem — and cross-domain evaluation is the deployable bar.** `ma2024_tl` (the canonical environmental-RS transfer survey) formalizes the vocabulary our paper makes operational: geographic / temporal / sensor shift (= covariate / prior shift), and the premise that a model trained in one domain "often demonstrates poor performance when directly applied to other domains." It catalogs the five adaptation remedies (fine-tuning, feature extraction, UDA, DANN, self-supervised) — all of which *consume target data*. Our contribution sits **upstream**: we measure zero-adaptation transfer where architectural inductive bias is the only lever, before any of those remedies. Cite `ma2024_tl` as the authoritative statement that in-region k-fold is the wrong number to optimize; cite `mai2025_hexi` as a concrete instance-based-transfer application of it. `butsko2025worldcereal` is the operational-system statement of the same bar (ESA/NASA Harvest): its deployment protocol prescribes geographic and temporal holdouts because benchmarks "miss deployment needs."
- **Pre-training is the missing prior, and it sits on the other side of our framing.** WorldCereal, Presto, and Cropformer all show dense temporal transformers transferring once given a large self-supervised prior, and failing without it (§4). But Presto's global pre-training corpus (21.5M pixels, Dynamic World sampling, includes southern Africa) has seen unlabeled imagery of the held-out regions, so foundation-model transfer is partial target access, not the no-target-access domain generalization we study. This sharpens rather than threatens our thesis: absent a prior, sparse inductive bias is what transfers; a prior is a different, data-hungry route to the same end.
- **Inductive bias governs transfer.** Sparse, axis-aligned, regularized feature selection (the two-decade RF/GBT workhorse property, `belgiu2016`/`breiman2001`/`friedman2001`/`chen2016`) is what degrades gracefully under covariate shift; dense end-to-end representation learning overfits region-specific signal (`ball2017` states transfer "typically fails" under sensor/region/time shift). `tabnet_orig`+`sparsemax` give the bridge: a deep net can keep the sparse bias and stay transfer-robust. The bake-off's explanation for weak deep TSC (no shared structure across problems for networks to exploit, `middlehurst2024bakeoff`) is an in-distribution counterpart to our explanation for dense nets failing to transfer.
- **Benchmark rankings shift on unseen data, even in general TSC.** HIVE-COTE 2's lead on the familiar UCR archive shrinks on 30 new datasets, which the authors partly attribute to embedded design bias (`middlehurst2024bakeoff`). A benchmark-level analogue of the validation artifact we document; cite the authors' finding, not the reviewer commentary in their arXiv appendix.
- **Sparse attention as interpretability + transfer lever.** `sparsemax` is the operator inside TabNet's masks and the basis for our sparse-attention L-TAE-S; `hohl2024` documents the field-wide XAI rigor deficit and the per-band-importance gap that sparse selection addresses.
- **Operational framing.** `begue2020` motivates why spatial/operational transfer (not in-distribution accuracy) is the right bar for the Global South; the Western Cape's large commercial fields make it comparatively tractable — our gaps are a *lower* bound on harder regions. WorldCereal's African holdouts are its hardest (crop-type macro-F1 0.23–0.63; cropland F1 0.26–0.44 in Tanzania), and it adds a compute constraint (users must retrain on CPU) that favours light models.

## 6. Project-specific caveats surfaced by the audit

- **Our holdout is an adjacent tile in the same agroecological zone** → a *local* spatial shift. Our reported gaps are a conservative lower bound on cross-region/cross-climate transfer (cf. `pankajakshan`/`jin_gee`, where shifts were larger and drops steeper). Already stated in `tgrs-article.tex` (§Discussion, Limitations: single season, adjacent tile, "conservative lower bound").
- **`cropformer2023` is not independently verified.** As of mid-2026: no independent reproduction (the only transfer follow-ups are the same China Agricultural University group's self-citations), **no released code, no pre-trained weights, no released pre-training corpus, framework unspecified**, and it uses GaoFen-1 (RGB+NIR, 16 m) — not Sentinel-2 multispectral. Its cross-region numbers therefore are *not directly comparable* to ours and rest entirely on the original authors. Cite it as the motivating exception, and frame its transfer success as evidence that a strong learned prior (self-supervised pre-training), not architectural capacity, buys transfer. `butsko2025worldcereal` (open code, Presto vs Presto-Rnd) is the stronger, reproducible citation for that framing.
- **`butsko2025worldcereal` numbers need four qualifiers whenever quoted:** point-level (not field) units; global multi-sensor inputs; single runs with 0.2K–0.5K test points in several countries (many per-country differences are within seed noise); and pre-training exposure to held-out imagery. Its crop-type GBDT is raw-series, untuned CatBoost, so "FM beats GBDT" there is against the weakest tree configuration. Several authors developed Presto and WorldCereal; it is a workshop paper without a DOI.
- **Any foundation-model baseline on our holdout breaks two design premises.** Presto needs bands we do not carry (`B3`, `B4`, `B5`, `B7`, `B8`, `B8A`; `EVI`/`hue` have no Presto channel) and likely saw 2020–2021 Western Cape imagery during pre-training. A Presto run would violate both the "same six optical features for every model" control and the no-target-access premise; it belongs in future work or the supplement with both caveats.
- **General TSC baselines degenerate on our 10-step series.** MiniRocket's dilation collapses to 1 (`log2(9/8)` ≈ 0.17) and its ~10k features are massively redundant (`dempster2021minirocket`); QUANT's interval pyramid is shallow at length 10 (`middlehurst2024bakeoff`); TimesNet's FFT yields "periods" `{10, 5, 3, 2}` that are spectral artefacts of one season, its positional index ignores the May/June gap (April→July treated as one step), and its reference period selection averages amplitudes over the mini-batch, so predictions depend on batch composition (`wu2023timesnet`). Their published strengths come from long, regularly sampled, mostly univariate series and do not carry over; report any result with that caveat.
- **Ridge-head baselines cannot produce the official metric.** ROCKET-family and MR-Hydra ridge classifiers give hard predictions with poor NLL/AUROC (`middlehurst2024bakeoff`), but the AI4FoodSecurity metric is field-level cross-entropy (our Xent column). Use a logistic head or calibration, and `class_weight="balanced"` given 43–56% lucerne/medics.
- **Class imbalance is more severe in our holdout (56% lucerne/medics) than training (43%)** — distribution shift compounds covariate shift. Justifies macro-F1 as primary (cf. `belgiu2016`: RF "favors the most representative classes"; `unet_kenya`: 95% OA vs 74% F1; `mai2025_hexi`: naked-transfer weighted-F1 0.76 vs macro-F1 0.45, with the rarest crop — canola, n=74 — collapsing to F1 0.47–0.61 even after target augmentation). When citing `mai2025_hexi` numbers, use its *naive-transfer* (zero-target) figures: the headline 0.92 OA is a random in-target point split, not a spatially-disjoint holdout, so it is not comparable to ours. None of the five new papers reports per-class F1 under shift (WorldCereal shows it only for the random split), so minority-crop behaviour under transfer remains a corpus-wide silence.
- **We lack an explicit red-edge band**, which several Tier 2/3 studies found highly discriminative (`teixeira`, `cnnrf_hybrid`). Note as a feature-set limitation, not a fixable gap given the input data.
- **Several in-region SOTA numbers we might be tempted to compare against are leakage-inflated** (`hcrnn` 97.6%, `multimodal_naip` 98.4%, `teixeira` 99.1%). Never benchmark our holdout macro-F1 against these, nor against UCR/UEA accuracies (bake-off 0.75–0.89 averages, TimesNet 73.6%), which are averages over unrelated i.i.d. problems.

## 8. Numerical targets the literature supports under genuine spatial transfer

- **Holdout macro-F1 in the ~0.55–0.65 band is a credible, defensible target for the best transferable model** — our best models at 0.60 (TabNet pixel ensemble κ 0.54, tied by both L-TAE-S variants) sit squarely in the range set by Tier-1 work: `rustowicz2019` smallholder best macro-F1 ~0.57–0.70; `jin_gee` cross-landscape maize OA 63–79%; `mai2025_hexi` naked (zero-target) cross-continental transfer macro-F1 ~0.45 with tree models — a *harder* shift than ours, so our 0.60 under a milder adjacent-tile shift is fully consistent and arguably conservative. `butsko2025worldcereal` now gives the closest in-kind number: geographic-holdout crop-type macro-F1 0.563 (raw CatBoost), 0.620 (from-scratch transformer), 0.650 (pre-trained Presto), and 0.23–0.63 for held-out African countries. Its shift is larger (whole countries) but its training pool is vastly larger and multi-sensor, so the two roughly offset; our optical-only, two-tile 0.60 sits between its raw GBDT and its foundation model. **Stand:** 0.58–0.62 is the realistic ceiling for supervised, optical-only, no-prior models in our setting; beating ~0.65 should require something our design excludes (a pre-trained prior, multi-sensor inputs, or target labels). Do **not** aim for or compare against the 0.80+ in-region/leaky figures (`teixeira`, `hcrnn`, `unet_kenya`, `mai2025_hexi`'s in-target 0.92, WorldCereal's random-split 0.81), nor against UCR/UEA accuracies.
- **A large, architecture-dependent in-region→holdout gap is the expected, literature-consistent result**, not an anomaly: `pankajakshan` 97–99% → "very poor"; `jin_gee` −16 pts; `butsko2025worldcereal` −0.16 to −0.18 macro-F1 for every crop-type model and −0.105 F1 for the from-scratch transformer on cropland. Our dense-net drops (pixel L-TAE/Transformer 0.78→0.58, −0.20; CNN-BiLSTM 0.72→0.46, −0.26) sit at or beyond that norm, while our sparse learners (field L-TAE-S −0.04, LR −0.05, TabNet −0.13) beat it. Note WorldCereal's random split is pooled points (possibly leaky, Tier-3-like) while our in-region baseline is field-wise (Tier 2), so our gaps are measured from a stricter starting point. A ~0.15–0.20 drop is the bar; a sparse model losing ≤0.10 is the notable result.
- **Kappa under transfer ~0.45–0.55 for the best models** is consistent with `penabarragan2011` (0.75 in-region field holdout) and `russwurm` (0.87 in-region block) once discounted for the move to a truly disjoint tile and a more imbalanced target. No Tier-1 crop paper in the corpus reports Kappa under a geographic holdout (WorldCereal reports F1 only; Presto's κ 0.58 is within-tile Tier 2), so this target remains an extrapolation.
- **Logistic regression / GBT transferring within a few points of the best model (our LR ~0.56)** is consistent with `rustowicz2019`'s RF matching deep nets, `maxwell2019`'s "no universal winner; tree ensembles robust under reduced/noisy data," and WorldCereal's raw CatBoost at 0.563 geographic macro-F1, within 0.06–0.09 of its transformers. The story is not "classical beats deep" but "the gap between simple and complex *shrinks or reverses* under transfer."
