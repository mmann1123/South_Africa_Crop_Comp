# Crop-Classification Literature: Project-Centric Findings Synthesis

Internal decision-support synthesis for the Western Cape Sentinel-2 crop-classification study. Organized around our modeling choices, evaluation bar, and concrete action items, not a general review (that is [general_literature_review.md](general_literature_review.md)). Every number below is tagged by the evaluation protocol that produced it; numbers from different tiers are not comparable. Rebuilt in full on 2026-10-21 from all 69 per-paper briefers (68 papers; the object-based crop identification paper has two briefer files) after the holdout fill check revised the project's central finding.

**Our study, in one line:** twenty-seven classical / deep / patch models on multi-temporal Sentinel-2 optical-only imagery (`B2 B6 B11 B12 EVI hue`, 10 months, May/June excluded), benchmarked twice: in-region field-wise CV on the training tiles (`34S_19E_258N`, `34S_19E_259N`) and on a spatially disjoint, same-season adjacent holdout tile (`34S_20E_259N`). Primary metric macro-F1; also Kappa, weighted-F1, log-loss (the AI4FoodSecurity official metric). Five winter crops, lucerne/medics-dominated (43% train, 56% holdout). **Revised central findings** (from `experiments/holdout_fill_check/` and `experiments/sparse_gate_backbones/`): (1) the holdout tile lacks September for 40.6% of pixels (39.3% of fields) while the training tiles never do; the published pipeline zero-filled that month before standardization for every raw-sequence deep model but median-filled it for pixel TabNet, a train/test and model-to-model missingness shift that cost the dense models 0.08 to 0.14 holdout macro-F1 and manufactured an apparent in-region/out-of-region ranking inversion. (2) With September filled consistently from training-tile medians (no retraining), out-of-region macro-F1 is CNN-BiLSTM 0.66, TempCNN 0.65, L-TAE 0.65, L-TAE-S 0.64, Transformer 0.64, TimesNet 0.63, pixel TabNet 0.62, field TempCNN / XGBoost / LightGBM / MiniRocket / logistic regression 0.61, field L-TAE / L-TAE-S / RF 0.60; deep pixel models lose 0.06 to 0.14 from in-region 0.72 to 0.78; the ranking is stable, not inverted. Patch CNNs were not re-run (0.50, 0.45). (3) A sparsemax channel gate (L-TAE-S, and the same gate on CNN-BiLSTM and TempCNN with softmax controls) gives no transfer gain on complete inputs and no consistent low-data gain; its one reproducible effect is about +0.06 robustness to the zero-filled month (CNN-BiLSTM +0.063 [0.040, 0.086] over dense, +0.034 over softmax). TabNet is not robust to zero fill (0.61 to 0.51). (4) Learning curves on the corrected holdout: at 10% of fields (about 270 fields, about 30 per class) every model is near 0.55 macro-F1 except XGBoost 0.51; deep models rise to about 0.65 at 100%; logistic regression and XGBoost plateau at 0.61, TabNet at 0.57. **Protocol remains the lens:** in-region validation still overstates holdout accuracy for every model, and the literature's protocol tiers still matter; what changed is that the apparent inversion was data handling, not inductive bias. Active manuscript: `writeup/CEA_submission/cea-article.tex` (earlier versions `writeup/tgrs-article.tex` + `tgrs-supplement.tex`, `writeup/sn-article.tex`).

## 1. Every paper sorted by evaluation protocol

Tier counts: Tier 1 = 16, Tier 2 = 17, Tier 3 = 17, Tier 4 = 21 (71 papers).

**Tier 1, true spatial or temporal transfer (different tile/region/country, or cross-year, or cross-sensor; source-only numbers reported):**

- `pham2024temporal`: Brandenburg, train on 2018 (the densest clear-sky year) in the northern half, test wall-to-wall in the southern half for every year 2010 to 2021 (spatial split plus cross-year and cross-sensor transfer), 14 classes, pixel 1D-CNN and Transformer, OA only, single runs. Annual clear-observation counts are reported. Accuracy loss tracks observation density; the model without augmentation "only performed well in the training year and poorly in unseen years"; Random Observation Selection (training-time observation dropout, 5 to 100% kept) recovers 20 to 30 OA points and beats phenology shifting by up to 30; linear interpolation falls to 30 to 54% OA in sparse years. The closest published precedent for our mechanism and remedy (cross-year, not cross-region).
- `pham2024fractions`: land-cover fraction regression (not crops), northern Germany and Poland, Sentinel-2 annual percentiles, single-year models 2017 to 2022 each mapped to every year, validated on 527 local and 473 external (eastern, sparser single-orbit) 30 m sites with VHR-annotated fractions, MAE only, single runs. Mean CSO per pixel reported per year (13 in 2017 to 35 in 2018). The densest-year model transfers worst; Random Observation Selection (percentiles recomputed on 80 to 20% of observations, no fill) cuts transfer MAE by up to 19% and also helps same-year transfer to the external tiles (up to 16%), the only published hint of a same-season spatial version of the effect.

- `pandzic2026transfer`: Slovenia (159,533 parcels) to Vojvodina, Serbia (2,399 parcels), about 420 km, cross-border, same year, nine classes, parcel-wise splits, complete spline-interpolated 15-day inputs identical for both models. Source-only F1 (S1+S2): RF 46.5, Transformer 66.8; Serbian in-region benchmark RF 84.4, Transformer 76.5. The one published in-region/out-of-region inversion with numbers, and it runs toward the dense model. F1 averaging convention unstated; single run.
- `nyborg2022timematch`: four Sentinel-2 tiles in Denmark, France, Austria, 2017, parcel-level, 15 classes, macro-F1 over three splits. Source-only PSE+L-TAE averages 39.2 vs 82.0 target-trained; the near-neighbour pair `FR1->FR2` keeps 70.3 of 85.7. No non-deep baseline; the collapse is diagnosed as a 30 to 35 day phenological shift, removable by ShiftAug (+10 points with zero target data).
- `mohammadi2024sourcefree`: Landsat, three 45 km US sites in different climate zones, 2019 model applied to 2020/2021, pixel-level, three classes, identical valid-observation filter plus linear interpolation for every model. Cross-regional source-only macro-F1 averages: Transformer 51.9, LSTM 51.9, MLP 46.9, CNN 46.1, RF 45.3 (last). SFUDA adds +9 to +20. No in-region reference; CDL labels both sides.
- `rusnak2023cropmapping`: Slovakia, Danube to East lowland (about 300 km), 2018 to 2022, Sentinel-2 monthly medians, eight balanced classes, pixel-level. Spatial-holdout OA: SVM 87.3, NN (shallow MLP) 87.0, QDA 85.4, RF 81.6; RF is the least transferable of four under identical inputs, worst in drought years, and the authors say so. No deep temporal model; no in-region score tabulated.
- `wijesingha2024transferability`: two German regions about 200 km apart, Landsat 2013 to 2018 trained, field-level means of four bimonthly composites, five classes. Spatial transfer costs mF1 -0.04 to -0.14 for RF and CNN alike; RF leads the 96k-parameter 2D CNN under transfer by 0.03 mF1 after trailing in-region in one region. Authors attribute the CNN deficit to only four time steps, not to density. The only tree-beats-deep spatial-transfer result with numbers, and the CNN is not a temporal sequence model.
- `xie2025transferability`: RF only, Kansas CDL to six European countries, 2019 to 2018, binary corn vs wheat on LUCAS points. Transferred model within 0.5 to 2.0 OA of a local model; source-state choice matters up to 21.5 OA points; merging heterogeneous source domains hurts. Deep tests exist only in the supplement ("varies by model type").
- `shibuya2025modis`: Mato Grosso MODIS, cross-year within region, RF vs TempCNN vs L-TAE at `sits` defaults, five land-use classes. Same-year RF 0.994 > TempCNN 0.981 > L-TAE 0.962 OA; cross-year ground samples RF 0.917 > 0.886 > 0.859. Ranking preserved; no inversion; deep models lose about 2 points more. Label-source shift confounds the year shift; 250 m, four bands.
- `butsko2025worldcereal`: same models under pooled random split, country holdout, and cross-year, point-level, global multi-sensor, macro-F1 for crop type. Geographic crop-type macro-F1 0.563 (raw CatBoost) to 0.650 (pre-trained Presto), near-uniform random-to-geographic drop of 0.16 to 0.18, ranking preserved, from-scratch Presto 0.620 beats raw CatBoost. Caveats in §6.
- `cropformer2023`: cross-region OA 62 to 64% (pre-trained) vs RF 25%. No code, no weights, GaoFen-1 not Sentinel-2, no independent reproduction (§6). Now reads as consistent with a dense net out-transferring RF, and it ingests irregular sequences so missing dates never become zeros.
- `pankajakshan`: single-date HSI, params fixed on one dataset and applied cross-sensor, open-set, with proxy validation and no labelled target accuracy. 3D-CNN "very poor" under transfer, sparse SVM holds. Demoted from lead corroboration to context: it is not a time-series result, the target has no labelled accuracy, and its mechanism (capacity) is the one our fill check removed.
- `jin_gee`: cross-landscape national holdout, maize OA 79% Tanzania to 63% Kenya; RF only, SAR fusion. Shows trees also pay a transfer penalty.
- `rustowicz2019`: Germany vs Ghana/South Sudan; RF 62.4 vs best deep 57.3 macro-F1 in label-scarce Ghana. A data-volume result (belongs with learning curves, §4), not a same-region disjoint tile.
- `mann_ethiopia`: spatial-autocorrelation-controlled CV plus a rare-event temporal holdout; loss forecasting, methodological ancestor.
- `mai2025_hexi`: US CDL to Hexi Corridor, trees only; zero-target "naive" XGBoost macro-F1 0.45 (weighted-F1 0.76); the headline 0.92 is an in-target random point split. Trees drop under transfer; no deep comparator; 191 of top-200 transfer features are Sentinel-2.

**Tier 2, field/parcel/block-disjoint within a single scene or region (no spatial transfer):**

- `ltae2020` (field-wise 5-fold, one French region; L-TAE mIoU 51.7, RF 32.5), `tempcnn` (parcel-disjoint k-fold, one scene), `penabarragan2011` (650-field holdout, one county), `teixeira` (polygon 60/20/20; 99.1% OA), `maize_kenya` (87 fields, 65/35), `russwurm` (3.84 km blocks, one AOI; ConvGRU 89.7% OA), `convstar_sar` (10 km grid-disjoint; 3D-ConvSTAR mean-F1 87.7), `nowakowski` (mosaic-disjoint, distribution-matched), `tseng2023presto` (S2-Agri100 parcel-disjoint within one tile; pre-trained Presto macro-F1 40.4, random-init 27.5).
- `russwurm2020selfattention`: 4.5 km blocks with 500 m margins across three Bavarian regions that all appear in every partition; kappa, OA, class-mean F1. On preprocessed inputs all six models are within noise (kappa 0.76 to 0.81); on raw cloudy inputs RF collapses (-0.24 kappa), TempCNN drops most among deep models (-0.17), LSTM/Transformer hold (-0.04/-0.05). A ranking change caused by the input, with retraining, and with a gradient analysis locating the mechanism.
- `obadic2022attention`: same split and dataset; varies only the training class set and the date set, never the region. Attention is a contrast detector; attended dates move when grassland is removed from training (F1 0.64 to 0.67).
- `garnot2022multimodal`: PASTIS-R official 5-fold, all four French tiles in every fold, mIoU. Temporal dropout (p = 0.4, observations deleted, never filled) lifts S2-only PSE+L-TAE 73.9 to 74.5 mIoU on clean test data and makes it markedly more resilient to deleted dates; early fusion least robust, decision fusion most.
- `kussul2017deep`: random 50/50 polygon split, Kyiv; 2D-CNN ensemble 94.6% > 1D-CNN 93.5 > MLP 92.7 > RF 88.7 OA; optical+SAR.
- `schulthess2023samplesize`: field-wise random 80/20 within one Yaqui Valley tile, RF only, nine classes; OA levels off near 0.9 after about 540 fields; fewer than 30 fields per crop is unstable; per-crop F breakpoint at 40 to 45 fields; proportional sampling beats balanced sampling because majority omissions become minority commissions.
- `mahmood2025features`: polygon-disjoint 70/30, Punjab, RF backward elimination over 122 S1+S2 features, ten splits; removing 80% of features costs 2 OA, 4 kappa, 3 macro-F points, never improves accuracy; spatial instability rises while OA is flat.
- `reuss2026eurocropsml`: random 20% parcel split within Estonia; cross-region pre-training (Latvia, Portugal) is the treatment, not the test. From-scratch Transformer OA 0.438 / 0.490 / 0.518 / 0.573 / 0.632 at 10 / 20 / 100 / 200 / 500 shots per class, kappa 0.25 at 20 shots, still 0.155 below the full-data ceiling at 500; no classical baseline.
- `che2024interpolation`: in-region 5-fold over 7,735 pixel samples spaced 10.7 km apart (effectively block-separated), one HLS basin, 2021, matched train/test missingness. Masking Bi-LSTM and Transformer: interpolation changes OA by under 0.5 points and per-class F1 by under 0.008, below run noise, including 17- and 10-class CDL legends; interpolation hurts by 1 to 5 points when fewer than 30% of composites are good.

**Tier 3, pooled or random splits; spatial-autocorrelation and FID leakage likely (numbers inflated):**

- `saini2018` (random pixel 70/30; RF 84.2% OA), `hcrnn` (random 10/90 pixel from contiguous patches; 97.6%), `ienco2017` (pooled 5-fold), `cnnrf_hybrid` (pooled pixel; 94.3%), `multimodal_naip` (random patch; 98.4%), `unet_kenya` (random patch; 95.3% OA but 73.6% F1), `optsar_iran` (pooled point 50/50), `bandar2024` (pooled 80/20 on a rebalanced subset), `cai_dl` (protocol incompletely recoverable), off-domain RGB `cnnsvm_grape`, `crop_disease`, `weeds_tl`.
- `portalesjulia2021abandoned`: pooled pixel 70/30, Valencia, 67 dates; BiLSTM 98.2% OA; RF-100 beats LSTM/BiLSTM at small training fractions and loses at large ones, shown only as a figure, where "small" is about 14,000 pixels and same-parcel pixels sit on both sides.
- `yuan2021sitsbert`: pooled pixel sampling on a 10-pixel grid within one tile, 100 samples per class; from-scratch CNN-1D and LSTMs at or below RF, from-scratch transformer marginal, pre-trained SITS-BERT beats RF at 50 to 500 samples per class (SITS-BERT at 50 per class matches RF at 500). SVM/RF inputs zero-padded consistently in train and test.
- `wang2024mctnet`: random pixel hold-out within one US state and year, 10,000 CDL points (95% confidence) per state, class-balanced 240-per-class training, no field IDs or spatial blocking; OA 0.968 / macro-F1 0.933 (Arkansas), 0.852 / 0.829 (California), single runs, margins over GL-TAE and L-TAE of 0.01 to 0.05. Two secondary checks move the ranking: on geographically separated mapping areas scored against CDL, L-TAE beats MCTNet in one California area and RF beats GL-TAE and L-TAE in another; cross-year (train 2021, test 2020/2022), RF is best in California 2022. Missing composites are zero-filled in both train and test, with a binary mask input.
- `wang2025jointfrp`: simulated regime is a random 256 x 256 patch split (in-region); the real-world regime applies the models to incomplete subsets from different administrative areas, so it is simultaneously a spatial and a missingness holdout and the two are not separable. Train-complete/test-incomplete U-TAE loses 2.7 / 12.7 / 36.3 mean-F1 at 25 / 50 / 75% missing (S4A); every interpolator is worse than feeding the shortened sequence; temporal dropout recovers 15 cropland-F1 points real-world on Hunan but loses 5 on S4A. Single runs.
- `zhang2024maskpstin`: preview-only briefer; protocol unknown, most likely three within-region experiments. Temporal random masking adds +3.5 OA / +0.047 mF1 over PSTIN; cannot be cited for splits, transfer, or fill values until the full text is read.

**Tier 4, reviews, theory, method papers, benchmarks without a spatial dimension:**

- Tree/boosting foundations `breiman2001`, `friedman2001`, `chen2016`, `ke2017`; sparse-deep `tabnet_orig`, `sparsemax`. RS reviews `belgiu2016` (§6.1 documents RF non-transferability across study areas), `maxwell2019` (excludes deep learning by design; RF insensitive to training-set reduction), `ball2017`, `dl_aerial_review` (DL beat classical in 35/36 in-region studies), `hohl2024`. Framing `ma2024_tl` (domain-shift taxonomy; five adaptation remedies, all consuming target data), `begue2020`, `wardlow` (separability is stage-dependent; signatures shift about one month across sub-regions).
- General TSC benchmarks `dempster2021minirocket`, `middlehurst2024bakeoff`, `wu2023timesnet`: UCR/UEA resamples or fixed splits, i.i.d. within dataset, accuracy-only, mostly univariate; the bake-off excluded LSTM-FCN for overfitting the test data and finds H-InceptionTime improves relative to others as training size grows.
- Missingness and benchmark-fairness theory: `zhou2023missingness` (defines missingness shift; with indicators it reduces to covariate shift; without them the source-optimal predictor can be worse than the label mean; Algorithm 1 re-masks source data to match target missingness; UCAR assumption, regression, tabular, real-data test failed), `yi2020zeroimputation` (variable sparsity problem: expected activations move with the input's missing fraction under zero fill; batch/layer norm do not repair it; Sparsity Normalization; matched regime only), `kapoor2023leakage` (taxonomy L1.2 names imputation; L3.3 names spatial sampling bias; civil-war case where fixing pooled imputation collapsed the RF-vs-LR AUC gap from 0.14 to 0.01; model info sheets), `shwartzziv2022tabular` (under one tuning and preprocessing protocol TabNet, NODE, DNF-Net lose 10.5 to 14.2% relative to the best model on unseen datasets vs 3.3% for XGBoost; deep-plus-XGBoost ensemble best; standardization statistics from training data only).

## 2. Directly comparable papers (define our realistic targets)

No paper is an exact match (Western Cape, Sentinel-2 optical-only, field-level, five winter crops, adjacent same-season tile, 27 models under one input pipeline). The credible comparators, in priority order, are now the multi-classifier source-only transfer studies with consistent inputs:

1. **`pandzic2026transfer`, the direction-of-inversion comparator.** Complete, identically interpolated inputs for both models, parcel-wise splits, same year, a confirmed covariate shift (324 of 325 KS tests reject). In-region the tree wins (RF 84.4 vs Transformer 76.5 F1), under source-only transfer the Transformer wins by 20 points (66.8 vs 46.5). This is the published inversion, and it runs opposite to the one our zero-filled table reported. Its Transformer loses 9.7 points from the target benchmark and 5.4 from its own in-region score at 420 km, which brackets our deep-model gaps of 0.06 to 0.14 at tile distance. Caveats: the Serbian in-region benchmark is trained on about 360 parcels, so part of the Transformer's advantage is the 159k-parcel source set; single run; F1 convention unstated.
2. **`mohammadi2024sourcefree`, the multi-backbone same-preprocessing comparator.** Five model families under one gap-handling path (valid-observation filter + linear interpolation, no zero fill), 12 cross-regional and 6 cross-time pairs, macro-F1. Deep sequence models match or beat RF in every condition (cross-regional 51.9 vs 45.3; cross-time LSTM 82.5 vs RF 74.3), and the deep-over-tree margin of 6 to 8 points is the same order as our corrected 0.63 to 0.66 vs 0.59 to 0.61. It also shows the deep ranking is unstable across pairs (LSTM collapses to 19.8 on `MS19->IA`), which justifies our multi-seed reporting. No in-region score, three classes, CDL labels.
3. **`rusnak2023cropmapping`, the trees-are-least-robust comparator.** Four classifiers on identical 50-feature Sentinel-2 monthly medians, two Slovak lowlands, five years; RF is last under both temporal and spatial holdouts (81.6 vs 87.3 OA spatial) and the gap widens to 8 to 14 points in distribution-shifted years. The authors explicitly ask for multi-algorithm transfer studies because "many transferability studies have exclusively used random forest". Composite observation quality, tabulated per region-month-year, did not explain transfer loss when handled identically for all models; this is the matched-handling condition under which our corrected ranking holds. No deep temporal model.
4. **`wijesingha2024transferability`, the gap-magnitude comparator and the one referee counter-example.** Spatial transfer at about 200 km, same years, five classes, field-level: absolute mF1 change -0.04 to -0.14, model-family agnostic, overlapping our deep-pixel range almost exactly. RF leads its 2D CNN by 0.03 mF1 under transfer, which the authors attribute to only four observations per year ("no substantial differences"). Cite it as the boundary case where sequence density is too low for a temporal network to have any inductive advantage. Its DI/AOA analysis (0.9 correlation between area-of-applicability share and OA) is the label-free diagnostic that would have flagged our September problem before training.
5. **`butsko2025worldcereal`, the closest-protocol crop-type comparator at global scale.** Same models under pooled and country-holdout splits with macro-F1: crop-type random-to-geographic drop 0.16 to 0.18 for every model, ranking preserved, from-scratch transformer 0.620 over raw CatBoost 0.563, pre-trained Presto 0.650. The structural agreement with our corrected result (no inversion, modest dense lead) is the citable point. Four qualifiers whenever quoted (§6).
6. **`shibuya2025modis`, the cross-year rank-stability comparator where the tree happens to win.** RF > TempCNN > L-TAE both same-year and cross-year, deep models losing about 2 points more; no inversion. Four differences (cross-year not cross-region; 250 m MODIS four bands; coarse land-use classes; untuned deep models) explain why its winner differs from ours without any contradiction in the structural claim that in-domain and out-of-domain rankings agree.
7. **`nyborg2022timematch`, the transfer-distance scale.** Our holdout is an `FR1->FR2`-type pair (adjacent, shared phenology, 15-point loss) not a `DK1->FR1`-type pair (44 to 66 points). The lesson to carry into the manuscript is theirs: name the shift before blaming the inductive bias. Not citable for tree-vs-deep in either direction (no non-deep baseline).
8. **`xie2025transferability`, the tree-only optimistic end.** Cross-continent RF within 2 OA of a local model on an easy binary task; domain similarity (temperature-profile correlation, R2 0.41 to 0.68) dominates model and year effects; merging heterogeneous source domains reduces transfer by up to 11 OA points. Not evidence that trees transfer well in general.
9. **Demoted to context: `pankajakshan`** (single-date HSI 3D-CNN, cross-sensor open-set, proxy validation, no labelled target accuracy; its capacity-based mechanism is the one the fill check removed), **`cropformer2023`** (now consistent with the corrected finding rather than an exception, with all §6 caveats), **`rustowicz2019`** (a label-scarcity result, moved to §4 learning curves), **`jin_gee` and `mai2025_hexi`** (trees also pay the penalty; no deep comparator), **`ltae2020`** (the in-region anchor for the architecture we run; its ranking L-TAE >> RF is preserved on our corrected holdout, 0.65 vs 0.60).

**What the comparator set cannot say.** No paper compares ten or more model families under identical input handling on a same-year adjacent tile; BreizhCrops-style regional folds have RF as the only non-deep baseline, `pandzic2026transfer` has two models, and no paper reports a rank correlation between in-region and out-of-region orderings. The published inversions are two- or three-model anecdotes whose direction flips with the deep architecture chosen. That gap is our contribution: a 27-model, consistent-input, adjacent-tile benchmark with a stability statistic (report Spearman rho between the in-region and holdout columns of `experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/table1_filled.csv`).

## 3. Feature-design findings (discounted by tier)

- **Raw bands, especially red-edge and SWIR, carry most of the discriminating signal; derived indices add little once bands are present.** `cnnrf_hybrid` (Tier 3) flags B5/B12; `tempcnn` (Tier 2) finds indices add little; `maize_kenya` (Tier 2) prefers bands to VIs. Transfer-validated corroboration: `mai2025_hexi` (Tier 1) top transfer features are red-edge/SWIR indices and `B5` to `B7`, `B11`/`B12`; `rusnak2023cropmapping` (Tier 1) ranks `B6`, `B7`, `B8A`, `B11` highest with visible bands and `B8` unimportant; `mahmood2025features` (Tier 2) retains SWIR `B11`/`B12`, red edge `B05` and NDVI, with `B11` in August and September the two most important features. Supports keeping `B6`, `B11`, `B12`; `B2` is the weakest member of our set on this evidence.
- **Spectral indices transfer across regions more stably than raw bands** is a Tier-4 claim from `belgiu2016` §6.1, not a measured one; mild support for retaining `EVI`/`hue`.
- **Every month contributes; a late-season month is informative enough that its absence matters.** `rusnak2023cropmapping` finds all months contribute with July most and August least; `obadic2022attention` (Tier 2) shows a one-layer transformer concentrates attention on a few contrast dates, so a missing attended month is a single point of failure; `wardlow` and `penabarragan2011` show stage-dependent separability. This is the feature-side reason the September gap cost 0.08 to 0.14 rather than a trivial amount (§5, §6).
- **Automated time-series feature extraction is under-used in crop classification and its value is operational, not accuracy.** `mann_ethiopia` is the lineage; FreshPRINCE is the general-TSC analogue (`middlehurst2024bakeoff`). Our controlled reviewer-4.2 experiment (raw ≈ `xr_fresh` under transfer, XGBoost ST macro-F1 0.59 vs 0.56, Table `tab:repr`) matches WorldCereal's expert-vs-raw CatBoost (0.810 vs 0.777 geographic cropland, flipped cross-year). `schulthess2023samplesize` notes the Sen2-Agri RF's accuracy depended on a dense cloud-free series, which is the setting where engineered gap-aware statistics buy nothing. New in this rebuild: the NaN-aware `xr_fresh` aggregates are also why the classical models moved only 0.02 to 0.05 under the consistent fill, since a missing month perturbs a statistic rather than inserting an out-of-distribution token (`zhou2023missingness` on trees routing zeroed features; Perez-Lebel-style native missing-value handling).
- **Feature selection does not raise accuracy and does not buy transfer.** `mahmood2025features` (Tier 2, RF, ten splits): removing 80% of 122 features never exceeds all-feature accuracy and costs 2 OA / 4 kappa / 3 macro-F; `rusnak2023cropmapping`: RF alone improved under PCA reduction, the dense models did not; our sparse gate and MiniRocket-L1 arms found no transfer or low-data gain on complete inputs. Orynbaikyzy-style spatial feature selection for RF (cited in the research notes, not briefed) reports the same null. The only reliable return on selection in both literatures is compute (theirs) or robustness to a corrupted input block (ours).
- **Random-kernel PPV features (MiniRocket) are a third representation arm**, zero-sum kernels blind to absolute level (`dempster2021minirocket`). Under consistent fill pixel MiniRocket reached 0.61, the same as logistic regression and the trees, up from 0.50 under zero fill; its published strengths come from long univariate series and do not carry over to T = 10 (§6).
- **Optical+SAR fusion helps in-scene (`optsar_iran`, `convstar_sar`, `kussul2017deep`) and is the structural fix for missing optical months, but is scale- and model-dependent.** `garnot2022multimodal` (Tier 2): fusion models hold almost constant performance with up to 50% of optical dates removed and decision fusion still beats radar-only at 90% removed; `mahmood2025features`: the SAR features that survive elimination are VH from July, the month with no optical scene; `pandzic2026transfer`: S1+S2 is the best modality for RF and the Transformer's transfer advantage grows with sequence richness. Against: `rustowicz2019` (SAR unreliable for smallholders), `mai2025_hexi` (2 of 200 transfer features are Sentinel-1). Our optical-only scope is defensible; SAR belongs in future work as the principled answer to the September gap rather than as a current omission.
- **Irregular-sequence inputs with day-of-year encoding sidestep imputation entirely.** `nyborg2022timematch`, `garnot2022multimodal`, `yuan2021sitsbert`, and `cropformer2023` all feed variable-length series with cloudy dates left in or deleted; `che2024interpolation` passes an explicit per-step mask. Our fixed 10-month grid is what forced the fill decision; rebuilding the L-TAE and Transformer inputs as `(observation, month)` tokens with a padding mask would turn September into an absent token (§7).

## 4. Model-architecture findings (discounted by tier)

- **In-region, dense temporal nets win by thin and unstable margins, and the in-region ranking is also the transfer ranking once inputs are consistent.** `ltae2020`, `teixeira`, `tempcnn`, `kussul2017deep` (Tier 2), `dl_aerial_review` (Tier 4) crown deep models in-region; margins are often 1 to 4 points (`cai_dl`, `convstar_sar`, `hcrnn`) and flip among temporal nets. The revised project result is that this ordering survives the adjacent tile: top six on the corrected holdout are pixel CNN-BiLSTM 0.66, TempCNN 0.65, L-TAE 0.65, L-TAE-S 0.64, Transformer 0.64, TimesNet 0.63, with trees/LR/MiniRocket at 0.59 to 0.61 and pixel TabNet 0.62 (`experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/table1_filled.csv`). `shibuya2025modis` (Tier 1, cross-year) and `butsko2025worldcereal` (Tier 1) show the same rank stability; `pandzic2026transfer` shows an inversion toward the dense model.
- **Under genuine transfer with identical inputs, dense temporal pixel models transfer at least as well as trees; the "sparse models transfer, dense nets overfit" ordering is not supported by any 2019 to 2026 pixel-time-series crop paper with numbers.** `mohammadi2024sourcefree` (RF last cross-regionally), `rusnak2023cropmapping` (RF least robust of four), `pandzic2026transfer` (+20 F1 for the Transformer), `butsko2025worldcereal` (from-scratch Presto over raw CatBoost). The counter-evidence is `wijesingha2024transferability` (0.03 mF1, 2D CNN on four dates, authors say "no substantial differences") and `shibuya2025modis` (cross-year, untuned deep models, about 2 points). The cross-country collapses in `nyborg2022timematch` are deep-vs-deep and attributed to phenological shift and calendar-time encodings. `pankajakshan` is single-date HSI with proxy validation.
- **Every model family pays a transfer penalty; the honest statement is about gap magnitude, not direction.** Trees: `jin_gee` -16 OA points, `mai2025_hexi` macro-F1 0.45 naive, `wijesingha2024transferability` RF -0.12 mF1 (NH), `pandzic2026transfer` RF -38 F1, `rusnak2023cropmapping` RF -6.5 OA, `belgiu2016` §6.1 (RF "not transferable to new areas" in two cited studies). Deep: `wijesingha2024transferability` CNN -0.10 to -0.14, `pandzic2026transfer` Transformer -10, `mohammadi2024sourcefree` cross-time to cross-regional -21 to -31, `butsko2025worldcereal` -0.16 to -0.18 for all. Our corrected gaps (deep pixel -0.06 to -0.14 from 0.72 to 0.78; field XGBoost -0.09; LR -0.005; field L-TAE-S -0.04) sit inside the adjacent-region band of -0.04 to -0.14 reported by `wijesingha2024transferability` and are mild relative to cross-climate pairs, as expected for a same-season neighbour tile (`nyborg2022timematch` `FR1->FR2`, `xie2025transferability` temperature-similarity logic).
- **Robustness to a corrupted or absent time step is a property of training exposure and gating, not of feature-selection sparsity.** `russwurm2020selfattention` (Tier 2): with clouds in training, LSTM and Transformer learn to zero their gradient at single cloudy dates, the shallow TempCNN needs two or three consecutive anomalies, RF on flattened raw inputs collapses (-0.24 kappa); `che2024interpolation`: masking nets trained on gapped series are indifferent to 15% or even 50% missing composites; `wang2024mctnet` (Tier 3): with zero-filled gaps present in training and a mask input, OA moves only 0.973 to 0.968 across missing-rate strata (0.963 to 0.950 without the mask-aware encoding), and deleting observations from fully observed corn pixels at test time hurts more when the gap is contiguous than when it is scattered, the closest SITS analogue to our single missing September; `wang2025jointfrp`: a U-TAE trained on complete series loses 2.7 to 36.3 mean-F1 as test gaps grow, and interpolation makes it worse; `garnot2022multimodal`, `zhang2024maskpstin`, `yuan2021sitsbert`, `tseng2023presto`: training-time temporal dropout, random masking, or masked pre-training are what make a dense attention model tolerate missing dates. Our sparse gate's +0.063 [0.040, 0.086] on the zero-filled month (CNN-BiLSTM, softmax control +0.034 less) and L-TAE-S losing 0.044 under zero fill where L-TAE lost 0.113 are the first such measurements for a sparsemax channel gate in a SITS encoder; TabNet's 0.61 to 0.51 drop shows that sparsity in feature selection is not the same thing as the ability to gate a corrupted observation (the old manuscript conflated the two; `russwurm2020selfattention` separates them). Keep the gate's claim behavioural; InterpreTabNet-style findings that TabNet masks are often dense and Meister-style findings that sparse attention weakly tracks behaviour (research notes, not briefed) argue against any interpretability claim.
- **Pre-training is the lever that separates deep models from trees at small label budgets, and we have not pulled it.** `yuan2021sitsbert` (Tier 3): from-scratch CNN-1D and LSTMs at or below RF at 100 samples per class, pre-trained SITS-BERT at 50 per class matching RF at 500; `butsko2025worldcereal` and `tseng2023presto`: random-init Presto collapses (macro-F1 40.4 to 27.5 within tile); `reuss2026eurocropsml`: SSL pre-training lifts minority-class accuracy 14 points at 500 shots; `pandzic2026transfer`: a source-pre-trained Transformer fine-tuned on about 360 target parcels reaches 91 F1 vs 76 to 84 from scratch. Caveat: foundation-model pre-training corpora have seen unlabelled imagery of the held-out regions, which is target exposure, not domain generalization (§5, §6).
- **Unsupervised adaptation on the unlabelled target is a larger lever than any architectural choice.** `mohammadi2024sourcefree`: SFUDA +9 to +20 macro-F1 cross-regionally with no source data at adaptation time; `nyborg2022timematch`: ShiftAug +10 with zero target data, TimeMatch +19; generic UDA (MMD, DANN, CDAN+E) adds only 4 to 8. Our holdout tile has exactly the ingredients (pre-trained source models, abundant unlabelled target pixels) and the no-target-access premise would need to be relaxed and stated.
- **Learning curves: all families converge near the RF instability threshold, and deep models keep rising where trees and linear models plateau.** Classical side: `schulthess2023samplesize` (Tier 2, RF) finds fewer than 30 fields per crop unstable, breakpoints at 40 to 45 fields per crop (about 500 fields total), OA levelling after about 540 fields; `maxwell2019` (Tier 4) RF loses under 5% from a 70% training cut; `rustowicz2019` and Kerner-style Togo results (research notes) show RF at or above from-scratch deep nets in label-scarce regimes; `portalesjulia2021abandoned` (Tier 3, leaky) shows RF ahead at small fractions and BiLSTM ahead at large ones. Deep side: `reuss2026eurocropsml` from-scratch Transformer rises monotonically to 500 shots with no plateau; `middlehurst2024bakeoff` finds H-InceptionTime improves relative to others as training cases increase; `yuan2021sitsbert` finds from-scratch deep models converge with RF at 50 to 100 per class. Our corrected curves (10 draws at 10% and 15%, 5 at 25 to 75%, single seed, `experiments/sparse_gate_backbones/results_sweep.csv` and `experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/sweep_*_trainmed.csv`): about 0.55 for every model at 30 fields per class (XGBoost 0.51), classical plateau from about 45 fields per class, deep models to 0.65 at 100%, LR 0.61, XGBoost 0.61, TabNet 0.57. The published near-flat L-TAE curve was a zero-fill artifact. No paper places both families on one out-of-region learning curve; the conjunction is ours. Note that `xie2025transferability` warns the monotonic gain holds only when added data come from the same domain.
- **Field/parcel aggregation remains a variance-reduction lever, but its interaction with the artifact needs a new account.** `ltae2020` PSE, `penabarragan2011`, `kussul2017deep` parcel filtering, `zhang2024maskpstin` PSAE. Under consistent fill the pixel models with FID majority vote beat their field-level counterparts (TempCNN 0.65 vs 0.61, L-TAE 0.65 vs 0.60, CNN-BiLSTM 0.66 vs 0.59); field-averaged TabNet still falls to 0.49 from pixel 0.62, and the old explanation (robustness already derived from sparse selection) is gone. `garnot2022multimodal`'s finding that decision fusion across independent sub-models is the most resilient scheme suggests the pixel-vote is itself a decision-fusion that damped the artifact.
- **Ensembling per se is not the lever, but a tree-plus-dense ensemble is now an open question.** `middlehurst2024bakeoff`: no significant ensembling effect on ranks; `shwartzziv2022tabular`: deep-plus-XGBoost ensemble best (2.32% relative deterioration vs 3.34% XGBoost alone, 6.91% deep-only), trees the component that cannot be dropped, three validation-loss-ordered members suffice. Our published vote mixed models that had seen different holdout inputs; it should be re-run on the filled holdout (§7).
- **The sparse inductive bias (tree splits, sparsemax masks) is still a real mechanism for in-distribution robustness to irrelevant features** (`breiman2001`, `friedman2001`, `chen2016`, `ke2017`, `tabnet_orig`, `sparsemax`; `zhou2023missingness` Table 1 redundant-features case: XGBoost source matches its oracle while the linear model collapses), but it is scenario-dependent (`zhou2023missingness` confounded case: XGBoost source 2.4x its oracle) and it is not a transfer property. The old §5 backbone is retained only in that narrowed form.

## 5. Conceptual and theoretical framing

- **Missingness shift is the name for what we found, and the manuscript should adopt it.** `zhou2023missingness`: source and target differ only in their missing-data mechanisms; without indicators this is neither covariate nor label shift, and the source-optimal predictor can be arbitrarily worse than predicting the label mean; with indicators appended it reduces to covariate shift (Proposition 1) and often needs no adaptation. Our old pipeline met the definition almost literally: training missingness zero for every month, holdout 0.41 for one month, no indicator, a zero that looked like a legitimate standardized value. The same paper predicts the old ordering (trees route a zeroed feature down a branch; affine layers multiply it into every output) and supplies the two remedies we tested (re-mask the source to match the target, i.e. temporal dropout; move the target toward the source, i.e. training-median fill) plus the one we have not (an indicator channel). Its UCAR assumption is violated by a band-coherent, tile-structured monthly gap, so only the conceptual results carry over.
- **The mechanism of the damage is the variable sparsity problem, aggravated by standardization.** `yi2020zeroimputation`: under zero imputation the expected pre-activation of every layer is a function of the input's missing fraction, visible before training; batch and layer norm do not repair it; mean/median fills equal zero fill only when zero is already in-range. In our pipeline a raw-scale zero passed through a training-fitted StandardScaler is a large negative z-score, an out-of-distribution value rather than an absent observation, which is why one month in 41% of pixels cost 0.08 to 0.14 while `che2024interpolation` (matched regime, masking) and `wang2025jointfrp` (25% missing, dropped steps, -2.7) report far smaller effects per unit of missing data. The reconciliation across the missing-data literature is the regime: robustness holds when the gap pattern is in the training support (`che2024interpolation`, `russwurm2020selfattention`, Turkoglu- and Zhao-style results in the research notes) and collapses when it is not (`wang2025jointfrp` baseline arm, our case). Zero-filling should be described as nodata encoded as an in-range value, the temporal analogue of the Sentinel-2 Processing Baseline 04.00 offset hazard.
- **Training-time exposure, not architecture, buys missing-date robustness.** `garnot2022multimodal` temporal dropout (+0.6 mIoU clean, marked resilience), `zhang2024maskpstin` random masking (+3.5 OA), `yuan2021sitsbert` and `tseng2023presto` masked pre-training, `nyborg2022timematch` ShiftAug and 30-step subsampling, `zhou2023missingness` Algorithm 1 and Theorem 4.1 (random zeroing acts as L2/dropout regularization). All work by putting gaps inside the training distribution, which is orthogonal to our failure mode and is the control the sparse gate must be measured against (§7). `pham2024temporal` (Tier 1) is the one that ties the remedy to transfer: observation dropout fixes a cross-year loss the authors attribute to clear-observation density rather than phenology, and adds only about 5 OA points in the training year, the same pattern as our month-dropout 2 x 2 (large gain under zero fill on the holdout, none under the training-median fill). The manuscript therefore cannot claim to be first to show missing observations masquerading as a transfer failure; it can claim the first cross-region, same-season case, caused by one fill inconsistency (an out-of-range value in a slot never missing in training, where Pham's zero is a consistent no-observation token), that reorders a multi-family benchmark.
- **Benchmark rankings are properties of pipelines until proven otherwise.** `shwartzziv2022tabular`: a deep tabular model's published advantage vanishes under one tuning budget and training-only standardization; `kapoor2023leakage`: in civil-war prediction the four papers claiming complex-ML superiority were exactly the four with leakage, three from a single pooled imputation, and correction collapsed an AUC gap from 0.14 to 0.01; high-capacity models "over-fit to spurious correlations more easily"; `middlehurst2024bakeoff`: LSTM-FCN excluded for overfitting the test data, HIVE-COTE 2's lead shrinks on 30 new datasets; `russwurm2020selfattention`: TempCNN goes from joint-best to worst deep model when the input changes, interpreted as a property of the input and located with a gradient analysis. Our case has the same structure with the opposite sign: the asymmetric fill deflated the flexible models. The reversal is a result, not an erratum; the framing norm is the re-analysis papers (name the asymmetric step, re-run under one protocol, show both rankings), and Kapoor's taxonomy items L1.2 (imputation applied inconsistently) and L3.3 (spatial sampling bias) are the labels to use. The TabNet holdout-median fill is additionally a mild L1.2 instance (a test-set statistic), removed by the training-tile fill.
- **Domain shift is still the central problem and cross-domain evaluation is still the deployable bar.** `ma2024_tl` names geographic/temporal/sensor shift and the five target-consuming remedies; `butsko2025worldcereal` prescribes geographic and temporal holdouts operationally; `kapoor2023leakage` L3.3 names spatial sampling bias as the first example. Our zero-adaptation setting sits upstream of the adaptation toolkit. What changed is the lesson: a holdout from a different distribution can carry a different missingness pattern, and that pattern must be handled identically for every model before any ranking is read off it. `rusnak2023cropmapping` (observation quality tabulated, no relation to transfer loss when handled identically) and `wijesingha2024transferability` (sensor mix differs between training and target years, acknowledged, unquantified, identical for both models) are the published analogues of the fair condition.
- **Transfer distance is governed by phenological and climatic similarity, not kilometres or model family.** `nyborg2022timematch` (30 to 35 day shifts explain cross-country collapse), `xie2025transferability` (temperature-profile similarity R2 0.41 to 0.68; source-state effect up to 21.5 OA), `wardlow` (signatures shift about one month across sub-regions), `rusnak2023cropmapping` (drought year widens every gap, RF most). Our holdout shares season, rainfall regime, and crop calendar with the training tiles; a summer-rainfall maize tile would be expected to collapse every model that encodes absolute timing, trees on month-indexed `xr_fresh` features included.
- **Attention is an explanation, not the explanation, and it is class-set dependent.** `obadic2022attention`: a one-layer transformer picks the few dates where one class looks unlike the rest; removing the top-5 attended dates costs about 5 points, removing 25 still leaves 85% of reference accuracy; attention moves when the class set changes. `russwurm2020selfattention`: softmax attention over time already becomes nearly one-hot on clear dates without a sparsity prior, which is why forcing sparsemax adds nothing on complete inputs. `hohl2024` documents the field-wide XAI rigor deficit; any attention figure in the supplement should be paired with remove-and-retrain or `dy/dX` attribution.
- **Pre-training is the missing prior and sits on the other side of our framing.** `butsko2025worldcereal`, `tseng2023presto`, `yuan2021sitsbert`, `reuss2026eurocropsml`, `cropformer2023` all show dense temporal transformers transferring or learning from few labels once given a self-supervised prior and failing or stalling without it; `reuss2026eurocropsml` also shows pre-training helps only classes seen during pre-training and that coordinate features (TIML) become a majority-class shortcut, a transfer-motivated inductive bias that bought no transfer, the same shape as our gate result.
- **Operational framing.** `begue2020` motivates spatial/operational transfer as the bar for the Global South; WorldCereal's African holdouts are its hardest (crop-type macro-F1 0.23 to 0.63) and users must retrain on CPU; `schulthess2023samplesize` and `kapoor2023leakage` both argue for reporting uncertainty (nine of twelve civil-war papers reported none). Western Cape fields are large and commercial, so our gaps remain a lower bound on harder regions.

## 6. Project-specific caveats surfaced by the audit

- **The September gap and the three fill operators (the central caveat).** `34S_20E_259N` has no September observation for 40.6% of pixels and 39.3% of fields; the training tiles have none missing. Published handling: raw-sequence deep models (L-TAE, L-TAE-S, TempCNN, Transformer, CNN-BiLSTM, TimesNet, MiniRocket) received a zero reflectance before scaling; pixel TabNet received the holdout's own column medians; `xr_fresh` classical models computed NaN-aware features on the shortened series. On the 1,352 fields with September observed the published predictions already ranked the dense nets first (CNN-BiLSTM 0.66, TempCNN 0.65) and pixel TabNet 14th (0.62); on the 1,063 September-missing fields the dense nets fell to 0.21 to 0.36 while TabNet held 0.58 and L-TAE-S 0.54 (`results/sept_observed_subset.csv`). The manuscript never disclosed the gap (data section lists only the May/June exclusion); it must now state the gap, the fill rule, and the before/after table (`results/fill_comparison.csv`, `results/table1_filled.csv`, paired bootstrap B = 10,000, seed 42). The training-median fill is a single-known-month fix, not a missing-data strategy: `wang2025jointfrp` and `che2024interpolation` show constant or interpolated fills hurt with multi-month or poorly anchored gaps, and `yi2020zeroimputation` shows removing the activation bias does not guarantee recovering lost information.
- **TabNet is not robust to zero fill, and its published lead was its fill.** Controlled re-score of the same checkpoints: median fill 0.609, zero fill 0.509 (535 fields change). TabNet must be removed from any robustness claim; the robustness evidence rests on the gated CNN-BiLSTM (+0.063 [0.040, 0.086], +0.034 over softmax) and L-TAE-S alone. This also removes the manuscript's explanation for why field-averaging hurts TabNet (0.62 pixel vs 0.49 field under consistent fill); the effect stands, the account is open.
- **TabNet checkpoint/prediction mismatch.** The published pixel-TabNet prediction file (2026-02-10) predates its saved checkpoints (2026-02-17/18); the current checkpoints differ on 221 of 2,415 fields and score 0.609 vs 0.603. Every other deep model reproduces field for field. State this in the revision; `kapoor2023leakage` ties info sheets to exactly this kind of computational-reproducibility detail. The February checkpoints load only under pytorch_tabnet 4.6.0 via the loader in `experiments/holdout_fill_check/tabnet_fill.py`.
- **Classical feature-file drift.** `data/combined_test_features.parquet` was built from an older pixel grid and differs from the rebuild on 85 fields (mostly B12); field XGBoost, pixel LR/RF/XGBoost and `xr_fresh` TabNet differ on 118 to 878 fields from library versions; Stacking, Voting, field LightGBM reproduce exactly. The classical comparison is rebuilt-original vs rebuilt-filled in one environment.
- **The `autocorr` bug in the `xr_fresh` extraction.** The original extraction looks up `"autocorr"` in `function_mapping`, which returns None, so the three `autocorr_lag_k` features are copies of `absolute_sum_of_changes` (`experiments/holdout_fill_check/rebuild_xrfresh.py` reproduces this; `results/xrfresh_rebuild_check.csv`). The feature count is therefore effectively 171 distinct of 174; disclose it, and either fix the mapping and re-run the classical models or state that the duplicates carry no information beyond the `absolute_sum_of_changes` column.
- **Patch CNNs were not re-run.** 3D CNN 0.50 and multi-channel 2D CNN 0.45 are published zero-fill numbers; hedge or drop the manuscript sentence attributing their deficit to the absence of sparse selection.
- **Two mis-citations independent of the revision.** (a) `belgiu2016` (with `maxwell2019`) is cited at cea-article.tex line 268 for RF and GBTs surviving application to an unseen region; Belgiu and Dragut §6.1 reports the opposite (OA decreases when RF is trained on one area and applied to another; a cited vegetation model "was not transferable to new areas"), and Maxwell excludes deep learning by design and has no spatial-transfer evidence. Full table in `reports/Missing data robustness novelty check.md`.

Gaps are a conservative lower bound on cross-climate transfer; state that this is the operational case for scaling a provincial map, not the cross-continent stress test.

- **We lack an explicit red-edge band**; a feature-set limitation, not a fixable gap (`teixeira`, `cnnrf_hybrid`, `rusnak2023cropmapping`, `mahmood2025features`).
- **Never benchmark against leakage-inflated in-region numbers** (`hcrnn` 97.6%, `multimodal_naip` 98.4%, `teixeira` 99.1%, `portalesjulia2021abandoned` 98.2%, `yuan2021sitsbert` 93 to 98%, `shibuya2025modis` same-year 0.96 to 0.99, `mai2025_hexi` in-target 0.92, WorldCereal random-split 0.81) nor against UCR/UEA accuracies.
