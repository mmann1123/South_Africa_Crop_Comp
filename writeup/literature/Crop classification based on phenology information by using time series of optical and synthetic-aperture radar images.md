# Crop classification based on phenology information by using time series of optical and synthetic-aperture radar images

**Citation:** Fatemeh Kordi and Hossein Yousefi (2022). *Remote Sensing Applications: Society and Environment*, Vol. 27, Article 100812. DOI: 10.1016/j.rsase.2022.100812
**BibTeX key:** `kordi2022phenology`

## Objectives

The study tests whether fusing optical (Landsat-8) and SAR (Sentinel-1) time series, plus a digital elevation model (DEM), and conditioning classification on extracted crop phenology, improves crop-type mapping when optical imagery is degraded by cloud cover. The setting is the Miandoab plain in the Lake Urmia catchment, northwest Iran, mapping five major crops: alfalfa, wheat, sugar beet, apple, and grape. The phenology angle is operationalized by extracting start-of-season, length-of-season, and end-of-season parameters from an NDVI time series via the `TIMESAT` model, then selecting the best images/VIs for separation.

## Methods

Inputs are multi-temporal Landsat-8 Level-2A surface reflectance (resampled 30 m to 10 m by cubic convolution) and Sentinel-1 C-band dual-pol (VV, VH) GRD imagery (Lee-3 speckle filter; two derived bands `VV+VH` and `VH/VV`), plus an SRTM DEM (30 m). Cloud gaps in the optical series were filled by linear temporal interpolation (Iota2/Inglada method) so a Landsat-8 image was available every 16 days. Phenological parameters from `TIMESAT` informed feature/image selection. A Support Vector Machine (`SVM`) classifier was trained on 18,500 labeled points from 2014-2015 ground truth (crop calendar, harvested-point locations, cadastral map). The headline comparison is fused multi-source (optical + SAR + DEM) versus single-sensor inputs.

**Evaluation protocol:** A pooled, within-scene, single-year point-based split — 18,500 ground points divided 50% train / 50% validation, balanced per class, all from one Landsat-8 frame over the Miandoab plain in the 2014 season. There is no spatially disjoint holdout, no cross-year test, and no k-fold cross-validation. The split is point-wise, not field-wise (`FID`-disjoint): the paper does not state that train and validation points are drawn from different fields, so points from the same cadastral parcel can fall on both sides of the split. Validation points are spatially interspersed across the same scene. This sits near the leakage-prone end of the spectrum — pooled within-scene point split — and reports overall accuracy and Kappa only, with no per-class F1 or recall, so minority-crop performance is invisible.

## Key Findings

The fused optical + SAR + DEM model reached overall accuracy 89% / Kappa 0.78, versus 77% / 0.64 for single-sensor inputs — a roughly 12-point OA gain from multi-source fusion. Classification performance rose through the season as inter-crop differences widened, peaking around July. The DEM contributed because crops in the area grow at different elevations. The authors emphasize that no single optimal date suffices and that the cloud-driven gap-filling, while necessary, injects interpolated (non-observed) values that limit accuracy. Optical generally outperformed SAR alone; SAR's value was as a cloud-independent complement.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This is the SAR-fusion comparator. Our pipeline is deliberately optical-only (`B2`, `B6`, `B11`, `B12`, `EVI`, `hue` from Sentinel-2, no SAR), and this paper quantifies what optical-only forgoes: a ~12-point OA gain from adding C-band SAR plus DEM in a cloud-prone setting. That gain, however, is measured under a pooled within-scene split that almost certainly inflates absolute accuracy, so it is an upper bound on what fusion would buy us, not a transfer-validated figure. The phenology-conditioning idea (align/select features by growth stage) parallels our temporal-feature engineering and our exclusion of low-quality months. The choice of `SVM` — a margin-based, non-tree classical learner — and the absence of any deep temporal network keeps this on the classical-ML side of our comparison, but the paper offers no inductive-bias contrast and no spatial-transfer evidence. The DEM-helps-because-elevation-stratifies-crops point is a reminder that in our flatter Western Cape tiles, terrain priors may matter less.

## Evaluation Caveats

The 50/50 point split is the chief weakness: it is point-wise rather than `FID`-disjoint, so spatial autocorrelation between adjacent pixels of the same parcel can leak across train and validation, inflating the 89% OA. There is no spatially disjoint holdout and no cross-year validation, so nothing here measures the misranking-under-transfer effect central to our manuscript. Only overall accuracy and Kappa are reported — no macro-F1, no per-class F1, no recall — so the five-crop result masks any poor minority-crop performance (e.g., whether sugar beet or apple is reliably recovered is unknown). Cloud gap-filling by linear interpolation substitutes synthetic for observed reflectance, a known source of optimism. Compute cost is not assessed. The fusion benefit is real and well-motivated but should be cited as a within-scene, single-year, point-split result (OA 89% / Kappa 0.78 vs 77% / 0.64), not as a transfer-robust improvement.
