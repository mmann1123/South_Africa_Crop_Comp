# Deep Learning Applications for Crop Mapping Using Multi-Temporal Sentinel-2 Data and Red-Edge Vegetation Indices: Integrating Convolutional and Recurrent Neural Networks

**Citation:** Tufail, R., Tassinari, P., & Torreggiani, D. (2025). Deep Learning Applications for Crop Mapping Using Multi-Temporal Sentinel-2 Data and Red-Edge Vegetation Indices: Integrating Convolutional and Recurrent Neural Networks. *Remote Sensing*, 17(18), 3207. DOI: 10.3390/rs17183207
**BibTeX key:** `tufail2025deep`

## Objectives

The paper benchmarks four hybrid CNN-RNN deep-learning architectures for multi-temporal crop classification from Sentinel-2 in a complex agricultural region of Northern Italy (Emilia-Romagna, extending into Lombardy and Veneto; ~22,446 km²). The four models are `1D CNN-LSTM`, `1D CNN-GRU`, `2D CNN-LSTM`, and `2D CNN-GRU`. The stated goals: test the synergy of convolutional (spatial/spectral) and recurrent (temporal-phenology) layers; assess whether red-edge-based vegetation indices improve discrimination; and determine whether adding 2D spatial context outperforms purely temporal-spectral 1D inputs. The crops include winter wheat, maize, rapeseed, barley, forage, sorghum, soybean, sugar beet, sunflower, plus other/non-crop/urban (12 classes total).

## Methods

Inputs are 11 cloud-filtered (<5%) Sentinel-2 dates spanning Nov 2022–Aug 2023, resampled to 10 m via SNAP GPT. Per date, 14 features are prepared: 10 spectral bands plus NDVI and three red-edge indices (`IRECI`, `MTCI`, `S2REP`). Note the band set overlaps ours (`B2,B6,B11,B12` are among the 10) but adds red-edge bands (`B5,B7,B8A`) and explicit red-edge indices — a richer spectral design than our `B2,B6,B11,B12,EVI,hue`. Ground truth: 2171 polygons digitized from the OneSoil portal, validated against Sentinel-2 phenology. The `1D` models ingest sequences of shape `(2171, 11, 14)` — 11 time steps × 14 features per polygon. The `2D` models extract `16×16` spatial patches per polygon over the 11 steps (TimeDistributed Conv2D → flatten → stacked LSTM/GRU). Both families use stacked Conv layers (1D: 128/64 filters, kernel 7; 2D: 32/64 filters, 3×3, with residual blocks), bidirectional LSTM/GRU (256→128 units), dot-product self-attention, dense head, softmax. Training: Adam, sparse categorical cross-entropy, 100 epochs with early stopping, ReduceLROnPlateau, class weights (scikit-learn balanced) to handle imbalance.

**Evaluation protocol:** Stratified random hold-out within a single scene/region: 60% of polygons for training, 20% validation, 20% test, with stratified sampling across crop classes. This is in-region, single-season validation — a polygon-level split within one contiguous study area. The split is by polygon (so whole fields go to one partition, reducing same-field pixel leakage at the patch level), but it is NOT spatially disjoint: train and test polygons are interleaved across the same landscape and tile, so adjacent-field spatial autocorrelation and shared atmospheric/phenological conditions are fully shared between train and test. There is no spatially separated holdout tile, no cross-year, and no cross-region transfer. Reproducibility: each model run 5 times with different seeds; OA reported as mean±SD; paired two-sided t-tests on OA across seeds establish significance of model differences. Metrics: overall accuracy and macro-F1 are primary; per-class precision/recall/F1 and confusion matrices reported; compute (training/inference time) is reported.

## Key Findings

All four models score extremely high in-region. `2D CNN-GRU` leads with OA 99.12%±0.29 and macro-F1 99.14%±0.29, closely followed by `2D CNN-LSTM` (98.51% / 98.58%). The `1D` variants trail substantially: `1D CNN-GRU` 93.46%/93.21% and `1D CNN-LSTM` 92.54%/92.11%. Paired t-tests confirm all pairwise differences are statistically significant (p<0.05; the 2D vs. 2D comparison only marginally so at p=0.046). The headline conclusion: adding 2D spatial context (field boundaries, canopy texture) on top of temporal-phenology recurrence gives a ~5–6 point OA jump over 1D-only models, and GRU slightly edges LSTM while training faster. Red-edge indices plus spectral bands are credited with enabling discrimination of spectrally similar crops. Compute: 2D models cost far more (2D CNN-LSTM ~1h13m training / ~3h inference vs. 1D CNN-GRU ~3m / ~24m), and GRU is consistently faster than LSTM at equal capacity.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This paper is a textbook example of the evaluation pattern our manuscript critiques, and is most useful precisely as a foil. Its near-perfect 99% in-region scores are obtained under stratified random polygon hold-out within a single tile/season — exactly the "conventional in-region validation" that our central finding shows systematically misranks models and overstates transferability. By our thesis, the dense 2D CNN-RNN models that win here (spatially convolved + recurrent, the densest in the comparison) are the architecture family we expect to overfit the training region and collapse out-of-sample; the paper never tests that, so its ranking (`2D CNN-GRU` > `1D` models) is unvalidated against spatial transfer. The 99% ceiling itself is a red flag for leakage-by-spatial-autocorrelation: train and test polygons share the same landscape, atmosphere, and phenology, and 2D patches especially benefit from this shared context. The contrast with our holdout-tile results is the citation hook: where this study finds dense 2D spatiotemporal nets best, our disjoint-tile evaluation reorders models and favors sparse transferable ones. On feature design, the paper is a useful reference point — it shows red-edge bands/indices help, which our `B2,B6,B11,B12,EVI,hue` (no red-edge) design trades away; it confirms multi-temporal sequences (11 dates) carry the phenological signal we also exploit; and it confirms field/polygon-level aggregation and class-weighting matter. Its good practices (multi-seed runs, mean±SD, paired t-tests, macro-F1, compute reporting) are worth emulating. But the absence of any spatially disjoint test means its model ranking should be treated as in-region only and explicitly contrasted with our out-of-sample reordering.

## Evaluation Caveats

- In-region validation only: stratified random 60/20/20 polygon split within one scene and one season (2022–2023). No spatially disjoint holdout, no cross-year, no cross-sensor transfer. The 99% scores measure interpolation across an interleaved landscape, not generalization.
- Spatial leakage risk is high despite polygon-level splitting: train and test fields are spatially interspersed in the same tile, sharing atmospheric, illumination, and phenological conditions; 2D CNN patches additionally absorb neighboring-field context. This plausibly inflates the 2D models' apparent advantage. Field-wise (polygon) splitting prevents same-field pixel leakage but does NOT achieve spatial separation.
- Class imbalance is acknowledged and partly mitigated (balanced class weights), and crucially the paper DOES report macro-F1 alongside OA plus per-class metrics and confusion matrices — better than accuracy-only. But with OA and macro-F1 both ~99%, the metrics cannot reveal minority-class transfer behavior, which is untested.
- Sensor/feature design: optical-only Sentinel-2, no SAR; richer than ours via red-edge bands and indices (`IRECI`, `MTCI`, `S2REP`); multi-temporal engineered into 11-step sequences; granularity is polygon-level with both 1D sequence and 2D patch variants. Differences from our band set matter when comparing absolute numbers.
- Silence on the question that matters most: the paper never asks whether the in-region ranking (dense 2D nets winning) survives spatial transfer — the exact gap our study fills. Cite the protocol and the untested-transfer silence, not the 99% headline.
