# Semantic Segmentation of Crop Type in Africa: A Novel Dataset and Analysis of Deep Learning Methods

**Citation:** Rustowicz, R., Cheong, R., Wang, L., Ermon, S., Burke, M., & Lobell, D. (2019). Semantic Segmentation of Crop Type in Africa: A Novel Dataset and Analysis of Deep Learning Methods. *Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR) Workshops* — Computer Vision for Agriculture / Vision for All Seasons, pp. 75–82. CVF open-access proceedings (Sustainability and AI Lab, Stanford University). DOI: not found in PDF (CVPR Workshop / CVF open-access paper; no DOI registered in Crossref — one Crossref title search returned no matching record, so the citation is given as the CVF proceedings entry; **unverified DOI**).

## Objectives

Release the **first crop-type semantic-segmentation dataset for smallholder farms in the Global South** — specifically Ghana and South Sudan — and provide the first systematic deep-learning evaluation of crop-type segmentation under the distinctive constraints of data-scarce, smallholder African agriculture: irregularly shaped and small fields, frequent cloud cover, small plots, and severe scarcity of labeled training data. Secondary aims: benchmark two deep architectures (a 2D U-Net + convolutional-LSTM and a 3D U-Net) against a random-forest baseline; demonstrate the value of high-resolution, high-frequency PlanetScope imagery for small fields; and show the deep methods also set state-of-the-art on a data-rich comparator (Germany). This paper is **highly relevant** to our study as a Global-South smallholder analogue and as a dense deep-net comparator with an explicit train/test spatial split — it directly probes the data-scarcity, field-size, and cloud-cover regime our manuscript also confronts.

## Methods

**Task:** per-pixel semantic segmentation — each pixel in a temporal stack of satellite imagery is labeled as one of several crop types. **Data fusion / sensors:** Sentinel-2 (`S2`, ten bands: blue, green, red, NIR, four red-edge, two SWIR, 10 m, 6–12 day revisit), Sentinel-1 (`S1`, VV+VH SAR), and **PlanetScope** (3 m, 1–2 day revisit, four bands) — i.e. **optical + radar + commercial high-res**, unlike our optical-only `S2` design. Day-of-year is added as a band; NDVI and GCVI (green chlorophyll vegetation index) are constructed for `S2`, VH/VV ratio for `S1`. Ground truth: geo-referenced field-boundary polygons with crop labels (World Food Programme / DIRTS sources), randomly jittered up to 3 km for privacy. Subsets use the top four crops (>90% of crop area): Ghana — Maize 51%, Groundnut 15%, Rice 14%, Soya Bean 10%; South Sudan — Sorghum 67%, Maize 10%, Rice 9%, Groundnut 7%. Germany (reference) reuses the Rußwurm & Körner [27] 137k-parcel, 17-class dataset. **Architectures:** (1) 2D U-Net encoder/decoder with a C-LSTM aggregating temporal observations (extends Rußwurm & Körner's recurrent line by putting an encoder *before* the C-LSTM); (2) 3D U-Net treating time as a 3D-conv dimension; (3) Random Forest baseline on temporally bucketed/aggregated bands. Trained end-to-end with **weighted cross-entropy** (loss weighted by inverse class frequency) and masking of unlabeled pixels — explicit handling of sparse labels and class imbalance. Tiles: 32×32 px (Ghana/South Sudan), 48×48 px (Germany).

**Evaluation protocol:** Area-of-interest subdivided into pixel-grid tiles and split **80/10/10 train/validation/test**, with the split algorithm constrained to **preserve crop-class proportions** across splits. Metrics: per-class F1 and **macro-average F1** (chosen explicitly because of strong class imbalance), plus overall accuracy and kappa. This is a **within-region, tile-level split** within each country's AOI — the split is per-tile (so test tiles are distinct image patches from training tiles, with the 3 km jitter adding incidental spatial separation), but it is **not a spatially disjoint distant holdout, not cross-region, and not cross-year** (Germany results are even on a custom class-balanced split of the same 2016 data). On our protocol spectrum it sits at tile-wise holdout within one AOI/year — better than pooled-pixel k-fold, weaker than a disjoint-tile holdout. The smallholder train/test tiles are interspersed within the same Ghana / South Sudan AOIs.

## Key Findings

On the **data-rich Germany** set the 2D U-Net + CLSTM reached macro-F1 91.4 / overall accuracy 95.8 / kappa 0.947, beating the prior Rußwurm & Körner [27] result by ~8 macro-F1 points and ~6.7 accuracy points; the 3D U-Net hit macro-F1 90.0. On the **data-scarce smallholder** sets, results dropped sharply: **Ghana** best macro-F1 57.3 (2D CNN+CLSTM) / OA 59.9–61.1 / kappa ~0.40, vs RF macro-F1 62.4 — i.e. RF was competitive or better on macro-F1 in Ghana; **South Sudan** best macro-F1 69.7 (3D U-Net) / OA 85.3 / kappa ~0.49, with RF at 66.6. **The deep nets' advantage over RF largely evaporated in the smallholder, cloud-heavy, small-field, label-scarce regime** — the very transfer-fragility our manuscript documents for dense temporal/patch nets. Per-crop F1 was highly uneven: rice was easiest (distinct temporal-spectral signature, e.g. 100% in South Sudan), groundnut hardest (e.g. 2.1–8.5% F1 in some configs) — a stark class-difficulty spread. Ablations found: **S1 (SAR) did not reliably help** (small fields lack differentiating scattering signal); high-res high-frequency Planet imagery *did* improve accuracy (combats cloud cover and small-field mixing); adding NDVI/GCVI gave marginal gains; day-of-year helped inconsistently; and explicitly modeling clouds did not improve peak performance (only convergence speed in Ghana). Random forest produced **less spatially homogeneous within-field predictions** because it ignores contextual information — the authors note this as a visible qualitative weakness of the non-contextual baseline.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This is one of the most directly relevant references for our manuscript. It is a **Global-South smallholder** study facing the same structural challenges we do — small/irregular fields, persistent cloud cover, scarce labels, severe class imbalance — and it benchmarks the **same model families** (a U-Net + C-LSTM dense temporal net, a 3D patch CNN, and a tree-based RF baseline) on `S2` time series. Its headline result is a quantitative instance of our central thesis: in the data-rich, in-distribution Germany setting the dense deep nets dominate (macro-F1 ~91), but in the harder smallholder African setting they collapse toward — and sometimes below — the random forest (Ghana RF macro-F1 62.4 ≥ best deep net 57.3). That is exactly the pattern our study formalizes as misranking under easy validation versus hard out-of-distribution conditions, with sparse/tree-like models holding up comparatively better. Methodological alignment is strong: optical `S2` time series with red-edge and SWIR bands, NDVI/GCVI engineered indices, **macro-F1 as the primary metric chosen explicitly for class imbalance** (matching our macro-F1 + kappa), and **field/parcel-grounded labels**. Their finding that **SAR did not reliably help small fields** lends external support to our optical-only design choice. Their finding that RF predictions are spatially less homogeneous within fields motivates our **field-level aggregation** (per-FID majority vote), which recovers contextual coherence for both tree and dense models. The reused Rußwurm & Körner Germany set ties this paper to our other assigned SITS-RNN comparator.

Where it differs and must be flagged when cited: it uses **SAR + commercial Planet** fusion (we are optical-only `S2`), it is **semantic segmentation** (pixel-dense) rather than per-field aggregated classification (though our pixel/patch models share the dense-prediction lineage), and — critically — its evaluation is a **within-AOI tile split, not a spatially disjoint distant holdout**. So while it demonstrates *cross-difficulty* misranking (data-rich vs data-scarce regions), it does **not** itself run a same-region disjoint-tile generalization test of the kind our `34S_20E_259N` holdout provides. Our study tightens precisely that protocol on a single smallholder region.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **Within-region tile split, not disjoint holdout:** Train/test tiles are interspersed within each country AOI (with only incidental 3 km privacy jitter). No distant-tile, cross-region, or cross-year generalization is tested — the smallholder numbers are in-AOI. Flag: tile-wise holdout, not OOS transfer.
- **Cross-difficulty comparison conflates region and data volume:** The instructive deep-net-collapse signal comes from comparing *different countries/datasets* (Germany vs Ghana/South Sudan) that differ simultaneously in region, field size, cloud cover, class count, and training-set size — so it cannot isolate which factor drives the collapse. Still, the misranking direction is exactly ours.
- **Macro-F1 + class-balanced split (strength):** Primary metric is macro-F1 with kappa, chosen explicitly for imbalance; the split preserves class proportions. Best-practice for the imbalance concerns our manuscript shares.
- **Pixel-level (segmentation) scoring:** Metrics are per-pixel, not field-aggregated; within-field pixel autocorrelation can inflate scores relative to per-FID scoring — the gap our field-level aggregation addresses.
- **Small, jittered, top-4-crop subsets:** Smallholder subsets use only the top four crops and small field counts (South Sudan subset 837 fields), so minority-crop F1 (e.g. groundnut 2–8%) rests on few samples; the 3 km label jitter adds geolocation noise.
- **Optical+radar+commercial sensor stack:** Results are partly attributable to Planet 3 m / `S1` SAR fusion not available in our optical-only `S2` pipeline; their own ablation that SAR does not reliably help small fields is the most transferable sensor-design lesson.
- **What it does not measure:** spatially disjoint same-region transfer, cross-year transfer, field-aggregated (per-FID) accuracy, and which specific smallholder factor (field size vs cloud vs labels) governs the deep-net collapse.
