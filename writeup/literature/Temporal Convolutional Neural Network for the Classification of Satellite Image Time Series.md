# Temporal Convolutional Neural Network for the Classification of Satellite Image Time Series

**Citation:** Pelletier, C., Webb, G. I., & Petitjean, F. (2019). Temporal Convolutional Neural Network for the Classification of Satellite Image Time Series. *Remote Sensing*, 11(5), 523. DOI: 10.3390/rs11050523 (verified via Crossref).

## Objectives

This paper introduces and exhaustively studies **TempCNN** — a 1D convolutional neural network that applies convolutions along the *temporal* axis of Satellite Image Time Series (SITS) to learn spectro-temporal features automatically. The goal is to quantitatively and qualitatively evaluate whether temporal convolutions beat the state of the art (Random Forest) and the leading sequential deep net (Recurrent Neural Networks, GRU-based) for pixel-level land-cover/crop classification, and to provide practical architecture/hyperparameter guidance (filter size, pooling, depth/width, regularization, batch size). We implement TempCNN, so this paper defines the model and characterizes its evaluation regime.

## Methods

The TempCNN baseline stacks three temporal convolutional layers (64 units, filter size 5), one dense layer (256 units), and a softmax — operating on a multi-variate pixel time series. Each pixel is treated as an independent spectro-temporal instance; **no spatial structure is used** (this is a pixel/1D model, not patch-based). Four regularizers: dropout 0.5, L2 weight decay 1e-6, a validation set, and batch normalization. Adam optimization, batch size 32, early stopping. Data: 46 Formosat-2 images (8 m, 2006, near Toulouse, France; Formosat-2 is positioned as a Sentinel-2 analog), 3 optical bands (NIR, Red, Green; Blue discarded), gap-filled by temporal linear interpolation and resampled to a regular 2-day grid (T grows 46→149). Three spectral feature configs: NDVI only, spectral bands only, and bands + NDVI + NDWI + brilliance index. 13 classes (3 winter crops, 5 summer crops, natural classes, urban). Benchmarks: Random Forest (500 trees) and bidirectional GRU-RNN (3 GRUs, 256 units).

**Evaluation protocol:** Reference data are randomly split **60/40 train/test at the polygon (parcel) level**, repeated five times, with neural nets retrained five times per split (25 runs averaged); the validation set is also built so its instances "do not come from the same polygons as the train set." This is **polygon-/parcel-disjoint (FID-disjoint) k-fold within a single 24×24 km scene** — a meaningful step above random pixel splitting because it prevents same-parcel pixels leaking across train/test. **However, it remains in-region:** all polygons are drawn from one Formosat-2 footprint, so train and test parcels are spatially interleaved across the same landscape. There is **no spatially disjoint holdout tile, no cross-year, and no cross-sensor evaluation.** Reported metrics: Overall Accuracy (averaged over folds), plus per-class user's/producer's accuracy and F-score, and qualitative map inspection.

## Key Findings

- TempCNN outperformed both Random Forest and RNNs for SITS classification on the Formosat-2 benchmark, establishing temporal convolutions as more accurate than the prior state of the art (in-region).
- Architecture guidance: temporal convolutions matter most; the spectral-bands+indices configuration helped, but hand-crafted spectral indices added little once bands were present; modest depth (3 conv layers) and width (64) suffice; some computer-vision intuitions (e.g., aggressive pooling) did not transfer.
- The authors argue RNNs are *less* suited to whole-series labeling than TempCNNs because back-prop depth scales with series length, making them harder and slower to train.
- They flag that deep nets are "prone to overfitting because of their very low bias … so many parameters they can fit a very large family of functions," motivating their four-regularizer regime — a tacit acknowledgment of the exact fragility our manuscript exploits.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

TempCNN is one of the dense temporal deep nets we implement and a key character in the manuscript's argument. This paper documents TempCNN's *in-region* superiority (it beats RF and RNN under polygon-disjoint k-fold within a single scene), which is precisely the conventional validation we show **misranks** models. Our finding is that under a spatially disjoint holdout tile, dense temporal nets like TempCNN overfit scene-specific phenology and collapse out-of-sample, ceding the ranking to sparse, feature-selecting models (gradient-boosted trees, TabNet). Pelletier et al.'s own observation that TempCNNs have "very low bias" and need four simultaneous regularizers is the mechanism behind that collapse: high-capacity dense temporal convolution memorizes the training landscape's temporal texture. The shared design ground is strong — optical SITS, temporal linear interpolation gap-filling, spectral indices (NDVI/NDWI vs our EVI/hue), pixel-level modeling — making TempCNN a fair and important comparator in our spectrum. The paper also supports our field-level-aggregation recovery argument indirectly: it works purely at the pixel level and explicitly leaves object/field-level modeling out of scope, so any transfer recovery via FID aggregation is unmeasured here and is our contribution.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **In-region, single-scene evaluation.** Despite the better-than-pixel polygon-disjoint split, all parcels come from one 24×24 km Formosat-2 footprint. This is **FID-disjoint k-fold within one scene, not out-of-sample**; it cannot reveal the overfitting that a spatially disjoint holdout exposes. Treat its "TempCNN beats RF/RNN" result as in-region only.
- **No cross-region, cross-year, or cross-sensor test** — generalization, the property our study cares about most, is entirely unmeasured. This silence is the central caveat.
- **Single sensor/year (Formosat-2, 2006).** Results are tied to one acquisition geometry and growing season; transfer to Sentinel-2 (the claimed analog) and to other regions is asserted by analogy, not shown.
- **Metric is accuracy-led with imbalance present.** Class counts are highly imbalanced (Grassland 202k, Urban 292k pixels vs Pea 9k, Soy 9k); Overall Accuracy is the headline, per-class F-score is reported but no single macro-F1 or Kappa is foregrounded, so minority-crop performance is under-summarized.
- **Optical-only, 3 bands, no SAR**; Blue discarded. Narrower spectral footprint than our `B2,B6,B11,B12,EVI,hue` set (no SWIR), which may limit small-grain separability.
- **Pixel-only, no field aggregation.** Object/field-level classification is explicitly out of scope, so the field-aggregation transfer recovery our manuscript demonstrates for dense models is not addressed.
