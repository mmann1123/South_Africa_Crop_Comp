# Deep Learning Classification of Land Cover and Crop Types Using Remote Sensing Data

**Citation:** Kussul, N., Lavreniuk, M., Skakun, S., & Shelestov, A. (2017). Deep Learning Classification of Land Cover and Crop Types Using Remote Sensing Data. *IEEE Geoscience and Remote Sensing Letters*, 14(5), 778–782. DOI: 10.1109/LGRS.2017.2681128

## Objectives

This letter proposes a multilevel deep-learning architecture for land-cover and crop-type classification from multitemporal, multisource (optical + SAR) satellite imagery, applied to the JECAM test site in the Kyiv region of Ukraine. The core aim is to show that an ensemble of CNNs outperforms an ensemble of MLPs and Random Forest, particularly for discriminating confusable summer crops (maize vs. soybeans), while reaching operationally useful accuracy (>85%) at national scale. A four-level pipeline handles preprocessing (cloud/gap restoration), supervised classification (the CNN core), postprocessing (parcel-based filtering), and geospatial analysis (crop-area estimation).

## Methods

Inputs for the 2015 season: 4 atmospherically-corrected Landsat-8 scenes (6 bands each) and 15 Sentinel-1A SAR scenes (2 polarizations each), forming a per-pixel multitemporal feature vector of dimension 54 (`15×2 + 4×6`). This is an optical+SAR fusion design — distinct from our optical-only Sentinel-2 (`B2,B6,B11,B12,EVI,hue`) — exploiting SAR backscatter to separate spectrally similar crops. Eleven classes: winter wheat, winter rapeseed, spring crops, maize, sugar beet, sunflower, soybeans, plus forest, grassland, bare land, water; the study area is ~28,000 km². Level I uses self-organizing (Kohonen) maps to restore cloud/shadow-masked optical pixels per band. Level II is the supervised core: the authors compare a 1D CNN (convolutions in the spectral domain), a 2D CNN (convolutions in the spatial domain, `7×7` window via sliding-window with 1-pixel step), an ensemble of MLPs (ENN), and Random Forest. Each CNN ensemble has five CNNs differing in hidden-layer width (60/70/80/90/100 neurons); two conv + max-pool layers, ReLU, L2 regularization, dropout 0.5, Adam-like optimizer, batch 32, implemented in TensorFlow. Level III applies parcel-based filtering using field boundaries; Level IV does crop-area estimation.

**Evaluation protocol:** In-region random hold-out by polygon. 547 ground-survey polygons (Table II) were "randomly divided into training (calibration) set (50%) and validation set (50%)." This is a single-region, single-season evaluation within the Kyiv test site; train and test polygons are drawn from the same landscape and the same 19-scene time series. The split is at the polygon level (whole fields assigned to one partition), which guards against the most blatant same-field pixel leakage, but it is NOT spatially disjoint — train and validation fields are interspersed across the same region under identical atmospheric/phenological conditions, and the 2D CNN's `7×7` sliding window additionally pulls in neighboring-field context. There is no spatially separated holdout tile, no cross-year, and no cross-region transfer. The authors frame >85% as "operational at national level," but that claim rests on this in-region 50/50 split. Metrics: per-class Producer's and User's Accuracy (PA/UA) and Overall Accuracy (Table III) — PA/UA give balanced per-class insight, but no macro-F1 or Kappa is reported, and model ranking is driven by OA.

## Key Findings

Overall accuracies: RF 88.7%, MLP-ensemble (ENN) 92.7%, 1D-CNN ensemble 93.5%, 2D-CNN ensemble 94.6%. The 2D-CNN ensemble achieved the highest PA and UA for ALL classes and outperformed the 1D-CNN, which in turn beat the MLP ensemble and RF (RF was the weakest). The headline improvement is on confusable summer crops: the 2D-CNN ensemble lifted maize to PA 94.6% / UA 93.6% and soybeans to PA 86.9% / UA 89.1%, the classes that drove most of the RF confusion. The authors attribute the CNN advantage to building a hierarchy of local and sparse spatial-spectral features that MLP/RF (global feature transforms) cannot. A noted side effect: the 2D-CNN's spatial smoothing misclassifies small linear objects (roads, narrow forest strips). Training cost: MLP ensemble ~10 min, 1D-CNN ~4 h, 2D-CNN ~12 h.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This is a frequently-cited foundational result for CNN crop classification, and it is most useful to our manuscript as another instance of the in-region evaluation pattern we critique — plus a useful SAR-fusion contrast. The ranking it establishes (2D-CNN > 1D-CNN > MLP-ensemble > RF) is obtained entirely under a random 50/50 polygon split within one region and season. By our thesis, this is precisely the conventional in-region validation that can misrank models against spatial transfer: the densest model (2D-CNN with spatial context) wins, but the paper never tests whether that win survives a spatially disjoint holdout. The 2D-CNN's reliance on `7×7` spatial windows — which absorb neighboring-field context shared between interspersed train/test fields — is exactly the mechanism by which dense spatial nets can overfit a training region and inflate in-region scores, the behavior our holdout-tile reordering exposes. The RF-is-weakest finding here also contrasts with our out-of-sample result that sparse tree models transfer best; the difference is the evaluation bar, not the models. On design, the paper is a clean reference for the value of optical+SAR fusion (something our optical-only study deliberately forgoes) and for parcel/field-level postprocessing as a variance-reduction step — analogous to our field-level FID aggregation. Cite it for the in-region CNN>RF ranking and the SAR-fusion design, while noting it offers no spatial-transfer evidence and reports OA/PA/UA but no macro-F1 or Kappa.

## Evaluation Caveats

- In-region validation only: random 50/50 polygon split within a single region (Kyiv) and single season (2015). No spatially disjoint holdout, no cross-year, no cross-sensor transfer. The >85% "operational" claim is an in-region interpolation result.
- Spatial leakage: split is polygon-level (whole fields to one partition), preventing same-field pixel leakage, but train and validation fields are spatially interspersed across the same tile under shared atmospheric/phenological conditions; the 2D-CNN `7×7` sliding window further mixes in adjacent-field context, plausibly inflating its margin. Polygon-disjoint ≠ spatially separated.
- Metric balance: reports per-class PA and UA (balanced per-class insight) and OA, but NO macro-F1 and NO Kappa; class imbalance (winter wheat and maize dominate area) is not explicitly corrected, and model ranking is by OA. Cite PA/UA per-class, not OA alone, when discussing minority crops.
- Sensor/feature design: optical (Landsat-8) + SAR (Sentinel-1A) fusion, multitemporal (19 scenes), pixel-level for 1D / spatial-window for 2D, with Kohonen-map gap restoration and parcel-based postprocessing. The SAR fusion and the band set differ from our optical-only Sentinel-2 pipeline, so absolute numbers are not directly comparable.
- Silences: no spatial-transfer, cross-year, or cross-region robustness is measured; minority-class transfer behavior and behavior under domain shift are untested — the gap our study addresses.
