## Objectives

Develop and validate the Object-based Crop Identification and Mapping (OCIM) methodology — OBIA segmentation followed by decision-tree (DT) classification — to map a large number of crop types and their within-field status in a diverse Mediterranean cropland (Yolo County, California). Specific objectives: (1) relate multiple vegetation indices (VIs) and textural features to crops at parcel scale; (2) quantify the contribution of visible, NIR and SWIR spectral regions to crop-type and management-practice identification; (3) assess how different growing-season periods drive crop discrimination.

## Methods

ASTER imagery (green/red + NIR at 15 m; six SWIR bands at 30 m → resampled to 15 m), three 2006 acquisition windows (mid-spring May 7, early-summer June 24, late-summer Sept 12), atmospherically corrected via FLAASH (MODTRAN4). **Twenty VIs** per date spanning visible, visible–NIR, NIR–SWIR, visible–SWIR and SWIR families (`NDVI`, `GNDVI`, `RDVI`, `SRI`, `MSR`, `MCARI`, `TCARI`, `TVI`, `NDI5`, `NDI7`, `NDSVI`, `NDRI`, `NDTI`, `LCA`, `ANSI`, `SINDRI`, etc.), each tied to a crop attribute (greenness, canopy structure, water/moisture, residue, non-photosynthetic components).

OCIM proceeds in two phases. **Phase 1 — segmentation:** Definiens multiresolution segmentation produces a hierarchical object network (tiny objects → 5–10 ha fields → >10 ha merged fields), parameters optimized against 350 reference fields at 94% averaged accuracy. **Phase 2 — DT classification:** a feature space of **336 object features** in three groups — object spectral (mean + SD of every VI and band), object texture (GLCM homogeneity, dissimilarity, entropy), and sub-object texture (relative area of sub-objects below VI thresholds) — feeds a recursive-partitioning DT (JMP 8) using likelihood-ratio chi-square splits. The DT is a sparse, interpretable, feature-selecting classifier that reduces 336 candidates to a small set of physically meaningful, thresholded splits and represents intra-class variation through multiple phenological sub-classes per crop (Alfalfa_1–4, Tomato_1–6, etc.). Four scenarios (three periods, or pairs) isolate each season's contribution.

**Evaluation protocol:** Independent three-way split of 1300 segmented fields (~20% of Yolo County's cropland): training and validation of n/4 each (with ten-replicate 50/50 random cross-validation for DT pruning and feature selection), and a held-out **independent test set of 650 fields** scored with a **field-scale confusion matrix** (overall + per-class producer's/user's accuracy and kappa). This is a **within-region, field-level (object-level) independent holdout in a single county and single year (2006)**: test objects are disjoint from training objects, and scoring is at field scale (no pixel-autocorrelation inflation) — but the test fields are spatially interspersed across the same county, and there is no second region, no second year, and no second sensor. On our protocol spectrum: field-wise holdout within one scene/year — not a spatially disjoint distant tile, not cross-year/cross-sensor.

## Key Findings

Three-period OCIM reached **79% overall accuracy, kappa 0.75** (overall producer's 77%, user's 80%) on 650 independent test fields across 13 crops in four super-groups. DT cross-validation error was 9% (three periods), degrading to 12/13/16% as mid-spring/early-summer/late-summer were removed — late-summer was the most decisive period because intra-class variability peaks at season's end. **Spectral VIs supplied 86–94% of discrimination (NDVI ~50% alone); texture only 6–14%** — yet texture (GLCM homogeneity/dissimilarity) was indispensable for separating spectrally similar permanent crops (alfalfa↔walnut, almond, vineyard). SWIR VIs (`NDSVI`, `NDI7`, `NDTI`, `LCA`, `SINDRI`) were essential for moisture, residue, non-photosynthetic and bare-soil signals. Late-summer features made ~60% of splits, mid-spring ~30%, early-summer ~10%. Of 20 candidate VIs only a subset were selected (RDVI, SRI, MSR, TCARI, TVI, NDIfA3-5 used in none); of 336 features the three-period model retained just 24 (16 spectral, 6 textural, 2 hierarchical) — a vivid demonstration of aggressive feature selection. Per-crop: rice 93–95% (distinctive flooded NIR/SWIR signal); alfalfa/vineyard/corn/sunflower/meadow >80% producer's; almond and rye lowest (~50–60%; almond↔vineyard, rye↔deciduous-meadow). Intra-class sub-classes encoded local planting dates, orchard structure and tillage/residue management.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This publisher PDF is the authoritative source for the classical, tree-based, feature-selecting comparator in our manuscript and a precedent for our field-level aggregation. Its OBIA-to-DT architecture is the archetype of the "sparse, feature-selecting, tree-like" model class our study finds transfers best out-of-sample; the explicit reduction of 336 features to 24 meaningful splits is the very mechanism we argue protects against scene-specific overfitting that sinks dense temporal/patch nets. It corroborates our feature/sensor design: optical-only (no SAR), multi-temporal phenology, heavy SWIR reliance (our `B11`,`B12`), engineered indices over raw pixels (our `EVI`, band ratios, `hue`), and field/object granularity matching our per-FID outputs. The dominance of NDVI plus the necessity of SWIR-residue indices for senescent vegetation underpins our band rationale (`B2`,`B6`,`B11`,`B12`,`EVI`,`hue`). The phenological sub-class strategy is a conceptual model for absorbing the intra-class heterogeneity of our lucerne/medics and small-grain-grazing classes. Table 4 (cleanest in this PDF) quantifying late-summer's ~59% contribution underscores that *which months* are observed materially governs separability — relevant to our deliberate exclusion of months 05 and 06 (cloud/missing data).

On our central validation thesis, this paper supplies a positive-control data point rather than a cautionary one: its strong field-level result is from a within-region/year holdout, so it cannot by itself demonstrate geographic transfer — but as a sparse DT scored at field level it is precisely the model archetype our manuscript predicts should hold up best under a disjoint holdout. Cite for the protocol (independent field-level holdout, single region/year) and for the feature-selection-aids-transfer argument, not for any tested claim of cross-region robustness.

## Evaluation Caveats

- **No spatial disjointness:** Test fields are interspersed across the same county and the same 2006 acquisition; field-disjoint but not space-disjoint or year-disjoint. Apparent accuracy benefits from a single shared crop calendar, climate, soils and sensor geometry. Transfer to another region/year is never tested.
- **Field-level scoring (strength):** Accuracy on independent test fields via a field-scale confusion matrix avoids pixel-autocorrelation inflation — methodologically aligned with our field-level macro-F1.
- **Balanced metric, partial:** Reports kappa (0.75) and per-class producer's/user's accuracy, but **no macro-F1**; overall accuracy on a non-uniform 13-class set still favors common crops.
- **Single-year, single-sensor, best-conditions year:** 2006 chosen for cloud-free coverage on all three key dates; cloud robustness is sidestepped. ASTER only.
- **Author-acknowledged transfer silence:** The conclusions explicitly note rules are calibrated to local crop calendars and that "future analyses are necessary to evaluate robustness" in other regions — the authors themselves flag the untested generalization.
- **What it does not measure:** geographic transfer, cross-year/cross-sensor transfer, macro-F1, and accuracy under cloud contamination.
