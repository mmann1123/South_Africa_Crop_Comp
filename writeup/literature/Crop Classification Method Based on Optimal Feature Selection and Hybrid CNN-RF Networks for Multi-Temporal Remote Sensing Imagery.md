# Crop Classification Method Based on Optimal Feature Selection and Hybrid CNN-RF Networks for Multi-Temporal Remote Sensing Imagery

**Citation:** Shuting Yang, Lingjia Gu, Xiaofeng Li, Tao Jiang, and Ruizhi Ren (2020). *Remote Sensing*, 12(19), 3119. DOI: 10.3390/rs12193119

## Objectives

The paper proposes (1) an Optimal Feature Selection Method (`OFSM`) that hybridizes filter, embedded, and wrapper feature selection to cut a 234-feature pool down to a compact, low-redundancy subset, and (2) two hybrid CNN-RF networks (`Conv1D-RF` and `VGG-RF`) that replace a deep network's final fully-connected (FC) softmax layer with a Random Forest classifier. The stated motivation is to balance classification accuracy against processing time on high-volume multi-temporal Sentinel-2 data, and explicitly to reduce overfitting that arises when an FC layer is trained on inadequate samples. The study maps four land-cover classes (rice, urban, corn, soybean) in Jilin province, northeast China.

## Methods

Three cloud-free Sentinel-2 scenes (28 Jun, 18 Jul, 11 Sep 2017) over one study area; ten 10-m/20-m bands resampled to 10 m. From these, 234 features were extracted: 30 raw spectral, 30 graph-based segmentation, 9 HSI color, 45 spectral-index (`NDVI`, `EVI`, `SAVI`, `GARI`, etc.), and 120 GLCM texture features. `OFSM` runs in three stages: (1) Spearman rank correlation between each feature and the label, drop `|rho|`<0.2 (234 to 88 features); (2) Spearman correlation between surviving features, drop one of any pair with `rho`>0.9 (88 to 33); (3) nested RF models ranked by feature importance, recursively eliminate the weakest until 16 features remain. The two hybrid nets feed the penultimate dense layer's high-dimensional vector (Conv1D: 512-d; VGG: 1024-d) into an RF instead of softmax. Baselines: traditional feature selection (`RF-FI` embedded, `RF-RFE` wrapper) and mainstream nets (`LSTM-RF`, `ResNet`, `U-Net`).

**Evaluation protocol:** A pooled, single-scene, single-year pixel split — the reference dataset of ~177k pixels was randomly assigned 25% train / 25% validation / 50% test, all from one study area in the 2017 season. There is no spatially disjoint holdout, no cross-year transfer, and no field-wise (`FID`-disjoint) splitting. The split is purely pixel-random, so adjacent autocorrelated pixels — and pixels from the same field — almost certainly appear in both train and test. This is the canonical spatial-leakage configuration and inflates absolute accuracy substantially. The paper does, commendably, report a balanced per-class panel (precision, recall, F1, IoU per class) plus OA and Kappa, so minority-class behavior is visible — but all of it is within-scene.

## Key Findings

`Conv1D-RF` + `OFSM` achieved the best overall accuracy (94.27%, Kappa 0.917), beating `VGG-RF` (93.23%), bare `Conv1D` (92.59%), and bare `VGG` (91.89%); it also beat mainstream `ResNet` (93.55%), `LSTM-RF` (92.91%), and `U-Net` (91.92%). Replacing the FC layer with RF lifted OA by ~1.3-1.7 points over the corresponding bare network, with the stated mechanism being reduced overfitting from RF's robustness under limited samples. `OFSM` (26 s) sat between fast `RF-FI` (2 s, OA ~4 points lower) and slow `RF-RFE` (133 s) while producing the least-redundant, most independent feature subset and matching or slightly beating `RF-RFE` accuracy. Selected optimal bands repeatedly emphasized red-edge (`B5`, 705 nm) and SWIR (`B12`, 2190 nm); segmentation and color features contributed strongly, texture weakly. The hard confusion was corn-vs-soybean (phenologically similar); soybean user accuracy was the worst class at 72.6% with 27.4% commission, driven by mixed pixels.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

This is the inductive-bias comparator the manuscript flags. The architecture instantiates exactly our central thesis mechanism: a dense deep net (CNN) is used as a feature extractor, but the *decision head* is swapped from a dense FC softmax to a sparse, tree-like Random Forest — and accuracy rises precisely because "the use of RF instead of the FC layer ... can effectively alleviate the occurrence of overfitting ... especially with inadequate samples." That is the same inductive-bias argument our study makes for why gradient-boosted trees and TabNet (attentive sparsemax masks) transfer better than dense temporal/patch nets: a tree-structured, feature-selecting final stage regularizes a model that would otherwise overfit its training distribution. The paper supplies a clean controlled contrast (`Conv1D` vs `Conv1D-RF`, `VGG` vs `VGG-RF`) showing the RF head helps in every case. `OFSM`'s finding that red-edge and SWIR (`B5`, `B12`) bands are most discriminative aligns with our band set (`B6`, `B11`, `B12`). The `Conv1D` temporal-sequence design and per-class F1 reporting also match our framing. Caveat: the RF head here is grafted onto a network trained and tested on the *same* scene, so the paper demonstrates the inductive-bias benefit only against overfitting-within-distribution, not against spatial transfer — it cannot confirm that the RF head would survive a disjoint-tile reordering, which is our key test.

## Evaluation Caveats

The 25/25/50 pixel-random split is the dominant weakness: it is neither field-wise nor spatially disjoint, so within-field and adjacent-pixel autocorrelation leak across train/validation/test, and the 94% OA is a within-scene, single-year number that overstates real-world performance. None of the comparisons (hybrid vs bare, OFSM vs TFSM, Conv1D-RF vs mainstream) is tested under spatial transfer or cross-year conditions, so the paper cannot say whether the RF-head advantage persists out-of-sample — the very question our study answers. The class panel is balanced and informative (per-class F1/IoU, with soybean correctly flagged as weak), so imbalance is not hidden, but it is all in-region. The corn-soybean confusion is attributed to mixed pixels, an artifact our field-level aggregation would partly suppress. Compute cost is reported for feature selection (a strength) but training cost of the hybrids vs baselines is only partially compared. Cite the inductive-bias mechanism (RF head reduces FC-layer overfitting; OA 94.27% within-scene), not the headline accuracy as transfer evidence.
