# Cropformer: A new generalized deep learning classification approach for multi-scenario crop classification

**Citation:** Hengbin Wang, Wanqiu Chang, Yu Yao, Zhiying Yao, Yuanyuan Zhao, Shaoming Li, Zhe Liu, and Xiaodong Zhang (2023). *Frontiers in Plant Science*, 14:1130659. DOI: 10.3389/fpls.2023.1130659

## Objectives

Cropformer aims to build a single, generalizable deep-learning classifier that works across multiple crop-classification *scenarios* rather than being tuned to one: full-season classification, in-season (early) classification, few-sample classification, and cross-region model transfer. The core architectural claim is that fusing a Transformer (global, whole-reproductive-period features) with a convolution module (local, key-growth-node features) captures complementary features that improve generalization, and that self-supervised pre-training on large unlabeled time series accumulates "a priori" crop-growth knowledge that reduces labeled-sample demand and aids transfer. This is the explicit transfer-generalization comparator for our manuscript.

## Methods

Data are GaoFen-1 imagery (4 bands: R, G, B, NIR; 16 m; 4-day revisit) over five Chinese study areas (three in NW China — Hexi Corridor, Ili River Valley, Tianshan Corridor; two in NE China — Western/Eastern Heilongjiang). Because cloud-screening leaves *irregular-length* time series, Cropformer ingests raw irregular sequences directly: a token embedding of spectral values is fused with a sinusoidal position encoding of day-of-year (`doy`), avoiding resampling. The architecture stacks Cropformer Blocks (multi-head attention to FFN, with a convolution module — depthwise-separable "Crop Residual" with double-start residual connections, GLU/Swish activations — embedded downstream of attention). Training is two-step: (1) self-supervised pre-training on 781,250 unlabeled 10-km-tile samples by masking and predicting sequence values (MSE loss); (2) supervised fine-tuning with a small classification head on labeled field samples (6:2:2 train/val/test). Comparators: RF, Res-18, Performer, SIFT-BERT, ALBERT. Metrics: OA, Average Accuracy (`AA`), F1.

**Evaluation protocol:** This paper spans nearly the full generalization spectrum, and the transfer scenario is the load-bearing one. (a) Full-season and few-sample: within-region 6:2:2 random splits per study area, single year — pooled, no spatial disjointness. (b) In-season: pre-train on historical-year data, fine-tune/test on current-year data with progressively truncated series — a cross-year temporal generalization test within a region. (c) **Spatial transfer (Section 5.4, the key protocol):** the model is trained in a *source region* and tested in a *different target region* — genuinely spatially disjoint cross-region transfer. Two variants: `TL1` (source and target geographically/climatically *similar*) and `TL2` (source = label-rich region transferred to a label-poor *different* region). This is the only paper in this assignment batch with an explicitly disjoint cross-region holdout. The within-region scenarios remain pooled (not stated to be field-wise), so leakage applies there; the transfer scenario does not suffer that leakage by construction. Importantly, the authors report `AA` alongside `OA` and explicitly note that under unbalanced crop distributions OA is dominated by majority classes while AA exposes minority-class collapse — a balanced-metric discipline our study shares.

## Key Findings

Full-season: Cropformer led OA in 4 of 5 areas (e.g., 81.93%, 86.32%, 85.70%, 84.39%, 79.15%), with its largest margins over RF (>5 points) in the small-sample NE areas; under severe class imbalance (areas II, III) AA was depressed for all methods and Cropformer's OA edge shrank. Pre-training lifted OA by ~7% on average for Cropformer (more than BERT's 3.2% or ALBERT's 5.2%). In-season: Cropformer reached late-July F1 scores comparable to late-September (full-maturity) values, enabling earlier classification; advantage over RF was clearest in early growth (end-of-May). Few-sample: with only 1% of labels Cropformer's OA dropped just 0.3-5.66% versus full-label fine-tuning, while RF dropped 8-11 points; RF's AA was lowest of all methods on unbalanced samples. **Spatial transfer:** with no pre-training, Cropformer hit OA 62.13% in `TL1` (vs RF 25.21%, Performer 30.54% — a >30-point advantage) and OA 62.98% in `TL2`; with `Pre_E` pre-training, `TL2` OA reached 64.26% (best overall). Transferring from a label-rich region (`TL2`) beat transferring from a merely climatically-similar region (`TL1`). RF collapsed badly under cross-region transfer.

## Relevance to Our Crop-Classification Study

Cropformer is the most important external comparator for our transfer thesis because it directly measures cross-region (spatially disjoint) transfer — exactly the axis on which our holdout-tile reordering operates. Two of its findings cut *with* and *against* our narrative and must be handled carefully. (1) It corroborates that classical RF, evaluated under genuine cross-region transfer, collapses (OA ~25-30% in `TL1`), consistent with our observation that in-region validation misranks models. (2) It complicates the simple "dense deep nets overfit and collapse out-of-sample" story: a *pre-trained, convolution-augmented Transformer* transfers far better than RF here (>30-point OA gap), the opposite ordering from our finding that sparse tree-like models transfer best while dense temporal nets (CNN-BiLSTM, L-TAE, TempCNN) collapse. The reconciling levers are (i) Cropformer's massive self-supervised pre-training on 781k unlabeled samples — a regularizer/transfer mechanism our dense models did not use — and (ii) GaoFen-1's 4 broad bands vs our 6 spectral features, plus its raw-irregular-sequence design. So the honest read for our manuscript: dense temporal nets *can* transfer if given a strong pre-training prior, but our (non-pretrained, supervised-from-scratch) dense nets did not — making pre-training a candidate explanation and a future-work direction, not a refutation. The AA-vs-OA imbalance discipline and the few-sample robustness result also reinforce our use of macro-F1/Kappa over OA.

## Evaluation Caveats

The within-region scenarios (full-season, few-sample) use pooled 6:2:2 splits not stated to be field-wise, so spatial autocorrelation leakage likely inflates those absolute accuracies; the genuinely transfer-relevant numbers are the cross-region `TL1`/`TL2` results, and those should be the ones cited. The transfer experiment is limited to a handful of source-target region pairs and a single sensor/year per region, so it is suggestive rather than exhaustive. Cropformer's transfer advantage is entangled with its 781k-sample pre-training corpus, so the comparison to a non-pre-trained RF is not a clean architecture-only contrast — pre-training is doing heavy lifting. The 4-band GaoFen-1 feature space and direct raw-sequence ingestion differ materially from our 6-feature Sentinel-2 engineered setup, limiting transferability of the exact numbers. Compute cost is reported (Cropformer slower than ALBERT/SIFT-BERT but its data preprocessing is cheaper than RF's). No SAR fusion. Cite the cross-region transfer protocol and the RF-collapse-under-transfer result, and explicitly note pre-training as the confound when contrasting with our dense-net collapse.
