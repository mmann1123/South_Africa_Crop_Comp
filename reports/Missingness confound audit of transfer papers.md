# Cloud gaps masquerade as transfer failure

Fourteen of the 45 published optical-time-series transfer papers audited here (nine crop-type, five other land-cover and vegetation) meet every condition of the rank-3 rubric: the holdout region or year changes, its cloud or gap structure plausibly differs from the training domain, no target-domain missingness statistic is reported, the pipeline fills or drops gaps in a way that changes the input distribution in proportion to local cloudiness, and the degradation is attributed to phenology, spectra or landscape without a missingness test. Only **five of the roughly 33 papers that report a transfer loss publish a per-domain valid-observation statistic** (Rusňák 2023, Mohammadi 2024, Cai 2018, Wang 2020, Pham 2024), and only **Pham et al. 2024 runs an ablation that isolates missingness**; it finds that a model trained on Brandenburg's clearest year "performed well in the training year and poorly in unseen years" until sparsity was simulated during training, with linear interpolation of long gaps costing 20 to 30 OA points in sparse years ([Pham et al. 2024](https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn068235.pdf)). That is independent published evidence for the mechanism behind our own case, in which **40.6 percent of AI4FoodSecurity holdout pixels lack a September observation while the training tiles have none missing**, and zero-filling that month rather than the region change produced most of the apparent deep-model transfer drop (CNN-BiLSTM macro-F1 0.51 to 0.66 once September is filled from training-tile medians) ([holdout fill check](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/README.md)). The confound is propagated by the public benchmarks the field reuses: TimeSen2Crop zero-fills any month without a clear observation, Sen4AgriNet's loader linearly interpolates or extrapolates empty months in code the paper never describes, CropHarvest replaces missing months with the pixel's own temporal mean, and DENETHOR's and AI4FoodSecurity's Planet Fusion cubes hide where days were synthesised. No remote-sensing journal requires code deposit, so for most of these papers the only auditable artefact is the data availability statement, and the post-hoc remedy is cloud forensics: a single unauthenticated STAC query returns granule-level cloud cover for any tile and date range since 2015, and for the 2017 Western Cape tiles it shows two winter blackouts (June and 11 to 21 August) plus a 15-day September gap, with train and holdout granules nearly identical on every shared date, so the September shift we measured is sub-tile and only a pixel-level SCL or Cloud Score+ reduction over the field footprints can quantify it (`research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/earthsearch_s2_l1c_cloudcover_SA_tiles_2017.csv`).

## The rubric, and what the 45-paper sample is and is not

The ranks below follow the rubric fixed in the notes. **Rank 3 (highly suspect)** requires all five conditions: region or year changes between source and target; a strong climatological or acquisition-density reason to expect different cloud, shadow or gap structure in the target; no target-domain missingness statistic (valid-observation count, cloud fraction, interpolated or filled fraction); gap handling that is undescribed or that changes the input distribution as a function of local gap rate (zero fill, constant fill, long-gap interpolation, silent row dropping, unmasked clouds); and degradation attributed to phenology, spectra, management or landscape without testing missingness. **Rank 2 (plausible)** covers papers that report a transfer loss and describe gap handling applied identically to both domains, but never report how often the fill rule fired in the target or whether its rate matched the source, or that inherit a benchmark with known cloud contamination without comment. **Rank 1 (low suspicion)** is earned by reported per-domain valid counts, identical acquisition structure across domains (single clear scenes), explicit missingness ablations, or a model interface that masks rather than fills; papers that report no transfer at all are rank 1 by default and labelled N/A. Ranks derived from abstracts, section snippets or code alone, with the methods text behind a paywall, are marked **provisional**.

The sample is 45 papers from four research threads (eight crop-type papers whose PDFs sit in `writeup/literature/`, eleven domain-adaptation and pretraining papers, thirteen additional crop-type transfer papers and benchmarks, and twelve non-crop land-cover, forest, wetland, burned-area and ecological-mapping papers), plus thirteen public SITS benchmark products. It is a forensic audit of the papers most cited for "models do not transfer", not a systematic review; paywalled full texts (Kerner 2022, Xu 2020, Konduri 2020, Wang-Azzari-Lobell 2019, Mask-PSTIN 2024, Che 2024; the Wang-Feng DACCN 2023 full text was obtained on 28 October 2026 and its card updated) and blocked journal author-guide pages are flagged where they limit a card. Confidence is stated per paper. Climatology statements rest on the papers' own text, on published cloud climatologies (Wilson and Jetz 2016, Whitcraft et al. 2015, Eberhardt et al. 2016, Lewińska et al. 2024) and on a Sentinel-2 L1C granule harvest done for this audit, whose script and outputs are cited by path in the forensics section.

Across the two tables the pattern is uniform: the papers that report a target valid-observation statistic (Rusňák, Mohammadi, Cai, Wang 2020, Pham, Inglada) are also the papers that consider missingness as a cause, and the papers that do not report one attribute the loss to phenology, spectra or landscape by default. The omission reads as a blind spot rather than a judged irrelevance, because in every rank-3 case the diagnostic would have been cheap.

## Per-paper evidence, crop-type papers

### Rank 3

**Wijesingha, Dzene and Wachendorf 2024** train on North Hesse and Weser-Ems for 2013 to 2018 and test on 2010 to 2012 (temporal), the other region (spatial) and both (spatio-temporal), using Landsat scenes under 60 percent cloud grouped into four bimonthly medians; the paper states that "the data from Landsat 7 data was not corrected to remove the gaps, and those gaps were considered as no data pixels similar to cloud and shadow areas", and that Landsat 5 ended in May 2012 and Landsat 8 began in 2013, so the temporal target is observed only by one TM sensor and a striped ETM+ ([Wijesingha et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020)). No scene counts per bimonth, no fraction of fields with missing composites and no SLC-gap coverage are reported; the discussion concedes only that "the number of scenes per area in a given two-month period could differ". The public repository confirms the paper and adds what it omits: `L578_WE_Download.py` filters `LANDSAT/LT05/C02/T1_L2`, `LE07` and `LC08` with `.filterMetadata('CLOUD_COVER', 'less_than', 60)`, calls `.maskClouds()` and `.median()` with no `unmask` or fill, and `00_1_SR_VI_NH_Data_model_training_TF_BCNN_RF.ipynb` reads `NH_L578_all_norm.csv` and applies `df_dropna = df_years.dropna()` with no `fillna`, `interpolate` or imputer anywhere in the first 100,000 characters ([repository](https://github.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability); [download script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability/main/L578_WE_Download.py); [notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jmatics/landsat-croptype-spatial-temporal-transferability/main/00_1_SR_VI_NH_Data_model_training_TF_BCNN_RF.ipynb)). Missingness therefore never enters the model as a value; it acts as unreported sample selection whose rate differs between domains, which feeds directly into the field-size and label-shift explanations the authors offer. The NH random forest losing 0.120 mF1 under temporal, spatial and spatio-temporal transfer alike is consistent with a floor set by target-year input quality. Confidence medium-high.

- **Transfer finding:** In-region reference mF1 (held-out district, 2017) is NH-RF 0.765, NH-CNN 0.758, WE-RF 0.722 and WE-CNN 0.740; under temporal transfer to 2010 to 2012 (S1) the four models fall to 0.645, 0.678, 0.698 and 0.695, under spatial transfer (S2) to 0.645, 0.614, 0.678 and 0.643, and under spatio-temporal transfer (S3) to 0.647, 0.598, 0.588 and 0.563, a loss of 0.024 to 0.177 mF1 with S3 costing "about 25 percent" of reference performance. The authors attribute the loss to "changes in environmental, climate, and management conditions", field size, elevation, class distribution and the DI/AOA dissimilarity index (correlation 0.9 with OA); the sensor change is acknowledged in one sentence and set aside with a citation to Frantz et al. 2023.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is silent row dropping: any field-bimonth whose Landsat median has no valid pixel becomes NaN and `dropna()` removes that field-year, so missingness never reaches the model but decides which fields are scored. The temporal target (2010 to 2012) is observed by one TM sensor plus SLC-off ETM+ while training years have ETM+ plus OLI, so the target domain loses more field-years and loses them preferentially in cloudier sub-areas and smaller fields, which shifts the class and field-size composition of the evaluated set and then shows up as the label-shift and field-size effects the authors report; the direction of the bias on mF1 is unknown because the surviving fields are the easier ones. The single check is a per-region-year count of scenes per bimonth and of fields removed by `dropna()`, with the 2013 to 2018 training set rerun on ETM+ scenes only to match target sensor density.

**Nyborg et al. 2022 (TimeMatch)** uses Sentinel-2 L1C over tiles 33UVP (Austria), 32VNH (Denmark), 30TXT and 31TCJ (France) for 2017, selecting "all available observations with cloud coverage <= 80% and coverage >= 50%" with no cloud masking, gap filling or interpolation described anywhere ([Nyborg et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2022.04.018)). The code settles it: `preprocessing/downloader.py` filters with `cloudy_pct = tileinfo['cloudyPixelPercentage']` and a CLI default `--max_cloudy_percentage 80.0`, fetches `qi/MSK_CLOUDS_B00` only for L2A and never reads it, while `preprocessing/prepare_pixels.py` stores parcel arrays with no nodata or NaN filtering, `dataset.py` returns an all-ones `valid_pixels` mask, and `transforms.py` only clips, subsamples and shifts dates ([downloader.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/preprocessing/downloader.py); [prepare_pixels.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/preprocessing/prepare_pixels.py); [dataset.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/dataset.py); [transforms.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jnyborg/timematch/main/transforms.py)). The 73.9 GB Zenodo archive carries no cloud statement ([Zenodo 5636422](https://zenodo.org/records/5636422)). A 55°N Danish tile and a 43°N southern French tile filtered only at tile level will differ in both retained dates and cloud-contaminated pixels, so part of what the shift estimator recovers may be the model learning to down-weight cloudy target dates rather than calendar alignment; no experiment varies contamination. Because SFUDA and later UDA papers reuse this archive, the rank-3 input structure propagates. Confidence high.

- **Transfer finding:** Target-trained macro F1 for the five tasks (DK1 to FR1, DK1 to FR2, DK1 to AT1, FR1 to DK1, FR1 to FR2) is 74.6, 72.4, 86.9, 90.6 and 85.7 (mean 82.0), while source-only transfer gives 28.3, 29.0, 43.4, 24.9 and 70.3 (mean 39.2), a 43-point collapse that TimeMatch largely recovers. The authors read the collapse as a calendar shift, estimating FR1 to DK1 at +32 days and DK1 to FR1 at -35 days, and propose class-wise alignment or time warping as future work.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is unmasked clouds entering as data: L1C scenes pass a tile-level 80 percent cloudy-pixel filter and nothing else, so cloudy pixels sit in the parcel arrays with the same `valid_pixels` ones mask as clear ones. The 55 N Danish tile in 2017 carries more cloud-contaminated dates and a different date count than the 43 N southern French tile, so a source-only model meets cloud spectra at frequencies it never saw, which inflates the measured transfer loss and lets the shift estimator and ShiftAug recover accuracy partly by learning to discount cloudy target dates rather than by realigning the calendar. The check is to compute per-tile, per-date cloud fraction from the Zenodo archive (s2cloudless on the stored pixels) and rerun the source-only baseline with cloud-masked or cloud-matched inputs; if the DK1 to FR gap shrinks materially, the 30 to 35 day attribution is overstated.
- **Measured (this repository, `experiments/external_audits/timematch/`):** Cloud Score+ and s2cloudless fractions over cropland for all 211 shipped dates show the Danish tile is the cloudiest of the four by every rule: a DK1 parcel series carries 28.6 cloud-contaminated and 23.4 clear observations (FR1 25.0 and 37.0, FR2 12.3 and 26.7, AT1 21.7 and 36.3), more than half of cropland is cloudy on 32 of its 52 dates (FR1 23 of 62, FR2 11 of 39), and between April and September it holds 11.7 clear observations against 18.5 on FR1 (1.6 times), 13.6 on FR2 (1.2 times) and 26.4 on AT1, with fewer than one clear observation in June. The three Denmark transfer cases therefore pair the cloudiest tile with clearer ones, which the dataset's tile-level 80 percent filter cannot see.

**The Hexi Corridor paper (Remote Sensing 17:1494, 2025)** transfers from Iowa, Missouri, Kansas, Minnesota and the Dakotas (CDL 2022) to the "typical temperate continental arid climate" of Gansu, with Sentinel-2 at "a maximum-value composite at a weekly scale", cloud removal "using the QA60 band for Sentinel-2 and the QA band for Landsat-8, with missing values due to cloud removal interpolated using linear interpolation", and target samples where "any samples affected by cloud cover were discarded" while source samples were only CDL-confidence filtered ([Hexi Corridor paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17091494)). No missingness statistic is given for either domain, the dataset is "available on request" with no code, and naive-transfer XGBoost macro F1 0.448 is explained with Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests and NDVI DTW distances. Weekly max composites in the humid Midwest will have many empty or partly cloudy weeks that QA60 (no shadow component) leaves contaminated and interpolation smooths, while the arid target has few; the KS tests that "prove" domain shift detect this just as well as phenology. Confidence medium-high.

- **Transfer finding:** Source-only transfer (Experiment 1) gives XGBoost OA 0.7833, macro F1 0.4480 and Kappa 0.6009, with RF at macro F1 0.4325; once target labels are added, RF_transfer reaches OA 0.9226 and macro F1 0.8431 against a target-only RF at OA 0.8990 and macro F1 0.7641, so naive transfer sits about 0.40 macro F1 below the target-informed models (the in-target numbers are random point splits, not a spatial holdout). The authors attribute the gap to domain shift demonstrated by Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests (all p < 0.05) and NDVI DTW distances (maize 1.09 most similar, oats 2.51 least), with minority crops absorbed into majority classes.
- **Takeaway:** Two mechanisms act together: linear interpolation of weekly maximum composites after a QA60 mask that has no shadow component, and asymmetric cleaning in which target samples "affected by cloud cover were discarded" while source samples were only CDL-confidence filtered. The humid Midwest source therefore carries many interpolated or shadow-contaminated weeks that the arid Hexi target lacks by climate and by construction, so the source feature distribution differs from the target in gap structure before any phenology is considered; a KS test on week-indexed features detects that difference just as readily, which overstates the phenological share of the naive-transfer loss. The check is a per-sample count of empty weekly composites in each domain plus the number of target samples discarded, followed by a source-only rerun with the same cloud criterion applied to source samples.

**Wang, Feng, Zhang and Tian 2023 (DACCN)** adapts from Minnesota and Nebraska to Wisconsin, Iowa, Heilongjiang and Jilin, and from each of the six regions to the other year, across 2018 and 2019 with MK-MMD alignment, with 3,000 randomly sampled pixels per class per region labelled from the CDL (confidence over 90 percent) and the You et al. 2021 Northeast China maps. The full text (obtained 28 October 2026) describes the pipeline in one paragraph: Sentinel-2 L1C top-of-atmosphere observations from the beginning of April to the end of October, "cloudy observations were removed using a cloud masking algorithm (Oreopoulos et al., 2011)", a band-threshold mask ported from MODIS to Landsat with no shadow detection, then "10-day composites were generated using the median values of the valid observations, with data gaps filled by linear interpolation", then Savitzky-Golay smoothing over seven adjacent composites with a quadratic polynomial, giving 12 features at 21 time steps, all in Google Earth Engine ([Wang et al. 2023, full text](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2023.04.002)). No per-domain count of valid observations, empty composites or interpolated steps is reported anywhere, there is no code or data availability statement, and the supplement holds only the training and validation accuracy tables (S1 to S6). Shift is attributed to "meteorology-induced phenological and crop calendar differences"; the results section says the US states "experienced a later planting of corn and soybean in 2019 due to an exceptionally wet spring" and the mapping discussion blames a "prolonged cool and wet spring" for Marquette's corn-to-soybean confusion, both of which are also descriptions of a cloudier April to June whose 10-day windows would be interpolated more often in 2019 than in 2018, yet the two readings are never separated. The same group's follow-up is also composite-based ([GIScience & RS 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15481603.2022.2156123)). DACCN released no code, but the pipeline it says it follows is public: You et al. 2021 deposited their Earth Engine scripts on figshare under CC BY 4.0 ("JavaScript code used to generate the cropland layer and crop type maps are available from the figshare repository"), and the crop-classification script implements the identical chain ([You et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-021-00827-9); [figshare 13090442](https://figshare.com/articles/figure/The_10-m_crop_type_maps_in_Northeast_China_during_2017-2019/13090442); local copy and notes in `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/you2021_gee_code/`). It shows what the prose hides: the mask is the Earth Engine simple cloud score at threshold 0.2 with no shadow test, empty 10-day composites are flagged `length` 0, interpolation searches at most nine composites (90 days) on each side for the nearest valid pixel, and every interpolated image is then passed through `.unmask(0)`, so a pixel without a valid observation on both sides within 90 days, including the first and last composites of the April to October window, is set to zero in all bands and indices before Savitzky-Golay smoothing spreads that zero across seven steps. Nothing in the script counts or exports valid observations. Rank 3 confirmed, confidence medium-high; the revise-to-2 condition (per-domain valid counts) is not met.

- **Transfer finding:** DACCN reports overall accuracies of 0.835 to 0.922 across the spatial, temporal and spatio-temporal cases and outperforms DCCN (the same network without alignment), RF and SVM. The unadapted cross-country losses are the large ones: in 2019 DCCN reaches OA 0.738 for Nebraska to Jilin and 0.791 for Minnesota to Heilongjiang against DACCN's 0.855 and 0.864, while within-US spatial transfer (0.848 to 0.885 for DCCN) and within-region cross-year transfer (0.797 to 0.916) lose little. In-domain validation accuracies are in supplementary Tables S1 to S6, not in the PDF, so the drop from in-domain to transfer cannot be read from the article. The authors attribute the shift to "meteorology-induced phenological and crop calendar differences" between the Corn Belt and NE China and between 2018 and 2019, and to the wet 2019 spring.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is now documented: linear interpolation of empty 10-day median composites followed by a 70-day Savitzky-Golay window, so a cloudy stretch does not produce a sentinel value but a smoothed straight line whose extent scales with local cloudiness, and the smoothing spreads any residual cloud or shadow (the Oreopoulos mask has no shadow test) into neighbouring steps. NE China's July to August monsoon and the record-wet 2019 Corn Belt spring therefore give the target domains different interpolated fractions from 2018 Minnesota and Nebraska, in exactly the windows (green-up and peak) the NDVI profiles in Fig. 4 are read for phenology. MK-MMD alignment pulls feature distributions together regardless of whether the mismatch is phenological or interpolation-induced, so the gain over DCCN cannot be assigned to crop calendar until missingness is excluded. The paper reports no valid-observation, empty-composite or interpolated-step count for any region-year, but the You et al. 2021 script it follows is public, so the interpolated and zero-filled fraction per region-year can be measured by re-running that script's compositing and interpolation stage over 2018 and 2019 for the six regions and exporting the `length` flags and the pre-`unmask` validity mask; the only private piece is the `sgsmooth` module, which does not affect the count.
- **Measured (this repository, `experiments/external_audits/daccn/`):** re-running the deposited You et al. 2021 compositing and interpolation stage with validity and zero counters over 16 random 5 km cells per region shows the recipe almost never zero-fills when given year-round imagery (under 4 percent of cropland pixels get any zero step). Given April to October imagery only, as DACCN states it used, the deposited script raises an error at the window edges (an empty before or after window has no bands to select), so DACCN must either have pulled wider imagery or altered the edge handling; under the latter, with the `unmask(0)` fallback intact, 16 to 96 percent of pixels are zero-filled at the first or last step, 0.3 to 1.6 steps per pixel, and the rate differs across DACCN's pairs by up to 80 points (Nebraska to Jilin 59 vs 96 percent in 2018 and 89 vs 16 in 2019; Jilin 2018 to 2019 96 vs 16; Iowa 26 vs 69; Heilongjiang 75 vs 38). The paper does not say which it did, so the size of the confound is conditional on an undisclosed choice; nothing in the recipe counts it.

**Kondmann et al. 2021 (DENETHOR)** trains on a 24 km Brandenburg tile with 2018 labels and tests on a separate tile with 2019 labels, with an ablation on the 2018 test tile splitting a 21-point PseLTae drop into "about 9 p.p." spatial and "12 p.p." temporal, explained because "the weather and growing cycles can vary notably from year to year" ([DENETHOR paper](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/5b8add2a5d98b1a652ea7fd72d942dac-Paper-round2.pdf)). The deep models consume Planet Fusion daily cubes in which "potential gaps because of clouds are filled with different points in time" with a QA confidence layer; the only quantitative cloud statement is that cloud-free filtering "discards around 55% of S2 observations", with no year or tile. In the repository, `dataset.py` stacks every daily GeoTIFF with no reference to the QA layer, `s2dataset.py` has the cloud-probability concatenation commented out (`# bands = np.concatenate([bands, clp], axis=-1)`), and `random_forest.py` applies `msk = cld < 40` then `np.nan_to_num(X, neginf=0, posinf=0)` without using the retained count ([repository](https://github.com/lukaskondmann/DENETHOR)). Pham et al. 2024, same region, chose 2018 as training year because it "had the highest number of clear sky observations" of 2010 to 2021, so DENETHOR trains on the clearest year of a 12-year record and tests on a cloudier one with the synthesised fraction invisible to the loader ([Pham et al. 2024](https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn068235.pdf)). Confidence medium-high; the fill is a proprietary multi-source reconstruction, so the induced shift is subtler than zero fill.

- **Transfer finding:** PseLTae validation accuracy of 88.02 percent on the 2018 training tile falls to 67.25 percent on the 2019 test tile (about 21 points); evaluating the test tile with 2018 data gives 78.77 percent, so the authors split the loss into "about 9 p.p." spatial and "12 p.p." temporal, explained because "the weather and growing cycles can vary notably from year to year", with NDVI curves for meadows differing most mid-year.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is interpolation across gaps by the vendor: Planet Fusion fills cloud gaps "with different points in time" and ships a QA confidence layer that `dataset.py` never reads, so synthesised days enter the daily cube as observed values. Pham et al. 2024 identify 2018 as the clearest year in Brandenburg's 2010 to 2021 record, so the 2019 test year holds a larger synthesised fraction than the training year by climate alone, and the 12-point temporal component conflates year-to-year phenology with a change in the share of reconstructed observations. The check is to read the QA layer, compute the gap-filled fraction per field and day for 2018 and 2019, and condition test accuracy on it, or retrain with QA-weighted inputs.
- **Measured (this repository, `experiments/external_audits/denethor/`):** from the shipped CLP, SCL and PF-QA layers over all four tile-years, a field in the 2019 test tile had a median of 35 clear Sentinel-2 acquisitions between April and October against 42 in the 2018 training tile (40 vs 31 for the full year under the authors' literal `cld < 40` rule), the unused tile-years match their year-mates so the tile change contributes 0 to 1 and the year change all of it, and the loss sits in May, August and October. The Planet Fusion cubes the models consumed were about two thirds synthetic in both years (62 percent of days in 2018, 67 percent in 2019), but May was mostly observed in 2018 and mostly interpolated in 2019 (41 vs 81 percent of days), October rose from 65 to 79 percent, and June went the other way (79 vs 49 percent).

**Hao, Di, Zhang and Guo 2020** train a random forest on 8,448 CDL 2017 samples from 294 HLS tiles and apply it in 2018 to Hengshui (North China Plain), Alberta and Nebraska, using 15-day maximum-NDVI composites where an empty window "is labeled as 'missing value'" and then "filled with a moving window method by calculating the average of the two neighboring high quality values" before Savitzky-Golay smoothing; Hengshui, "not covered by HLS", used Landsat-8 SR plus Sentinel-2 L1C with "the cloud mask of the L1C product", while no QA step is described for the HLS training data ([Hao et al. 2020](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10193713)). No count of missing composites is reported for any region or year, no code exists, and the transfer deficit is attributed to "phenological and crop calendar difference" and to climate and irrigation, with Figs. 11 to 13 comparing temperature and NDVI curves. Hengshui's June to September monsoon and Alberta's convective summer guarantee different composite completeness from the 2017 CONUS pool. Confidence high.

- **Transfer finding:** With the full DOY 60 to 330 NDVI series, transferred (TL) overall accuracy is 97.79 percent in Hengshui, 86.45 percent in Alberta and 94.86 percent in Nebraska, against locally trained (LO) 96.55 percent in Alberta and 96.73 percent in Nebraska (McNemar tests show no significant TL-LO difference in Hengshui at full length), so the deficit is about 10 points in Alberta and 2 in Nebraska; at shorter in-season lengths the gap is far larger (Alberta OA 72.91 TL versus 78.45 LO at DOY 150; Nebraska 80.72 versus 92.06 at DOY 180). The authors attribute the deficit to "phenological and crop calendar difference among different regions", climate, irrigation and the heterogeneity of the CONUS-wide training pool.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is neighbour-average filling of empty 15-day windows followed by Savitzky-Golay smoothing, with the Hengshui target built from a different sensor set (Landsat-8 plus Sentinel-2 L1C with the coarse L1C cloud mask) than the HLS training pool. Alberta's convective June and Hengshui's June to September monsoon produce more empty windows than the 2017 CONUS pool, and empty windows cluster exactly in the early-season lengths where the TL deficit is largest, so a filled or smoothed early window is out of the training distribution for reasons unrelated to crop calendar; the deficit is overstated and its phenological attribution untested. The check is a count of filled windows per region-year and an accuracy breakdown by number of filled windows in the sample.

**Kerner et al. 2020 (growth-stage normalization)**, the open-code stand-in for the paywalled 2022 Science of Remote Sensing paper, trains on HLS tile 16TBL for 2017 and 2018 and tests on 2019, chosen because "2019 saw delayed planting"; clouds are removed with the HLS QA layer and "to fill in no-data pixels and mitigate outliers ... we smoothed the time series for each pixel and each band using the Savitzky-Golay method" with no window stated ([Kerner et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.10189)). The single notebook `lstm-cnn.ipynb` in the repository then does `X[np.where(np.isnan(X))] = 0` in `load_data` and again in `add_lswi_channel` and `add_ndwi_channel`, while the smoothing step that produced `pheno_timeseries_16TBL_2018.npy` is not in the repository ([croptype-mapping-gsn](https://github.com/nasaharvest/croptype-mapping-gsn)). No per-year valid-observation counts appear, and the 30-point gap between fixed-DOY (55.9 percent) and growth-stage inputs (85.4 percent) is read entirely as planting-date shift, although a fixed DOY 152 in the record-wet 2019 spring is far more likely to be an interpolated or zero-filled value than in 2017 or 2018. The 2022 version reports 71 to 85 percent accuracies for Iowa 2019 and Illinois 2020 but its gap handling could not be read ([DOAJ abstract](https://doaj.org/article/abd9c50018054a7a9460702ce8ab0ef9)); its rank 3 is provisional. Confidence medium.

- **Transfer finding:** On the held-out 2019 season (trained 2017 and 2018, tile 16TBL) the late-season CNN-LSTM reaches 55.9 percent accuracy with fixed-DOY inputs and 85.4 percent with growth-stage-normalised inputs, a 29.5-point gap; the 2022 journal version reports 71 to 72 percent early-season, 80 to 84 mid-season and 81 to 85 late-season for Iowa 2019 and Illinois 2020. The authors attribute the fixed-DOY failure to planting delays from the 2019 flooding.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is interpolation of HLS cloud and track gaps by Savitzky-Golay smoothing of unstated window, with surviving NaNs set to zero in `load_data`; the fixed DOYs (149 and 152) fall in the record-wet 2019 spring, so the test year's fixed-DOY inputs are far more often smoothed or zero-filled than the 2017 and 2018 training inputs, which depresses the fixed-DOY baseline and inflates the apparent benefit of normalisation independent of planting date. The growth-stage inputs partly dodge the problem by selecting dates from the curve itself, so the asymmetry in the comparison is the suspect part; for the 2022 version the evidence is thin because its gap handling could not be read. The check is a per-year count of valid HLS observations within 15 days of each fixed DOY, and a fixed-DOY rerun with dates chosen to equalise valid coverage across years.

**Sykas et al. 2022 (Sen4AgriNet)** evaluates Scenario 2 (Catalonia 2019 and 2020 to France 2019) and Scenario 3 (France 2019 to Catalonia 2020) on Sentinel-2 L1C scenes under 10 percent cloud, aggregated "into 12 time-bins by calculating the median of each month" over April to September; the paper defers the treatment of empty months to the code and never mentions them again, attributing the collapse (PAD weighted F1 82.6 to 85.2, then 52 to 58, then 34 to 44) to phenological cycles that "are also stretched or shrunk down" and to wheat being "two orders of magnitude less in the train set" ([Sykas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00951)). In `S4A-Models/utils/PAD_dataset.py`, `get_medians` runs `.groupby_bins('time', ...).median(dim='time')`, resamples so "months without data are initiated with NaN values", then `band_data.interpolate_na(dim='time_bins', method='linear', fill_value='extrapolate')`; `pad_experiments.py` defaults `--return_masks` and `--clouds` to False, so the published runs used no pixel cloud mask ([S4A-Models](https://github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/S4A-Models); [S4A](https://github.com/Orion-AI-Lab/S4A)). The paper's own "30 to 50 timestamps depending on the region" implies a 40 percent density difference, and the OAD result where the LSTM holds F1 91.3 while the transformer and TempCNN collapse to 30 is the signature expected when month-indexed inputs carry domain-specific interpolated months. Confidence high.

- **Transfer finding:** PAD weighted F1 is 82.6 to 85.2 under a random split (Scenario 1), 52 to 58 when training on Catalonia 2019 and 2020 and testing on France 2019 (Scenario 2), and 34 to 44 when training on France 2019 and testing on Catalonia 2020 (Scenario 3); in the OAD setting the transformer and TempCNN fall to F1 about 30 under Scenario 2 while the LSTM holds 91.3. The authors attribute the drops to phenological cycles that are "stretched or shrunk down" by soil and climate, to shifted sowing dates between years, and to wheat being "two orders of magnitude" rarer in the training set.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is linear interpolation and extrapolation across empty months in month-indexed inputs, with no pixel cloud mask so the monthly medians also inherit residual cloud; the paper's own 30 to 50 acquisitions per region after the 10 percent scene filter means oceanic France loses more spring months than Mediterranean Catalonia and so carries more interpolated months. A model trained on Catalonia's mostly real months then meets French patches whose April or May bin is a straight line between neighbours, and the reverse holds in Scenario 3, so part of the collapse is fill structure rather than phenology; the LSTM surviving while position-indexed models fail is consistent with that reading. The check is a count of NaN months per patch per country-year (the loader's mask before `interpolate_na`) and a rerun with `--clouds` masking on and interpolation rate matched between domains.

**Gallo et al. 2023** train a 3D CNN on Lombardy areas L1 and L2 for 2016 to 2018 and test on the same areas in 2019 (test Y) and on a new area L3 in 2019 (test A), stating "No cloud masks were applied to the data" and harmonising length so that "if the number of dates ... is less than 71 then images with zero values are added" while longer series are randomly subsampled ([Gallo et al. 2023](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/34fcbc0e-c3cb-4707-aadf-35f65c8b4b57/prod_475295-doc_194139.pdf)). No cloud fraction or usable-date count per area-year is given; 2016 (Sentinel-2A only) necessarily had fewer dates than 2019, so the zero-padded fraction differs between training and test years by construction. The OA drop from 77.2 to 72.8 to 70.5 is called "slightly lower" and blamed on the "Unknown cropland" label. The GitLab repository's `utils/probs_dataset.py` does `dates = random.sample(dates, n)` and `predict_daily_annual.py` initialises `torch.zeros(71,9,5,48,48)`, but the padding step itself is not in the released code ([GitLab repository](https://gitlab.com/artelabsuper/in-season-and-dynamic-crop-mapping)). Confidence medium-high.

- **Transfer finding:** Aggregator overall accuracy is 77.2 percent on validation, 72.8 percent on test Y (same areas, 2019) and 70.5 percent on test A (new area L3, 2019), drops of 4.4 and 6.7 points that the authors call "slightly lower" and attribute to the "Unknown cropland" label rather than to any domain effect.
- **Takeaway:** Two mechanisms apply: unmasked clouds enter as data because "No cloud masks were applied", and zero fill to a fixed 71 dates whose rate depends on how many acquisitions the area-year had. Training years include 2016 with Sentinel-2A alone, so the training set has more zero slots on average than the 2019 test year, which has more real (and more cloudy) dates from two satellites, and L3's single-orbit coverage differs again; the input distribution therefore shifts across year and area for reasons unrelated to crops, so the 4 to 7 point drop may be partly padding and cloud frequency, and the claim that the network learned around clouds is untested. Random date subsampling softens but does not remove this. The check is a table of real dates and cloud fraction per area-year, with a rerun subsampling every year to the 2016 date count.

### Rank 2

**Rusňák et al. 2023** is the most transparent paper in the set about composite quality and is still only rank 2. Training is the Danubian Lowland (tiles 33UYP, 33UXP, 33UYQ) and testing the East Slovakian Lowland (34UEV, 34UEU) for 2018 to 2022, from GEE `COPERNICUS/S2_SR` April to August, "771 images covering Danubian lowland and 215 images covering eastern Slovakia lowland", SCL-masked monthly medians; Fig. 4 tabulates for every region-month-year the share of area built from one, two or three-plus observations, and Section 4.2 explicitly tests and rejects a year-level relationship between quality and accuracy ([Rusňák et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414)). What remains open is the region-level confound: the East tiles received roughly 108 images per tile against 257 over the Danube, so the spatial target systematically has lower-quality medians in every Scenario 3 and 4 run, the value a pixel receives in a month with zero clean observations is never stated, and samples were cut in 2020 and 2022 "due to observational limitations", which pre-filters test pixels to complete vectors. Models were built in MATLAB with no code; only labels and supplementary figures are deposited. Confidence medium-high.

**Xie, Zhang, Liu and Sun 2025** train on Kansas, South Dakota and Minnesota CDL 2019 to 2023 and test on LUCAS 2018 points in six European countries, with QA_PIXEL and QA60 masks, monthly medians of all Landsat-8 and Sentinel-2 observations, and linear interpolation of empty months "to ensure that each pixel in the entire study area has the same temporal interval" ([Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211)). Only raw image totals per area are reported (Table 1), never valid-observation or interpolated-month fractions; the authors excluded U.S. 2018 from training for "insufficient Sentinel-2 images" while using 2018 as the only European target year, and Romania, the least-imaged country (6,820 images), is also the worst-transferred (OA 88.1) while Spain (16,093) is best (96.8). Attribution is entirely to temperature-profile similarity. The figshare deposit holds 21 CSV feature files and no scripts ([figshare 29925809](https://api.figshare.com/v2/articles/29925809)). Not rank 3 because there is no sentinel value or date dropping and the binary task transfers within 0.5 to 2 OA points. Confidence medium.

**Pandžić, Pavlović, Marko and Kilibarda 2026** transfer from 159,533 Slovenian parcels to 2,399 Vojvodina parcels for 2021 with Cloud Score+ masking, per-parcel medians per date, and cubic-spline resampling to a 15-day grid where "instead of extrapolating, in the case of missing data at the beginning or end of the time period, forward and backward padding was used"; Table 1 reports 617 versus 510 Sentinel-2 images but never the cloud-free count per parcel or the padded fraction ([Pandžić et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)). The paper's repository URL returns 404; the live `milospandzic/TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping` contains `03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py`, which runs `CubicSpline(group['Date'].astype('int64'), group[col], bc_type='natural', extrapolate=False)` per parcel and column followed by `.groupby('ID').ffill().groupby('ID').bfill()`, with no minimum-observation filter ("every ID group is interpolated regardless of how many observations it has") and a possible monthly rather than 15-day `DateOffset` that should be verified by reading the file ([interpolation script](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/milospandzic/TL_4_cross-regional_crop_mapping/main/03_interpolate_at_15daysCubic.py)). Here the shift likely runs the unusual direction: Alpine-adjacent, fragmented Slovenian parcels are plausibly gappier than the Pannonian plain, so the model learns on smoother spline-synthesised curves and is tested on sharper observed ones, which a KS test would register as covariate shift. Confidence medium.

**Mohammadi, Belgiu and Stein 2024 (SFUDA)** defines a valid observation as "not labeled as filled, shadow, cloudy, dilated cloud, or unclear", keeps pixels with "at least two valid observations in every third of the growing season", linearly interpolates to 28 weekly steps, and reports a per-site, per-year validity table: Iowa 99.37, 99.99, 96.92 percent; Missouri 91.99, 86.39, 99.60; Mississippi 98.78, 97.59, 99.65; Site L 97.15; France 100 because PASTIS was "already preprocessed by the authors" ([Mohammadi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385)). That is the best disclosure of any transfer paper here, yet the per-step interpolated fraction is unreported, excluded pixels are also excluded from evaluation so scores describe the cleaner landscape, and the cross-regional loss of 21 to 31 macro-F1 points is attributed to "temporal discriminativeness patterns and key phenological periods" and label shift, with clouds listed only as Limitation 4. The TimeMatch sub-experiment takes that dataset's unmasked L1C inputs as delivered. The repository distributes preprocessed arrays and retention masks, with interpolation done upstream ([SFUDA-CropMapping](https://github.com/Sina-Mohammadi/SFUDA-CropMapping)). Confidence medium-high.

**Shibuya et al. 2025** train on Mato Grosso TerraClass 2020 labels (crop year 2019/2020) and test on pooled Embrapa ground samples from 2016/17, 2020/21 and 2021/22 with MOD13Q1 and MYD13Q1 16-day composites, a CLOUD band from the pixel-reliability layer and bilinear interpolation of flagged gaps ([Shibuya et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2025.2527988)). No per-year count of interpolated composites is given, and the 7.7 to 10.3 point OA drop is attributed to sugarcane/pasture confusion and 250 m mixed pixels. For Brazil's Center-South, MODIS clear-sky occurrence during the November to February cropping period averaged about 15 percent in the northern states ([Eberhardt et al. 2016](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/8/3/219)), so the interpolated footprint almost certainly differs by year. Software is the `sits` R package; no project repository. Confidence medium.

**Reuss et al. 2026 (EuroCropsML benchmark)** pre-trains on Latvia, or Latvia plus Portugal, and fine-tunes and tests on a random 20 percent of Estonian parcels, using Sentinel-2 L1C 2021 parcel medians screened by a B4 brightness heuristic (cloud-free below 0.07, cloudy above 0.25, observations above 50 percent probability removed) and padding "all input sequences to 366 days" with masked padding ([Reuss et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ophoto.2026.100117); [EuroCropsML dataset paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.17458v2)). No per-country observation counts exist in either paper; the accuracy loss when Portugal is added is attributed to "geographically and phenologically dissimilar data". A brightness threshold passes thin cirrus and shadow, so residual contamination is country-dependent, but scoring in-region after fine-tuning makes any missingness effect second-order. Code at `jsreu/EurocropsML-Meta-Learning` and `jsreu/EuroCropsML-SSL`. Confidence medium.

**Tseng et al. 2023 (Presto)** is rank 2 for its pipeline, not for a transfer claim. The Earth Engine exporter sorts by `CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE` and returns `imgC.qualityMosaic("cloudShadowScore")` with no SCL or cloud-probability mask and nothing that checks for an empty collection, so in a month with no clear pixel the "least cloudy" pixel is still cloudy and enters as a legitimate observation ([cropharvest/eo/sentinel2.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nasaharvest/cropharvest/main/cropharvest/eo/sentinel2.py)); `presto/dataops/utils.py` builds `mask = torch.ones(...)`, `x = torch.zeros(...)` and sets mask 0 where bands are observed, with missing timesteps neither interpolated nor filled ([presto/dataops/utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nasaharvest/presto/main/presto/dataops/utils.py); [pipeline](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nasaharvest/presto/main/presto/dataops/pipelines/s1_s2_era5_srtm.py)). The paper reports F1 as a function of the number of input months, a genuine missingness ablation, and motivates structured masking because "certain regions experience very high (>90%) cloud coverage" ([Presto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14065)). The structured-masking pretraining makes Presto robust to tokens declared missing, not to tokens present but cloudy; WorldCereal's hypothesis H3 is a direct response to exactly this. Confidence medium.

**Zhang, Cai et al. 2024 (Mask-PSTIN)** evaluates Auvergne, Otog Front Banner and the Jianghan Plain separately and reports mF1 rising from 0.781 to 0.865 with temporal random masking that "selectively remov[es] certain temporal information"; the masking rate, sensor, composite interval, cloud mask and any inference-time gap test are behind the paywall and no repository exists ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.10.013); [Semantic Scholar, closed](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.10.013?fields=openAccessPdf)). It supplies citable prior art that gap handling beats gap filling, not a quantified gap tolerance. Provisional rank 2, low confidence.

**Cai et al. 2018** train on Champaign County Landsat 2000 to 2013 and test on 2014 and 2015 (1,322 scenes), averaging "values from non-cloud only pixels" within CLU polygons, removing values outside the 95th percentile per time stamp, and interpolating to daily steps with Savitzky-Golay ("a simple linear interpolation has achieved almost the same performance") ([Cai et al. 2018](https://carbon.nasa.gov/pdfs/feature_may2018_2_Cai.pdf)). Fig. 3 plots per-year availability and the authors use it causally: "low data availability will lead to poor performance, which can further explain low accuracies in 2004 and 2008", and SLC-off "may explain why performance in 2002 is high but slightly decreases in 2003". No controlled ablation separates this from phenology (2012 is attributed to drought), the cloud mask product is unnamed, and no code exists. Confidence high.

**Xu et al. 2020 (DeepCropMapping)** is audited from code and the ICML CCAI precursor because the RSE text is paywalled. `preprocessing/preprocess_ARD.ipynb` derives validity from PIXELQA fill, clear, cloud-shadow and cloud bits, keeps a pixel only "if the number of valid observations after May 15 >= 7", and fills each weekly date from 22 April to 23 September with `np.interp` between the nearest valid observations, extrapolating at the edges; `experiments/spatial_transfer.ipynb` trains on `Site_A` and evaluates on `Site_B` 2018 with the training-site scaler and an attention Bi-LSTM that has no mask input ([DeepCropMapping](https://github.com/Lab-IDEAS/DeepCropMapping); [precursor](https://ccai-papers.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/icml2019/25/paper.pdf)). The pipeline logs a "Validity percentage" and saves `availability.npy`, so the numbers exist, but whether the paper reports them per site and year could not be checked ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2020.111946)). A cloudier target site is silently thinned of its cloudiest pixels and fed smoother interpolated values. Provisional rank 2, low-medium confidence.

**Konduri et al. 2020** train a MODIS-NDVI phenoregion classifier on 2008 to 2014 and test on 2015 to 2018 across CONUS; only the abstract was readable, which mentions ecoregion-based labelling to reduce "spatial variability in phenology caused by variations in climate" and nothing about quality flags or gaps ([ORNL abstract](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/mapping-crops-within-growing-season-across-united-states); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2020.112048)). Provisional rank 2 as a method-family prior; low confidence.

**Wang, Azzari and Lobell 2019** transfer random forests "across geographic distance and time" in the US Midwest using harmonic-regression coefficients of Landsat surface reflectance in Earth Engine; only the abstract and a methods snippet were reachable, so the cloud mask, any minimum-observation threshold and the discussion's attribution could not be found ([Stanford FSI abstract](https://fsi.stanford.edu/node/28551); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2018.12.026)). Harmonic fits avoid explicit filling but their amplitude and phase depend on clear-observation count and timing. Provisional rank 2, low confidence.

**Weikmann, Paris and Bruzzone 2021 (TimeSen2Crop)** build 12 monthly composites over 15 Austrian tiles for September 2017 to August 2018 by "discarding the cloudy, snowy, and shadowy samples. If no cloud-free images are available for a considered pixel within the month, the harmonization process set all the reflectance values of the month to zero", split 13 training tiles from one test and one validation tile, and distribute a 33UVP 2018 to 2019 tile as a cross-year domain-adaptation challenge ([TimeSen2Crop paper](https://rslab.disi.unitn.it/web/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/09408357.pdf); [description](https://rslab.disi.unitn.it/timesen2crop/TimeSen2Crop_Description.pdf); [Zenodo 4715631](https://zenodo.org/records/4715631)). The paper acknowledges that "for some tiles no images are available for some months" and ships per-sample clear/cloud/shadow/snow flags and per-tile acquisition dates, so the zero-month fraction per tile is computable, but it is never reported and no official loader exists. Confidence high.

**Tseng, Zvonkov, Nakalembe and Kerner 2021 (CropHarvest)** pre-train globally and fine-tune and test in Kenya, Brazil and Togo using Sentinel-2 L1C "cloud-free" monthly composites built by taking "the least-cloudy pixel within each 30-day window" and Sentinel-1 medians where an empty window takes "the temporally closest available observation" ([CropHarvest paper](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/54229abfcfa5649e7003b83dd4755294-Paper-round2.pdf)). In code, `cropharvest/eo/sentinel2.py` uses `cloudThresh = 0.2`, `computeS2CloudScore` and `qualityMosaic("cloudShadowScore")`, and `Engineer.fillna` in `cropharvest/engineer.py` fills NaN "with the mean of each band across the timestep" via `np.nan_to_num(array[:, i], nan=mean_per_band[i])`, dropping the instance only if a whole band is NaN ([cropharvest repository](https://github.com/nasaharvest/cropharvest)). No per-region cloud statistics are given and task differences are attributed to imbalance and initialization, although tropical forest biomes carry a mean annual cloud frequency of about 80 percent ([Wilson and Jetz 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1002415)). Not rank 3 because the target's cloud regime is seen during in-region fine-tuning and SAR channels are gap-free. Confidence medium.

### Rank 1, the careful counter-examples

**Pham et al. 2024** is the paper the manuscript should cite as the published demonstration of the mechanism. Training on Brandenburg 2018 (14 classes, LPIS reference) and mapping every year 2010 to 2021, the authors place "all annual clear sky observations (CSO)" from FORCE/Fmask on a 365-day grid where "for the DOYs without observation (no-data observations) and non-clear observations (cloud, cloud shadow), 0 is assigned", report annual CSO counts (Fig. 1b, Supplement 1; "on average, pixels have more than 80 CSO in 2018"), and find that "the level of decrease was closely related to the number of annual CSOs", that "the TE-only model is highly overfitted without the use of augmentation methods, i.e., only performed well in the training year and poorly in unseen years", and that the linear-interpolation baseline fell to 30 to 54 percent in sparse years while the baseline exceeded 60 ([Pham et al. 2024](https://literatur.thuenen.de/digbib_extern/dn068235.pdf)). Random Observation Selection, which zeroes a random fraction of clear observations during training, recovers most of the loss and matters more than the phenology augmentation. No code is released ("data on request"). Confidence high.

**Wang, Di Tommaso, Deines and Lobell 2020 (CSDL)** hindcast corn and soybean for 1999 to 2007 from models trained on CDL 2008 to 2018, masking with `pixel_qa`, fitting second-order harmonics only where "at least 5 points" exist and leaving other pixels unclassified, and reporting that "the median cloud-free image count at a pixel during key crop growing months June-August was 7" (4 in 2012); an Ohio 2006 failure is traced to a Landsat track with no clear images from July to September ([Wang et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7493954/)). The repository's `validate_map/4_LandsatAvailability.ipynb` generates the availability figure by state and year; the GEE feature script `users/sherrie/csdl:1_exportLandsatHarmonics` is external ([csdl](https://github.com/LobellLab/csdl)). Borderline 2 because harmonics fitted on five observations are noisier than on ten and that is acknowledged, not quantified. Confidence high.

**The WorldCereal deployment paper (2025)** holds out countries (Spain, Nigeria, Latvia, Tanzania, Ethiopia, Argentina for cropland; ten countries for crop type) and the year 2021, and states a processing-shift hypothesis outright: "Presto uses the least cloudy Sentinel-2 pixel within a time window and was trained on the L1C processing level, while we only keep cloud-free observations and work with the L2A processing level ... Presto may struggle to adapt to this shift in data processing. To solve this, we introduced an additional SSL step" ([local WorldCereal PDF](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Deploying%20Geospatial%20Foundation%20Models%20in%20the%20Real%20World%3A%20Lessons%20from%20WorldCereal.pdf)). The pipeline's `src/worldcereal/openeo/preprocessing.py` applies an SCL dilation mask (`kernel1_size=17`, `kernel2_size=77`, `mask1_values=[2, 4, 5, 6, 7]`, `mask2_values=[3, 8, 9, 10, 11]`, `erosion_kernel_size=3`) and `median_compositing(s2_data, period=compositing_window)` with `"month"` default, and the Presto data README sets "the general no data value ... at 65535" passed "through as masked values" ([preprocessing.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldCereal/worldcereal-classification/main/src/worldcereal/openeo/preprocessing.py); [presto-worldcereal README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldCereal/presto-worldcereal/main/data/README.md)). The v1 system paper describes 10-day medians with remaining gaps "linearly interpolated" and no regional availability breakdown ([Van Tricht et al. 2023](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/15/5491/2023/)). No per-country valid counts appear, so Tanzania (F1 0.44) and Ethiopia (0.68 to 0.73) remain open to a persistent-cloud explanation. Rank 1 leaning 2, confidence medium.

**Yuan and Lin 2021 (SITS-BERT)** process L1C to L2A with Sen2Cor 2.8 and discard per pixel "low-quality observations belonging to clouds (including cirrus), cloud-shadows, and snow", require "at least three clear observations", keep sequences irregular with day-of-year encoding so "no smoothing, gap filling, or compositing method is needed", corrupt 15 percent of observations in pretraining with positive noise "to simulate abnormal reflectance increases caused by clouds and snow/ice" and negative noise for shadows, and report image counts per dataset (219 pretraining, 45, 26, 20 fine-tuning) ([SITS-BERT](https://github.com/linlei1214/SITS-BERT)). Fine-tuning is within tile, so there is no transfer degradation to confound. The padding rule to `--max_length 64` is not stated. Confidence medium.

**Rustowicz et al. 2019** train and test within Ghana, within South Sudan and within Germany, filter out "all scenes with less than 25 observations", leave Sentinel-2 unmasked, and offer cloud information as input: `preprocess.py` has `remap_cloud_stack`, `sample_timeseries` drawing dates with `np.random.choice(..., p=probabilities)` weighted by clear fraction, `get_least_cloudy_idx` and `preprocessClouds` appending a scaled cloud band; `datasets.py` has `sample_w_clouds`, `include_clouds`, `least_cloudy` flags, `pad_to_equal_length` zero-padding to the batch maximum with returned lengths, and `split_and_aggregate` substituting `np.zeros` for an empty bucket; `remove_bad_timestamps.py` drops grids listed in `bad_timestamp_grids_list.npy` ([crop-type-mapping](https://github.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping); [preprocess.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping/master/preprocess.py); [datasets.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/roserustowicz/crop-type-mapping/master/datasets.py)). The authors say South Sudan beats Ghana "likely because Ghana is a much cloudier region" and ablate cloud inputs. The zero-padding and `np.zeros` fallback are the same sentinel-value pattern as our September artefact, but distributed identically across in-country splits; they would become a confound the moment anyone applied these models across countries. Confidence medium-high.

**Pankajakshan, Padmasanan and Sundar 2025** train on Indian Pines, Salinas and Pavia and run open-set inference on one PRISMA scene over Barengo, Piedmont, acquired 25 April 2020; with single-date hyperspectral inputs no temporal missingness can exist, and the 3D-CNN collapse ("not transferable ... despite ... a training accuracy of 99.49%") is a cross-sensor spectral shift validated only by Sentinel-2 NDVI proxies ([Pankajakshan et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26010174); [code](https://github.com/praveenpankaj/mdpi_sensors_hsi/)). Contrast case, not to be pooled with time-series papers.

**Schulthess et al. 2023** classify the Yaqui Valley from "12 images, acquired by Sentinel-2A for tile 12RXR between 13 November 2016, and 22 April 2017" inside Sen2-Agri, where MAJA-masked pixels are "gap filled, based on a linear interpolation between cloud-free pixels of the previous and subsequent image(s)" into 10-day composites, with a random 80/20 field split; the authors credit "the dry and relatively cloud-free winter months" and warn that results need testing "in regions with ... frequent cloud cover" ([Schulthess et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608)). No transfer, and the cleanest gap documentation of the eight local PDFs.

**Tufail, Tassinari and Torreggiani 2025** use eleven hand-selected dates with "less than 5% cloud coverage over the study area" on tile T32TPQ with a stratified polygon split and no pixel mask, compositing or fill ([Tufail et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs17183207)). There is no target domain; the first failure on any other tile would be the absence of those eleven dates. **Nowakowski et al. 2021** fine-tune ImageNet CNNs on drone RGB orthomosaics in Malawi and Mozambique with an in-campaign, distribution-matched hold-out; clouds appear only as within-image shadows ([Nowakowski et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2021.102313)). Both are N/A.

## Per-paper evidence, other land-cover and vegetation papers

### Rank 3

**Masolele et al. 2021** compare continental and pan-tropical models of land use following deforestation for 2000 to 2005 from Landsat 5 and 7 "median composite images (January–June, July–December)" filtered "using the quality assessment band" with scenes under 50 percent cloud, and find "all models performed better when the scope of the problem was limited to a single continent", attributed to land-use spatial patterns that "share more commonalities" within continents ([Masolele et al. 2021](https://edepot.wur.nl/551332)). What a pixel receives when a six-month window has no valid observation (a GEE median of an empty stack is masked) is never described; cloud fractions per continent are never reported, although the authors write that clouds "cover most tropical areas of Africa and Asia" and that attention models "are able to cope with these high levels of cloud coverage". Pre-Landsat-8, with SLC-off striping from 2003, over the Congo basin and insular Southeast Asia is the sparsest optical archive in the audit; a 2022 HLS assessment finds "the tropical regions have the lowest number of clear observations" ([USGS HLS 2022](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/towards-seamless-global-30-meter-terrestrial-monitoring-evaluating-2022-cloud-free)). No code or data statement with a URL was found. Confidence high.

- **Transfer finding:** The pan-tropical Hybrid CNN-LSTM and CNN-MHSA models reach micro-average F1 of 58 and 66 percent and macro-average F1 of 53 and 61 percent, and "all models performed better when the scope of the problem was limited to a single continent"; the continental values appear only in Figure 4, so the magnitude of the gap is not reported in accessible text. The authors infer that land-use spatial patterns "share more commonalities" within a continent than across continents.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is six-month Landsat 5 and 7 median composites built from scenes under 50 percent cloud, with the result for a pixel that has no valid observation in a window never described (a GEE median of an empty stack is masked, so the model received either a nodata constant or a filled value). Africa's Congo basin and insular Southeast Asia are far cloudier than much of Latin America and all three carry SLC-off striping from 2003, so the pan-tropical model must fit three gap regimes at once while each continental model sees one; the within-continent commonality the authors describe is equally well explained by common composite completeness, and the pan-tropical penalty is therefore overstated as a landscape effect. The check is a per-continent count of valid observations per composite, followed by a pan-tropical rerun restricted to samples with matched observation counts or with the count supplied as an input.

**Stoian et al. 2019** train FG-UNET and RF on ten Sentinel-2 tiles across France for 2016 and test on the eleventh, gap-filled by "linear temporal gapfilling ... for pixels flagged as clouds, cloud shadows, saturation or non acquired" and resampled to 33 dates; they report "a drop in performance when a tile is not part of learning ... particularly high for mountain tiles (T30TYN and T31TGK) whose content is different from other tiles" and that clear acquisitions range "from five ... in northern and mountainous areas to more than 30 in the sunny mediterranean areas" ([Stoian et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs11171986)). The tiles with the largest drop are the tiles at the sparse end of the authors' own range, and per-tile counts are not cross-tabulated with Figure 19. The repository `vpoughon/RT_DL_OSO_public` expects already gap-filled 330-channel iota2 output; `Sentinel2_prepare_data.py` computes percentiles for normalisation and excludes label pixels only where band 1 equals zero, with no nodata, cloud or NaN handling ([RT_DL_OSO_public](https://github.com/vpoughon/RT_DL_OSO_public); [Sentinel2_prepare_data.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vpoughon/RT_DL_OSO_public/master/Sentinel2_prepare_data.py)). Rank 3 because the confound was testable from the paper's own evidence and was not tested; borderline 2 because the gap-filling is fully described. Confidence high.

- **Transfer finding:** Figure 19 compares Cohen's kappa per tile for a model trained on all eleven tiles against a model trained on ten and tested on the eleventh: the drop is "particularly high for mountain tiles (T30TYN and T31TGK)" and for T30TXQ, there is "almost no drop for tile T30UXV", and "FG-UNET suffers from a higher decrease than RF"; the kappa values are shown graphically only, so the magnitude is not reported in accessible text. The authors attribute the drop to tile content that "is different from other tiles" and to scarce labels.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is linear interpolation across long gaps in the iota2 gap-filling, which resamples every pixel to 33 dates whatever the number of clear acquisitions; by the authors' own statement that number ranges from five in northern and mountain areas to more than 30 in the Mediterranean, so a held-out mountain tile presents profiles that are mostly straight-line segments of a shape the ten training tiles rarely contain, whereas including the tile exposes the model to those shapes. The cross-tile drop therefore conflates landscape content with gap structure, and FG-UNET's larger decrease is what a model fitting texture and temporal shape would show when the shapes are interpolation artefacts; the attribution to content is untested. The check is to cross-tabulate per-tile clear-acquisition counts with the Figure 19 kappa drop, or to hold out a cloudy lowland tile and a clear mountain tile and compare.

**Verhulst et al. 2024** classify five tree species in Flanders with Sentinel-2 dekadal medians for 2018 to 2022 and report cross-year OA losses from 2.81 to 14.92 percentage points, keeping only SCL 4 and 5 dilated with an 11-pixel Gaussian kernel, then "linear interpolation ... to fill the remaining gaps", then "a backward or forward fill, copying the first or last available observation", then Savitzky-Golay smoothing ([Verhulst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16142653)). No cloud-free dekad count per year is reported; the authors describe 2021 as "a very wet year" whose NDVI curves "deviated most from the other years from January until June", and concede "it is likely that interannual differences in climate and image quality play a role" and that "it is, however, difficult to attribute these differences to a specific cause". A wet year has the fewest valid dekads and the longest interpolated spans, so the anomaly year and the gappiest year coincide. Data are "not publicly available due to being part of an ongoing study" and no code was found. The multi-year-training remedy is equally consistent with the missingness explanation. Confidence medium-high.

- **Transfer finding:** Average cross-year OA losses range from 2.81 ± 0.77 (2022) to 8.12 ± 2.71 percentage points (2021) for RF, from 3.19 ± 2.65 (2019) to 14.92 ± 3.10 (2018) for SVM, and from 2.30 ± 5.38 (2022) to 9.03 ± 2.74 for the MLP, with multi-year training reducing the loss. The authors state that spectral-class relationships "are not necessarily consistent in time", that "interannual differences in climate and image quality play a role", and that "it is, however, difficult to attribute these differences to a specific cause".
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is interpolation across long gaps followed by forward and backward edge fill and Savitzky-Golay smoothing after an SCL mask dilated by 11 pixels; the fill rate scales with cloudiness, so the wet year 2021 has the fewest valid dekads, the longest interpolated spans and the most copied edge values. The January to June NDVI deviation the authors observe in 2021 is what interpolation across a cloudy winter and spring produces, so a model trained on a dry year meets smoother, flatter series in the wet year and the measured loss is inflated relative to any true change in species spectra; multi-year training also helps by exposing the model to varied gap patterns, so the remedy does not discriminate between the two causes. The check is a count of valid dekads per plot-year regressed against the per-pair OA loss, or a rerun with dekads masked to the set valid in every year.

**Maleki and Rahdari 2026** transfer wetland classifiers between the Camargue and the Étangs de la Champagne humide for 2021 using Sentinel-2 "resampled to a regular 6-day interval, resulting in 60 observations", after a `CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE < 50` filter, `MSK_CLDPRB < 1`, `MSK_SNWPRB < 10` and SCL masking, with gaps "filled using linear interpolation over time for each pixel"; OA reductions are 3.8 to 16.9 percent in one direction and 11.0 to 23.8 in the other, attributed to "a stronger distributional mismatch" and hydrology ([Maleki and Rahdari 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18081234)). Valid counts per site are not reported. The two sites sit at the opposite ends of the French gradient documented by Stoian (five versus more than 30 clear acquisitions), with the Camargue in a Sentinel-2 orbit overlap, so the Champagne 60-step series is far more interpolated; a model trained on heavily interpolated smooth series meeting sharp real Camargue dynamics predicts exactly the observed asymmetry. The paper's repository URL is dead; the live `Saeideh-Maleki/Wetland_Spatial_Transferability-` (trailing hyphen) holds `InceptionTime.py`, `MLP.py`, `RF.py`, `XGBOOST.py`, `ensemble_main_wetland.py`, `model.py` and no preprocessing or interpolation code ([repository](https://github.com/Saeideh-Maleki/Wetland_Spatial_Transferability-)). Confidence high.

- **Transfer finding:** Relative to within-site baselines, mapping Champagne with Camargue-trained models reduces OA by 3.8 to 16.9 percent (best transferred OA 90 percent), while mapping Camargue with Champagne-trained models reduces it by 11.0 to 23.8 percent (best 84 percent). The authors attribute the larger loss in the second direction to "a stronger distributional mismatch" and to hydrology, turbidity and phenology differences.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is linear interpolation onto a regular 6-day grid of 60 steps after SCL and cloud-probability masking, so the fraction of synthetic steps scales with cloudiness: Champagne humide in continental NE France has far more winter cloud than the Camargue, which also sits in a Sentinel-2 orbit overlap, so the Champagne series are much more interpolated. A model trained on smooth, heavily interpolated Champagne series meeting sharp real Camargue dynamics loses more than the reverse, which is exactly the observed asymmetry, so the asymmetric loss is explainable by fill rate alone and the spectral-temporal mismatch attribution is unsupported. The check is a per-site count of real post-mask observations per pixel, with the Camargue series degraded to Champagne's observation pattern before retraining.

**Huppertz et al. 2021** train a burned-area segmenter on Portugal 2016 and apply it to West Nile, Uganda for 2015 to 2020 from single-date Sentinel-2 L1C B8A/B3/B12 stacks with no cloud handling at all: `src/preparedata/merge_bands.py` fixes `dict_of_bands = {"SWIR-band": "B12", "NIR-band": "B8A", "Green-band": "B03"}` and writes values unchanged, `extract_patches.py` filters fires by end date and area with no cloud mask, QA check or nodata test, and `sort_patches.py` removes only empty or broken patches ([Huppertz et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14372); [merge_bands.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prhuppertz/Burned_Area_Detection/master/src/preparedata/merge_bands.py); [extract_patches.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prhuppertz/Burned_Area_Detection/master/src/preparedata/extract_patches.py)). Portuguese fire-season scenes are near cloud-free; West Nile's bimodal wet season puts shadows into the SWIR/NIR composite as dark patches that Hu and Ban list among the disturbances spectrally similar to burn scars ([Hu and Ban 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13081509)). The "large amount of additionally detected BAs" over MCD64A1 is read as a sensitivity gain with validation pending. Confidence high for the mechanism, low for the magnitude, since no Uganda score exists.

- **Transfer finding:** The Portugal test gives Dice 0.661 ± 0.3 and IoU 0.559 ± 0.3; Uganda has no quantitative score, only "a large amount of additionally detected BAs" relative to MCD64A1 with validation pending, so the transfer degradation is not reported in accessible text. The authors attribute any shift to "the significant domain shift between Portugal and Northern Ugandan vegetation" and to L1C atmospheric biases.
- **Takeaway:** The mechanism is unmasked clouds and shadows entering single-date L1C B8A, B3 and B12 stacks as data, since `merge_bands.py` and `extract_patches.py` apply no cloud, QA or nodata test. Portuguese fire-season scenes in summer 2016 are near cloud-free while West Nile's bimodal wet seasons put cloud shadow into the SWIR and NIR bands as dark patches that Hu and Ban list among the disturbances spectrally similar to burn scars, so the extra detections read as a sensitivity gain are plausibly shadow false positives; because no Uganda metric exists, the contamination inflates mapped burned area rather than depressing a score. The check is a per-scene cloud and shadow fraction (SCL or s2cloudless) for the Uganda scenes, with the additional detections overlaid on the shadow mask, or a rerun restricted to scenes under 5 percent cloud.

### Rank 2

**Brown et al. 2022 (Dynamic World)** run single-scene inference on Sentinel-2 L1C scenes with `CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE <= 35%`, masking with S2 Cloud Probability at 65 percent, the Cloud Displacement Index, a B10 cirrus threshold and a shadow mask projected 5 km opposite the solar azimuth; agreement with 409 expert tiles is 73.8 percent and regional variation is stated only as performing "most strongly in temperate and tree-dominated biomes" ([Brown et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01307-4)). The authors do say performance "varies spatially and temporally as a function of both the quality of S2 cloud masking and variability in land cover", but no per-biome accuracy or cloud fraction is tabulated, and the public `single_image_runner.ipynb` applies no mask at all, normalising with `np.log(image * 0.005 + 1)` and `NORM_PERCENTILES` ([google/dynamicworld](https://github.com/google/dynamicworld); [notebook](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/dynamicworld/master/single_image_runner.ipynb)). Per-scene inference avoids gap-filling artefacts, which is why this is not rank 3; downstream annual composites inherit very different valid-scene counts by region. Confidence medium-high.

**Corbane et al. 2021 (GHS-BUILT-S2)** train per UTM zone with "far range transfer learning" to 28 poorly labelled zones from "a global cloud-free image composite for reference year 2018", report per-continent balanced accuracy above 0.7 with errors "mainly observed in mountainous areas with bare rocks", and assert the CNN is "robust to noise in satellite imagery (e.g., presence of snow, clouds, haze)" while acknowledging a "lack of cloud-free satellite data in some regions" ([Corbane et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.03267)). Composite valid counts are never compared to accuracy; no model code was found. Confidence medium.

**Ploton et al. 2020** show Central African biomass models falling from random-CV R² 0.53 to spatial-CV R² 0.14 using MODIS MAIAC 10-year (2000 to 2010) mean and standard-deviation layers kept "using AOD and BRF quality flags", with no count of retained observations per pixel ([Ploton et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18321-y)). This is a spatial-CV finding rather than region transfer and a decade-long mean dampens single-year gaps, but the SD layers built from a variable number of quality-flagged observations encode cloudiness as a smooth coast-to-interior covariate, which is exactly what blocked CV removes; the figshare CV scripts do not process raw MAIAC ([figshare scripts](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.12790085)). Confidence medium.

**Ludwig, Bahlmann, Pebesma and Meyer 2022**, with Meyer et al. 2019 and the Area of Applicability, test Sentinel-2 land cover in Germany on "independent test data of geographically distinct areas" (0.71 versus spatial-CV 0.84) using "a median composite of all available Sentinel-2 scene with less than 20% overall cloud cover" with SCL masking; empty-stack behaviour and valid counts are not described ([Ludwig et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-archives-XLIII-B3-2022-135-2022); [Meyer et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07805)). The AOA is a predictor-space dissimilarity, so a composite built from fewer valid pixels registers as "outside AOA" indistinguishably from landscape novelty. Code at `LOEK-RS/ISPRS2022LCC` (not opened; compositing is server-side in openEO). Confidence medium-high.

**Pinto et al. 2020 (BA-Net)** map and date burned areas from daily VIIRS sequences "tested in five regions around the globe"; `banet/data.py` reads no quality flags, applies the union of I-band netCDF masks, runs `mask2nan` then `nan2zero` so masked observations become zero, and saves tensors with `mask_value = 65535` ([banet/data.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mnpinto/banet/main/banet/data.py); [banet](https://github.com/mnpinto/banet)). Zero fill of cloudy daily observations is the rubric mechanism, but whether the paper claims region-to-region degradation and how it attributes it was not verified from full text. Provisional rank 2, low confidence.

### Rank 1

**Inglada et al. 2017 (iota2)** is the operational baseline that Stoian and the French school inherit and the only operational chain here that maps its own clear-observation density: "Figure 8 displays the map of the count of clear pixels ... The count goes from 2 to 29 for the whole 2014 year", with linear interpolation of invalid pixels implemented in the OTB Temporal Gapfilling library and the statement that "the quality of the classification strongly depends on the amount of cloud-free imagery in every given point" ([Inglada et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs9010095); [iota2 gapfilling docs](https://iota2.readthedocs.io/en/master/_sources/sentinel_2_L3A.rst.txt)). It does not quantify accuracy against clear count, so it supplies the diagnostic that downstream transfer papers then omit. **Ortega Adarme et al. 2020** train and test within the Amazon and within the Cerrado from two clear dry-season Landsat 8 dates per site and never cross biomes ([Ortega Adarme et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12060910)). **Hu, Ban and Nascetti 2021** train on six fires and test on three unseen fires and a Landsat-8 transfer from single clear post-fire scenes with listed dates, noting that "the acquisition of Sentinel-2 or Landsat-8 testing images depends on the cloud coverage" ([Hu and Ban 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13081509)). Identical acquisition structure in every domain means missingness cannot differ, which is the structural protection the time-series papers lack.

Outside the two tables, the only non-crop papers found that tested missingness directly both confirm the mechanism: Pham et al. 2024 (RSE 308) show for land-cover fraction regression in Germany and Poland that "models trained in high-density years performed well in that same year but poorly when transferred to lower-density years" and that Random Observation Selection improves transfer by up to about 16 percent in MAE ([Pham et al. 2024, RSE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114206)), and Schwieder et al. 2022 find grassland mowing detection "less influenced by data availability when at least 16 cloud-free observations were available" ([Schwieder et al. 2022](https://eolab.geographie.hu-berlin.de/publication/schwieder_2022/)). No paper in any domain was found that tests region-to-region transfer with matched valid-observation counts or density as a covariate.

## Benchmarks propagate the confound through loader code the papers never describe

The datasets most used for cross-domain tests encode their fill policy in loader code rather than in the paper, so downstream transfer papers inherit it silently. Across thirteen benchmark products, only ZueriCrop and EuroCropsML ship a per-observation cloud flag and an official loader that uses it; BreizhCrops, DENETHOR and the AI4FoodSecurity starter ship flags that the default loaders discard; and none of the datasets whose official split crosses regions or years documents the clear-observation count or missing-month distribution per domain.

Three of these deserve emphasis for the manuscript. TimeSen2Crop most closely reproduces our mechanism: its official recipe zero-fills months without a clear observation, the test tile and the 2018-19 challenge tile differ from the 13 training tiles in cloud and snow regime by the authors' own account, and no loader masks the zero months, so any model benchmarked on either split inherits the confound ([TimeSen2Crop description](https://rslab.disi.unitn.it/timesen2crop/TimeSen2Crop_Description.pdf)). Sen4AgriNet's OAD result, where the LSTM is almost unaffected by the Catalonia-to-France move while the transformer and TempCNN collapse to F1 about 30, is the signature expected when month-indexed inputs carry domain-specific interpolated months; the paper reads it as phenological stretching, and the per-month empty-bin rate per country is recoverable from the released netCDF `timestamp` arrays. Planet Fusion's "gap-free" promise moves the problem upstream: the fraction of synthesised days differs by year (Brandenburg 2018 versus 2019) and tile (SA 258N/259N versus 20E_259N) and is recoverable only from the QA layer that neither the DENETHOR loader nor the AI4Food starter reads. In-domain benchmarks (PASTIS, ZueriCrop, SICKLE, SustainBench, AgriFieldNet, Kenya) are not confounded in their official form because folds share one acquisition calendar, but their zero-pad collates and zero-filled grids would propagate the confound unchanged on any cross-tile or cross-year reuse; the PASTIS-derived cross-year work of Quinton and Landrieu already has 36, 27 and 29 valid entries per year without a per-year accuracy breakdown by valid count ([Quinton and Landrieu 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.08187)). The irregular-length datasets that resample to a fixed length (BreizhCrops at 45 steps "either 51 or 102 observations per field parcel" from orbit overlap; Gallo at 71) hide region-dependent density behind a uniform tensor shape ([BreizhCrops paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.11893)). A practical reanalysis for the manuscript is to compute the per-domain filled-slot fraction for each public cross-domain benchmark from the released date vectors and flags (TimeSen2Crop `dates.csv` and flags, Sen4AgriNet `timestamp`, EuroCropsML DOY arrays, BreizhCrops `sequencelength` and `meanCLD` index columns, PASTIS `dates-S2`); only CropHarvest and the Planet Fusion products lack the information.

## No remote-sensing journal requires code, so the data availability statement is the audit trail

The reason so many cards above rest on paper text alone is structural. As of 2026 no remote-sensing journal in scope requires code deposit; the only venues where custom code must reach editors and reviewers are Nature Communications ("for all studies using custom code or mathematical algorithm that is deemed central to the conclusions, a statement must be included under the heading 'Code availability' ... we require sharing of code/algorithm during the peer review process") and Scientific Data ("Authors must make available, to editors, reviewers, and eventual readers, any previously unreported custom computer code") ([Nature Portfolio reporting standards](https://www.nature.com/nature-portfolio/editorial-policies/reporting-standards); [Scientific Data policies](https://www.nature.com/sdata/policies/editorial-and-publishing-policies)). Elsevier "encourage[s] and support[s] authors to share their research data rather than mandating them to do so", with each journal choosing one of five options from "encouraged" to reviewer-checked deposit, and the exact option adopted by RSE, ISPRS JPRS, JAG, CEA, AFM and Science of Remote Sensing could not be read because the ScienceDirect guide-for-authors pages returned 403 to every fetch; registries list RSE and Science of Remote Sensing as "Deposit recommended" ([Elsevier research data FAQ](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/research-data/faq); [Elsevier data guidelines](https://www.elsevier.com/researcher/author/tools-and-resources/research-data/data-guidelines); [CIRAD entry for RSE](https://ou-publier.cirad.fr/en/node/3576)). IEEE states "all IEEE authors are encouraged to share their data, code, and other research outputs" with an optional Code Ocean upload dating from a 2017 partnership; the TGRS Information for Authors contains no code, data or reproducibility clause at all; JSTARS lists an "Optional Code Ocean Submission" and the GRSS Remote Sensing Code Library "is not currently accepting new submissions" ([IEEE Author Center](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/create-your-ieee-journal-article/research-reproducibility/); [TGRS Information for Authors](https://www.grss-ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Information-for-Authors_TGRS_pdf.pdf); [RSCL](https://tools.grss-ieee.org/rscl1/about.html)). MDPI requires a data availability statement "on every published article" while deposit "is recommended, not strictly mandatory", with no code requirement ([CASRAI comparison](https://www.casrai.org/compare/data-sharing-policy-publisher-comparison)). Earth System Science Data requires a DOI and anonymous review access for data but not code ([ESSD data policy](https://www.earth-system-science-data.net/policies/data_policy.html)). Among machine-learning venues, CVPR "highly encourage[s]" but does not require code, the NeurIPS checklist asks for code but "no is an acceptable answer", ICLR's reproducibility statement is optional, and only the NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks track moved to "required dataset and benchmark code submission" in 2025 ([CVPR 2025](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2025/AuthorSuggestedPractices); [NeurIPS checklist](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/PaperChecklist); [NeurIPS D&B 2025](https://blog.neurips.cc/?p=1241); [ICLR 2026](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorGuide)).

The implication for auditability is direct. For transfer papers published 2019 to 2025 in RSE, ISPRS JPRS, JAG, CEA, TGRS, JSTARS, GRSL or MDPI Remote Sensing, a vague cloud-handling description cannot be resolved from a mandated repository; only voluntarily linked GitHub or Code Ocean repositories exist, and in this sample those voluntary repositories were decisive in both directions. Code confirmed and extended the paper in Wijesingha (`dropna()`), TimeMatch (no mask applied), DENETHOR (QA layer ignored), Kerner (NaN to zero), Sen4AgriNet (interpolation and extrapolation), Pandžić (no minimum-observation filter), Dynamic World (public notebook unmasked), and DACCN at one remove (the You et al. 2021 figshare script it follows ends its interpolation with `unmask(0)`), while repositories that ship only classifiers consuming pre-filled arrays (RT_DL_OSO_public, Wetland_Spatial_Transferability-, SFUDA-CropMapping) left inference-time gap behaviour uninspectable. Reviewers at every venue except Nature Communications, Scientific Data, ESSD (data) and NeurIPS D&B 2025 worked from the manuscript alone, so a reviewer-stage check of missingness handling was structurally unavailable for nearly every paper in the two tables. What remains mandatory almost everywhere is the data availability statement, which usually names the imagery source (a GEE collection, Copernicus, Planet) and sometimes the preprocessing product; that is enough to re-derive cloud conditions after the fact by the procedure below, even when code is absent. A manuscript making the missingness argument should therefore report, for its own pipeline, the per-domain filled fraction that these papers omit; our corrected pipeline (training-tile median fill applied to every model) belongs to the same family and must be held to the same standard.

## Post-hoc cloud forensics works without an account, and 2017 Western Cape shows two winter blackouts

Because the data statement fixes the imagery provenance, a reader can reconstruct the target's cloud history after publication. The fastest route with no credentials is a single GET to AWS Earth Search, which for the Western Cape returned 167 Sentinel-2 L1C granules for May to September 2017 with `datetime`, `grid:code` (for example `MGRS-34HDH`) and `eo:cloud_cover` per granule ([Earth Search query](https://earth-search.aws.element84.com/v1/search?collections=sentinel-2-l1c&bbox=19.9,-34.4,20.3,-34.0&datetime=2017-05-01T00:00:00Z/2017-09-30T23:59:59Z&limit=100&fields=id,properties.datetime,properties.eo:cloud_cover,properties.grid:code,-geometry,-assets,-links); [collection](https://earth-search.aws.element84.com/v1/collections/sentinel-2-l1c)). The Copernicus Data Space OData endpoint does the same with a `cloudCover` attribute filter and `OData.CSC.Intersects` footprint, its STAC endpoint accepts `filter=eo:cloud_cover<15`, and Microsoft Planetary Computer `sentinel-2-l2a` items additionally carry `s2:cloud_shadow_percentage`, `s2:high_proba_clouds_percentage`, `s2:thin_cirrus_percentage` and `s2:nodata_pixel_percentage` ([CDSE OData](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/OData.html); [CDSE STAC](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/APIs/STAC.html); [Planetary Computer items](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1/collections/sentinel-2-l2a/items?limit=1)). Google Earth Engine gives the richest per-scene fields (`CLOUDY_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE`, `CLOUDY_SHADOW_PERCENTAGE`, `HIGH_PROBA_CLOUDS_PERCENTAGE`, `THIN_CIRRUS_PERCENTAGE`, `NODATA_PIXEL_PERCENTAGE`) plus the pixel-level products needed to map where gaps fell inside a field footprint: the SCL band (3 shadow, 8 and 9 cloud, 10 cirrus), `COPERNICUS/S2_CLOUD_PROBABILITY` (s2cloudless) and `GOOGLE/CLOUD_SCORE_PLUS/V1/S2_HARMONIZED` (`cs_cdf`, with 0.50 to 0.65 the recommended clear threshold), all back to 2015 ([GEE S2_SR_HARMONIZED](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/COPERNICUS_S2_SR_HARMONIZED); [Cloud Score+](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_CLOUD_SCORE_PLUS_V1_S2_HARMONIZED)). For Landsat, `QA_PIXEL` bits 1 to 4 (dilated cloud, cirrus, cloud, shadow) and scene `CLOUD_COVER_LAND` serve the same roles ([GEE LC08 C02 T1_L2](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/LANDSAT_LC08_C02_T1_L2)). The GEE recipe reduces to three steps: print the per-scene metadata table over the label footprint, map each scene to an SCL occlusion indicator and take the mean, or link Cloud Score+ and sum `cs_cdf >= 0.6` per pixel, then reduce to the label polygons for per-field clear counts by month. For MODIS-era or climatological context, EarthEnv monthly cloud frequency from fifteen years of twice-daily MODIS flags is in GEE as `projects/sat-io/open-datasets/gcc/MODCF_monthlymean` ([Wilson and Jetz 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4816575/); [GEE community catalog](https://gee-community-catalog.org/projects/gcc/)). Two cautions apply: granule-level cloud cover is tile-wide (about 110 km), so it ranks regions and dates reliably but a field can be clear inside a 40 percent cloudy granule, and 2017-era metadata came from the older ESA L1C algorithm, for which Sudmanns et al. flag "systematically incorrect cloud-cover estimates over high, non-vegetated terrain", so audits of 2017 papers should prefer s2cloudless or Cloud Score+ recomputed today over the QA60 or metadata the paper used ([Sudmanns et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2019.1572799)).

This procedure was run for the audit at two scales, and the outputs are in the notes folder rather than reproduced here. The harvest script `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/earthsearch_harvest.py` pulls granule-level `eo:cloud_cover` from Earth Search for 33 representative cropland points for 2018 to 2024 and summarises per region and month the granule count, median cloud percentage and the fraction of granules under 20 and under 50 percent cloud; its output is `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/earthsearch_s2_l1c_cloudcover_by_month_2018-2024.csv`. Measured as the in-season share of granules under 20 percent cloud, the cloudiest common targets are the monsoon and Guinea-savanna belts (Mato Grosso October to March 0.13, Nigeria Kaduna and Ghana Tamale May to October 0.14 to 0.15, Sichuan 0.15, Yunnan 0.17, Andhra Pradesh kharif 0.19, Ethiopian meher 0.26, Sahel 0.31 to 0.32, Tanzania 0.32), the usual European sources (Brittany, Denmark, Bavaria, Brandenburg, Netherlands, Belgium) sit at 0.23 to 0.27 for April to September with winter rather than growing-season maxima, and targets clearer than European sources include the Western Cape winter (0.49 to 0.52 for May to October), Catalonia (0.53), Punjab (0.53 to 0.55), the WA wheatbelt (0.64), Iran (0.65) and the Central Valley (0.81); the Corn Belt sits at 0.42. Published corroboration exists for several of these: Center-South Brazil's northern states show about 15 percent clear sky during November to February ([Eberhardt et al. 2016](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/8/3/219)); valid observations are "relatively lower in southern China (6.4 observations), especially in Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau" and the Sichuan Basin is "cloud-prone" ([Liu et al. 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/14/13/3134)); over Europe sub-monthly series "in some geographies requires interpolation of up to 50% of data" ([Lewińska et al. 2024](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.5tb2rbp94)). The implication for pairings is that any European or US source transferred to Nigeria, Ghana, Ethiopia, the Sahel, Andhra Pradesh, Yunnan, Sichuan, Mato Grosso or Tanzania faces a missingness shift by default, that the shape differs (European gaps spread through the season, monsoon targets a single June to September blackout so that most "growing-season" features come from shoulder months), and that European to Western Cape is clearer at granule level but carries two cloud pulses in the tillering-to-heading window.

For the AI4FoodSecurity tiles the same harvest was run on the exact label extents for calendar 2017, with output in `research_notes/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers/earthsearch_s2_l1c_cloudcover_SA_tiles_2017.csv`. The label collections on disk place `34S_19E_259N` at 20.52 to 20.78°E, 34.20 to 33.98°S (MGRS 34HDH only, 110 granules), `34S_19E_258N` at 20.52 to 20.78°E, 34.41 to 34.20°S (34HDG plus 34HDH, 214 granules) and the holdout `34S_20E_259N` at 20.78 to 21.04°E, 34.20 to 33.98°S (34HDH plus 34HEH, 164 granules), with the tile names unrelated to MGRS or to the longitudes they suggest ([source.coop dataset](https://source.coop/radiantearth/south-africa-crops-competition)). Three findings follow. First, 2017 winter had **two cloud pulses**: June, with a holdout median granule cloud of 59 percent and only 2 of 10 granules under 20 percent, and 11 to 21 August, three consecutive overpasses at 58 to 96 percent cloud, followed by a 15-day gap from 10 to 25 September in which the 15 and 25 September granules were 86 to 98 percent cloudy; at the holdout centre only 17 of 27 May to September acquisitions were under 20 percent and the longest clear-free runs were 9 May to 19 June (41 days), 7 to 25 August and 11 to 22 September. The derived IEEE DataPort dataset for this project independently records that "June 2017 had no usable imagery" after s2cloudless and shadow masking ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/south-africa-crop-comp)). Second, **2017 was cloudier than the 2018 to 2024 norm at the same points**: June P(clear granule) 0.33 to 0.40 against a climatology of 0.50 to 0.55, August 0.33 to 0.50 against 0.58 to 0.59, so monthly compositing with a missing June and a thin August and September is the dominant preprocessing artefact of this dataset in an unusually bad year. Third, and decisive for the manuscript's framing, **train and holdout granules were nearly identical on every shared date**: on the 23 May to September dates when both 34HDH and the holdout-only 34HEH were acquired, no date had one tile under 20 percent and the other at or above 50 percent (for example 17 June 93.5 versus 94.6, 11 August 89.2 versus 81.5, 15 September 98.4 versus 86.3). At granule scale the two regions saw the same cloud history, so the 40.6 percent September deficit measured on holdout pixels, versus none on training pixels, lives at the sub-tile scale (the holdout's eastern third in 34HEH, and local cloud and shadow footprints inside 34HDH) and can only be quantified by the pixel-level SCL or Cloud Score+ reduction over the field footprints. Acquisition density also doubled from July 2017 (holdout 10 granules in May and June, 19 in July) as Sentinel-2B ramped up, so the season mixes a 10-day and a 5-day revisit regime. The per-field pixel-level counts were not computed here because the GEE credentials on this machine could not be inspected under the session's permission policy and `light_learn/0_SA_ee_download_data_prep.py` has `ee.Initialize()` commented out with no project id; the recipe above is ready to run.

## Conclusion

The audit changes the shape of the claim the manuscript can make. It is not that nobody has noticed missingness: Rustowicz names clouds as why Ghana scores lower, Inglada maps clear counts, Cai and Wang 2020 link low availability to bad years, WorldCereal builds an intervention for a processing shift, and Pham 2024 isolates density as the primary driver of cross-year loss. It is that the field reports cloud handling as a preprocessing step and transfer degradation as a phenology result and almost never connects them with a per-domain statistic, and that the disconnect is written into the benchmarks (TimeSen2Crop's zero months, Sen4AgriNet's interpolated months, CropHarvest's mean months, Planet Fusion's unread QA layer) so that downstream papers inherit it without a sentence. The direction of the induced shift is not fixed: Wijesingha's and Verhulst's targets are gappier than their sources, Rusňák's spatial target is less densely acquired, Pandžić's source is plausibly cloudier than its target, and Xie's European target is cloudier than Kansas, so a missingness confound can inflate, deflate or merely redistribute an apparent transfer loss, and a KS test or an Area of Applicability registers it as covariate shift either way.

Two consequences follow for our own work. The Pham 2024 and DENETHOR pair, same region and training year with opposite conclusions, is the cleanest citable illustration that the per-domain clear-observation count is cheap and decisive, and the 2017 Western Cape forensics show that granule metadata alone would have declared our train and holdout tiles equivalent; the September shift is a sub-tile phenomenon that only a per-field Cloud Score+ clear-count reveals, which is the statistic the manuscript should publish for every tile and month, and the control (training-time observation dropout in the style of Random Observation Selection) is the one a referee will ask for before any architecture is credited with robustness. Seven of the ranks here (Kerner 2022, Mask-PSTIN, Xu, Konduri, Wang 2019, BA-Net, Che 2024) are provisional pending institutional access to ScienceDirect and Taylor & Francis (DACCN's full text, obtained after the first draft, confirmed its rank 3 and raised its confidence), and the per-tile cloud statistics that would convert several rank-3 inferences into measurements (TimeMatch's Zenodo archive, Sen4AgriNet's timestamps, TimeSen2Crop's flags, DACCN's six region-years through the You et al. 2021 Earth Engine script) are computable from released data and have not yet been computed by anyone.
