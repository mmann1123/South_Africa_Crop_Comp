# Add Presto, broaden transfer citations, trim eight

For R2-Q3a, add **a pretrained Presto pixel encoder as the primary new baseline and TimesNet (via THUML TSLib) as the second**. Presto is the baseline the reviewer named that is most likely to change the paper's conclusions, because it is pixel-native, tiny (about 0.4M parameters), MIT-licensed and backed by published crop-type geographic-shift evidence. TimesNet is the reviewer's first-named supervised model and the top classifier on TSLib's leaderboard. Drop PatchTST and TSViT/U-TAE: PatchTST's patching collapses to one or two tokens on about 10 monthly steps, and TSViT/U-TAE change the input to image patches and overlap the existing 3D CNN. MiniRocket is a cheap optional third baseline that runs in CPU minutes. For the associate editor (AE), the 2023 to 2026 literature from China, Europe, the Americas and Africa agrees on two points that sharpen the paper's framing. First, **phenological (temporal) misalignment, not capacity, is the dominant cause of cross-region failure**. Second, almost every published fix (unsupervised domain adaptation, sample migration, thermal-time re-indexing) uses target-domain data or explicit time normalization, so the paper's no-target-access, inductive-bias setting is a real gap in that literature. A prioritized list of 14 new citations follows, mapped to cea-article.tex lines 57, 59, 66, 75, 260 and 281. It is paired with **eight trims** (mann2017, mann2019, zhu2017, james2013, fan2023hcrnn, bandar2024bilstm, espejo2020, koklu2022) and about ten bib corrections. Together these take the reference list from 42 to roughly 48 entries while making it more on-topic and international.

## Presto plus TimesNet answer R2-Q3a better than PatchTST or TSViT

The choice of baseline should follow from what the reviewer is really testing: whether a more modern model would overturn the claim that sparse feature selection beats deep-learning capacity under transfer. Two axes matter, and each recommended model covers one. **Pretraining** is the axis the paper does not yet cover. **Modern supervised capacity** is the axis the reviewer named first.

### Why Presto is the primary addition

Presto pretrains a pixel time-series transformer on monthly Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 data, ERA5, SRTM, Dynamic World and location, using structured channel and timestep masking. It therefore accepts partial band sets and missing months by design ([Presto arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4)). It reported a CropHarvest mean F1 of **0.835 versus 0.441 for random forest** ([Presto arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4)). The closest published analogue to the paper's question is WorldCereal, where fine-tuned Presto beat a raw-feature CatBoost baseline on crop-type macro F1 under a **geographic split (0.650 vs 0.563)**, and the gain was larger for geographic than for temporal splits ([Butsko et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00858v1)). The evidence also points the other way. Presto and AlphaEarth embeddings fall to about **28 to 46 percent accuracy** in cross-continent and cross-hemisphere CropGlobe transfer ([Tong & Wang 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.03497)). Patch-based geospatial foundation models do not consistently beat supervised UNet/ViT baselines on PANGAEA crop datasets ([PANGAEA](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204)). Under region-held-out U.S. CDL splits they collapse on minority crops, for example SatMAE at 45.68 mIoU in Iowa versus 3.20 in Minnesota ([Shang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29664v1)). Either outcome is therefore interpretable. If Presto transfers well, pretraining is a lever the paper should acknowledge alongside sparsity. If it does not, the result strengthens the thesis that inductive bias, not representational power, drives transfer. Because the holdout tile is adjacent and label-rich, the PANGAEA and WorldCereal patterns predict small or no gains, but that is a prediction and must not be presented as a result.

The biggest risk is a crippled input, and the repository can avoid it. With the paper's six channels, only B2, B6, B11 and B12 map onto Presto's Sentinel-2 slots. EVI and hue have no slot, and each of Presto's channel groups (RGB, red-edge) would be only partly populated ([Presto GitHub](https://github.com/nasaharvest/presto)). A reviewer could fairly call that baseline handicapped. A local check of the raw AI4FoodSecurity archive shows that `ref_fusion_competition_south_africa_train_source_sentinel_2/.../bands.npy` holds **all 12 Sentinel-2 bands at 76 dates per 2400 × 2400 tile** (uint16, with SCL and cloud-probability layers). B3, B4, B5, B7, B8, B8A and NDVI can therefore be re-extracted at the training-field pixels. The test-tile source still sits in a 4.4 GB `.tar.gz` that needs to be unpacked. Run the full-band input as the main Presto result and the 4-band masked input as a sensitivity check.

Implementation should follow the repository API ([Presto GitHub](https://github.com/nasaharvest/presto)). Build inputs with `construct_single_presto_input`. Mask S1, ERA5 and Dynamic World (set DW to 9). Pass real latitude and longitude. Set `month` to the true first month and **mask** the May and June timesteps rather than deleting them, so that month positions stay aligned. Report two variants. The first is frozen pooled embeddings (`eval_task=True`) fed to the paper's standard head (logistic regression or XGBoost), which is the original CropHarvest protocol. The second is full fine-tuning through `construct_finetuning_model`, which is the WorldCereal protocol. Use the same FID-wise split (random_state=42), class weighting, 5-seed ensemble, field-level aggregation, and the same F1 macro, kappa and Xent columns as the other pixel models, with paired bootstrap CIs from `experiments/paired_bootstrap/`.

Five pitfalls belong in the methods text. (a) Presto was pretrained on least-cloudy L1C monthly composites, while operational pipelines use L2A, and WorldCereal flags this preprocessing mismatch ([Butsko et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00858v1)). Match Presto's `NORMED_BANDS` scaling exactly. (b) Pretraining covered 2020 to 2021, and the data here are from 2017; the risk is low for a reflectance model but should be stated. (c) A mean-pooled 128-d frozen embedding can blur the phenology that separates wheat, barley and small-grain grazing, so fine-tuning will probably be the stronger variant. (d) Pretraining is what makes Presto work: random-init Presto scored only 45.98 OA on S2-Agri100 ([Presto arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4)). Do not let the weight loading fail silently. (e) The Presto paper is arXiv-only (with a NeurIPS 2023 workshop listing), so cite it as such.

### Why TimesNet is the second, and why PatchTST and TSViT are not

TimesNet ranked first on the THUML Time-Series-Library classification (UEA) leaderboard as of March 2024. That library runs TimesNet, PatchTST and iTransformer with `--task_name classification` on N × T × C tensors, which matches the existing pixel tensors ([TSLib GitHub](https://github.com/thuml/time-series-library)). Only one crop paper has run TimesNet, and it reported no cross-region experiments ([Cheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.18119v2)). A TimesNet result would therefore be novel, but no external number exists to sanity-check it against. The honest framing is "does a general-purpose time-series advance carry over to short crop sequences under spatial shift?". On 8 to 10 monthly points, TimesNet's FFT period discovery finds at most one or two periods, so set `top_k` to 1 or 2 and keep `d_model` small (16 to 64) to avoid gross overparameterization. In practice the run tests a modern, higher-capacity CNN, which is exactly what the capacity thesis needs.

PatchTST is a poor fit because typical patch lengths of 8 to 16 reduce a 10-step series to one or two tokens. Making it work requires patch length 2 to 3 and stride 1 to 2, far from its design regime, and its UEA classification record against TimesNet is inconsistent ([pretrained-TS survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10716)). TSViT tokenizes spatio-temporal patches ([Tarasiou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04944)) and costs about **74 GFLOPs per 48 × 48 sample** ([Gatti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01944)). Fitting it to the pixel protocol would reduce it to a Transformer or L-TAE already in the paper. U-TAE is cheaper but mostly re-tests the patch-versus-pixel axis that the 3D CNN already covers. If the reviewer insists on a patch model, a lightweight U-TAE on the existing patches is the defensible third choice. The response letter can name these reasons explicitly.

MiniRocket with a RidgeClassifierCV is worth adding if time allows, because it costs almost nothing and its result is useful whichever way it falls. It is a dense, unselected random-feature expansion of about 10k features feeding a linear model. MultiRocket+Hydra sits at the top of the 2024 TSC "bake off redux" ([Middlehurst et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)), and MiniRocket is up to 75 times faster than ROCKET at about the same accuracy ([Dempster et al. 2021](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3447548.3467231)). If it transfers worse than the sparse models, the result supports the sparse-selection thesis directly. If it transfers well, it shows that capacity is not what matters. Check the minimum series length in aeon/sktime; with 8 monthly steps the series may need padding to 9 or more.

Cross-region benchmarks give context for what to expect. In BreizhCrops, the higher-capacity InceptionTime and MS-ResNet were the **least stable across regions (0.71±0.07 and 0.72±0.06 OA)**, while all other models clustered near 0.79 to 0.80 ([Rußwurm et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.11893v2)). Sen4AgriNet is an honest counterexample. Under Catalonia→France transfer, an LSTM held at 91.55 percent while the Transformer and TempCNN collapsed to 39.17 and 36.90 percent. That result is confounded by a roughly 60M versus under-1M parameter gap and by class distributions that differ by up to two orders of magnitude ([Sykas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00951)). Cite it rather than leave it for a reviewer to find.

## Worldwide literature converges on phenology shift, not capacity

The AE asked for worldwide coverage, and the 2023 to 2026 literature organizes cleanly into five themes. Each theme lands at a specific sentence in cea-article.tex. Line numbers were re-checked against the working copy on `cea-revision1`; the article is one paragraph per line.

**Phenological misalignment is the mechanism (A:66, A:75, A:281).** European work shows that cross-region failure of deep crop classifiers is largely a phase shift in phenology. TimeMatch estimates that shift and beats competing UDA methods by about **11 F1 points** over five Danish, French and Austrian adaptation tasks ([Nyborg et al. 2022](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271622001216)). T³S re-indexes Sentinel-2 observations by growing degree days and shows how severe unadjusted collapse is: a PSE+L-TAE reaches only **31.6 percent accuracy FR→DK and 36.5 percent DK→FR**, rising to 38.6 and 57.7 percent with thermal time ([Turkoglu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12885v5)). The most direct support for sparse feature selection comes from Germany. Across 18 spatial-transfer scenarios, transferred F1 **fell as the training window lengthened**, from 82 percent (XGBoost, single date) to 61 percent (full season), because region-specific phenology was encoded in the extra features ([Hoppe et al. 2024](https://elib.dlr.de/205064/1/remotesensing-16-01493-with-cover.pdf)). Chinese groups reach the same diagnosis. The Phenology Alignment Network treats cross-phenological-region shift as the domain gap for winter crops ([Wang Z. et al. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092427162200257X)). ClimID-UDA corrects target series with a climate-indicator discrepancy across four Chinese and European regions, gaining **more than 11 percent** in average accuracy ([Wang H. et al. 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.34133/remotesensing.0439)). These sources replace wardlow2007towards at A:66, which never tested transfer.

**Spatial shift outweighs temporal shift, so even adjacent tiles are a meaningful test (A:59, A:75, A:281).** An RF trained on U.S. CDL labels and applied to six European countries varied by **up to 21.6 OA points across training regions but only 0.6 to 2.1 across years**. Merged regions performed worse than the best single region, and temperature similarity predicted transferability ([Xie et al. 2025](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211?fields=abstract)). EuroCropsML reports that transfer between distant regions such as Estonia and Portugal "poses significant challenges to all investigated algorithms" ([Reuss et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04952-7)). Together these let A:75 cite evidence for its "conservative lower bound" claim instead of asserting it: published drops scale with distance, so an adjacent-tile gap is a floor.

**Unsupervised domain adaptation dominates, and it needs target imagery (A:59).** The paper's sentence on "labels, unlabeled imagery, or self-supervised pre-training" needs one representative per strategy. For unlabeled target imagery, the reference work is DACCN, an MMD-based adaptation network tested across U.S. Corn Belt states and Northeast China, with adapted **OA 0.835 to 0.922** ([Wang Y. et al. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271623000850)). A source-free variant from ITC on U.S. Landsat series found that cross-region adaptation gains differ by backbone: **+20.1 macro-F1 for CNN, +12.5 for Transformer and +8.8 for LSTM**. Different architectures therefore lose different amounts when unadapted, which directly supports an architecture-level transfer study ([Mohammadi et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425724004115)). PLCM pairs a pixel-set encoder with an **L-TAE backbone** and class-aware MMD for U.S. 2022 → Xinjiang 2024. Its ablations show that parcel-level aggregation is critical, which parallels the paper's field-aggregation result ([Li et al. 2025](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/rs17223762)). For pre-training, EuroCropsML follow-up work finds that pre-training on distant data can strengthen domain shift and degrade overall accuracy, while helping minority classes ([Reuss et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667393226000037)). Because all of these use the target domain in some way, the paper's statement at A:59, "Our setting is the no-target-access case (domain generalization)", currently has no citation and becomes the natural place to note that this setting is under-studied.

**Label-free generalization in China relies on phenology-guided samples plus shallow classifiers (A:59, A:260).** The dominant Chinese route to new regions and years is not deep DG. It is knowledge-guided sample migration followed by RF or TrAdaBoost. CMSTA reached **OA 0.90 on the North China Plain, against 0.44 for feature-only transfer**, using phenology-informed instance and feature transfer with RF ([Zhang J. et al. 2026](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.srs.2026.100442)). DTW-based sample migration in Heilongjiang reached 94.13 percent OA in a year with no ground samples ([Zhang S. et al. 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01431161.2023.2192881)). A CEA paper (KT-SimSeg) maps winter wheat across seven regions and three years with phenological-similarity labels and an RF ([Yang G. et al. 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1016/j.compag.2024.109536)). The implicit lesson matches the thesis: a small set of phenologically meaningful features transfers well. The paper's contribution is that the sparsity is learned inside the model rather than hand-engineered. The main counterpoint should also be cited. DSH, a deep model with histogram matching across U.S., Chinese and French sites, beat RF, but only through explicit input normalization ([Che et al. 2024](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/15481603.2024.2395700?fields=abstract)).

**Africa: label scarcity, weak global products, few transfer studies (A:57, A:59, A:260).** Eleven public land-cover maps assessed in eight Sub-Saharan countries show that global products transfer poorly to African agriculture ([Kerner et al. 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11087537/)). A Mali↔Uganda study found that MLP, RF and SVM all lost accuracy across regions ([Valinho et al. 2024](https://repository.rsif-paset.org/jspui/bitstream/123456789/475/1/Cross-Regional%20Transferability.pdf)). No peer-reviewed results paper from the AI4FoodSecurity challenge was found, and the South Africa track appears elsewhere only as one dataset among many, for example in Fields of The World ([Kerner et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16252v2)). This supports a careful claim at A:59 that the paper is, to the authors' knowledge, the first Sentinel-2-only, field-level out-of-region model comparison on these data. One caution: a search-engine summary describing "dense temporal networks" losing most on a "South Africa benchmark" traces back to the authors' own GitHub repository. It must not be cited as independent support.

Coverage outside these regions is thin. No peer-reviewed 2023 to 2026 cross-region crop-type DA/DG paper of comparable visibility was found for India, Southeast Asia beyond rice-extent products, Central Asia, the Middle East or Canada. This may reflect search limits. The response letter can say that coverage spans four continents rather than claim that it is exhaustive.

## Conclusion

The revision can turn both reviewer comments into evidence for its own thesis rather than defend against them. The worldwide literature has effectively split cross-region crop mapping into two camps. One adapts to the target with unlabeled imagery or alignment losses. The other hand-builds phenological features for shallow classifiers. Neither asks which model properties transfer when nothing about the target is known, and that is precisely the paper's question, so the "domain generalization" sentence at A:59 should become the paper's positioning statement, now with citations. The Presto experiment is the high-information addition. It is the one baseline whose outcome could qualify the thesis, and the local raw archive makes a full-band, non-handicapped version feasible. Running it, and reporting it honestly either way, is a stronger response to R2-Q3a than adding several architectures that the literature already predicts will cluster within seed noise on an adjacent-tile holdout.
