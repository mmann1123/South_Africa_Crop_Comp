# Consistent imputation rewrites four crop-benchmark claims

The four revised findings are consistent with the 2019–2026 literature and novel in their specific form, but the novelty lies in the audit, not in the headline. No published satellite-image-time-series (SITS) paper has shown that an inference-only imputation inconsistency (a month present in training, absent in 40.6% of holdout pixels, zero-filled before standardization) can masquerade as a cross-region transfer failure and reorder a model benchmark; the closest analogues are Rußwurm and Körner's raw-versus-preprocessed ranking change, Kapoor and Narayanan's imputation-leakage reversal in civil-war prediction (AUC gap 0.14 to 0.01), and the Sentinel-2 Processing Baseline 04.00 offset warnings. The formal name for the failure mode is **missingness shift** (Zhou, Balakrishnan and Lipton, AISTATS 2023), and the manuscript should adopt it. The corrected ranking (dense pixel temporal models 0.63–0.66 macro-F1 ahead of trees, logistic regression, TabNet and MiniRocket at 0.59–0.62, no in-region/out-of-region inversion) matches the only comparable regional-fold benchmark (BreizhCrops) and the WorldCereal crop-type result, so Finding 1 should be framed as the first 27-model, consistent-input, adjacent-tile confirmation rather than a discovery. Findings 2 and 3 are the genuinely new measurements: a sparsemax channel gate inside a SITS encoder has no published precedent, and no paper measures any gate or sparse model under a zero-filled timestep (MCTNet's test-time deletion comes closest, but for one mask-aware attention model with gaps already in training). One caveat the notes did not catch: a controlled re-score of pixel TabNet under zero fill gives **0.509 versus 0.609 under median fill**, a 0.10 drop equal to the dense models', so TabNet's apparent robustness in the published table came from its pipeline's median fill, not from sparsemax; the robustness claim must rest on the gated CNN-BiLSTM (+0.063 [0.040, 0.086]) and L-TAE-S alone. Finding 4's crossover near 15% of fields (~400 fields, ~45 per class) has no published counterpart, but its two halves are each anticipated (Schulthess 2023 for the classical plateau, EuroCropsML and the bake-off redux for the rising deep curve). Roughly fourteen manuscript passages and eight citation roles must change, including two outright mis-citations: Belgiu and Drăguţ 2016 is cited for RF surviving transfer when its §6.1 documents RF non-transferability, and Obadić et al. 2022 is cited for attention dates being contingent on the training region when the paper varies only the training class set. The 22 papers flagged for download have since been read in full and briefed (2026-10-21). They confirm every inference above rather than overturn one: Obadić never varies the region, Pandžić's inversion is verified and runs in the deep model's favour, Rusňák's RF is the least transferable of four classifiers in every holdout, and Yi et al. show that normalization layers do not repair zero fill. They also name the control a referee will demand before the sparsemax gate can be presented as a contribution: training-time month dropout, the standard remedy in Garnot, Mask-PSTIN and Wang/Belgiu. The section "Ways to push this forward" ranks that control and ten other candidate experiments into what the revision needs, what strengthens it, and what is future work.

## Finding 1: dense temporal networks lead by 0.02–0.07, and the literature already leaned that way

Where deep temporal models and tree or linear baselines have been run on the same spatially disjoint region without adaptation, the deep model wins or ties in nearly every case. In BreizhCrops, the one benchmark whose protocol matches ours (train three adjacent NUTS-3 regions of Brittany, test the fourth, same year), **RF reached OA 0.77–0.78 and class-average accuracy 0.53 while TempCNN scored 0.79–0.80 / 0.55, LSTM 0.80 / 0.57 and Transformer 0.80–0.81 / 0.59** ([Rußwurm et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11893)). Pandžić et al. 2026 report a Slovenia-to-Serbia transfer in which RF fell to F1 46.5% while an encoder-only Transformer held 66.8%, and this is an inversion in the deep model's favour: in-region RF led 84.4 to 76.5 ([Pandžić et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.15292/geodetski-vestnik.2026.02.268-288)). The full text (now briefed; DOI verified on Crossref, which records the title as "Spatial Transfer Learning for Cross-Regional Crop Mapping") shows the inversion cannot be an imputation artefact: both models received the same spline-interpolated, edge-padded 15-day sequences, Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests confirm a real covariate shift, and the Transformer's margin over RF under transfer grows with the information content of the input (+7.6 F1 on S1, +17.3 on S2, +20.3 on S1+S2) while RF's loss is nearly constant at 38 to 41 points ([Pandžić briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Transfer%20Learning%20for%20CrossRegional%20Crop%20Mapping.md)). Rusňák et al. 2023 found RF the least transferable of four classifiers (QDA, SVM, MLP, RF) in every holdout, temporal and spatial: spatial OA 81.6 vs 87.0 for the MLP and 87.3 for SVM on identical 50-feature monthly medians, the deficit widening to 8 to 14 points in the shifted drought year, when RF's accuracy fell as late-season months were added while the NN and SVM were unaffected; they called RF "less robust" under distribution shift and asked for multi-algorithm comparisons because "many transferability studies ... have exclusively used random forest algorithms" ([Rusňák et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15133414); [Rusňák briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Crop%20Mapping%20without%20Labels%3A%20Investigating%20Temporal%20and%20Spatial%20Transferability%20of%20Crop%20Classification%20Models%20Using%20a%205-Year%20Sentinel-2%20Series%20and%20Machine%20Learning.md)). Mohammadi, Belgiu and Stein 2024 give the cleanest same-preprocessing, source-only table in the corpus: with one valid-observation filter plus linear interpolation applied to every model and no zero fill, cross-regional macro-F1 runs Transformer 51.9 = LSTM 51.9 > MLP 46.9 > CNN 46.1 > RF 45.3, cross-time LSTM 82.5 > RF 74.3, and there is no condition in which RF leads; their source-free adaptation then adds +9 to +20 macro-F1 on the unlabelled target, a larger lever than any architecture ([Mohammadi et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114385); [Mohammadi briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/A%20source-free%20unsupervised%20domain%20adaptation%20method%20for%20cross-regional%20and%20cross-time%20crop%20mapping%20from%20satellite%20image%20time%20series.md)). WorldCereal's crop-type task shows the same pattern at global scale: the random-to-geographic drop was 0.16–0.18 macro-F1 for all four models, the ranking was preserved, and even a randomly initialised Presto beat a raw-feature CatBoost under transfer (0.620 vs 0.563) ([Butsko et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.00858v1)). Our margin (deep pixel 0.63–0.66 vs 0.59–0.62) sits inside BreizhCrops' class-average band (0.55–0.59 vs 0.53), and our deep-pixel gaps of −0.06 to −0.14 sit inside the adjacent-region band of −0.04 to −0.13 reported for RF by Orynbaikyzy et al. (median F1 loss −0.04 to −0.08, worst tiles −0.09) ([Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs14061493)) and for a CNN by Wijesingha et al. (−0.13) ([Wijesingha et al. 2024](https://kobra.uni-kassel.de/items/1dfa9786-3981-4178-9cac-598b6137126e/full)).

The counter-evidence is thin and specific. Wijesingha et al. 2024 is the only paper with numbers in which a tree beats a deep model under spatial transfer (RF macro-F1 0.66 vs CNN 0.62), and that CNN is a 96,000-parameter 2D convolution over a 10 x 4 band-by-bimonth grid of field-level aggregates on Landsat, not a temporal pixel model. The full text (DOI 10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.05.020, verified) shows the inversion is mild and explained by the authors themselves: the CNN leads in-region by 0.018 mF1 in one region, trails by 0.025 to 0.049 under spatial transfer in a single run, and the authors attribute the deficit to "only four time steps" being too few for a convolutional model; their headline conclusion is that the two algorithms do not differ substantially, and the absolute spatial-transfer losses (RF −0.044 to −0.120, CNN −0.097 to −0.144) bracket ours ([Wijesingha briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Evaluating%20the%20spatial%E2%80%93temporal%20transferability%20of%20models%20for%20agricultural.md)). Shibuya et al. 2025 rank RF (OA 0.917) above TempCNN (0.886) and L-TAE (0.859) on MODIS 250 m data, but across years within Mato Grosso, with five coarse land-use classes, map-derived labels and untuned `sits` defaults; the same-year ranking is preserved under the shift, so the paper corroborates rank stability rather than a tree advantage ([Shibuya et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/01431161.2025.2527988); [Shibuya briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Cross-temporal%20domain%20generalization%20approach%20combined%20with%20self-organizing-maps%20for%20classifying%20MODIS%20time%20series%20data.md)). Sen4AgriNet's Catalonia-to-France collapse of Transformer and TempCNN (39.2 and 36.9% weighted accuracy against LSTM's 91.6%) is deep-versus-deep and confounded by a roughly 60M-versus-sub-1M parameter gap ([Sykas et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00951)). The large cross-country failures (TimeMatch's PSE+L-TAE at 39.2 source-only vs 82.0 target-trained macro-F1) are attributed to phenological shift and calendar-time positional encodings, not to model family ([Nyborg et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02682); [Nyborg et al. TPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09175)). The "deep nets overfit the region, trees transfer" narrative that the current manuscript asserts is therefore not supported by any 2019–2026 pixel-time-series crop paper with numbers; the published pitfalls are RF-only designs, random hold-outs, calendar-time encodings, and merged-region training ([Xie et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2025.2563211); [Hoppe et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs16091493)).

What is novel is the breadth and the stability statistic, not the direction. No paper compares L-TAE, Transformer, TempCNN, LSTM, TimesNet, TabNet, MiniRocket, XGBoost, LightGBM and logistic regression under identical input handling on a same-year adjacent-tile holdout; BreizhCrops has eight models with RF as the only non-deep baseline and reports no in-region reference, Pandžić has two models, Mohammadi has five backbones but no in-region score, Rusňák has four classifiers but never tabulates the in-region result, and no paper reports a rank-correlation between in-region and out-of-region orderings. The published inversions are two- or three-model anecdotes whose direction flips with the deep architecture chosen. The defensible framing is: "To our knowledge this is the first many-model comparison of deep temporal, tree, linear and sparse-attention classifiers under a consistent input pipeline on a same-year adjacent Sentinel-2 tile; it finds that in-region and out-of-region rankings agree (report Spearman rho), that dense temporal pixel models transfer at least as well as tree ensembles, and that this confirms BreizhCrops and WorldCereal under a stricter no-pretraining, label-free protocol while closing the RF-only gap Rusňák et al. identify." One unverified risk: Chen and Liu 2021 (GF-6, Lindian to Hailun) may contain a seven-model in-region versus transferred table, which would be the closest precedent; the site refused connection and only a search snippet ("7.2–41% decline; Transformer and RF dropped significantly") was recovered ([Chen & Liu 2021](https://www.aeeisp.com/nygcxb/en/article/Y2021/I15/161)).

## Finding 2: sparse gates buy robustness, not transfer, and TabNet's robustness is unproven

The published record on learned feature selection under transfer is a null result, which makes our clean-input null unremarkable rather than anomalous. Orynbaikyzy et al. ran forward feature selection inside spatial cross-validation across seven German sites, kept 8% of the optical-SAR features, and concluded that spatial feature selection "had no substantial effect on the spatial transferability of Random Forest models", with median F1 differences below 3% and never above 0.05 ([Orynbaikyzy et al. 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-01493/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-01493.pdf)). Christofi et al. added a learned band-by-date weighting to a Sentinel-2 grassland classifier and measured **0.954 ± 0.004 with the gate against 0.953 ± 0.003 without**, concluding the mechanism "does not significantly boost accuracy" and is valuable for identifying informative dates ([Christofi et al. 2025](https://ktisis.cut.ac.cy/bitstream/20.500.14279/36330/1/1381610.pdf)). Mahmood, Usman and Conrad 2025 (PFG 93(2):173–196, now briefed) reach the same null from the tree side: a ten-split, importance-ranked backward elimination for RF never produced a reduced set that exceeded the all-feature accuracy; removing 80% of features cost 2 OA, 4 kappa and 3 macro-F points while per-pixel spatial instability rose from 1.0 to 1.28 classes, and the authors state that spatial and temporal transfer "remains an open task" ([Mahmood briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Selecting%20Relevant%20Features%20for%20Random%20Forest-Based%20Crop%20Type.md)). The few remote-sensing TabNet papers evaluate in-region only and claim accuracy or interpretability: Tian et al. gain +0.044 macro-F1 from a TabNet branch on single-date Sentinel-2 in Henan, with the rationale that TabNet "simulate[s] the RF operation process" and improves generalization by analogy, never tested ([Tian et al. 2023](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-15-01990/article_deploy/remotesensing-15-01990.pdf)); Shah et al. use entmax attention in TabNet for hyperspectral pixels and claim interpretability and efficiency ([Shah et al. 2022](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-14-00716/article_deploy/remotesensing-14-00716.pdf)). Three OpenAlex sweeps and the web search found no paper putting sparsemax or entmax attention inside a SITS temporal encoder, so L-TAE-S and the gated TempCNN and CNN-BiLSTM appear to be the first. TabNet's own paper tests robustness to irrelevant features in training, not to features missing at test time, and never evaluates under missingness ([Arik & Pfister 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07442)); the sparsemax paper claims parity with softmax and a more compact focus, not higher accuracy ([Martins & Astudillo 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.02068)). Our own results fit this record exactly: on training-median-filled inputs the gate gives TempCNN 0.654 (sparse) vs 0.663 (softmax) vs 0.655 (dense), and CNN-BiLSTM 0.638 vs 0.621 vs 0.656, so any gate slightly hurts the stronger backbone, and L-TAE-S trails L-TAE 0.643 to 0.652 ([sparse_gate_backbones README](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/sparse_gate_backbones/README.md); [table1_filled.csv](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/table1_filled.csv)).

The robustness half is where the literature has a mechanism but no measurement. Rußwurm and Körner showed that on raw, cloud-contaminated Sentinel-2 series LSTM and Transformer gradients vanish on cloudy dates while "the comparatively shallow TempCNN" keeps non-zero gradients there, and TempCNN's 12-class kappa fell from 0.88 to 0.71 against the Transformer's 0.87 to 0.82; this is dense softmax attention and recurrence, retrained on the contaminated distribution, and the robustness is qualitative ([Rußwurm & Körner 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)). Grinsztajn et al. showed MLPs are "less robust to uninformative features" than trees and FT-Transformer, with GBT accuracy "barely affected by removing up to half the features", a training-time feature-noise result ([Grinsztajn et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.08815)). The only published arguments that sparse attention specifically resists corruption are theoretical and outside remote sensing: PLADIS argues noise affects sparse (entmax, alpha > 1) retrieval error linearly but dense softmax error exponentially ([PLADIS 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07677)), and Vasylenko et al. show softmax lets "non-informative tokens accumulate attention mass" whereas alpha-entmax "can assign exact zeros to irrelevant tokens" ([Vasylenko et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.16640)). No tabular or remote-sensing benchmark measures TabNet or a sparsemax gate under injected missing features or a zero-filled timestep; the nearest is TabTransformer's noise test, which excludes TabNet and credits contextual embeddings ([Huang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.06678)). The sparse gate on CNN-BiLSTM, which lifts zero-fill holdout macro-F1 from 0.513 (dense) to 0.576 (**+0.063, 95% CI +0.040 to +0.086**) and beats the identical softmax gate by +0.034, is therefore the first such measurement in SITS, and the softmax control is what separates sparsity from added capacity ([sparse_gate_backbones README](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/sparse_gate_backbones/README.md)). L-TAE-S corroborates it: under zero fill it lost 0.044 (0.643 to 0.599) where L-TAE lost 0.113 (0.652 to 0.540) ([table1_filled.csv](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/table1_filled.csv)).

TabNet cannot be listed alongside them. The fill-check README records that pixel TabNet's published pipeline filled the missing September with the holdout's own column medians while the raw-input deep models zero-filled, so TabNet was never exposed to zeros in the published table ([fill-check README](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/README.md)). The scripted zero-versus-median comparison in `tabnet_fill.py` stopped before scoring because the current checkpoints do not reproduce the published predictions (221 of 2,415 fields differ) ([run.log](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/run.log)). Scoring the two saved prediction files against the holdout labels with the same checkpoints gives **median fill 0.609, zero fill 0.509 macro-F1**, with 535 fields changing prediction ([predictions_tabnet_zerofill.csv](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/predictions_tabnet_zerofill.csv)). A 0.10 drop is inside the dense models' 0.08–0.14 range. TabNet's standing on the September-missing fields in the published table (0.575 while dense nets fell to 0.21–0.36) is an artifact of its median fill, not evidence that sparsemax masks ignore a corrupted input ([sept_observed_subset.csv](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/sept_observed_subset.csv)). This also removes the manuscript's explanation for why field-averaging hurts TabNet (0.62 pixel vs 0.49 field under consistent fill); that effect persists but needs a new account. Two of the newly read papers explain the TabNet result rather than merely accommodating it. Rußwurm and Körner's RF row (kappa −0.23 to −0.24 on raw, cloud-contaminated inputs where LSTM and Transformer lost 0.05 to 0.06) shows that sparsity in feature selection over a flattened vector (trees, TabNet) is a different thing from the ability to gate a corrupted observation in sequence (LSTM cells, attention over time, a channel gate inside the encoder); a selector trained with every feature present has no mechanism to recognise that a value is "not a measurement" ([Rußwurm & Körner briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Self-attention%20for%20raw%20optical%20Satellite%20Time%20Series%20Classification.md)). Zhou et al.'s Example 1 makes the same point formally: a sparse selector that learned under zero source missingness to rely on a feature that is zeroed in the target has no way to re-route, and sparsity concentrates the dependence rather than diluting it ([Zhou briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Domain%20Adaptation%20under%20Missingness%20Shift.md)). The old manuscript conflated the two kinds of sparsity; the revision should separate them explicitly. Obadić et al. and Rußwurm and Körner both report that softmax attention over time is already nearly one-hot per crop on clean inputs without any sparsity prior, which is the simplest reason that imposing sparsemax adds nothing on complete inputs ([Obadić briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Exploring%20Self-Attention%20for%20Crop-type%20Classification.md)).

The low-data half of Finding 2 is also uncontested because nobody has measured it. The claim that band or feature selection helps most when labels are scarce is widespread in hyperspectral work but rests on curse-of-dimensionality reasoning and single-size ablations (Shah et al. at 10% of Indian Pines, Tian et al. at one size, Pankajakshan et al. at 1–10% labels compared to deep nets at full data, Zimmer et al. with no fraction sweep) ([Zimmer et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.11420)). Our sweep (TempCNN gate +0.017 at 10% in 8 of 10 draws, no L-TAE effect, a small CNN-BiLSTM loss) is the first with a matched dense control in SITS, and its null is informative because the folk expectation runs the other way. Two cautions for the text: Meister et al. find only "a weak relationship" between sparse attention weights and model behaviour across three text tasks ([Meister et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.01087)) and InterpreTabNet reports TabNet's "inferred attention masks are often dense" ([Si et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00426)), so keep the gate's claim behavioural (measured degradation under a missing month) and make no interpretability claim. The defensible sentence: "To our knowledge this is the first evaluation of a sparsemax channel gate inside SITS temporal encoders against a softmax control; the gate yields no accuracy or transfer gain on complete inputs and no consistent low-data gain, but degrades gracefully when a month absent from training is zero-filled at inference."

## Finding 3: a zero-filled month is a missingness shift no SITS paper has quantified

The literature shows repeatedly that dense SITS models degrade when optical observations are removed at inference, and the magnitudes bracket ours. Ofori-Ampofo et al.'s Sentinel-2-only PSE-TAE fell from F-score 0.587 with all 27 dates to 0.443 with 20% of them (−0.144), while a Sentinel-1-only model held 0.522 ([Ofori-Ampofo et al. 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/22/4668)). Garnot et al.'s U-TAE on PASTIS-R "drops drastically" as optical acquisitions are removed from the test set, falling below the radar-only model at 70% occlusion, and panoptic quality at 30% optical was 33.0 versus 37.6 for late fusion ([Garnot et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07558)). The one paper that isolates the train-complete, test-incomplete protocol is Wang, Belgiu et al. 2025: a U-TAE trained on complete Sen4AgriNet series and tested with 75% of timesteps dropped scored **mean-F1 48.5 against 70.7** with their reconstruction scheme, and the paper argues augmentation alone "can only handle limited missing patterns" ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19159)). The paper is now published as Wang, Belgiu, Wu, Zhong, Cao, Li and Tao 2026, "Joint Learning for Feature Reconstruction and Prediction in Agricultural Semantic Segmentation From Incomplete Satellite Image Time Series", IEEE TGRS 64, DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2025.3649017 (revised title, one author added), and the journal version should be the one cited ([Wang briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/A%20Joint%20Learning%20Framework%20with%20Feature%20Reconstruction%20and%20Prediction%20for%20Incomplete%20Satellite%20Image%20Time%20Series%20in%20Agricultural%20Semantic%20Segmentation.md)). The full text sharpens the comparison with our case in two ways. Their Baseline arm *drops* the missing step, so the network sees a shorter but in-distribution sequence, and 25% missing (three of twelve months) costs the U-TAE only 2.7 mean-F1 points, 50% costs 12.7; we removed one month of roughly ten for 41% of pixels and lost 8 to 14 macro-F1 points, which says the penalty is dominated by the *value* inserted, not by the gap. And every interpolator they test (last, closest, linear) and both deep reconstructors under-perform the shortened-sequence Baseline in five of six dataset-by-regime comparisons, by up to 12 cropland-F1 points, so our training-median fill must be presented as a single-known-month fix, not a missing-data strategy. Wang et al. 2024 (MCTNet, in this journal) give the nearest pixel-level SITS analogue: on 1,361 Arkansas corn pixels fully observed in the growing season they delete observations at test time without retraining, and contiguous gaps cost more than scattered ones, which is the shape of our single missing September. Their test is milder than ours, because the training series already contained zero-filled gaps and the model receives a mask, and it compares only MCTNet with its own no-mask ablation, so it does not bear on benchmark ordering ([Wang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compag.2024.109370), DOI verified; [MCTNet briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/A%20lightweight%20CNN-Transformer%20network%20for%20pixel-based%20crop%20mapping.md)). Against this, matched-regime studies find almost nothing: Che et al. report under 0.2% (Bi-LSTM) and under 0.5% (Transformer) OA difference between gappy and interpolated HLS composites, "both smaller than model training randomness", and their Section 3.2 excludes CNNs precisely because "CNN usually assigns missing values as zero ... known as zero-padding", a published acknowledgement that zero-filling is a different and worse thing than masking ([Che et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2024.04.021), DOI verified; [Che briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Linearly%20interpolating%20missing%20values%20in%20time%20series%20helps%20little%20for%20land%20cover%20classification%20using%20recurrent%20or%20attention%20networks.md)); Turkoglu et al. note RNNs "normally exhibit good robustness against missing data due to clouds" and that a cloud-mask channel left performance "practically the same" ([Turkoglu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.08820)); Zhao et al.'s masked versus interpolated inputs differ by at most 1.6 OA points across five architectures ([Zhao et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13142790)). The reconciliation is the regime: robustness holds when the gap pattern is in the training support and collapses when it is not. Our 0.08–0.14 drop for one missing month in 41% of pixels is large relative to the amount of missing data, and the standardization pathway explains why: a zero reflectance passed through a training-fitted StandardScaler becomes a large negative z-score, an out-of-distribution value rather than an absent observation. No paper makes this argument explicitly; the only published statement about zero-filling in SITS is Zhao et al.'s unmeasured design justification that "if we set null values as zeros, the training and testing results will be highly biased", used to motivate a GRU-D-style mask layer ([Zhao et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13142790)). Generic machine-learning evidence supports the mechanism: Yi et al. show zero imputation induces a "variable sparsity problem" in which network outputs shift with the input missing rate ([Yi et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.00150)). The full paper (now briefed) adds three facts the notes lacked. Theorems 1 to 3 make the expected pre-activation of every layer a function of the observed fraction, and a footnote states the drift "is observed throughout the entire learning process (even before the training)", so it is a forward-pass property, not a learned one; their Figure 1 PhysioNet panel, an LSTM over standardized-then-zero-filled time steps whose predicted probability drifts with the number of zeroed entries, is a near-exact analogue of our pipeline. Table 3 shows that batch normalization and layer normalization do *not* repair zero fill (BN is worse than plain zero fill on four of five NHIS tasks), which answers the objection that our networks' normalization layers should have absorbed the shift. And mean and median fills were indistinguishable from zero fill there only because the NHIS inputs were min-max scaled, so zero was already in range; in a standardized pipeline the raw-scale zero is far outside the band distribution, which is why our median fill restores accuracy where theirs did not ([Yi briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Why%20Not%20to%20Use%20Zero%20Imputation%3F%20Correcting%20Sparsity%20Bias%20in%20Training%20Neural%20Networks.md)). SITS-BERT supplies the benign counterpart: its SVM and RF inputs are zero-padded to the maximum series length in both training and test, and RF is still the best classical model, so the fill is harmless when it is in the training distribution and catastrophic when it appears only at test time. MCTNet is a second benign case with a deep model: zeros mark missing ten-day composites in both training and test, fewer than 20% of test pixels have under 10% missing steps, and OA still moves only 0.973 to 0.968 across missing-rate strata (0.963 to 0.950 for the variant without mask-aware positional encoding) ([MCTNet briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/A%20lightweight%20CNN-Transformer%20network%20for%20pixel-based%20crop%20mapping.md)) ([SITS-BERT briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Self-Supervised%20Pretraining%20of%20Transformers%20for%20Satellite%20Image%20Time%20Series%20Classification.md)).

The restoration by training-median fill is likewise supported but unstated in SITS. Salvador et al. tested 36 datasets under train-test missing-rate shift and found column median the strongest classical imputer (recovery ratio 0.70 against 0.82 for a task-aware joint imputer) ([Salvador et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20342)); Le Morvan and Varoquaux show that for expressive predictors "improving imputation only has a minor effect on prediction performance" and that XGBoost depends least on imputation quality ([Le Morvan & Varoquaux 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19804)); Perez-Lebel et al. find native missing-value handling in gradient-boosted trees (missing-incorporated-attribute splits) "predicts better than state-of-the-art imputation" ([Perez-Lebel et al. 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9012100/)). The last point explains why the xr_fresh classical models moved only 0.02–0.05 under the consistent fill: their NaN-aware aggregate features perturb a statistic rather than insert an out-of-distribution token. Every published remedy (temporal dropout with p = 0.4 adding +0.6 to +1.6 mIoU on PASTIS-R ([Garnot et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07558)); temporal random masking lifting Mask-PSTIN from 83.9 to 87.4% OA ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271624003897)); Presto's structured masking so it performs "well even with incomplete inputs" ([Tseng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14065v4)); GRU-D mask layers; learnable missing tokens) works by putting gaps inside the training distribution, which is orthogonal to our failure mode.

No paper reports that correcting a train/test imputation inconsistency reversed a published model ranking in remote sensing. The precedents are all outside SITS or about a different preprocessing step. Rußwurm and Körner's ranking depends on preprocessing (TempCNN co-best on cleaned data, worst deep model on raw), but with retraining ([Rußwurm & Körner 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.10536)). Kapoor and Narayanan found that in civil-war prediction "errors appeared in 4 of the 12, exactly the 4 that claimed complex ML models beat baseline logistic regression", that Muchlinski et al. "impute the training and test data together", and that after correction the AUC gap "drops from 0.14 to 0.01" ([Kapoor & Narayanan 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856)). The full paper (Patterns 4(9):100804, DOI verified) places this under taxonomy item L1.2, "preprocessing on training and test set", and reports that three of the four flawed civil-war papers trace to one pooled imputation; it also notes that the artifact "helped the flexible ones most" because "complex ML models tend to over-fit to spurious correlations more easily", which is the mirror of our case, where the asymmetric fill deflated the flexible models. Our pixel TabNet path is itself a mild L1.2 instance, since its fill statistic was computed on the holdout; the re-run with training-tile medians moves it only 0.603 to 0.616, inside the bootstrap interval, but it is exactly the test-set preprocessing the taxonomy says to report ([Kapoor briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Leakage%20and%20the%20reproducibility%20crisis%20in%20machine-learning-based%20science.md)). Shwartz-Ziv and Armon showed deep tabular models losing 7.6–14.2% relative performance on unseen datasets against 3.3% for XGBoost ([Shwartz-Ziv & Armon 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03253)); their Table 3 gives TabNet 10.51% behind the best model on datasets outside its own paper against 3.34% for XGBoost, and each deep model "was better only on the datasets that appeared in its own paper", the tabular version of an advantage that was a property of the pipeline (tuning effort there, the fill operator here) rather than of the model ([Shwartz-Ziv briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/TABULAR%20DATA%3A%20DEEP%20LEARNING%20IS%20NOT%20ALL%20YOU%20NEED.md)); the bake-off redux excluded LSTM-FCN after finding it "heavily overfit the test data" ([Middlehurst et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)). Earth observation recognises the general hazard through the Sentinel-2 PB 04.00 radiometric offset: a model trained on the old baseline and applied to unadjusted imagery "will yield incorrect results" ([Planetary Computer notebook](https://nbviewer.org/github/microsoft/PlanetaryComputerExamples/blob/main/datasets/sentinel-2-l2a/baseline-change.ipynb)), and Sialelli et al. state that "each correction applied during training must be replicated identically at inference time" ([Sialelli et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24532)). Our case is the temporal analogue: a constant injected at inference that the training distribution never contained. The novelty statement should be narrow: "first to show that an inference-time imputation inconsistency confined to the target region can masquerade as a transfer failure and reorder a SITS crop-classification benchmark", never "first to show dense models are sensitive to missing timesteps". Report the before/after table in the style of the re-analysis papers (name the asymmetric step, re-run under one protocol, show both rankings), and consider a supplement table modelled on Kapoor and Narayanan's model-info sheet listing, per model, the fill operator and whether the inference missingness pattern was in the training support.

**Addendum (2026-10-28): Pham et al. 2024 narrows this finding.** The missingness-confound literature review (`reports/Missingness confound audit of transfer papers.md`) surfaced a paper this check missed. Pham et al. train pixel 1D-CNN and Transformer crop classifiers on Brandenburg's densest clear-sky year (2018) and map 2010 to 2021. Without augmentation the model "only performed well in the training year and poorly in unseen years", with the loss "closely related to the number of annual CSOs". Random Observation Selection (training-time observation dropout) recovers 20 to 30 OA points and beats phenology shifting by up to 30 ([Pham et al. 2024, IJAEOG 129:103867](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2024.103867); [briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Temporally%20transferable%20crop%20mapping%20with%20temporal%20encoding%20and%20deep%20learning%20augmentations.md)). A companion paper shows the same for land-cover fractions across years, with no fill at all (annual percentiles over whatever observations exist), and finds that the model trained on the densest year transfers worst and that observation subsampling also improves same-year transfer to sparser external tiles by up to 16% MAE ([Pham et al. 2024, RSE 308:114206](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114206); [briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Learning%20the%20variations%20in%20annual%20spectral-temporal%20metrics%20to%20enhance%20the%20transferability%20of%20regression%20models%20for%20land%20cover%20fraction%20monitoring.md)). So a published SITS paper has already shown that a missingness difference can pass for a transfer failure, and that observation dropout is the fix, across years. What remains new here is narrower: a cross-region, same-season, same-sensor case; a single fill inconsistency (an out-of-range value in a month never missing in training, where Pham's zero is a consistent no-observation token filling over 75% of daily cells even in training); the separation of fill value from information loss (step deletion, fill sweep); and the reordering of a 27-model, multi-family benchmark. Pham reports OA only, from single runs. The opening claim of this report ("no published SITS paper has shown...") should be read with this qualification.

## Finding 4: the deep-over-classical crossover near 400 fields is unpublished

No paper gives a numeric deep-versus-classical crossover for crop classification, and none places both model families on one learning curve under out-of-region evaluation. Portalés-Julià et al. state the qualitative pattern directly for Valencia Sentinel-2: deep models "outperformed the machine learning algorithms when large datasets were used", and with reduced data "the random forest is able to perform at the same level, and even better" ([Portalés-Julià et al. 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4395/11/4/654)). The full text (now briefed) shows this claim rests on a pooled pixel split: a single random 70/30 split of 141,475 pixels within one scene, with pixels from the same parcel on both sides, so their "small" regime (10%, about 14k pixels) is larger than our whole training set in pixel terms and the BiLSTM's rise with data is partly reward for memorising parcels it will be tested on. It should be cited as a widely repeated claim that we tested under a transfer protocol and did not reproduce (XGBoost is our worst model at 10%, 0.51), not as corroboration ([Portalés-Julià briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Assessing%20the%20Sentinel-2%20Capabilities%20to%20Identify%20Abandoned%20Crops%20Using%20Deep%20Learning.md)). Kerner et al.'s Togo study is the clearest head-to-head at ~1,300 labels: **RF F1 0.76 against a locally trained LSTM at 0.72**, with the LSTM pulling ahead only when augmented with global labels ([Kerner et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16866)). Few-shot work shows from-scratch deep models collapsing below non-parametric baselines at 5–20 shots per class: DTW nearest-neighbour passes the deep baseline at 10 and 20 shots in Ghana (64.2 vs 61.4; 70.9 vs 64.7 macro-F1) ([Mohammadi et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/6/1026)), and a compressor method beats TempCNN at 5-shot on PASTIS (33.9 vs 29.2 mIoU) while TempCNN climbs to 75.6 at 50 shots and 96.5 at full data ([Cheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18119)). The two halves of our curve each have a precedent. On the classical side, Schulthess et al.'s RF in the Yaqui Valley (6,084 digitised fields, nine classes, wheat-dominated) gives three thresholds, all now verified from the full text: fewer than 30 fields per crop yields "not only low but also unstable classification accuracies" across six seeds; the fitted per-crop F-score curves reach their breakpoint (gain under 2.5% per ten added fields) at 40 fields on average under equal sampling (SD 15.7) and 46 under proportional sampling (SD 9.1); and OA levels off near 0.9 at about 500 fields in total. That plateau is in-region, on a same-tile validation set, so it cannot be used to say 500 fields "saturate" a tree model for transfer; proportional sampling also beat class-balanced sampling, which matches our null resampling ablation ([Schulthess et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs15030608); [Schulthess briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Optimal%20Sample%20Size%20and%20Composition%20for%20Crop%20Classification%20with%20Sen2-Agri%E2%80%99s%20Random%20Forest%20Classifier.md)); Kaijage et al.'s RF lost 11–14 OA points when randomly cut to 28–40% of labels ([Kaijage et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11014375/)). On the deep side, EuroCropsML's from-scratch Transformer under Latvia-to-Estonia transfer rises 0.438, 0.490, 0.518, 0.573, 0.632 at 10, 20, 100, 200, 500 shots per class with no plateau, but the benchmark has no tree or linear baseline ([Reuss et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11022); the journal version is Reuss et al. 2026, ISPRS Open J. 19:100117, DOI 10.1016/j.ophoto.2026.100117, verified, and its Transformer is always fine-tuned on target parcels, so its "geographic transfer is hard" finding is about pre-training value, not zero-shot transfer ([EuroCropsML briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Benchmarking%20for%20practice%3A%20Few-shot%20time-series%20crop-type%20classification%20on%20the%20EuroCropsML%20dataset.md))), and the bake-off redux finds H-InceptionTime "improves relative to other algorithms as the number of training cases increases" ([Middlehurst et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.13029)). Two newly read papers add single low-data points with a tree baseline, both in-scene and both pointing to pretraining rather than architecture as the lever at the bottom of the curve. SITS-BERT's label-fraction tables put the from-scratch CNN-1D and LSTMs at or below RF at 50 to 100 samples per class, with the from-scratch Transformer only slightly above, and the deep models beat RF at every budget only after self-supervised pretraining on 6.5 million unlabelled series; its test pixels share scenes and fields with training, which rewards RF's memorisation of local spectra ([SITS-BERT briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Self-Supervised%20Pretraining%20of%20Transformers%20for%20Satellite%20Image%20Time%20Series%20Classification.md)). Pandžić's 15% Serbian subset (about 360 parcels) has from-scratch RF 84.4 above from-scratch Transformer 76.5, and the Slovenia-pretrained, fine-tuned Transformer at 91.1 above fine-tuned RF at 87.3 ([Pandžić briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Transfer%20Learning%20for%20CrossRegional%20Crop%20Mapping.md)).

Our numbers map onto these thresholds closely. The 10% point (~270 fields over nine classes, ~30 per class) is exactly Schulthess's instability threshold, where all models converge at ~0.55 and XGBoost dips to 0.51; the classical plateau beginning around 15% (~400 fields, ~45 per class) matches his breakpoint; and the deep models' continued rise to ~0.65 at full data matches the EuroCropsML and bake-off shapes ([sparse_gate_backbones README](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/experiments/sparse_gate_backbones/README.md)). Logistic regression on flattened monthly composites is essentially absent as a baseline in this literature, so the LR plateau at 0.61 fills a gap; it is consistent with SustainBench's finding that a linear LDA beat RF cross-region in Kenya ([SustainBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.04724)). The configuration that is novel is the conjunction: multiple deep architectures, tree and linear baselines, a repeated-draw fraction sweep, and an out-of-region test tile. The second, smaller novelty is that the published "flat" L-TAE curve was itself an imputation artifact; no paper reports learning-curve shape changing with imputation, since the literature treats gap filling and label efficiency as separate questions. Frame both with "to our knowledge". One cross-reference to protect: the manuscript's data-efficiency subsection previously leaned on Pankajakshan et al.'s 1–10% labels and Maxwell et al.'s "<5% RF loss from a 70% training reduction"; neither supports a flat deep-model curve, and Maxwell's review excludes deep learning by design ([local literature audit](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/research_notes/Missing%20data%20robustness%20novelty%20check/local_literature_audit.md)).

## Adopt missingness shift and the re-analysis framing

The vocabulary to use is already standard in machine learning and absent from Earth observation. Zhou, Balakrishnan and Lipton define domain adaptation under **missingness shift** as the case "where source and target data differ only in their missing-data mechanisms", prove that when missingness indicators are observed the problem "reduces to covariate shift" (Proposition 1), and show that without indicators adaptation is needed ([Zhou et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/zhou23b.html); PMLR 206, no DOI, not Crossref-registered, cite via PMLR). The full paper (now briefed) fits our case almost literally: source missingness zero for every month, target missingness 0.41 for one month, and no indicator, because the zero-filled September entered the network looking like a legitimate standardized value. Its Table 1 predicts our old ordering (linear source predictor worse than the label mean at 1.259 vs oracle 0.178; XGBoost equal to its oracle in the redundant case; MLP in between), and its two remedies are the two fixes we have: Algorithm 1 re-masks the *source* to match the target, which is training-time temporal dropout at a matched rate, and our median fill moves the *target* toward the source. Its MissForest arm, which treats every zero as missing and collapsed to chance on eICU, is a warning against the one thing we did not do. The caveats are that its UCAR assumption (independent per-feature Bernoulli missingness) is violated by a month-wise, band-coherent gap, that it is regression-first and tabular, and that its only real-data test failed ([Zhou briefer](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/writeup/literature/Domain%20Adaptation%20under%20Missingness%20Shift.md)). MIRRAMS motivates the same shift by "data collection policies [that] can vary across time or locations", the tabular analogue of a region-specific cloud gap ([Lee et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08280)); Rockenschaub et al. distinguish ignorable shifts (Bayes predictor unchanged) from non-ignorable ones and note "different methods appear robust to different types of shift" ([Rockenschaub et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16484)). Three further terms belong in the text: **train/test missingness mismatch** (Wang, Belgiu et al.'s "train on complete, test on incomplete" protocol), **missingness indicator** or mask channel (Lipton et al. found LSTMs did better with zero-filling plus indicators than with imputed values ([Lipton et al. 2016](http://proceedings.mlr.press/v56/Lipton16.pdf))), and **temporal dropout** (Garnot et al.'s training-time removal of observations with probability p_m, "At inference time, the network can use all available observations"). Zero-filling should be described as "nodata encoded as an in-range value", the same class of error as the PB 04.00 offset. Leakage vocabulary applies too: Kapoor and Narayanan's taxonomy item L1.2 holds that imputation fit on or applied inconsistently across train and test is leakage, and their model-info sheet is the natural template for a per-model fill-operator table ([Kapoor & Narayanan 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856)).

The framing norm for a reversed claim is set by the re-analysis papers, not by remote-sensing corrigenda. RS corrections almost always "re-executed the experiment" and report conclusions unchanged (an MDPI FY-4A correction; the AR-DAVID errata; a Frontiers corrigendum stating the fix "does not change the scientific conclusions") ([MDPI correction](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/15/18/4368)), and no RS corrigendum was found in which a deep-versus-classical ranking reversed because of imputation. That makes the reversal a result, not an erratum. A draft positioning paragraph the notes assembled reads: "Benchmark re-analyses in time-series classification, tabular learning and ML-based science have repeatedly shown that published deep-versus-classical rankings can be artifacts of asymmetric protocols: test-set model selection, imputation leakage, or baselines tuned under unequal budgets. Earth-observation guidance recognizes the analogous hazard for radiometric preprocessing (the Sentinel-2 Processing Baseline 04.00 offset) but, to our knowledge, no SITS study has documented a nodata-fill asymmetry confined to the inference region reversing a model ranking" ([benchmark_pitfalls.md](/home/mmann1123/Documents/github/South_Africa_Crop_Comp/research_notes/Missing%20data%20robustness%20novelty%20check/benchmark_pitfalls.md)). The protocol the paper should state, assembled from Kapoor L1.2, Sialelli "replicate identically", Zhou et al. and the bake-off principle, has five parts: one fill operator fitted on training tiles and applied unchanged to every model; a missingness-indicator channel; a training-time augmentation that drops months so the inference gap is in the training support; a per-model fill-operator sheet; and regeneration of every baseline under the shared pipeline rather than quoting published numbers ([Middlehurst et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-024-01022-1)).

## Ways to push this forward

The 22 briefers converge on one judgement: each of the paper's remaining novelty claims is one control or one table away from being defensible, and the same experiments would fill gaps the literature leaves open. Eleven candidates were raised across the briefers; each is evaluated below by the question it answers, the experiment and the repo location it builds on, its cost (inference-only against existing checkpoints, or retraining, where a 5-seed TempCNN or CNN-BiLSTM run is about 5 GPU-hours and L-TAE similar), the precedent it responds to, and what result would be new. The ranking that follows sorts them into what the revision needs, what strengthens it, and what belongs in future work.

**1. Training-time month dropout as the control for the sparsemax gate (candidate a).** Question: does the gate's +0.063 under zero fill survive against the standard, cheaper remedy, and does putting the missing month inside the training support by itself remove the artifact? Experiment: add a month-dropout flag to `experiments/sparse_gate_backbones/train.py` (drop each month with probability 0.2 to 0.4 on the fixed monthly grid and write zero into the dropped slot so the inference pattern is in the training support; a second arm drops only September at the holdout's rate of 0.41, which is Zhou et al.'s Algorithm 1 with a known relative rate), retrain the dense CNN-BiLSTM and TempCNN there and the L-TAE via `experiments/field_reduction/train_ltae_pixel.py` with five seeds, and score on the zero-filled, training-median-filled and published holdouts produced by `experiments/holdout_fill_check/make_filled_holdout.py` with `holdout_fill_check/score.py`. A 2 x 2 (dropout yes/no by sparsemax gate yes/no) tests additivity. Cost: retraining; about 15 GPU-hours for the three dense arms, about 30 for the full 2 x 2. Precedent: Garnot et al. 2022 (p = 0.4, +0.6 mIoU on clean data and markedly more resilient to deleted dates), Mask-PSTIN (+3.5 OA, +0.047 mF1 over three regions), Wang/Belgiu DA-TD (+15 cropland-F1 in the hardest regime but −5 real-world on S4A, so the remedy can overfit the degraded regime), TimeMatch's 30-step random temporal subsampling, Sialelli et al.'s "simulate corruptions during training". Novel result: every precedent tests random date deletion inside the training distribution; none measures a dropout-trained model against a month that is absent from training and arrives at inference as an in-range constant. All three outcomes are publishable: dropout matches or beats the gate (the gate is redundant and the paper reports the first measurement of the standard remedy under missingness shift), the gate beats dropout (the gate has a niche), or the two add.

**2. Scoring the published predictions on September-observed versus September-missing fields (candidate h). Done.** `experiments/holdout_fill_check/results/sept_observed_subset.csv` already splits the 2,415 holdout fields into 1,352 with September observed and 1,063 without and scores every published prediction file on each: dense networks lead on the observed subset (CNN-BiLSTM 0.66, TempCNN 0.65, TabNet fourteenth at 0.62) and collapse to 0.21 to 0.36 on the missing subset where TabNet holds 0.58. This is the imputation-free check, because it involves no fill choice at all, and it belongs in the main text next to the before/after table, with paired bootstrap intervals per subset and the changed-field counts Che et al. recommend (equal aggregates there hid 8 to 13% of pixels changing class). Cost: zero. Precedent: Kapoor and Narayanan's L3 recommendation to report per-subset scores; Che et al. Novel result: a ranking that holds on one missingness stratum and inverts on the other inside a single holdout has not been shown in SITS.

**3. A per-model fill-operator information sheet in the supplement (candidate g).** One row per model in Table 1: fill operator at the missing month (zero, holdout column median, training-tile median, NaN-aware feature on the shortened series), the dataset the fill statistic came from, the order of fill and standardization, whether the model was retrained or re-scored, and whether the re-run reproduces the published predictions field for field (every deep model does; pixel TabNet differs on 221 of 2,415 fields because its published file predates its checkpoints; the rebuilt classical features differ on 85 fields; the xr_fresh `autocorr_lag_k` features are copies of `absolute_sum_of_changes`). All of it is in `experiments/holdout_fill_check/README.md` and `results/fill_comparison.csv`. Cost: zero. Precedent: Kapoor and Narayanan's 21-question model info sheet, which they show would have caught each civil-war case; Shwartz-Ziv and Armon's call to report tuning conditions alongside accuracy. Novel result: none by itself, but it is the instrument that makes the re-analysis framing credible.

**4. The missingness-shift framing and a label-free diagnostic (candidate k).** Question: could the artifact have been detected before any model was trained, and is the Results 4.3 error cluster the gap's footprint? Experiment: a per-month NaN-rate table for the two training tiles and the holdout at pixel and field level (from the gap mask built in `make_filled_holdout.py`), the fill rule stated in the data section, and a map of the September gap overlaid on the spatial error map in `out_of_sample/scoring_results/`. Optionally compute Wijesingha et al.'s dissimilarity index and area of applicability on the field feature vector with training-fitted statistics, to show that zero-filled fields fall outside the AOA and median-filled fields do not. Cost: zero to one CPU-hour. Precedent: Wijesingha (AOA share correlates 0.9 with OA across scenarios), Rusňák (composite quality tabulated per region-month-year and shown not to predict transfer loss when handled identically), Mohammadi's Limitation 4 (cloud-induced missingness an unresolved transfer confounder), Xie (temperature-profile similarity as a label-free predictor of transfer). Novel result: missingness shift shown as geography in a cross-region crop paper, and a diagnostic the reader can run on any new tile.

**5. A sweep of fill values (candidate i).** Question: is the training median special, or does any in-range value restore the ranking? Experiment: extend `make_filled_holdout.py` to emit holdout variants with zero (published), training mean, training median (done), holdout median (TabNet's old path), per-pixel linear interpolation from August and October, and deletion for the models that accept variable length (L-TAE and Transformer with a padding mask; TempCNN and CNN-BiLSTM cannot), then re-score the existing checkpoints with `run_deep_field.py`, `run_classical.py` and `run_ts_baselines.py`. Cost: inference-only, under one GPU-hour. Precedent: Wang/Belgiu (last, closest and linear interpolators under-perform sequence shortening by up to 12 F1), Che (interpolation changes OA by under 0.5 points but hurts by 1 to 5 when fewer than 30% of anchors are good), Pandžić (spline to a regular grid for both models), Yi (mean and median equal zero fill only when zero is already in range). Novel result: no SITS paper compares fill operators at inference under a train-complete, test-incomplete protocol; a dose-response in which zero is far worst and every in-range value is near-equal proves that the inserted value, not the gap, did the damage, and it pre-empts the objection that the median was chosen because it favours the deep models.

**6. Gradient attribution at the September slot (candidate d).** Experiment: Rußwurm and Körner's Section 8.3 recipe, a dy/dX backpropagation to the input for each raw-sequence model on the published zero-filled holdout and the median-filled holdout, reported as each month's share of absolute gradient, plus the column-mean attention of the L-TAE and Transformer on the training tiles (Obadić et al. Eq. 4 to 6) to show how much weight September carries. Checkpoints are those already used by `holdout_fill_check/run_deep_field.py`. Cost: inference-only, under one hour, no labels needed. Precedent: Rußwurm and Körner (LSTM and Transformer gradients vanish on cloudy dates, TempCNN's do not), Obadić (attention weights are one of several valid sparse explanations, so pair them with attribution). Novel result: a mechanism figure in which the September slot's attribution dominates under zero fill and falls back in line with the other months under median fill; it would also show whether the gated models suppress the slot, which is the claimed mechanism for Finding 2.

**7. A missingness indicator channel, or deletion with day-of-year encoding (candidate b).** Question: does Zhou et al.'s Proposition 1 hold empirically, so that with an indicator the shift becomes covariate shift and no fill value is needed? Experiment: (i) add a seventh observed/missing channel to TempCNN and CNN-BiLSTM and train with month dropout so the indicator is informative, which folds into item 1's runs; (ii) rebuild the L-TAE and Transformer inputs as (observation, month) tokens with a padding mask, as TimeMatch, SITS-BERT and Garnot et al. do, so the missing month is an absent token and Table 1 gains a "no imputation" row. Cost: retraining; about 10 GPU-hours for (ii) over two backbones and five seeds, and about 10 more for (i) if run alongside item 1. Precedent: Zhou Proposition 1, Lipton et al. 2016 (zeros plus indicators beat imputed values for LSTMs), Wang et al. 2024's MCTNet (a binary mask zeroes the positional encoding at missing steps; dropping the mask costs 0.038 macro-F1 in Arkansas, single run; a likely referee comparator because it appeared in this journal), Che et al. (masking layer and masked pooling with open code for both of our dense families), Zhao et al.'s GRU-D mask layer. Novel result: an empirical test of Proposition 1 on SITS and a "no imputation" row; this is the fix the paper will recommend, so showing it works is persuasive, but item 1 already supplies the control the gate needs.

**8. Sparsity Normalization applied at the September slot (candidate c).** Experiment: apply Yi et al.'s SN, rescaling the observed entries of each time step by K over the observed count, as an inference-time transform on the zero-filled holdout for the raw-sequence models. Cost: under one hour. Precedent: Yi et al. Figure 1 (the drift exists in the forward pass before training) but also their Table 6, where SN removed the bias on a PhysioNet LSTM without improving AUROC (0.8177 vs 0.8152). Novel result: it separates activation-scale damage (which SN repairs) from information loss (which it cannot), but the closest precedent is a null and item 5 answers most of the same question more directly.

**9. A tree plus dense ensemble on the filled holdout (candidate f).** Experiment: re-run `experiments/field_reduction/voting_hybrid_oos.py` and `stacking_consistent_oos.py`, and `out_of_sample/compare_predictions.py`, on the filled prediction files, adding members in validation-loss order as Shwartz-Ziv and Armon recommend (three suffice in their data). Cost: under one hour, no retraining. Precedent: Shwartz-Ziv and Armon (deep plus XGBoost best at 2.32% deterioration, deep ensemble without trees 6.91%), WorldCereal. Novel result: modest; it reports whether the submission ensemble beats the best single dense model (0.66) once every member sees the same inputs, which is the operational recommendation the paper closes on.

**10. Extending the learning curves to 5% and adding TabNet at 10 to 15% (candidate j).** The filled-holdout sweeps already cover 0.10 and 0.15 for L-TAE and L-TAE-S (`holdout_fill_check/results/sweep_deep_trainmed.csv`) and for TempCNN and CNN-BiLSTM (`sparse_gate_backbones/results_sweep.csv`), but TabNet only from 0.25 (`sweep_tabnet_trainmed.csv`). Experiment: add a 0.05 fraction (about 135 fields, about 15 per class) for every model and 0.10 and 0.15 for TabNet, ten draws each, through `holdout_fill_check/run_sweep.py` and `field_reduction/seed_sweep_tabnet.py`. Cost: retraining on small subsets, roughly 10 to 20 GPU-hours in total. Precedent: Schulthess (fewer than 30 fields per crop is unstable, which predicts seed variance at 5%), Mohammadi's few-shot DTW baseline, Cheng's 5-shot result, TimeMatch's 200-parcel minimum per class. Novel result: it locates the floor below the 0.55 convergence point and tests whether the crossover is sharp, at the risk that macro-F1 at 15 fields per class is dominated by minority-class noise.

**11. Source-free adaptation or masked pretraining on the unlabelled holdout pixels (candidate e).** Experiment: Mohammadi et al.'s SFUDA on the holdout pixels for L-TAE and Transformer, or Presto or SITS-BERT-style masked pretraining on unlabelled pixels from all three tiles followed by fine-tuning on 10% of fields, or TimeMatch's ShiftAug during source training. Cost: new code plus retraining, 20 to 50 GPU-hours or more. Precedent: Mohammadi (+9 to +20 macro-F1 cross-regionally), EuroCropsML (SSL pretraining lifts minority-class accuracy by 14 points at 500 shots), SITS-BERT (deep models beat RF at every label budget only after pretraining), Pandžić (fine-tuned Transformer 91.1 vs from-scratch 76.5 at 360 parcels). Novel result: it would say whether the 0.55 floor at 10% is a label-scarcity ceiling that pretraining lifts or a transfer ceiling it does not, but it requires target-pixel access and changes the paper's protocol and genre.

**12. SAR fusion and multi-year source training.** Both are raised repeatedly (Garnot: decision fusion keeps performance with 90% of optical dates gone; Pandžić: S1+S2 best for transfer; Mahmood: retained SAR features are VH from the one month with no optical scene; Rusňák: adding source years narrows the spatial gap by 2 to 5 OA points). Neither can be run on the present data and both belong in the discussion as the principled long-term answers to a missing month.

## Conclusion

The revision changes the paper's genre as much as its numbers. The old manuscript claimed a property of models (sparse selection transfers); the corrected one documents a property of pipelines (an inference-only imputation asymmetry can invert a 27-model ranking and flatten a learning curve) and then measures what remains once the pipeline is fixed: a modest, rank-stable deep advantage of 0.02–0.07 that matches BreizhCrops and WorldCereal, a sparse gate whose only robust effect is graceful degradation under a never-seen missing month, and a deep-over-classical crossover near 45 fields per class that the few-shot and RF-saturation literatures each predicted from opposite sides. That is a stronger contribution than the original because every piece has a dense control and a before/after table, and because no SITS paper has put the three ingredients (region-specific gap, constant fill before standardization, model families with and without native missing handling) together.

Three things must happen before any novelty sentence is written. The sparsemax gate has to be benchmarked against training-time month dropout, the standard remedy in Garnot et al., Mask-PSTIN and Wang/Belgiu and the formal prescription of Zhou et al.'s Algorithm 1; without that control a referee can reasonably say the +0.063 is what any augmentation would have bought (see "Ways to push this forward"). TabNet has to be removed from the robustness claim, since the same checkpoints lose 0.10 macro-F1 under zero fill once its median-fill advantage is taken away, which leaves the sparsemax gate on CNN-BiLSTM and L-TAE-S as the only evidence for Finding 2's robustness half and sharpens it (the softmax control fails where sparsemax holds). And the spatial error cluster in Results 4.3 should be overlaid on the September-gap map, because if it is the gap footprint, the paper gains a clean figure that shows missingness shift as geography, which no cross-region crop paper has shown either.
